# Fly Ash-GGBS Binary Binder System

## Definition

A binder system for strain-hardening cementitious composites (ECC/SHCC) and alkali-
activated composites (AAS/EGC) in which spherical fly ash (FA) and latent-hydraulic
ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) are blended as mutually complementary
substitutes for large-scale cement replacement.

## Complementary mechanisms

1. **Rheology and flow improvement (ball-bearing effect)**:
   - Spherical FA particles lower slurry viscosity and greatly improve flow, preventing
     fiber balling and promoting uniform dispersion.
2. **Reaction rate and early strength complementarity**:
   - GGBS's fast reactivity compensates the delayed early hydration and reduced early
     strength of FA-only systems, securing 3–7 day strength.
3. **Micromechanical PSH margin optimization**:
   - **Matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) control**: higher FA fraction moderates matrix
     toughness and lowers crack-tip energy ($J_{tip}$).
   - **Suppression of interfacial chemical bond ($G_d$) and increase of frictional bond
     ($\tau_0$)**: brittle chemical bonding between fiber and matrix is lowered while
     dense C-S-H / C-A-S-H gel formation establishes appropriate frictional shear stress,
     inducing complete pullout instead of fiber rupture.

## Representative mix ratios and reaction products

- Ordinary cementitious ECC: FA/cement ratio 1.2–4.0 (High-Volume Fly Ash).
- Low-cement / cement-free systems: FA : GGBS from 8:2 to 5:5.
- Principal hydration/geopolymerization products: composite C-A-S-H and N-A-S-H gel
  structures forming a dense fine pore structure.
