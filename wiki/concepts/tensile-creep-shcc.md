# Tensile Creep of SHCC

## Definition

Time-dependent deformation in which strain and crack width increase gradually over time
under a sustained tensile load.

## Behavior characteristics and mechanisms

1. **Viscoelastic creep of the uncracked state (basic tensile creep)**:
   - Deformation from interlayer water movement in C-S-H gel and viscoelastic slip within
     the cement matrix; behavior similar to tensile creep of ordinary concrete.
2. **Crack-bridging fiber creep in the cracked state**:
   - Once sustained load exceeds first-cracking strength and fine cracks form, crack
     openings widen over time through viscoelastic stretching of the polymer fibers
     (PVA, PE) spanning cracks and progressive interface creep debonding/slip between
     fiber and matrix.
3. **Formation of new cracks (time-dependent multiple cracking)**:
   - Under sustained load, matrix stress relaxation and subcritical crack growth at flaw
     tips can generate new delayed fine cracks over time without any load increase.

## Short-term versus long-term performance

- **Long-term crack width stability**: for PVA fibers, thanks to the hydrophilic
  interface bond and the fiber's own viscoelasticity, creep rate decays and stabilizes
  when load levels stay at or below 60–70 % of maximum bridging stress.
- **Creep rupture**: at high load levels (>80 % $\sigma_0$) interface slip accelerates,
  deformation concentrates into a single crack, and premature rupture may occur; long-term
  allowable tensile stress design must therefore include a creep reduction factor.

## Related parameters

- $\sigma_{sustained} / \sigma_0$: sustained load ratio
- $\epsilon_{cr}(t)$: time-dependent tensile creep strain
- $w_{cr}(t)$: time-dependent crack width growth (µm)
- $\phi(t, t_0)$: tensile creep coefficient
