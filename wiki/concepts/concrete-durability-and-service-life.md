# Concrete Durability and Service Life: Deterioration Mechanisms and Composite Life Prediction

## 1. Overview

The durability of concrete structures is the material and structural resistance capacity
that maintains required performance throughout the design service life under ingress of
aggressive ions (chlorides, carbon dioxide, sulfates), moisture movement, and freeze-thaw
cycling. Conventional RC structures reach premature deterioration as ingress accelerates
once cracks form; strain-hardening cementitious composites (ECC/SHCC/UHPC) can
dramatically extend service life through fine multiple-crack control ($< 100\ \mu\text{m}$)
and dense matrices.

## 2. Principal deterioration mechanisms and test methods

- **Rebar corrosion**:
  - Depassivation by chloride threshold or carbonation $\rightarrow$ electrochemical
    corrosion and rust ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O}$) expansion
    pressure $\rightarrow$ cover cracking and spalling.
  - Accelerated testing: impressed current technique (standard recommendation: current
    density $\le 200\ \mu\text{A/cm}^2$).
- **Carbonation**:
  - pH drop from ≈13 to ≈9 by atmospheric $CO_2$ diffusion and reaction with calcium hydroxide.
  - Variability control in accelerated tests: standard protocol of $CO_2$ concentration
    $3\sim 5\%$ and relative humidity $65 \pm 5\%$.
- **Transport properties**:
  - Gas permeability, capillary absorption, chloride diffusion coefficient. With crack
    widths controlled ≤ 100 µm, water-tightness approaches the uncracked-concrete level.
