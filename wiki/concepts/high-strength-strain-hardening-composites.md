# High-Strength Strain-Hardening Cementitious Composites (HS-SHCC / UHDCC)

## Summary

The advanced composite design theory and micromechanics framework that overcomes the
embrittlement limit imposed by rising matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) in high-strength
and ultra-high-strength cement matrices of 80–150 MPa, simultaneously achieving direct
tensile strength of 10–15 MPa and tensile strain capacity of 4–8 %.

## 1. The strain-hardening dilemma in high-strength matrices and its resolution

- **The micromechanical contradiction**:
  - As matrix strength rises (W/B 0.15–0.25), fracture toughness ($K_m$) and crack-tip
    energy ($J_{tip} \approx K_m^2/E_m$) surge.
  - Simultaneously interfacial bond ($\tau_0$) becomes excessive: fibers rupture in
    tension before pullout slip can occur, making the PSH energy condition
    ($J'_b/J_{tip} \ge 1.0$) extremely hard to satisfy.
- **Resolution strategies (UHDCC micromechanical optimization)**:
  1. **Ultra-high-strength hydrophobic PE fibers**: polyethylene short fibers at
     2700–3200 MPa eliminate chemical interface bonding and induce purely frictional slip
     pullout.
  2. **High aspect ratio ($L_f/d_f$) and diameter refinement**: fibers of 12–16 µm
     maximize bridging count density per volume ($N_f \propto 1/d_f^2$) and minimize
     scatter of bridging capacity.
  3. **Controlled first-cracking strength margin**: securing a strength index
     ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} \ge 2.0$) to induce saturated fine multiple cracking.

## 2. Representative performance and material indices

- **Compressive strength**: 80–160 MPa.
- **Uniaxial direct tensile strength**: 8.0–15.0 MPa.
- **Tensile strain capacity**: 4.0–7.9 % (400–800 times ordinary concrete).
- **Crack width control**: mean residual width at peak load 30–120 µm.
