# Self-Healing in ECC

## Definition

The autonomous healing phenomenon in which ECC/SHCC members that develop fine cracks
under mechanical or environmental loading, when exposed to moisture and carbon dioxide,
physically fill their cracks through spontaneous chemical reactions and recover
mechanical strength and water tightness without external repair injection.

## Physicochemical mechanisms

1. **Continued hydration of unhydrated binders**:
   - ECC mixes contain large quantities of mineral admixtures (fly ash, GGBS) to meet
     ductility and micromechanical requirements, at low water-to-binder ratio.
   - The matrix therefore retains abundant unhydrated cement and pozzolanic particles;
     when moisture penetrates a crack, they generate additional C-S-H gel that fills it.
2. **Calcium carbonate precipitation**:
   - Calcium hydroxide leached from pore solution reacts with dissolved CO_2 from air or
     water and precipitates as insoluble calcite:
     $$\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{CO}_3^{2-} \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3 \downarrow$$
   - The precipitated crystals nucleate on the fiber bridging network and tightly seal
     the crack gap.

## Essential coupling with crack width control

- The critical crack width for reliable 100 % healing is **$w \le 50\ \mu\text{m}$**
  (at most $100\ \mu\text{m}$).
- Ordinary concrete opens cracks to 200–500 µm or wider; healing agents wash out and
  self-healing is impossible.
- ECC, through its intrinsic multiple cracking, holds individual widths at 30–60 µm even
  at extreme tensile strain (3–5 %), naturally providing the environment for complete healing.

## Performance assessment metrics

- **Water permeability recovery**: zeroing of outflow in hydraulic testing.
- **Resonant frequency / dynamic modulus recovery**: NDT-based tracking of stiffness recovery.
- **Strength recovery under retensioning (tensile strength & ductility retention)**.
