# One-Part Strain-Hardening Geopolymer Composites (SHGC)

## Definition

Highly ductile strain-hardening cement-free composites (SHGC/EGC) manufactured by dry
mixing solid powder activators with precursors (fly ash, slag) and then simply adding
water ("just-add-water"), in contrast to conventional two-part geopolymers that require
highly concentrated, corrosive liquid alkaline activators (sodium hydroxide solution,
liquid sodium silicate).

## Technical advantages and material mechanisms

1. **Constructability and safety**:
   - No handling of hazardous caustic solutions or control of exothermic reactions on
     site; compatible with ordinary ready-mix and precast production lines.
2. **Tailored micromechanical PSH indices**:
   - Geopolymer matrices have lower fracture toughness ($K_m$) and lower chemical bonding
     to fibers ($G_d$) than ordinary cement pastes, forming excellent frictional bonds
     ($\tau_0$) with hydrophobic PE fibers as well as hydrophilic PVA fibers.
   - Energy index ($J'_b / J_{tip}$) and strength index ($\sigma_0 / \sigma_{fc}$)
     stably exceed 2.0, delivering high tensile strain capacity of 3–5 %.
3. **Carbon footprint reduction**:
   - With Portland cement excluded 100 % and industrial by-products (FA/GGBS) as main
     raw materials, CO_2 emissions fall 60–80 % relative to conventional ECC.

## Key mix design variables

- **Precursors**: fly ash (Class F), ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS).
- **Solid activators**: anhydrous sodium silicate powder
  ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}\cdot r\text{SiO}_2$), solid sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$),
  sodium carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$).
- **Reinforcing fibers**: high-strength PE fibers, PVA fibers.
