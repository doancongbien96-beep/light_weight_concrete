# Sustainability and Low-Carbon Binders

## Definition

The technology framework of eco-friendly binders and the highly ductile composites built
on them (EGC/SHCC), using industrial by-products, limestone calcined clay (LC³), and
alkali-activated slag/fly ash as replacements to slash the carbon dioxide emitted by
conventional Portland cement clinker production (about 7–8 % of global emissions).

## Principal low-carbon binder routes

1. **High-volume mineral admixture substitution (HVFA / High-Volume GGBS)**:
   - 50–80 % of cement replaced by fly ash and slag.
   - Long-term strength secured through pozzolanic and latent-hydraulic reactions while
     minimizing clinker use.
2. **Limestone Calcined Clay Cement ($\text{LC}^3$)**:
   - Clinker 50 % + calcined clay (metakaolin) 30 % + limestone powder 15 % + gypsum 5 %.
   - Alumina from clay and carbonate from limestone interact to form carboaluminate
     (hemicarboaluminate/monocarboaluminate) crystals that densely fill fine pores
     (~40 % $CO_2$ reduction).
3. **Cement-free geopolymer / alkali-activated materials (AAM)**:
   - With Portland cement eliminated 100 % and industrial by-products (FA/GGBS) as the
     main raw materials, embodied $CO_2$ falls far below conventional concrete.
4. **Circular economy integration (recycled aggregates and fibers)**:
   - Recycled fine aggregate (RFA), glass powder, and bottom ash replacing natural silica
     sand; waste PET and selvage fabric fibers as reinforcement.

## Embodied carbon and the performance balance

- **Embodied carbon reduction**: carbon intensity per unit volume
  ($\text{kg CO}_2/\text{m}^3$) falls far below conventional concrete.
- **Overcoming the ductility-durability paradox**: low-carbon mixes tend to lower matrix
  toughness — which actually makes the micromechanical PSH criterion ($J'_b/J_{tip}$)
  easier to satisfy, enabling tensile strain capacity above 3–5 % together with fine crack
  control.
