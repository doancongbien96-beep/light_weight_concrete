# Lunar ISRU Construction Materials

## Definition

Construction composites based on **In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)**: using lunar
regolith — infinitely available on the surface — as the primary raw material to build
lunar bases, landing pads, radiation shielding walls, and other space infrastructure,
minimizing the enormous payload transport cost from Earth.

## Principal materialization technology routes

1. **Waterless / low-water geopolymer (alkali-activated materials)**:
   - Lunar regolith is rich in silica ($\text{SiO}_2$), alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$),
     iron oxide ($\text{FeO}$), and calcium components, making it a suitable geopolymer
     precursor.
   - Combined with dry solid-activator mixing or closed-loop water recovery to cope with
     hard vacuum and water scarcity.
2. **Sulfur concrete**:
   - A thermoplastic composite binding regolith aggregate with molten sulfur (melting
     point 115–120 °C) using no water at all. Ultra-rapid strength gain on cooling
     (30–50 MPa within hours).
3. **Microwave / solar sintering**:
   - Binder-free solidification that heats regolith particle surfaces to 1100–1250 °C to
     melt and fuse them into high-strength ceramic/rock-like blocks.
4. **Basalt fiber reinforcement**:
   - Melt-spinning lunar basaltic regolith directly into high-strength continuous basalt
     fibers, used as composite reinforcement to impart ductility and impact resistance.

## Requirements imposed by the space environment

- **Ultra-high vacuum ($10^{-9}\ \text{Torr}$)**: prevent rapid evaporation/boiling of
  moisture and volatiles in binders.
- **Extreme diurnal temperature swings (-180 °C to +120 °C)**: resistance to thermal
  fatigue cracking from repeated expansion/contraction.
- **Radiation and micrometeorite shielding**: high mass shielding coefficients and
  highly ductile energy absorption.
