# Crack Width Control in ECC/SHCC

## Definition

A material-intrinsic property: even when total tensile strain grows to 3–5 % under
load or environmental deformation, the opening width of each individual crack does not
scale with load or strain but stays tightly capped at a fixed limit (typically
50–100 µm or below).

## Principle: steady-state cracking

1. **Flat crack mode**:
   - When the energy criterion ($J'_b \ge J_{tip}$) is met, the crack propagates flat,
     maintaining a constant steady-state opening displacement ($\delta_{ss}$) at its tip.
   - Even after a single crack has traversed the full section, elastic and frictional
     fiber bridging hold the width $w$ at or below $\delta_0$ (the maximum bridging displacement).
2. **Strain-independent crack width**:
   - In ordinary reinforced concrete (RC), crack width grows roughly linearly (0.2–1.0 mm)
     with tensile strain as rebar stress rises.
   - In ECC, when tensile strain increases 6-fold from 0.5 % to 3.0 %, individual crack
     widths barely grow; instead the number of fine cracks increases 6-fold.

## Consequences for durability

- **Suppression of water and chloride ingress**: at crack widths ≤ 100 µm the water
  permeability coefficient falls to nearly the uncracked-concrete level, and the chloride
  diffusion coefficient drops drastically, preventing rebar corrosion at its source.
- **Maximized self-healing**: widths ≤ 50 µm induce capillary moisture condensation and
  calcium carbonate precipitation, enabling fully complete healing.
- **Improved freeze-thaw resistance**: freezing expansion pressure of water inside fine
  cracks is buffered through the fiber bridging network, preventing spalling failure.

## Related parameters

- $w_{max}, w_{avg}$: maximum and mean crack width (µm)
- $\delta_{ss}$: steady-state crack opening displacement (µm)
- $K_m$: matrix fracture toughness ($\text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$)
- $J'_b$: complementary fiber-bridging energy (J/m²)
