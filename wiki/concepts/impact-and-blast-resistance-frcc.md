# Impact and Blast Resistance Mechanisms of FRCC / UHPFRC / ECC

## Summary

The core protective-structure concept covering strain-rate effects, dynamic fracture
mechanics, and layered protective design of high-strength steel-fiber composites
(UHPFRC) and highly ductile composites (ECC/HPFRCC), for safeguarding structural
integrity under extreme dynamic loads such as terrorist explosions, military projectile
impact, and high-velocity falling-object impact.

## 1. Classification of failure modes under impact and blast

1. **Local penetration and front-face spalling**: cone-shaped crushing and ejection of
   the front surface by the ultra-high-pressure shock wave at projectile contact.
2. **Rear-face scabbing**: when the compressive shock wave reflects off the rear free
   surface as a tension wave, rear concrete ejects as high-velocity fragments — the
   principal cause of personnel injury.
3. **Shear plugging**: a shear cone block around the penetration path is pushed out
   intact by the projectile's shear thrust.
4. **Perforation**: the limiting state in which the projectile exits through the member.

## 2. Hybrid and layered mechanisms for maximum protection

- **Asymmetric layering: front face ultra-high-strength (HS) + rear face ultra-high-
  ductility (HD)**:
  - **Front HS layer (UHPFRC, ~150 MPa)**: dissipates the projectile's kinetic energy and
    deforms/fragments the nose, minimizing penetration depth.
  - **Rear HD layer (ECC, 6–8 % tensile strain)**: absorbs the reflected rear tension wave
    through strong PE/PVA fiber bridging and dense fine multiple cracking, sealing out
    rear scabbing and perforation 100 %.
- **Dynamic Increase Factor (DIF)**:
  - As strain rate rises from quasi-static ($\dot{\varepsilon} \approx 10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1}$)
    to impact/blast regimes ($\dot{\varepsilon} \approx 10^1\sim 10^3\ \text{s}^{-1}$),
    the tensile-strength DIF rises 2.0–3.5x and fracture energy 3–5x.
