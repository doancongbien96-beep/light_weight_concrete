# Concrete: Microstructure, Properties, and Materials (Mehta & Monteiro, textbook)

## Summary

Standard textbook of concrete materials science co-authored by P. Kumar Mehta and Paulo J. M. Monteiro (both UC Berkeley). The source PDF (239 pp., LaTeX typeset, good text layer — about 1 million characters) is an **author-typeset revision dated 2001-10-20**, carrying both the first-edition front matter (including a foreword by Ben C. Gerwick, Jr.) and a 'Preface to the Second Edition'. According to that preface, the revision keeps the three-part organization while rewriting Chapter 4 (dimensional stability) in full and adding a new Chapter 12 (advances in concrete mechanics). Not a research paper, so it is recorded as a reference-book note.

**Edition remark**: the file itself carries no publisher or ISBN marking and no 'Third Edition' string. The formally published book appeared from McGraw-Hill as the 1st (1986), 2nd (1993), and 3rd (2006) editions, but which edition this typeset draft corresponds to cannot be pinned down from the file alone — consult the original before citing a precise edition or page numbers.

## Main Table of Contents (3 parts, 13 chapters)

**I. Structures and Properties of Hardened Concrete**

1. Introduction to Concrete — concrete as a structural material, constituents, types of concrete
2. The Structure of Concrete — three-phase structure of aggregate, paste, and the **ITZ** (interfacial transition zone); microstructure of hydrated cement paste
3. Strength — strength-porosity relationship, influence of the ITZ on strength, tensile and compressive strength
4. Dimensional Stability — elasticity and creep (viscoelasticity), drying shrinkage and thermal expansion, crack types
5. Durability — permeability; chloride, sulfate, and alkali-aggregate reaction; freeze-thaw; steel corrosion

**II. Concrete Materials, Mix Proportioning, and Early-Age Properties**

6. Hydraulic Cements — Portland cement manufacture, composition, and hydration; heat of hydration; blended and specialty hydraulic cements
7. Aggregates
8. Admixtures
9. Proportioning Concrete Mixtures — mix design procedure with example calculations
10. Concrete at Early Ages — setting and hardening; early-age cracking (including plastic settlement cracking and crazing)

**III. Recent Advances and Concrete in the Future**

11. Progress in Concrete Technology — lightweight, heavyweight, high-strength, flowable, shrinkage-compensating, **fiber-reinforced (FRC)**, mass concrete, roller-compacted concrete
12. Advances in Concrete Mechanics — chapter newly added in this revision
13. The Future of Concrete

## Position in the Wiki

- A textbook from the 'structure-property correlation' materials-science perspective: the fundamental reference behind the microstructure (especially ITZ), durability, and dimensional stability descriptions cited by the wiki's ECC/EGC/UHDCC papers.
- Chapter 2's ITZ structure theory and Chapter 3's ITZ-strength discussion are the background origin for interface-oriented papers.
- Chapter 11's FRC and high-strength concrete sections provide context for the fiber-reinforced ductile cement composite paper family.
- Not a deep-read target; used for citing specific chapters from individual paper notes.
