# Novel Applications of Distributed Fiber-Optic Sensing in Geotechnical Engineering (ETH Doctoral Dissertation)

## Summary

Michael Iten's ETH Zurich doctoral dissertation (279 pp) applying Brillouin distributed fiber-optic sensing (BOTDA, and Brillouin Echo Distributed Sensing, BEDS, with improved spatial resolution) to geotechnical monitoring. Because it is a dissertation rather than a research paper, it is treated as a reference-type note; its bibliography and structure were established through table-of-contents and abstract-level reading.

## Main Contents

- Part I (technical foundations): optical fiber and scattering principles, BOTDA/BEDS techniques, methods for interpreting BOTDA data around fixed points (single-point selection, truncated averaging, convolution analysis), and development and calibration of strain sensing cables (S06/P07/M07/S08 series — references/iten-2012-strain-sensing-cables.md is the technical-document version of this chapter).
- Part II (soil–structure interaction): pull-out tests on soil-embedded cables (Tessin sand, BEDS instrumentation), fabrication, installation, and pull-out testing of BOTDA-monitored ground anchors in the field, and a simplified analytical model of the shear stress distribution — measured evidence of progressive failure, in which residual shear stress grows with increasing pull-out load.
- Part III (landslide monitoring): road-embedded, soil-embedded, and borehole-embedded sensors installed in the Brattas and Laret areas of St. Moritz, Switzerland, to assess landslide boundaries. The road-embedded sensor detected an additional shear zone, whose location was subsequently confirmed as a water-main rupture. The Laret soil-embedded sensor independently reconfirmed seasonal displacement patterns alongside inclinometers.
- Summary of contributions: drastic improvement of spatial resolution with BEDS, participation in the development of commercial sensing cables, and establishment of cable-integration techniques and data interpretation options.
