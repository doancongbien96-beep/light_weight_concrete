## Summary
A comprehensive review that consolidates the theory, experimental techniques, and constitutive models for the tensile creep, tensile stress relaxation, and post-cracking pull-out slip creep (crack bridging creep) behavior of short-fiber-reinforced cementitious composites (FRC) and strain-hardening high-performance fiber composites (HPFRCC/ECC). The matrix viscoelastic viscous flow mechanism in the uncracked state and the fiber-matrix interfacial slip creep in the multiple fine cracking state were separately formalized, and the long-term durability of composites under sustained load and prediction models for the creep rupture limit were systematized.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a two-stage multiscale mechanism model for FRC tensile creep**:
  1. Stage 1 (uncracked): viscoelastic creep of the matrix driven by interlayer water movement in C-S-H gel.
  2. Stage 2 (crack bridging): shear-friction slip creep at the fiber-matrix interface under sustained tensile stress.
- **Definition of the critical creep load ratio ($\sigma_{sus}/f_t$)**: quantification of the finding that ratios up to $0.60$ reach stable secondary creep, whereas exceeding $0.75$ carries the risk of premature failure through tertiary creep caused by progressive fiber pull-out.
- **Analysis of early crack prevention through restraint relief**: formulation of the principle whereby the stress relaxation capacity of tensile creep relieves 30 ~ 50 % of the restrained tensile stress.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of some 150 publications on tensile creep experiments and theoretical modeling.
- Comparison of the viscoplastic Burger model, the Kelvin chain model, and micromechanics-based creep bridging models.

## Results
- It was revealed that the interfacial frictional characteristics and aspect ratio of the fibers govern long-term tensile creep rupture resistance, and reliability-based design guidelines under sustained service loads were presented.
