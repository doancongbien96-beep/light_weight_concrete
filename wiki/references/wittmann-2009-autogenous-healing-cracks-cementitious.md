## Summary
A RILEM-based classic review paper systematizing the intrinsic autogenous healing mechanisms of fine cracks in cementitious materials, the physicochemical reaction kinetics, and the experimental measurement techniques for quantitatively evaluating healing efficiency. The four major autogenous healing mechanisms — (1) secondary delayed hydration of unhydrated clinker, (2) precipitation of $CaCO_3$ crystals by reaction of leached $Ca^{2+}$ with dissolved $CO_2$, (3) mechanical blocking of pores by fragmented particles, and (4) swelling of C-S-H gel — are compared quantitatively, and standardized procedures for water-tightness recovery permeability tests and ultrasonic nondestructive inspection are presented.

## Key Contributions
- **Contributions of the four major physicochemical autogenous healing mechanisms clarified**: calcium carbonate precipitation was proven stoichiometrically to dominate more than 80 % of long-term crack closure under atmospheric exposure and underwater environments.
- **Healable critical crack width defined**: the maximum crack width limit for complete healing under underwater environments is given as $w_{max} \approx 50 \sim 100 \mu m$.
- **Quantitative indicators of water-tightness and mechanical stiffness recovery established**: permeability reduction ratio ($\eta_k = 1 - k/k_0$) and resonant frequency recovery indices standardized.

## Methods
- Comprehensive analysis of some 100 autogenous healing publications with chemical thermodynamic equilibrium interpretation.
- Comparison of optical microscopy, head-differential permeability test, and ultrasonic resonant frequency test data.

## Results
- It was confirmed that autogenous healing reliability is maximized in high-ductility ECC maintaining crack widths of at most $50 \mu m$ and in concrete with high replacement of mineral admixtures.
