# Alkali-Activated Cements and Concretes (textbook)

## Summary

Classic textbook in the field of alkali-activated cements and concretes co-authored by Caijun Shi (Central South University/CJS Technology), Pavel V. Krivenko (Kyiv University of Construction and Architecture), and Della Roy (Professor Emerita, Penn State) (Taylor & Francis 2006; body 13 chapters + references to p. 334 + index to p. 370; PDF 388 pages). Not a research paper, so recorded as a reference-type note. Centered on alkali-activated slag (AAS), it is an integrating monograph running from activator chemistry through structural applications and standards, and serves as the theoretical background source for the alkali-activated EGC/SHCC papers in this wiki.

## Main Table of Contents (13 chapters)

1. Introduction — history (timeline from Kuhl 1930 and Purdon 1940 onward)
2. Alkaline activators — NaOH, Na2CO3, Na2SiO3 (water glass), Na2SO4: properties and reaction behavior of each activator
3. Cementing components — blast-furnace slag, phosphorus slag, steel slag, pozzolans
4. Hydration and microstructure of AAS cements — heat of hydration, degree of reaction, hydration products, C-S-H composition, pore solution chemistry, activator selectivity
5. Properties of AAS pastes/mortars — workability, setting, strength, shrinkage, porosity-strength relationship
6. Properties of AAS concrete — placeability, strength, stress-strain, fracture, interfacial transition zone, creep
7. Durability — water permeability·chloride transport, freezing, acid·sulfate attack, carbonation, AAR, rebar corrosion, fire resistance
8. Mix design of AAS concrete — empirical methods and design of experiments
9. Alkali-activated portland blended cements
10. Alkali-activated lime-pozzolan cements — lime-fly ash, lime-metakaolin
11. Other systems — metakaolin/fly ash (geopolymer family), BFS-fly ash, BFS-MgO
12. Applications and case studies — structural AAS concrete in China, Mariupol bricks·pavement in Ukraine, foamed concrete, oil-well cement, radioactive waste immobilization
13. Standards and specifications
