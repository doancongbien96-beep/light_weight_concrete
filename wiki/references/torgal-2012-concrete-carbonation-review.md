## Summary

This review paper comprehensively analyzes the dual influence of rising atmospheric greenhouse gas ($CO_2$) concentrations and climate change on the rate of carbonation progress in reinforced concrete structures, on the corrosion service life of reinforcement, and on the lifecycle $CO_2$ reabsorption (carbon uptake) of concrete. It organizes the physicochemical reaction mechanism by which $Ca(OH)_2$ and C-S-H gel react with $CO_2$ to convert into $CaCO_3$, together with the influences of relative humidity (maximum reaction rate at $RH = 55 \sim 65 \%$) and pore structure, and presents assessment models for the carbonation resistance of concrete incorporating industrial by-products.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of a carbonation acceleration model under rising global-warming $CO_2$ concentrations**: predicts that an increase in atmospheric $CO_2$ concentration (400 ppm $\rightarrow$ 800 ppm) shortens the corrosion initiation period of reinforcement by 25 ~ 35 %.
- **Carbonation vulnerability of SCM-blended concrete and countermeasures**: presents cover thickness and surface coating criteria to compensate for the pH reduction caused by the loss of the $Ca(OH)_2$ buffer.
- **Quantification of the permanent carbon capture capacity of concrete**: establishes that up to 15 ~ 25 % of the $CO_2$ emitted during the lifecycle of structures and of recycled aggregates from demolished concrete is reabsorbed.

## Methods

- Meta-analysis of some 120 publications on carbonation reaction kinetics, climate change scenarios, and durability modeling.
- Analysis of the Fick's-first-law-based carbonation depth equation ($x = k \sqrt{t}$).

## Results

- Emphasizes that while carbonation somewhat improves matrix strength, it destroys the reinforcement passivation film (pH falling below 9), making crack width control and formation of a dense cover layer essential when designing highly durable composites.
