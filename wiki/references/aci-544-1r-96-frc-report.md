# ACI 544.1R-96: State-of-the-Art Report on Fiber Reinforced Concrete (FRC)

## Summary

Standard comprehensive report (66 pp.) on fiber reinforced concrete (FRC) prepared by ACI Committee 544 of the American Concrete Institute (committee chair: James I. Daniel). It covers the basic principles of FRC, in which discontinuous short fibers are incorporated into a brittle cement matrix to improve crack control, flexural toughness, impact resistance, and fatigue durability, together with terminology definitions, the characteristics of the 4 major fiber families (steel, glass, synthetic, and natural fibers), mix design, and construction and structural design considerations. The foundational standard guidance document of the FRC field.

## Key Themes

1. **Characteristics and classification by fiber type (organized in 5 chapters)**:
   - **Steel fiber reinforced concrete (SFRC)**: post-cracking residual flexural toughness, shear reinforcement, and shotcrete and industrial floor slab design.
   - **Glass fiber reinforced concrete (GFRC)**: alkali-resistant (AR) glass fibers, precast facade panels, and thin-sheet elements.
   - **Synthetic fiber reinforced concrete (SNFRC)**: plastic shrinkage crack control and impact absorption using polypropylene (PP), nylon, polyethylene (PE), and PVA fibers.
   - **Natural fiber reinforced concrete (NFRC)**: low-cost building applications of sisal, jute, and cellulose fibers.
2. **Mix design and construction control**: adjustment of aggregate grading for fiber aspect ratio ($L_f/d_f$) and balling prevention, mixing sequence, and pumped-placement construction guidance.
3. **Standardized mechanical performance evaluation**: determination of ASTM C1018 flexural toughness indices ($I_5, I_{10}, I_{20}, I_{30}$) and quantification of post-cracking energy absorption capacity.
