# FHWA concrete rheology and workability final report

## Summary

Final report performed by the US Army Engineer Research and Development Center (ERDC) Structures Laboratory under a Federal Highway Administration contract (DTFH61-96-Y-30076; conducted October 1996 through May 1999, published April 2001). Addressing methods for quantitatively measuring the workability of low-slump concrete with slump of at most 51 mm, it organizes the principles and limitations of existing measurement devices from the literature survey (Powers-Wiler, Tattersall two-point tester (Colebrand), BML viscometer, BTRHEOM rheometer, free-orifice rheometer, K-slump tester, etc.) and laboratory-evaluated four candidate approaches: two moving-object schemes, one free-orifice scheme, and one vibrating-slope scheme. The vibrating slope apparatus (VSA), which yields rheological parameters (values corresponding to yield stress and viscosity) at two or more shear rates, was selected finally; portable hardware and data acquisition software were developed, a discharge-chute form of field evaluation validated a workability index, and extension to an embedded system is proposed.
