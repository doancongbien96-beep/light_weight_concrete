# Comparative Analysis of the Strength, Stiffness, and Damping Characteristics of Concrete with Rubber, Latex, and Carbonate Additives (Bowland Doctoral Dissertation, 2011)

## Summary

Doctoral dissertation of Adam G. Bowland at Virginia Tech (advisor: Finley A. Charney, 351 pages), which systematically tested and modeled the compressive strength, elastic modulus, dynamic modulus, and structural damping ratio (Damping Ratio, $\zeta$) of damping-specialized concretes incorporating crumb rubber powder, styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) latex polymer emulsion, and fine calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) powder to suppress earthquake- and wind-induced vibration of structures.

## Key Themes

1. **Comparison of mechanical and damping behavior by damping admixture type**:
   - **Crumb rubber**: viscoelastic particle behavior raises the damping ratio substantially (2–3× the control), but weakened aggregate interfacial bonding sharply reduces compressive strength and elastic modulus.
   - **Latex emulsion**: formation of a continuous polymer film improves tensile/flexural strength and interfacial bond while achieving a moderate increase in damping ratio.
   - **Fine calcium carbonate powder (carbonate)**: fills micro voids and induces interfacial friction damping, improving damping capacity marginally without strength loss.
2. **Comparison of dynamic damping measurement techniques**: verification of the precision and frequency dependence of the resonance frequency test (ASTM C215), impact-hammer modal testing, and free-vibration logarithmic decrement measurement.
3. **Structural dynamic-response modeling**: numerical seismic response analysis of multistory RC buildings applying damping concrete quantifies the reduction of inter-story drift and acceleration response.
