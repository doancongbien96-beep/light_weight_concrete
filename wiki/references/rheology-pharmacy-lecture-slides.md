# Pharmacy Rheology Lecture Slides

## Summary

Introductory lecture slides on rheology (63 slides). Not a research paper, so recorded as a reference-type entry. The content comprises: (1) definition (rheo=flow + logos=science, the discipline dealing with flow and deformation under stress), (2) Newton's law of flow and viscosity (unit poise, cgs = g/(cm·s)), fluidity, kinematic viscosity, and relative viscosity, (3) three classes of non-Newtonian systems — plastic flow (Bingham body, where the yield value f is a measure of the flocculated state), pseudoplastic flow (shear thinning, polymer dispersions), and dilatant flow (shear thickening, in which the particle-packing structure of high-solids-content suspensions breaks down under shear and resistance increases — reversible), (4) thixotropy (viscosity decreases with time under shear and recovers at rest, a gel-sol transition; two measurement approaches via coefficients B and M; negative thixotropy, rheopexy, antithixotropy), (5) four types of viscometers — capillary (Ostwald), falling-ball, cup-and-bob (Stormer), and cone-and-plate (Ferranti-Shirley) — each including numerical examples, and (6) pharmaceutical applications (fluidity of injectables, ointment spreading and tube extrusion, tablet-filling powder flow, etc.). No concrete content.
