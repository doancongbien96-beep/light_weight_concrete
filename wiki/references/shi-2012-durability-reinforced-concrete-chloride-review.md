## Summary
The top-authority review paper comprehensively covering the mechanism of chloride-induced rebar corrosion in reinforced concrete (RC) exposed to marine environments and deicing salts on road structures, the critical chloride concentration for passive film breakdown ($C_{crit}$), numerical diffusion models based on Fick's second law, and corrosion protection technologies (mineral admixtures, corrosion inhibitors, epoxy coatings, fiber-reinforced matrix overlays). It systematized that densification of the concrete microstructure and crack-width control are the key factors delaying the initiation of rebar corrosion by decades or more.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a four-stage model of the full rebar corrosion process**: (1) chloride penetration diffusion stage, (2) passive film breakdown initiation stage, (3) rust-expansion crack propagation stage, (4) structural capacity degradation stage.
- **Standardization of the critical chloride concentration ($C_{crit} = 0.05 \sim 0.10 \%$ by binder weight)**: analysis of chloride binding by cement hydrates (Friedel's salt).
- **Systematization of the standard algorithm for predicting the service life of structures (Life-365 model)**: presents service-life design guidelines by environmental exposure condition.
