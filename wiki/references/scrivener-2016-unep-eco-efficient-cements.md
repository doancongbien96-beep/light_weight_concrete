# UNEP Eco-Efficient Cements Report (Scrivener · John · Gartner, 2016)

## Summary

Final consensus report of the working group of some 20 academic and industry experts that UNEP-SBCI convened in 2015. Whereas the 2009 IEA/WBCSD cement roadmap leaned on CCS (carbon capture and storage), the central claim here is that **a substantial share of CO₂ mitigation is achievable through materials-based solutions alone, and more cheaply than CCS**. Starting from the premises that cement is the largest manufactured product on Earth by mass and concrete the second most consumed material after water, the report evaluates every low-carbon technology available within planetary geochemical constraints (the Ca–Si–Al oxide system) and quantifies estimates up to the 2050 mitigation potential.

## Main Conclusions

Two levers can sharply reduce the need for CCS investment over the next 20–30 years:

1. **Expanded clinker substitution (SCM)** — today's average cement carries ~20 % SCM (limestone · GBFS · fly ash). Quality GBFS and FA are supply-limited to 15–25 % of total cement consumption. The calcined clay + crushed limestone combination (calcined clay + limestone, the LC³ family) can replace up to 50 % of clinker. Reaching an average substitution rate of 40 % would **avoid up to 400 Mt CO₂ per year**.
2. **More efficient use of clinker** — mix optimization alone improves eco-efficiency (CO₂ per m³ per MPa) by **up to 4×** between worst and best practice. Particle-packing optimization + dispersants + filler exploitation, matching high-strength grades to the right applications, and industrializing production (ready-mix · dry mortars).

## Technology Assessments and Key Figures

- **GBFS**: ~330 Mt per year worldwide; its share of cement production fell from 17 % in 1980 to 8 % in 2014 (rising electric-arc-furnace scrap). Already more than 90 % is consumed as SCM → almost no additional mitigation headroom. A byproduct that sells at a premium over cement clinker, not a waste.
- **Fly ash**: power-plant ash runs ~900 Mt per year, but quality scatter means only 1/3 is exploited. North America faces an FA shortage driven by shale gas.
- **AAM**: slag- and FA-based binders can deliver **40–80 % reductions** versus OPC, but feedstock supply is the bottleneck. Alkali-activated calcined clay is scalable; however, at a 15 % market share in 2050 the sodium silicate requirement, ~250 Mt, would equal **42×** current production → success hinges on low-carbon silicate production technology.
- **BYF (belite-ye'elimite-ferrite) clinker**: the most realistic alternative hydraulic clinker. Constrained by ye'elimite: even devoting the entire ye'elimite supply to BYF caps output at ~650 Mt of cement.
- **Carbonation-cured cement (CCSC)**: net CO₂ up to **−70 %**, with partial commercialization. Yet it is confined to factory products and requires captured CO₂ embedded in a circular economy → difficult as a global mainstay for construction at large, where onsite casting is essential (modeled with a 15 % market-share ceiling).
- **Ultrahigh-iron rock (MOMS) clinker**: the only family that could in principle be carbon-negative, but the industrial process remains uninvented — a breakthrough candidate worth researching.
- **Geochemical logic (§6)**: 98 % of the crust consists of the eight elements O·Si·Al·Fe·Ca·Na·K·Mg. For hydrates to fill the water-filled space, only Si·Ca·Al oxide systems (~90 % of OPC) satisfying ionic mobility and solubility conditions qualify. Limestone decarbonation (CaCO₃→CaO+CO₂) accounts for **~60 %** of cement CO₂ — because chemistry rather than fuel is the problem, compositional change alone underwrites low-cost mitigation, which is the basis for optimism.

## Mitigation Potential Estimate (§14)

- Baseline scenario: IEA ETP 2016 2DS low-demand — cement at 4556 Mt in 2050; of the total 900 Mt CO₂ reduction target, existing measures (alternative fuels · energy efficiency · fillers at 10 % including existing SCMs) contribute 347 Mt + **CCS 552 Mt**.
- The report's claim: the limestone filler + calcined clay combination (clinker ≥50 %, calcined clay 25–35 % + limestone 15 %) together with dispersant-assisted engineered fillers can reach **potential on the scale of what the IEA scenario assigns to CCS** → the argument for drastically curtailing CCS dependence.
- Emission factors (kg CO₂/kg): clinker+gypsum 0.86, calcined clay 0.2–0.35, limestone filler 0.008, sodium silicate 0.90–1.8, RBPc 0.79, BYF 0.62–0.66, CCSC 0.54. ("chemical" CO₂ = 0.507 kg CO₂/kg clinker)

## Standards, Education, and Government Roles

- Recommendations: shift to performance-based standards, coordinate international joint research, industrialize supply chains (curbing informal dredged-aggregate markets, limiting bagged cement as in the Chinese case), and leverage government procurement power.
