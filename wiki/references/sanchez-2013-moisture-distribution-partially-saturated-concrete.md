## Summary
A review paper that systematically examines a range of non-destructive and destructive experimental measurement techniques for capturing the moisture distribution inside concrete and the relative humidity (RH) profile — the key deterioration-environment factor governing the carbonation, chloride ingress, freeze-thaw, and reinforcing steel corrosion rates of concrete structures. It compares and analyzes the measurement principles, spatial resolution, accuracy, and field-application limitations of embedded humidity sensors, neutron radiography, electrical resistivity mapping, time-domain reflectometry (TDR), and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR).

## Key Contributions
- **Classification of six major moisture measurement techniques and comparison of their advantages and disadvantages**: evaluation of the spatial resolution and sensitivity of electrical, electromagnetic, radiographic, and thermal measurement methods.
- **Systematization of desorption isotherm models relating relative humidity ($RH$) and moisture saturation ($S_w$)**: Kelvin equation and moisture equilibrium analysis according to pore size distribution.
- **Quantitative linkage to reinforcement corrosion rate prediction**: presents the mechanism behind the sharp surge in corrosion current density in the critical relative humidity ($RH > 75 \%$) range.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of some 100 publications on moisture measurement and durability analysis.
- Review of steady-state and unsteady-state drying/absorption transport equations (Fick diffusion and Richard transport models).

## Results
- Shows that electrical resistivity and TDR sensors are the most promising for long-term field structural health monitoring (SHM), while neutron radiography and NMR are best suited to elucidating pore-scale moisture movement in the laboratory.
