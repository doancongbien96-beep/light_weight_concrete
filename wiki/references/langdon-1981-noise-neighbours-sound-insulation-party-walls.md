## Summary

A classic review paper from the UK Building Research Establishment (BRE) that precisely established, via a large-scale social survey combined with field measurements of airborne sound insulation performance (sound transmission loss, STL), the correlation between the sound insulation performance of party walls separating dwellings in apartment blocks and terraced housing and residents' complaints about neighbour noise. It analyzes how wall mass per unit area (mass law, $kg/m2$), cavity double-wall construction, and flanking transmission paths affect insulation performance, and was the first study in the world to statistically establish the minimum acoustic attenuation index ($D_{nT,w} \ge 52 dB$) required for more than 80 % of residents to be satisfied.

## Key Contributions
- **Established dose-response curves linking insulation performance and resident annoyance**: quantified the rate of reduction in noise complaint rates per 1 dB improvement in insulation performance.
- **Systematized the mass-law behavior of single walls versus the resonance-based insulation characteristics of cavity double walls**: presented the principle by which the resonance frequency of double walls is reduced through air cavities and absorbent infill.
- **Demonstrated the necessity of flanking transmission control**: identified the mechanism by which performance degrades through gap transmission at floor/external-wall junctions even when the party wall itself performs well.
