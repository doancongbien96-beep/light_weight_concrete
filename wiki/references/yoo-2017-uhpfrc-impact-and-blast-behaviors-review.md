## Summary
The most authoritative review article in the global protective and blast-resistance field, comprehensively covering the dynamic compressive/tensile behavior of ultra-high-performance fiber-reinforced concrete (UHPFRC) under extreme dynamic loads from impact (drop weight, high-velocity projectile) and explosion (close-range contact explosion, far-field air blast), the dynamic increase factor ($DIF$), strain-rate effects ($\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4} \sim 10^3 s^{-1}$), local penetration depth, suppression of rear-face spalling (scabbing), and the failure mechanisms of shear/flexural protective panels. The review compared the dynamic pull-out bridging efficiency of straight, hooked, and twisted steel fibers and established a blast-resistant structural design framework.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a standard broadband dynamic increase factor ($DIF$) envelope for UHPFRC**: formulation of the strain-rate dependence of compressive and tensile $DIF$.
- **Elucidation of the mechanism blocking fragmentation by explosion pressure waves and scattering of rear-face fragments**: at a fiber volume fraction of $V_f \ge 2.0 \%$, rear-face spalling under explosion was prevented 100 %.
- **Presentation of integrated engineering models for predicting local damage (cratering, penetration, rear-face spalling)**: provision of practical design equations for protective and blast-resistant structures.
