## Summary

A review paper offering a comprehensive examination of the historical development of cement and concrete materials science, molecular- and nano-scale hydration reaction kinetics, microstructure characterization techniques (high-resolution XRD, synchrotron X-ray, NMR, atomic force microscopy AFM), and the past, present, and future outlook of multi-scale mechanical modeling. The paper analyzes the paradigm shifts in materials science spanning elucidation of the nanostructure of C-S-H gel, ultra-high-performance fiber composites (UHPC/ECC), carbon-neutral binders ($LC^3$, geopolymers), and digital concrete fabrication, and presents a roadmap for the development of sustainable future infrastructure materials.

## Key Contributions

- **Consolidated history of C-S-H nanoscale hydrate structure models**: Jennite-Tobermorite layer models and atomic-scale bonding analyses.
- **Establishment of a multi-scale computational materials science framework**: integrated linkage of molecular dynamics (MD) $\rightarrow$ micromechanics $\rightarrow$ continuum structural analysis.
- **Definition of the three core pillars of twenty-first-century concrete research**: (1) decarbonized low-carbon binders, (2) ultra-ductile and ultra-durable composites, (3) the science of 3D-printing additive manufacturing.
