## Summary
The most authoritative state-of-the-art review comprehensively covering precursor selection, nano/micro particle packing optimization, alkali activator modulus control, single PE fiber interfacial friction slip engineering, high-temperature fire resistance, impact resistance, and life-cycle carbon reduction effects of ultra-high-strength strain-hardening geopolymer composites (HS-EGC) that simultaneously exhibit compressive strength above 100 MPa and direct tensile strain capacity of at least 5.0 %. It systematized that HS-EGC cuts embodied carbon by 75 % relative to cement-based HS-ECC while providing equal or greater ultra-high strength-ultra-high ductility, making it a core solution for future carbon-neutral resilient infrastructure.

## Key Contributions
- **Three baseline PSH micromechanics design criteria for HS-EGC established**: matrix fracture toughness $K_m \le 0.65 MPa\sqrt{m}$, interfacial frictional stress $\tau_0 = 1.8 \sim 2.5 MPa$, and the zero chemical bond principle.
- **Mix database for simultaneous high strength-ultra-high ductility constructed**: mapping of the compressive strength-tensile ductility interrelationship across some 80 HS-EGC mixes worldwide.
- **Roadmap for seismic, blast-resistant, marine, and 3D printing applications presented**: field quality management standards proposed for practical deployment.
