## Summary
An authoritative review paper comprehensively covering the direct tensile strain-hardening behavior of high-performance fiber-reinforced cementitious composites (HPFRCC / ECC / UHPFRC), fiber-matrix interface micromechanics, mix design factors, and test evaluation standards. It meta-analyzes the effects of fiber type (steel, PVA, PE, carbon, hybrid) and matrix characteristics (water-binder ratio, SCM incorporation, aggregate size) on first-cracking strength, ultimate tensile strength, tensile strain capacity, and multiple fine-crack spacing, and presents standardization guidance for grip methods and gauge length in uniaxial direct tensile testing.

## Key Contributions
- **The three core indicators of HPFRCC tensile behavior systematized**: correlations among first-cracking strength ($\sigma_{fc}$), ultimate tensile strength ($\sigma_{tu}$), and tensile strain capacity ($\epsilon_{tu}$) organized.
- **PSH micromechanics criteria (energy and strength conditions) synthesized**: global applicability analysis of the $J_b'/J_{tip} > 3.0$ and $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} > 1.2$ design principles.
- **Direct tensile test methodology standardization compared**: deviation factors identified among JSCE dogbone, RILEM rectangular plate, and fixed-end versus pin-end grip test results.
