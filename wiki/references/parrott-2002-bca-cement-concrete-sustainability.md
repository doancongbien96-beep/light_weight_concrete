# BCA Cement & Concrete Sustainability Report (Les Parrott, 2002)

## Summary

An industrial environmental report issued in 2002 by the British Cement Association (BCA). Not a research paper: it organizes the resource, energy, and emissions data of the UK cement and concrete industry against a policy indicator framework (the DETR sustainable development indicators) and quantifies how large a share of the UK's overall environmental burden the concrete industry represents. Core message: **building operation (31 %) and transport (31 %), far more than concrete itself, account for most of the UK environmental burden**; the priority for sustainable construction is therefore better design of buildings and transport infrastructure, with concrete able to act as a tool of that improvement.

## Main Contents

### Policy Context
- DETR 1996 "Indicators of Sustainable Development": 120 indicators in 21 families → the 1999 "Quality of life counts" baseline → the Environment Agency's 2000 "Environment 2000 and beyond".
- The five contributions of the construction industry ("Building a better quality of life"): profitability, customer value, stakeholder engagement, respect for the natural environment, and minimization of carbon/energy and resource use.

### The UK Cement Industry
- Per-capita cement consumption ~210 kg/year (half the European average). Industry turnover ~£4 bn, direct employment ~27,000.
- ISO 14001/EMAS certification at every cement plant. In return for the climate change levy, an energy target was agreed: **1.25 kWh/kg cement by 2010 (-25 % versus 1990)**.
- Participation in the WBCSD "Towards a Sustainable Cement Industry" project through 13 sub-studies (the forerunner of the later CSI).

### Environmental Indicators per kt of Concrete (1994→2001)
- Fossil fuel 483→426 GJ, electricity 91.7→87.5 GJ
- CO₂ 126→114 t, SO₂ 0.640→0.273 t, NOx 0.795→0.606 t, dust 77.9→53.0 kg, heavy metals 0.592→0.477 kg
- Landfill waste 66.7→62.8 t, water 1450→1297 m³, mineral extraction 1.021→0.914 kt
- The indicator basket improved on average **about 17 % across 1994→2001** (mostly emissions improvements).

### Structural Facts
- **85 % of concrete's CO₂ originates in cement manufacture**. GGBS+PFA supply ~14 % of cementitious material; without them, CO₂ per tonne would be +10 kg higher.
- Including reinforcement adds steel-production electricity demand (reinforced concrete 116 MJ/t vs 73 MJ/t unreinforced).
- Concrete accounts for ~40 % of UK mineral extraction yet amounts to only **0.1–4.3 %** of the national total on the remaining ten indicators. Building operation at 31.0 % plus transport at 30.8 % is overwhelming by contrast.

### Recommended Actions (Joint Industry Action)
Maintaining the database, case collections, periodic environmental reporting, benchmarking, and indicator monitoring; supporting minimization of burdens at the building-operation stage (fossil energy) and the waste/recycling stage; owner-user communication; and support for renewable-energy projects that utilize concrete.

## Position in the Wiki

- As an industry-data report from the 2002 vantage point, it forms one end of the sustainability context together with the UNEP 2016 report (global materials solutions). Its conclusion that building operation and transport dominate is an early version of the later embodied carbon vs operational carbon framing; the report is the historical baseline source for the Western cement-industry environmental statistics cited in ECC/EGC papers.
