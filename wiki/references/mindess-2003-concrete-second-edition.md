# Concrete, 2nd ed. (Mindess · Young · Darwin, textbook)

## Summary

Standard textbook on concrete materials science, 2nd edition, co-authored by Sidney Mindess (UBC), J. Francis Young (Illinois Urbana-Champaign), and David Darwin (Kansas) (Prentice Hall/Pearson, © 2003·1996, ISBN 0-13-064632-6, LC card TA439.M49 2002, main text ~630 pages plus appendices and index). The source PDF is a scan (657 pages, image-only — pypdf text extraction yielded 0 characters); the cover, CIP bibliographic page, table of contents, and preface were read via OCR (200–300 dpi) to verify the bibliographic data. Not a research paper, so it is recorded as a reference-type note.

## Main Contents (22 chapters + appendices)

1. Concrete as a Material
2. Historical Development of Cement and Concrete
3. Cements — non-portland cements, manufacture and composition of portland cement, modified portland cements, non-portland inorganic cements
4. Hydration of Portland Cement — hydration chemistry, properties of hydration products, microstructure and properties of hardened cement paste
5. Mineral Admixtures and Blended Cements — pozzolans, blast-furnace slag, binder cements, blended-cement proportioning
6. Water
7. Aggregates — properties required for mix design, durability, non-standard aggregates
8. Chemical Admixtures — air-entraining agents, water reducers, set-control agents
9. Fresh Concrete — workability, setting, tests on fresh concrete
10. Proportioning Concrete Mixes — ACI mix design method
11. Concrete Construction Practices
12. Curing — ambient-temperature and elevated-temperature curing
13. Response of Concrete to Stress — ITZ, elastic modulus, tension and fracture, compression, cyclic loading, multiaxial stress, strength-governing factors
14. Testing of Hardened Concrete
15. Quality Control — quantifying variability, QC control charts
16. Time-Dependent Deformation — plastic shrinkage, drying shrinkage, **strain-rate effects**, creep, shrinkage and creep prediction
17. Other Properties — thermal properties, abrasion resistance
18. Durability — permeability, chemical transport and attack, physical attack, cracking, repair and maintenance
19. High-Strength Concrete
20. Concretes for Special Applications — lightweight concrete, heavyweight concrete, architectural concrete
21. Cement-Polymer Composites — latex-modified concrete, polymer-impregnated concrete, organoceramics
22. Fiber Reinforced Concrete — definitions, fiber–matrix bonding, mechanics of fiber reinforcement, FRC production, properties, and applications

Appendices: Tests and Specifications / numerical answers / index
