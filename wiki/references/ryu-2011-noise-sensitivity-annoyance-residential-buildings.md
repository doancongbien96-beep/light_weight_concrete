## Summary
A representative paper by the research team of Prof. Jin Yong Jeon at Hanyang University that identified, through a large-scale social survey and subjective listening experiments, the influence of apartment residents' individual noise sensitivity (Noise Sensitivity, Weinstein scale) on annoyance responses to indoor noises (floor impact sound, water supply/drainage noise) and outdoor noises (road traffic noise, railway noise). The study revealed that the noise-sensitive group perceives annoyance $8 \sim 10 dB$ higher than the general group at the same heavy-weight impact sound level, providing the scientific basis for establishing high-performance sound insulation criteria.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of dose-response curves by noise source type**: formalization of a %HA (Percent Highly Annoyed) model for heavy-weight impact sound ($L_{i,Fmax,AW}$).
- **Clarification of the correlation between psychoacoustic indices of floor impact sound (Loudness, Sharpness)**: interpretation of the annoyance mechanism of low-frequency thumping sounds.
- **Proposal of building acoustic construction criteria for improving residential livability**: emphasis on the necessity of grade-1 ($\le 43 dB$) sound insulation design.
