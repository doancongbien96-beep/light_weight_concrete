## Summary
A study by a research team at the University of La Rochelle, France, that elucidated the thermophysical properties, moisture sorption/desorption characteristics, and indoor heating/cooling energy-savings efficiency of composite hemp concrete in which a plant-oil-based bio phase change material (Bio-based PCM: caprylic acid/myristic acid eutectic mixture, phase change temperature $22 °C$, latent heat $\Delta H = 148 J/g$) was vacuum-impregnated into the porous hemp skeleton — undertaken to overcome the low heat storage capacity of eco-friendly hemp concrete combining hemp shivs, a biomass by-product, with a hydraulic lime binder and to realize an ultra-low-carbon integrated insulation/heat-storage building envelope. It demonstrated that bio-PCM impregnation secures an effective latent heat capacity of **32.5 J/g** and cuts building indoor heating/cooling loads by **42 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Development of direct vacuum impregnation technology for plant-based bio PCM into natural hemp aggregate**: succeeded in loading 85 wt % PCM into the porous cell walls of hemp fibers without any capsule shell.
- **Perfect fusion of insulation and heat storage**: simultaneous achievement of ultra-low thermal conductivity ($0.095 W/(m\cdot K)$) and high latent heat capacity ($32.5 J/g$).
- **Preservation of indoor temperature-humidity buffering (MBV) performance**: retained high vapor permeability to secure comfortable indoor environmental indices.
- **Realization of a carbon-negative building material**: synergy between hemp's photosynthetic carbon fixation and building energy savings.

## Methods
- Materials: natural hemp chips (Hemp shivs, density $110 kg/m^3$), hydraulic lime binder (Batichanvre).
- PCM: natural fatty acid ester bio PCM ($T_m = 21.8 °C$, $\Delta H = 148.5 J/g$).
- Tests: Hot Disk thermal conductivity meter, DSC latent heat analysis, dynamic vapor sorption (DVS), full-scale exterior-wall simulated chamber thermal performance testing.
