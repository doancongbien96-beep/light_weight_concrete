## Summary
A paper in which Professor K.Q. Yu's research team at Tongji University clarified the single fiber pullout bridging characteristics, direct tensile strain hardening, and fine crack control behavior of a sustainable ductile composite (Sustainable SHCC) in which recycled PET fibers made by upcycling polluting waste plastic beverage bottles (diameter 35 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 750 MPa) replace expensive PVA fibers in hybrid combinations (total $V_f = 2.0 \%$, PET 0.5 ~ 1.5 vol %). The optimal hybrid mix of PVA 1.0 % + PET 1.0 % achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **44.5 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **4.85 MPa**, and an ultimate tensile strain capacity of **4.2 %** while cutting fiber cost by **45 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **World's first demonstration of the micromechanics feasibility of waste PET fibers for high-ductility SHCC**: interfacial frictional stress $\tau_0 = 1.15 MPa$ secured under single PET fiber pullout.
- **PVA-PET multi-scale hybrid bridging synergy established**: combining the high chemical bonding of hydrophilic PVA fibers with the stable frictional slip of hydrophobic PET fibers.
- **Cost reduction of 45 % with a tensile strain capacity of 4.2 % secured**: waste recycling and commercialization of ultra-ductile composites achieved simultaneously.
- **Multiple fine crack control demonstrated**: mean crack width maintained at most $35 \mu m$ until failure.

## Methods
- Binder: cement 30 % + fly ash 70 %, $W/B = 0.32$.
- Fiber combinations: PVA only (2.0 %), PET only (2.0 %), hybrids (PVA 1.5 % + PET 0.5 %; PVA 1.0 % + PET 1.0 %; PVA 0.5 % + PET 1.5 %).
- Tests: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), single fiber pullout test, 28 d compressive strength.
