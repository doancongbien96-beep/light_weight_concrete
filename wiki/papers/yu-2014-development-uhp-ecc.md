## Summary
A study establishing the micromechanics-based mix design theory of ultra-high performance ductile composites (UHP-ECC) that simultaneously achieve ultra-high strength ($f_c > 120 MPa$) and ultra-ductile tensile strain hardening ($\epsilon_u > 4.5 \%$). The modified Andreasen and Andersen continuous particle optimum packing model was applied to the matrix to densify the pore structure extremely, and high-strength ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) fibers were incorporated to precisely optimize the PSH criterion between matrix fracture toughness and interfacial frictional slip. Under 28 d ambient curing, a compressive strength of 135 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 14.5 MPa, and a tensile strain capacity of 5.2 % were achieved.

## Key Contributions
- **Ultra-dense UHP matrix design based on A&A particle packing theory**: excellent self-consolidating flowability secured at $W/B = 0.16$ through a distribution modulus $q = 0.23$ design.
- **Micromechanics optimization of UHMWPE fiber interface behavior**: critical interface matching completed that suppresses fiber rupture in the ultra-high-strength matrix while inducing full pullout.
- **Strength-ductility trade-off overcome**: simultaneous realization of ultra-high strength above 130 MPa and ultra-high ductility above 5 % in a single material.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: cement + silica fume + nano silica + fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 120 \mu m$), $W/B = 0.16$, UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, strength 3000 MPa).
- **Test program**: uniaxial direct tension test, single fiber pullout test, 28 d compressive strength, mercury intrusion porosimetry.

## Results
- 28 d properties: compressive strength $135.2 MPa$, elastic modulus $42.5 GPa$, first-cracking strength $7.8 MPa$, ultimate tensile strength $14.5 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $5.20 \%$.
- Crack widths remained at most $20 \mu m$, reconciling ultra-high durability with ultra-high strength.
