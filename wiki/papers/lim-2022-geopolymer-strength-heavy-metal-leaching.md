## Summary

An environmental materials study evaluating the compressive strength development and heavy-metal immobilization performance when hazardous industrial solid wastes containing heavy metals (Pb, Cd, Cr, As) — slag, bottom ash, contaminated soil — are recycled as binder and aggregate in fly-ash-based geopolymer concrete. Via the Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP) and a semi-dynamic leaching test, the study elucidates the mechanism of heavy-metal ion capture within the three-dimensional aluminosilicate network structure of the geopolymer, and demonstrates that all heavy-metal leaching concentrations are safely blocked below US EPA limits while securing a room-temperature-cured 28 d compressive strength of 45 ~ 60 MPa.

## Key Contributions

- **Elucidation of the chemical/physical heavy-metal immobilization mechanisms of the geopolymer matrix**: confirms the crystallographic principle that heavy-metal cations ($Pb^{2+}, Cd^{2+}$) become substituted and fixed as charge-compensating ions for the negative charge ($[AlO_4]^-$) of the geopolymer framework.
- **Quantification of the long-term leaching diffusion coefficient ($D_e$)**: continuous diffusion tests demonstrate that the effective diffusion coefficients of the individual heavy metals are on the order of $10^{-14} \sim 10^{-16} m2/s$ — more than 10 times lower than ordinary Portland cement.
- **Optimization of mechanical strength at high waste replacement**: establishes mix proportions that develop a 28 d compressive strength of $52.4 MPa$ even at 40 wt % industrial waste replacement.

## Methods

- **Material proportioning**: Class F fly ash + industrial solid waste (0 ~ 50 wt %), alkali activator (sodium silicate $Ms = 1.5$ + NaOH 10 M).
- **Curing conditions**: heat curing at $60 °C$ for 24 h, followed by room-temperature air curing.
- **Leaching tests**: US EPA Method 1311 (TCLP) and EPA Method 1315 (diffusion leaching test), with heavy-metal concentration analysis by ICP-OES.

## Results

- TCLP test results: lead (Pb) leaching concentration $0.25 mg/L$ (a 95 % safety margin versus the limit of $5.0 mg/L$), cadmium (Cd) $0.04 mg/L$ (limit $1.0 mg/L$).
- Confirmed that the dense N-A-S-H gel structure physically traps the harmful ions (encapsulation) while simultaneously fixing them within chemical crystal lattices.
