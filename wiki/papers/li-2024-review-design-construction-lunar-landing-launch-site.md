# Comprehensive Review of Space-Civil-Engineering Design and Construction Technology for a Permanent Crewed Lunar Landing and Launch Site (LL Site): Hypersonic Rocket Plume ($>2000\ \text{K}$) Thermal Shock, Regolith Blast-Ejection (PSI) Protection, Sintered Pavements, and Blast-Protection Berm Mechanisms

## Summary

To fundamentally block the Plume Surface Interaction (PSI) disaster in which the ultra-hot rocket engine exhaust plume (Rocket Exhaust Plume: gas temperature $2000\sim 3000\ \text{K}$, ejection velocity $3000\ \text{m/s}$) generated during landing and lift-off of large crewed spacecraft under Artemis, ILRS, and similar programs erodes and blasts lunar regolith at high speed, destroying nearby lunar-base habitat modules, solar power plants, and optical observation equipment — this latest top-authority landmark review of 2024 in Aerospace Science and Technology by the research team of **Professor J. Li of Tongji University, China and Professor L. Xiao of the China University of Geosciences** comprehensively codifies the **structural layout of the Lunar Landing and Launch site (LL Site), thermal-fluid-shock multiphysics design criteria, in-situ lunar-regolith-based ultra-high-temperature ceramic sintered pavements (Sintered Pavements), interlocking landing-pad tiles, and unmanned robotic autonomous construction methods for vertical blast deflector berms (Blast Deflector Berms)**, and demonstrates through Apollo lander data and CFD-DEM (Computational Fluid Dynamics - Discrete Element Method) numerical analysis **standard civil-engineering dimensions of pad diameter $50\sim 100\ \text{m}$ and berm height $5\sim 10\ \text{m}$ together with crack-free refractory performance under $1500\ ^\circ\text{C}$ rapid thermal shock**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first comprehensive systematization of the five core systems of LL-site space-civil engineering**:
   - Completes the design criteria for pavement pads (Pavement Pad), blast deflector berms (Blast Deflector Berms), ejected-plume exhaust trenches (Trench), access road networks, and remote monitoring systems.
2. **Elucidation of the physical mechanism of hypersonic rocket plume erosion (Plume Erosion) under vacuum conditions**:
   - Proves that, unlike Earth's atmospheric pressure, in the Moon's high vacuum the plume expands radially beyond $180^\circ$, so horizontally blasted regolith particles (velocity $>1500\ \text{m/s}$) can damage even orbital satellites hundreds of km away.
3. **Establishment of the heat resistance of in-situ lunar-regolith (ISRU) microwave/laser high-temperature sintered pavement materials**:
   - Through $1200\sim 1400\ ^\circ\text{C}$ sintering, manufactures vitrified basaltic interlocking tiles with compressive strength $80.0\ \text{MPa}$ and flexural strength $18.5\ \text{MPa}$, preventing plume erosion $100\%$.
4. **Establishment of a 100-ton-class landing-pad construction standard for future crewed lunar landers (Starship HLS etc.)**:
   - Provides civil-engineering guidelines for ground compaction, polymer anchoring, and berm placement that support the landing impact force ($10\ \text{MN}$) and thermal loads of heavy launch vehicles.

## Methods

- **Plume-surface interaction and heat transfer modeling (Sections 2~4 & Figs. 1~8)**:
  - Gas kinetics: DSMC (Direct Simulation Monte Carlo) + Navier-Stokes hybrid flow analysis.
  - Heat flux: rocket stagnation-point heat flux $q \approx 5\sim 15\ \text{MW/m}^2$, surface temperature $T_{surf} > 1800\ \text{K}$.
  - Regolith ejection model: Roberts erosion penetration-rate formula and particle trajectory tracking.
- **Landing-pad construction technology analysis (Sections 4~6 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 9~16)**:
  - 1. Microwave/solar-concentration sintered tiles (in-situ fabricated interlocking blocks).
  - 2. Geopolymer/phosphate ceramic binder-injected consolidated layers.
  - 3. Metal mesh and expandable berm structures.

### 2. Aerodynamic analysis of particle shielding by blast deflector berms (Deflector Berms)
- **Plume deflection mechanism of the $30^\circ$ inclined berm**: A sintered-regolith berm of height $6\ \text{m}$ installed around the landing pad refracts horizontally ejected gas upward at $45^\circ$ above the horizon, blocking $99.9\%$ of the particle impact energy reaching surrounding infrastructure.
- **Interlocking structure preventing sharp thermal-expansion cracking**: Expansion slits of $5\ \text{mm}$ between tiles together with flexible graphite gaskets completely prevent thermal-stress fracture even under $2000\ \text{K}$ thermal shock.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the highest-level landmark original review in which the research team of Professor J. Li of Tongji University comprehensively codifies the plume-surface interaction (PSI) physical mechanics of lunar surface landing and launch sites together with in-situ regolith sintered interlocking pavement and berm construction technology, establishing the construction of a safe and reusable lunar Spaceport infrastructure.
