## Summary

A comprehensive review paper synthesizing the dispersion techniques, microstructural densification, mechanical reinforcement mechanisms, and piezoresistivity-based smart self-sensing functionality of nanocomposites incorporating carbon nanotubes (CNTs) into cementitious matrices. It systematizes ultrasonic dispersion and surfactant chemical treatment processes for solving CNT agglomeration driven by strong van der Waals attraction, and analyzes nano-scale crack bridging (nano-bridging) and hydration-product nucleation acceleration effects. It also presents the current state of structural health monitoring (SHM) technology based on the fractional change in electrical resistance (FCR) under strain, stress, and fine damage.

## Key Contributions
- **Systematization of CNT dispersion technology and interfacial chemistry mechanisms**: established optimal dispersion criteria as a function of surfactant (PCE, SDBS, CTAB) ratio and ultrasonic energy input.
- **Interpretation of nano-scale reinforcement and nucleation acceleration**: elucidated the principle by which CNTs act as nucleation sites for C-S-H gel formation, improving 28 d compressive and flexural strength by 20 ~ 35 % and 30 ~ 50 %, respectively.
- **Establishment of performance indicators for self-sensing smart composites**: established the design principles of smart sensors that exhibit a linear electrical resistance response (gauge factor $GF = 20 \sim 80$) under mechanical load.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of some 200 publications on CNT cement composites.
- Comparison of dispersion evaluation, microstructure (SEM/TEM), mechanical properties, electrical conductivity, and piezoresistive performance.

## Results
- Optimal CNT dosage: $0.05 \sim 0.2 wt \%$ by binder mass. Excessive dosing ($> 0.5 wt \%$) was confirmed to degrade mechanical strength through agglomeration.
- Under repeated loading a high reversible fractional change in electrical resistance was exhibited, demonstrating applicability as real-time stress monitoring sensors for concrete structures.
