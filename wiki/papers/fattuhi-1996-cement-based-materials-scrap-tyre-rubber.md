# Mechanical Behavior of Cement Composites Containing Scrap Truck Tire Rubber (Shredded Chips / Crumb): 3-Fold Impact Energy Absorption, Ductile Deformation, and Lightweight Mechanisms

## Summary

To mitigate the environmental impact of scrap tires discarded annually worldwide and develop ductile construction materials with high impact and damping capacities, Dr. N. I. Fattuhi and Professor L. A. Clark at the University of Birmingham investigated cement paste, mortar, and concrete (with Portland cement and PFA fly ash) incorporating shredded rubber chips and fine crumb rubber from scrap truck tires as partial-to-total aggregate replacements (0~100% volume replacement of sand). The authors evaluated density, compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, modulus of rupture (MOR), ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), and drop-weight impact resistance. The results demonstrated that while compressive strength decreased with increasing rubber content, the composites avoided explosive brittle failure, sustaining substantial plastic deformation energy, amplifying impact fracture energy more than 3 times, and reducing self-weight by up to 20%.

## Key Contributions

1. **First systematic formulation of the mechanical behavior of rubberized concrete (Rubcrete)**:
   - Established quantitative relationships between rubber particle geometry (coarse shredded chips vs fine crumb) and concrete engineering properties.
2. **Demonstration of transition from brittle failure to ductile plastic deformation**:
   - Unlike conventional concrete which fails explosively at peak compressive stress, rubberized concrete sustained high residual stresses and ductile post-peak compression without disintegration.
3. **3-fold amplification in impact load and vibrational energy absorption**:
   - Elastic rubber particles absorbed impact stress waves and arrested crack propagation, indicating applicability to crash barriers, runway buffers, and acoustic damping screeds.
4. **Interfacial transition zone refinement via fly ash (PFA) incorporation**:
   - Pozzolanic PFA densified the interfacial transition zone (ITZ) around rubber particles and lowered production costs.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–2)**:
  - Binders: Ordinary Portland cement (OPC) + pulverized fuel ash (PFA, 20 wt.%).
  - Rubber types (two geometries):
    1. `Crumb Rubber`: Mechanically granulated fine rubber ($d \le 2\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 1.15).
    2. `Shredded Rubber`: Coarse shredded chips ($2\sim 10\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 1.12).
  - Sand replacement levels: 0%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100% by sand volume.
- **Mechanical testing program (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 3–5)**:
  - 28 d compressive strength (100 mm cubes), flexural MOR ($100 \times 100 \times 500\ \text{mm}$ prisms).
  - Splitting tensile strength ($\phi 150 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ cylinders), UPV ultrasonic velocity.
  - Drop-weight impact testing: 4.5 kg steel hammer repetitive impact (initial crack and ultimate failure blows).

### 2. Crack bridging and energy absorption mechanisms of rubber particles
- **Stress relaxation by low-modulus rubber**: Low-stiffness rubber particles deformed elastically at matrix crack tips, blunting stress concentrations and halting rapid crack propagation.
- **Post-failure integrity**: Embedded rubber particles held fractured fragments together, preventing shatter and amplifying drop-weight impact blow capacity by over 3.3 times.

- **Overall conclusion**: This foundational paper by Fattuhi and Clark systematized the mechanical properties and impact enhancement mechanisms of rubberized concrete, providing principles for recycled tire utilization in impact-absorbing civil infrastructure.
