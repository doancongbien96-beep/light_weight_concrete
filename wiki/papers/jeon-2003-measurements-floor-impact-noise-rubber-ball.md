# Measurements of Floor Impact Noise Using a New Impact Source

## Summary

This paper evaluates the dynamic impact force and acoustic characteristics of a newly developed standard impact source, the impact rubber ball, addressing the excessive impact force of the conventional automobile tire drop method (bang machine) and its mismatch with real-life residential impacts (such as children jumping). By dropping the rubber ball (outer diameter 180 mm, mass 2.5 kg, coefficient of restitution 0.8) from a height of 1.0 m, the authors compared its impact force spectrum and receiving-room frequency-dependent sound pressure levels against the bang machine, demonstrating that the rubber ball closely replicates actual human impact forces ($F_{max} \approx 2500\ \text{N}$).

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of dynamic impact force characteristics of the new standard impact source (rubber ball)**: Demonstrated that its contact duration ($\approx 20\ \text{ms}$) and peak impact force ($F_{max} \approx 2000 \sim 2500\ \text{N}$) match the waveform profiles of children jumping.
- **Resolution of excessive low-frequency sound pressure distortion from bang machines**: Compared to the disproportionate low-frequency modal excitation of bang machines ($F_{max} \approx 5000\ \text{N}$), the rubber ball provides a realistic energy distribution across the $125 \sim 250\ \text{Hz}$ octave bands relevant to residential living.
- **Enhanced discrimination in evaluating resilient floor interlayer performance**: Clarified that the rubber ball allows precise discrimination of acoustic isolation improvements associated with variations in the dynamic elastic modulus of different resilient materials.

## Methods

- **Impact source specifications**:
  - Rubber ball: Hollow silicone rubber sphere, mass $2.5 \pm 0.1\ \text{kg}$, outer diameter $180\ \text{mm}$, drop height 1.0 m.
  - Bang machine: Automotive tire, mass $7.3\ \text{kg}$, drop height 0.85 m.
- **Experimental environment**: Standard apartment floor slabs (thicknesses 150 mm, 180 mm, 210 mm) with various resilient interlayers (EPS, fine-aggregate interlayers, etc.).
- **Measured parameters**: Impact force time-history ($F(t)$), receiving room maximum sound pressure level ($L_{Fmax}$), octave-band sound pressure spectra.

## Results

- The contact duration of the rubber ball was $20.5\ \text{ms}$, which was longer and more gradual than that of the bang machine ($18.0\ \text{ms}$).
- Sound pressure levels in the receiving room showed smooth attenuation across the $63 \sim 500\ \text{Hz}$ frequency range, yielding a higher correlation coefficient ($R = 0.88$) with subjective auditory annoyance compared to the bang machine ($R = 0.72$).
