# Sustainable high-ductility EGC using recycled fine aggregate (RFA)

## Summary

Development of EGC with silica sand replaced by recycled fine aggregate (RFA, parent-concrete compressive strength 46.3 MPa), a crushed by-product of concrete waste. The key finding is that alkaline hydration products contained in the adhered old mortar of RFA promote secondary geopolymerization of the GGBS-FA precursor, so that unlike cementitious systems, strength and ductility actually improve at 100 % RFA replacement. Evaluated with replacement ratio (0~100 %), particle size (four grades), silicate modulus (1.1~1.7 M), and PE fiber length (6~18 mm) as variables.

## Key Contributions

1. Paradoxical benefit of 100 % RFA replacement: tensile strength +5.7 %, tensile strain +8.6 %, flexural strength +5.5 %, compressive strength +3.2 % (versus EGC-Ref). Two mechanisms — (i) RFA microcracks and fine pores act as artificial flaws lowering σ_c (first-cracking strength −18.9 % at 100 % replacement) → promoting multiple cracking; (ii) C-S-H in the adhered old mortar repolymerizes to form C(N)-A-S-H → strengthening the interface and matrix.
2. Opposing effects of RFA size: reducing particles (to 0.15 mm and below) cuts porosity by up to 23.2 % and improves strength (precursor role, effectively a lower W/B); conversely, larger particles (0.3~1.18 mm) supply flaws and raise tensile strain by +12.9 % (0.3~0.6 mm). Size selection follows the design target (strength versus ductility).
3. Optimum silicate modulus 1.5 M: tensile strength +8.1 % and strain +24.8 %. Excess (1.7 M) degrades performance by hindering geopolymerization.
4. Fiber length: 6 mm gives insufficient bridging with flexural strength −45.5 %; 12→18 mm raises tensile strength +30.1 % while maintaining strain.
5. Durability-related trade-offs stated explicitly: RFA replacement increases drying shrinkage (+22.6 % at 100 %), and moisture transport improves only at the moderate replacement (25 %).

## Methods

FA:GGBS = 1:1 + silica fume 20 kg/m3, activator Na2O 6.5 % (water glass), PE fiber (25 μm, 3100 MPa) at 2 vol %. RFA was pre-wetted to SSD condition. MIP (with XRD and FTIR), compression (70.7 mm cubes), three-point bending (40×40×160), direct tension dog-bone (LVDT), drying shrinkage (JC/T 603), capillary absorption (ASTM C1585).

## Results

- Pore classification applied: harmless (<20 nm)/low-harm (20~50 nm)/harmful (50~200 nm)/severely harmful (>200 nm).
- Crack width decreased as RFA size increased (up to −21 %) and crack number increased (up to +27.4 %).
- Strain energy density up to +43.6 % (1.5 M mix).
