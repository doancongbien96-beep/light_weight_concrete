## Summary
A landmark work in which the research team of Prof. Felekoglu (B. Felekoglu) at Dokuz Eylul University, Turkey precisely elucidated — through artificial-flaw introduction experiments (Artificial Flaws: $1.0 \sim 5.0 mm$ micro hollow beads) and a probabilistic micromechanics model — the interaction between the initial flaw size distribution of the matrix (Initial Flaw Size Distribution $a_0$) and the spatial fiber dispersion (Fiber Dispersion $\alpha_{disp}$) and their effects on uniaxial direct-tension strain capacity and the propagation stability of multiple fine cracking within the micromechanical PSH strain-hardening criteria of PVA-ECC. It demonstrated that when artificial flaws of appropriate size ($2.0 \sim 3.0 mm$) are uniformly dispersed within the matrix, the crack initiation stress $\sigma_{fc}$ is stably guided below the fiber bridging peak strength $\sigma_0$, raising tensile strain capacity nearly twofold, from $2.8 \%$ to **5.4 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the correlation between initial flaw size and PSH strength margin ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$)**: elucidation of the mechanism whereby flaws that are too small ($< 0.5 mm$) push the initial cracking strength excessively high, degrading the behavior into single-crack failure.
- **Establishment of a tensile ductility control technique through the introduction of artificial flaws**: adding 2.5 mm plastic micro beads at 1.5 vol % increased the density of multiple fine cracking by 140 %.
- **Presentation of a combined failure envelope of the fiber dispersion coefficient ($\alpha_{disp}$) and flaw size**: formalization of the premature-fracture risk limit when flaws grow large in poorly dispersed fiber regions.
- **Establishment of guidelines for reducing quality scatter in ECC**: securing field-casting reliability through control of aggregate gradation and mixing processes.

## Methods
- Mix: ordinary Portland cement + fly ash ($FA/C = 1.2$), $W/B = 0.28$, microsilica sand ($d_{max} = 0.4 mm$).
- Fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa).
- Artificial flaw variables: none added, 1.0 mm, 2.5 mm, 5.0 mm beads (replacement ratios 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 vol %).
- Testing: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain measurement), fluorescence dye-impregnated image analysis of fiber dispersion, 28 d compressive strength.

## Results
- Comparison table of uniaxial tensile properties by flaw size and incorporation ratio:

| Flaw condition | Flaw size (mm) | Flaw volume fraction (%) | 28 d compressive strength (MPa) | Initial cracking strength (MPa) | Ultimate tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | Number of cracks |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Control (natural flaws) | natural distribution | 0.0 | 48.5 | 4.25 | 5.10 | 2.85 | 24 |
| Flaw-1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 46.2 | 3.95 | 5.20 | 3.65 | 35 |
| **Flaw-2.5** | **2.5** | **1.5** | **42.5** | **3.20** | **5.45** | **5.40** | **58** |
| Flaw-5.0 | 5.0 | 2.0 | 32.0 | 2.10 | 3.80 | 2.10 | 14 |

- The mix incorporating 2.5 mm artificial flaws secured the highest strength margin ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} = 1.70$), exhibiting peak tensile ductility of 5.4 %.
