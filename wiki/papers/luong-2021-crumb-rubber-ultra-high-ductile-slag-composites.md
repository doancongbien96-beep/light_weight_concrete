# Sustainability of eco-friendly ultra-high-ductile slag composites incorporating waste-tire crumb rubber (Crumb Rubber) particles: tensile strain-hardening behavior ($\varepsilon_u = 8.5\%$) and $68\%$ carbon emission reduction

## Summary

To recycle scrap tires — more than 1 billion of which are discharged annually, causing severe environmental pollution and fire hazards — into construction materials and to reduce greenhouse gases from cement manufacturing, this study developed **cement-free alkali-activated ultra-high-ductile composites (Ultra-High-Ductile Rubberized Slag Composites: $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$ PE fiber) in which fine waste-tire crumb rubber (Crumb Rubber: CR, particle size $0.6\sim 1.2\ \text{mm}$) is incorporated as a binder-weight replacement (replacement ratios 0 %, 5 %, 10 %) with ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) used as a 100% binder**, and evaluated uniaxial direct tensile tests, compressive strength, impact fracture energy, DIC multiple-cracking analysis, and life-cycle carbon emissions (LCA). The results demonstrated that the S-CR5 mix with 5 % binder-weight CR replacement achieves a uniaxial ultimate tensile strain capacity of $10.67 \pm 0.80\%$, lightweighting to a density of $1820\ \text{kg/m}^3$, a 3.8-fold amplification of uniaxial tensile fracture energy, and a $68\%$ reduction in carbon emissions versus conventional OPC-ECC — the Journal of Cleaner Production 2021 top-authority landmark original paper by the research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University (corresponding author), Prof. Jeong-Il Choi, and Prof. Hyeong-Ki Kim of Chosun University**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the paradoxical maximization of PSH strain-hardening performance through crumb rubber particles**:
   - Proved that rubber particles, which degrade strength in ordinary concrete, appropriately lower the matrix fracture toughness ($J_{tip}$) in strain-hardening composites, reducing crack-initiation resistance and thereby amplifying tensile strain capacity from $4.2\% \rightarrow 8.5\%$ — more than twofold.
2. **Achievement of a uniaxial direct tensile strain capacity $\varepsilon_u = 10.67 \pm 0.80\%$ (world-record class)**:
   - Synergy of $1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$ ultrahigh-molecular-weight PE micro fibers and rubber particles formed more than $60$ extremely fine saturated multiple cracks ($w \le 25\ \mu\text{m}$).
3. **Mass recycling of waste tires and a $68\%$ cut in carbon footprint (Carbon Footprint)**:
   - Combined $100\%$ slag alkali activation using no cement clinker at all with waste-rubber resource circulation, establishing a carbon-neutral standard for eco-friendly building materials.
4. **Lightweighting (density reduced by 15%) and securing excellent vibration damping/impact absorption capacity**:
   - Thanks to the viscoelastic nature of rubber, dynamic impact toughness and noise damping performance were dramatically improved.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS $100\%$, Blaine fineness $4350\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Alkaline activator: mixed solution of liquid sodium silicate + sodium hydroxide ($W/B = 0.35$).
  - Aggregate and rubber: fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$) + waste-tire crumb rubber (CR: $d = 0.6\sim 1.2\ \text{mm}$, replacement ratios 0%, 20%, 40%, 60%).
  - Reinforcing fiber: ultrahigh-molecular-weight PE fiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Mechanical and eco-friendliness evaluation (Sections 2.2~3.5 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Uniaxial tensile test: dogbone specimens ($80\ \text{mm}$ gauge, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Compression and density: 50 mm cube compressive strength and dry density measurement.
  - LCA analysis: estimation of $\text{CO}_2$-equivalent emissions from raw-material extraction through manufacturing.

### 2. Micromechanics energy condition and fine multiple-crack analysis

- **PSH margin maximization by lowering $J_{tip}$**: rubber particles with low elastic modulus drastically lower the matrix fracture toughness $J_{tip}$ from $35\ \text{J/m}^2 \rightarrow 12\ \text{J/m}^2$, elevating the complementary energy ratio ($J_b'/J_{tip}$) to $5.2$, thereby realizing ultra-high ductility of $8.5\%$.
- **Ultra-fine crack width confinement**: tensile deformation finely dispersed across more than 60 cracks keeps individual crack widths at or below $25\ \mu\text{m}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-level landmark original paper in eco-friendly high-ductility composite materials and resource-recycling sustainable engineering, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University simultaneously demonstrated world-best-class ductility — a uniaxial tensile strain capacity of 10.67 % — and a 68% reduction in carbon emissions by fusing waste-tire crumb rubber with cement-free ground granulated blast-furnace slag.
