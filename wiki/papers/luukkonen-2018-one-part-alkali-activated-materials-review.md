# Comprehensive Review of Precursor and Solid Activator Reaction Mechanisms and Mechanical–Environmental Performance of One-Part Cement-Free Alkali-Activated Materials (One-Part "Just Add Water" AAMs)

## Summary

To overcome the hazards of handling corrosive, viscous strongly alkaline solutions and the field-placement workability limitations of conventional two-part alkali-activated materials (Two-Part Geopolymers), this is the world's most authoritative landmark review paper (Cement and Concrete Research, 2018) from the University of Oulu (Finland) research team of Prof. Mirja Illikainen and Tero Luukkonen, comprehensively covering **One-Part ("Just Add Water") cement-free alkali-activated materials (One-Part AAMs)** — mixed in the factory in advance as dry powder precursors and solid alkali activators, with only water added on site like ordinary Portland cement (OPC) — including their raw-material precursors (slag, fly ash, metakaolin, silica fume, volcanic ash), solid powder activators (solid sodium silicate, solid sodium hydroxide, sodium carbonate $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$, sodium aluminate, quicklime $\text{CaO}$), alkali fusion/calcination (Alkali Fusion/Calcination) processes, ambient-cure 28-day compressive strength ($30\sim 85\ \text{MPa}$), setting and flow characteristics, drying shrinkage, efflorescence control, and cradle-to-gate carbon emission (GWP) reduction effects.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of the One-Part ("Just Add Water") AAM concept and a construction-safety innovation over two-part systems**:
   - Establishes a ready-mix powder binder system that eliminates the risks of transporting and mixing strongly alkaline liquid reagents and can be placed simply by adding water, in exactly the same manner as ordinary bagged cement.
2. **Systematization of reaction mechanisms by precursor and solid activator combination**:
   - Calcium-rich systems (slag): C-(A)-S-H gel formation under solid silicate activation $\rightarrow$ achieving ambient-temperature 28-day compressive strengths of $50\sim 85\ \text{MPa}$.
   - Aluminosilicate systems (fly ash/metakaolin): formation of three-dimensional N-A-S-H geopolymer networks under solid alkali fusion (Fusion) or solid $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ activation.
3. **Establishment of dissolution-rate and setting control technologies for solid powder activators**:
   - Analyzes the exothermic dissolution problem of solid NaOH and the retarded dissolution behavior of solid sodium silicate, and presents combined retarder (citric acid, boric acid) and accelerator control techniques.
4. **Demonstrated whole-life environmental performance (LCA) and $\text{CO}_2$ reductions of $50\sim 80\%$ versus OPC**:
   - Demonstrates the viability of one-part AAMs as eco-friendly construction materials capable of cutting greenhouse-gas emissions by $50\sim 80\%$ relative to OPC, even when the carbon emissions from manufacturing the solid activators are included.

## Methods

- **Three one-part AAM production routes (Section 2 & Fig. 1)**:
  1. Simple dry blending (Dry Blending): solid precursor + finely ground solid alkali activator powder.
  2. Alkali fusion (Alkali Fusion): co-calcination of precursor and solid alkali at $500\sim 800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to synthesize a hydraulically reactive powder at room temperature.
  3. Hydrothermal self-activation: hydrothermal alkaline leaching followed by drying into powder form.
- **Optimization ranges of key reaction parameters (Table 1)**:
  - Molar ratios: $\text{SiO}_2/\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 2.0\sim 4.5$, $\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{SiO}_2 = 0.15\sim 0.40$, $\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 0.8\sim 1.2$.
  - Liquid-to-solid ratio (L/S or W/B): $0.25\sim 0.45$.

### 2. One-part hydration kinetics and efflorescence control mechanisms
- **Dissolution Synchronization**: a two-step reaction model in which the fine solid activator particles rapidly dissolve the instant water is added, creating a locally highly alkaline ($\text{pH} > 13.5$) environment and swiftly breaking the Si-O and Al-O bonds on the surfaces of precursor particles to precipitate gel.
- **Efflorescence suppression**: the efflorescence phenomenon, in which unreacted free sodium ions ($\text{Na}^+$) leach to the surface and carbonate, can be blocked at its source by incorporating $20\sim 30\%$ reactive slag (a calcium source), thereby locking $\text{Na}^+$ stably within the interlayer structure of C-A-S-H.

- **Overall conclusion**: By consolidating the chemical principles of one-part ("Just Add Water") cement-free binders, solid activator formulation technology, and their mechanical and environmental performance, this is the foremost enduring landmark review paper of the cement-free concrete engineering field that propelled alkali-activated materials from the laboratory scale to large-scale field commercialization.
