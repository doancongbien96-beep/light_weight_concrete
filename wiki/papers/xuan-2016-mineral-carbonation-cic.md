# Carbon-neutral construction products from cement and concrete wastes via mineral carbonation

## Summary

An award article (research-paper style) that won second prize in the academic division of Hong Kong's CIC iCON program. The research makes carbon-neutral construction products by using concrete batching-plant slurry waste (CSW) and fine recycled concrete aggregate (FRCA) both as CO2 sequestration media and as partial cement-replacing binders. It demonstrated that the calcium-silica constituents of CSW simultaneously serve as a cementitious paste in the fresh state and as a CO2 uptake medium during mineral carbonation.

## Key Contributions

- CO2 fixation kinetics of CSW+FRCA mixtures: more than 75 % of total CO2 uptake occurred within 3 hours of starting carbonation — the basis for early strength development and process efficiency.
- CSW fixation capacity: 110 g of CO2 sequestered per kg dry mass after 7 days.
- Post-carbonation firing characteristics: early strength gain within hours plus reduced drying shrinkage — securing a process window favorable to precast product manufacture.

## Methods

- Materials: batching-plant slurry waste (CSW, arising at about 0.02 t per m³ of concrete produced in Hong Kong) and fine recycled concrete aggregate (FRCA), a by-product of demolition concrete recycling.
- Quantification of CO2 uptake after accelerated mineral carbonation treatment (thermogravimetric analysis based) and evaluation of product properties (strength development rate, drying shrinkage).

## Results

- CO2 uptake: over 75 % absorbed within the initial 3 h for CSW, FRCA, and mixtures alike; CSW alone 110 g/kg (7 days).
- Strength: acquired within hours right after carbonation → curing time can be shortened.
- Dimensional stability: carbonated products show low drying shrinkage, reducing cracking risk.
