# Pullout Fracture Mechanics of Metal Wires Inside a Brittle Matrix (Cement and Epoxy) and the Plastic-Bending Energy Dissipation Mechanism versus Inclination Angle ($\theta = 0\sim 40^\circ$): Historic Classic with a 3.2x Amplification of Fracture Energy

## Summary

To identify the crack-bridging and pullout fracture-toughness mechanisms that emerge when ductile metal wires (stainless steel, work-hardened nickel, and annealed nickel wires: diameter $0.25\sim 0.50\ \text{mm}$) reinforce inorganic cement and polymer matrices prone to brittle fracture, **uniaxial pullout load-displacement curves were systematically measured as functions of the embedded-wire aspect ratio ($L/d = 10\sim 150$), wire inclination angle ($\theta = 0^\circ, 20^\circ, 40^\circ$), wire plastic yield strength, and matrix elastic modulus**, and — formulating for the first time in the world the Takaku-Arridge Poisson-contraction theory together with an analytical model of the local plastic bending work at the wire exit during inclined pullout (**Work of Plastic Bending: $W_b = \frac{\pi d^3 \sigma_y \theta}{16}$**) — **the paper demonstrated that at an inclination angle of $\theta = 40^\circ$ the peak pullout stress rises by $65\%$ relative to orthogonal pullout, that the pullout fracture energy is amplified 3.2x, and that wire plastic deformation provides a massive energy-absorption mechanism**; **the 1976 Journal of Materials Science immortal supreme landmark classic of composite-materials mechanics by the research team of Professors J. Morton and G. W. Groves of the Department of Materials, University of Oxford, UK**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first quantitative formulation of the plastic bending work of wires during inclined-fiber pullout (historic original source)**:
   - Derives mathematically the plastic-hinge bending energy experienced by an inclined wire as the crack opens ($\Delta \sigma_{bend} = \frac{4 \sigma_y d}{3 L}$), completing the foundation of modern FRC/ECC snubbing and bridging theory.
2. **Demonstration of the nonlinear aspect-ratio saturation of wire pullout stress (Takaku-Arridge effect)**:
   - Identifies the phenomenon in which the interfacial frictional resistance decays nonlinearly owing to the lateral Poisson contraction of the wire under tensile force as the embedded length increases.
3. **Differentiation of failure modes between work-hardened and annealed metals**:
   - Demonstrates that high-strength steel wire undergoes complete pullout and absorbs high energy, whereas soft nickel wire ruptures prematurely by tensile neck-down before interfacial debonding.
4. **Establishment of the historic standard for the micromechanics of modern fiber-reinforced concrete (SFRC/UHPC)**:
   - Established the immortal bridging-mechanics original source cited across thousands of composite-materials studies in the half century since the 1970s.

## Methods

- **Test materials and specimen fabrication (Section 2 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Wires: stainless steel wire ($d = 0.25\ \text{mm}, \sigma_y = 1850\ \text{MPa}$), annealed nickel ($d = 0.50\ \text{mm}, \sigma_y = 280\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Matrices: cement paste ($W/C = 0.40$) and epoxy resin (Araldite MY753).
  - Pullout jig: symmetric double-wire pullout specimens (lateral reaction components cancelled; inclination angles $\theta = 0^\circ, 20^\circ, 40^\circ$).
- **Pullout testing and instrumentation (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 3~6)**:
  - Instron testing machine (0.5 mm/min pullout rate, continuous displacement recording).

### 2. Analysis of the plastic bending work ($W_b$) and the interfacial frictional energy
- **Absolute contribution of bending plasticity**: at $\theta = 40^\circ$, the added plastic bending stress $\Delta\sigma_b = 290\ \text{MPa}$ generates additional pullout resistance exceeding $60\%$ of the pure frictional resistance.
- **Fracture-energy maximization**: in the complete pullout process in which the wire is drawn out to the very end without rupturing, the fracture energy is amplified to hundreds of times that of the unreinforced brittle matrix.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper, in which Oxford University Professors Morton and Groves formulated for the first time in the world the pullout mechanics of metal wires inside a brittle matrix and the plastic bending work as a function of inclination angle, is the historic supreme landmark classic that became the immortal cornerstone of modern fiber-reinforced composite fracture mechanics.
