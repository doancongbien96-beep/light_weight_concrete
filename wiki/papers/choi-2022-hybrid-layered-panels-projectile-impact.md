# High-Velocity Projectile Impact Protection Behavior and Layer Optimization of Hybrid Panels of Ultra-High-Strength (HS) and Ultra-High-Ductility (HD) Cementitious Composites

## Summary

To maximize extreme-impact protection performance for military defense, nuclear power plants, and major national infrastructure, this study fabricated seven types of 40 mm thick panels (HS0.3, HS1.5, HD, HD-HS0.3, HS0.3-HD, HD-HS1.5, HS1.5-HD) combining a 146 MPa-class ultra-high-strength steel-fiber composite (HS1.5) and an ultra-high-ductility PE fiber composite (HD) with a tensile strain capacity of **6.33 %** in single and double layered configurations, and carried out gas-gun based high-velocity projectile impact tests (hemispherical steel projectile with mass 66.14 g, caliber 22 mm, velocity 217 m/s, kinetic energy **1,557 J**), precisely analyzing penetration depth, mass loss ratio, front/rear-face craters, and affected damage area (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University; co-authors Se-Eon Park and Yoon Lee, Daejeon University). Experimentally, the **HS1.5-HD hybrid panel placing the ultra-high-strength composite (HS) on the front face and the ultra-high-ductility composite (HD) on the rear face** achieved the best protection performance among all specimens (non-perforating capture at an 11.5 mm penetration depth, lowest mass loss ratio **0.87 %**, lowest rear-face crater damage area **0.90 %**). In particular, even the low-strength HS0.3 panel that was perforated when applied alone completely blocked perforation by fusing an HD layer onto its rear face (HS0.3-HD), establishing the hybrid layered protection mechanism of "front-face compression/penetration resistance + rear-face tensile membrane bridging/scabbing arrest."

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the directionality (asymmetry) mechanism of hybrid layer arrangement**:
   - **Front HS + rear HD (HS1.5-HD / HS0.3-HD)**: the front ultra-high-strength layer rapidly attenuates the projectile's local kinetic energy, and the rear ultra-high-ductility layer suppresses cone shear and scabbing spall caused by the reflected rear tensile wave 100 % through PE fiber tensile bridging, achieving successful non-perforated protection.
   - **Front HD + rear HS (HD-HS1.5 / HD-HS0.3)**: the low-strength HD front layer is penetrated early without slowing the projectile, which then strikes the brittle rear layer at nearly undiminished velocity, so every specimen failed through complete perforation and severe mass loss (14.4 %).
2. **Overcoming the limits of single materials (perforation arrest in HS0.3-HD)**:
   - The HS0.3-HD panel combining HS0.3 (steel fiber 0.3 %) and HD (PE 1.75 %), each fully perforated when used alone, completely stopped perforation at a 40 mm total thickness (penetration depth 14.0 mm), demonstrating the synergy effect of layering.
3. **Dramatic reduction of damage indices (HS1.5-HD)**:
   - Relative to the HS1.5 single panel, the HS1.5-HD panel cut the mass loss ratio from 0.97 % to **0.87 %** and the rear-face crater damage area from 2.27 % to **0.90 %**.

## Methods

- **Base material compositions (Tables 1 & 2)**:
  - **HS0.3 (low-content UHPFRC)**: W/B 0.19, cement 0.8 + SF 0.2, filler 0.24, silica sand 0.88, steel fiber 0.3 vol %. (Compressive strength 141 MPa, strain-softening.)
  - **HS1.5 (high-strength UHPFRC)**: W/B 0.19, cement 0.8 + SF 0.2, steel fiber 1.5 vol % (diameter 200 µm, length 19.5 mm, 2500 MPa). (Compressive strength **146 MPa**, tensile strength 17.0 MPa, strain capacity 0.47 %.)
  - **HD (ultra-high-ductility AAS-PE)**: AAS binder 1.0, W/B 0.30, silica sand 0.50, PE fiber 1.75 vol % (diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, 2700 MPa). (Compressive strength 45 MPa, tensile strength 8.4 MPa, tensile strain capacity **6.33 %**.)
- **Panel specimen configuration (7 types, dimensions $200\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}$, Table 4)**:
  - Single panels: HS0.3 (40 mm), HS1.5 (40 mm), HD (40 mm).
  - Layered panels: HD-HS0.3 (front HD 20 mm + rear HS0.3 20 mm), HS0.3-HD (front HS0.3 20 mm + rear HD 20 mm), HD-HS1.5 (front HD 20 mm + rear HS1.5 20 mm), HS1.5-HD (front HS1.5 20 mm + rear HD 20 mm).
- **High-velocity projectile impact test conditions**:
  - Steel frame fully clamped on all four edges, gas-gun launch system.
  - Hemispherical-nosed steel projectile (mass 66.14 g, caliber 22 mm, impact velocity 217 m/s, kinetic energy $E_k = 1557\ \text{J}$).
  - Precise laser measurement of penetration depth, pre/post-test mass loss ratio (%), and digital image-processing quantification of front/rear-face crater and affected areas (%).

## Results

### 1. Quantitative results of the high-velocity projectile impact tests on the seven panels (Table 4)

| Panel | Layer configuration (front - rear) | Failure mode | Penetration depth (mm) | Mass loss ratio (%) | Front-face crater area (%) | Rear-face crater area (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HS0.3** | HS0.3 single (40 mm) | **Perforation** | Perforated | 5.28 ± 0.43 | 7.34 % | 28.0 % |
| **HS1.5** | HS1.5 single (40 mm) | **Non-perforating capture (penetration)** | **9.6 ± 1.9** | 0.97 ± 0.23 | 4.12 % | 2.27 % |
| **HD** | HD single (40 mm) | **Perforation** | Perforated | 6.32 ± 0.96 | 6.30 % | 19.6 % |
| **HD-HS0.3** | Front HD + rear HS0.3 | **Perforation** | Perforated | 9.34 ± 0.60 | 6.97 % | 39.9 % |
| **HS0.3-HD** | **Front HS0.3 + rear HD** | **Non-perforating capture (penetration)** | **14.0 ± 6.8** | **2.71 ± 1.35** | 5.57 % | **10.8 %** |
| **HD-HS1.5** | Front HD + rear HS1.5 | **Perforation** | Perforated | 14.4 ± 3.81 | 6.96 % | 30.3 % |
| **HS1.5-HD** | **Front HS1.5 + rear HD** | **Non-perforating capture (penetration)** | **11.5 ± 0.9** | **0.87 ± 0.06 (lowest)** | **4.08 %** | **0.90 % (lowest)** |

### 2. Analysis of the hybrid protection mechanism
- **Optimal combination (HS1.5-HD)**: compressive shear resistance of the front ultra-high-strength layer sharply reduces the projectile velocity, and the dense multiple cracking and fiber membrane tension of the rear ultra-high-ductility layer completely block rear-face fragment spalling, delivering the highest protection performance.
- **Fatal consequences of reversed directionality**: placing the ductile layer on the front face (HD-HS) lets the front be punched through easily, concentrating the impact energy into the rear high-strength layer as a shock wave and producing large brittle spalling failures of up to 14.4 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: for high-velocity projectile protection panel design, heterogeneous layer placement of "high strength on the front (HS) + high ductility on the rear (HD)" was established as the optimal design paradigm that maximizes protection efficiency and residual integrity compared with single materials or reversed arrangements.
