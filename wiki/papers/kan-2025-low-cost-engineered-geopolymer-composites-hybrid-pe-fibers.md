# Development of Low-Cost Ultra-Ductile Engineered Geopolymer Composites (Hybrid-EGC) via High- and Low-Modulus PE Fiber Hybridization: 45 % Fiber Cost Reduction and 6.80 % Tensile Ductility

## Summary

To resolve the high material cost barrier that hinders large-scale commercialization of ductile EGC due to expensive high-modulus polyethylene fibers (HM-PE: $E_f = 110\ \text{GPa}$), Professor Lili Kan at USST and Professor Chao Wu at Imperial College London developed a cost-effective hybrid EGC. The material synergistically combines high-modulus HM-PE with low-cost, low-modulus PE fibers (LM-PE: $E_f = 12\ \text{GPa}$, approximately 1/5 the unit cost) at an optimal 1.0:1.0 ratio (total volume fraction $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) within a cementless alkali-activated slag/fly ash geopolymer matrix. Direct tension tests, 28 d compressive strength, DIC strain tracking, and SEM pullout fractography demonstrated a **45 % reduction in fiber material cost, a 28 d compressive strength of $48.5\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strength of $4.50\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strain capacity of 6.80 %, and over 50 saturated microcracks ($w \le 35\ \mu\text{m}$)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First hybrid fiber bridging architecture combining HM-PE and LM-PE**:
   - Engineered dual bridging synergy between rigid high-strength support (HM-PE) and flexible high-elongation pullout (LM-PE), achieving saturated pseudo-strain-hardening at low cost.
2. **45 % reduction in fiber raw material cost while securing 6.80 % tensile ductility**:
   - Halved the dosage of expensive HM-PE while reliably delivering 6.80 % direct tensile strain capacity, securing commercial constructability.
3. **Sequential energy dissipation mechanism during pullout and rupture**:
   - High-modulus HM-PE resisted initial cracking stresses, while compliant LM-PE stretched and pulled out progressively in the large deformation stage, providing secondary bridging energy.
4. **Formulation of design standards for eco-friendly building envelopes**:
   - Provided practical mix design rules for ultra-low-carbon, cost-effective structural elements using blended slag/fly ash binders and hybrid PE fibers.

## Methods

- **Materials and Hybrid-EGC mixture design (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binder: GGBS + Class F fly ash (GGBS/FA = 1.0, liquid $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{NaOH}$, modulus 1.2, W/B = 0.35).
  - Aggregate: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fibers (total $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    1. HM-PE: $d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 110\ \text{GPa}$.
    2. LM-PE: $d_f = 20\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1200\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 12\ \text{GPa}$ (unit cost 1/5).
  - Hybrid series: `HM2.0`, `HM1.5+LM0.5`, `HM1.0+LM1.0`, `HM0.5+LM1.5`, `LM2.0`.
- **Mechanical testing (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 4–6)**:
  - Direct tension: Dogbone specimens (80 mm gauge length, 0.5 mm/min).
  - Compressive strength: 50 mm cubes at 28 d and SEM fractography.

### 2. Sequential bridging synergy and interfacial microstructure
- **Dual bridging synergy**: High-modulus HM-PE arrested initial crack opening, while ductile LM-PE pulled out without rupturing under large deformations, achieving 6.80 % tensile strain capacity.
- **Economic viability**: Halved costly fiber consumption while maintaining more than 300-fold higher tensile strain capacity than ordinary concrete.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that hybridizing high- and low-modulus PE fibers cuts fiber material cost by 45 % while achieving 6.80 % tensile strain capacity in sustainable geopolymer composites.
