# Skid resistance and surface water drainage performance of pavement ECC

## Summary

ECC pavement functional-performance research by the En-Hua Yang group at NTU (TUMCREATE Singapore collaboration). It starts from the recognition that ECC's structural weakness for road use stems from the absence of coarse aggregate: the smooth surface texture leaves skid resistance (BPN) and surface drainage short of pavement requirements. The study experimentally validated a two-stage approach: incorporating corundum (Al2O3, Mohs hardness 9) as a fine aggregate replacing silica sand to secure micro-texture, and reinforcing macro-texture with machined grooves. Corundum alone achieved BPN above 70; grooving simultaneously secured BPN above 90 and an outflow time (OFT) under 4 s; and an empirical drainage–texture–pavement-geometry correlation model was proposed.

## Key Contributions

- Clarified the mechanism by which corundum incorporation raises ECC's first-cracking strength while lowering its strain capacity: the high-hardness aggregate increases matrix toughness, undermining strain-hardening and multiple-cracking potential (strain capacity dropping from 2.1 % to 0.8–1.1 %), yet flexural strength is maintained at 10–12 MPa.
- Quantified the importance of surface exposure timing: day-1 wire-brush exposure after casting greatly reduced BPN scatter and raised values compared with day-3 exposure (BPN 75 for ECC-Cor 100, a 40 % improvement over the control).
- Proposed the spread method: instead of mixing, corundum is laid at 150 kg/m³ on the mold bottom, the ECC is cast over it, and the surface is then exposed — securing BPN 75 equal to ECC-Cor 100 with only a small amount of corundum, a decoupled design strategy separating preservation of mechanical properties from surface functionality.
- Quantified the dual drainage/skid benefit of grooving: shrinking the land area from 25 to 7.5 mm raised MTD from 0.74 to 1.33 mm, cut OFT from 7.73 to 3.38 s, and lifted transverse-direction BPN to 92.8 (+71 %).
- First empirical ECC drainage model reflecting pavement geometry (cross slope and longitudinal grade): an exponential OFT = f(MTD, Cs, Gr) model that identifies why existing ASTM and Sarsam models deviate systematically in the low-MTD region (their neglect of geometric effects) and recovers prediction accuracy.

## Methods

- Mix: cement 559 kg/m³ + fly ash 671 kg/m³ + fine silica sand (mean 110 μm)/corundum replacement + PVA fiber 26 kg/m³ (12 mm, 39 μm, 1600 MPa), w/cm 0.27. ECC-Cor 50–100 with 50–100 % corundum replacement, the unreplaced ECC-Cor 0, and the spread-method ECC-Cor 0-S.
- Specimens: prisms 280×70×50 mm (four-point bending, strain capacity estimated by ASTM C78 back-calculation), plates 500×300×10 mm (BPT, SPM, OFT).
- Evaluation: British Pendulum Test (ASTM E303, wet condition) for micro-texture, sand patch (ASTM E965) for MTD, outflow meter (ASTM E2380M-15) for OFT, and wheel tracking (TRL 176, 520 N at 42 cycles/min, 100,000 passes ≈ 3.7 million ESALs) for durability.
- Grooving: four parallel groove patterns cut with a concrete cutter (blade diameter 350 mm, thickness 3.2 mm) (land area 25/20/15/7.5 mm, width and depth 3.2 mm).
- Geometry variables: a two-tier wooden jig controlled longitudinal grade 0–5 % and cross slope 0–4 %, verified directly on the specimen with a digital inclinometer.

## Results

- Properties: first-cracking strength 3.4 MPa (ECC-Cor 0) rising to 5.4–6.2 MPa (with corundum); strain capacity 2.1 % falling to 0.8–1.1 %; flexural strength maintained at 10–12 MPa across all mixes.
- Skid resistance: ECC-Cor 0 scored BPN 53±9.1, below Singapore LTA's newly set road threshold of 55. Under day-1 exposure, BPN increased with corundum content (ECC-Cor 100 = 75). Day-3 exposure brought limited improvement because the hardened matrix exposed the corundum unevenly.
- Durability: after 100,000 wheel passes BPN remained above 65 and MTD was unchanged at 0.34 mm — thanks to corundum's high hardness, no resurfacing is needed within the pavement life.
- Drainage: OFT was unmeasurable for ECC-Cor 0 (>500 s); ECC-Cor 80 took 62.11 s, exceeding the 9–10 s limits used by US state DOTs. Grooving brought Gro 1 to 7.73 s and Gro 4 (land 7.5 mm) to 3.38 s.
- Geometry effects: on ungrooved surfaces a 4 % cross slope reduced OFT by 9.7 %, a 5 % longitudinal grade by 17.2 %, and both together by 30 %. On grooved surfaces the effect fell to the 1–2 % level and geometry dependence disappeared — experimental support for the model's claim that geometric effects dominate at low MTD.
- Practical recommendations: use ECC-Cor as an overlay rather than a full-depth pavement to reduce consumption, and provide regular maintenance against groove clogging (dirt, sand, clay).
