# Constitutive Rheological Control (MFS Superplasticizer - HPMC Viscosity Modifier Interaction) for Self-Consolidating PVA-ECC (SC-ECC) and Tensile Strain-Hardening Behavior

## Summary

In hydrophilic poly(vinyl alcohol) fiber-reinforced engineered cementitious composites (PVA-ECC), relatively higher water-to-cement ratios (W/C = 0.40~0.45) are required to moderate excessive chemical interfacial bonding ($G_d$). However, this elevated water content frequently leads to cement particle sedimentation segregation and fiber clumping. To overcome these issues and create **Self-Consolidating PVA-ECC (SC-ECC)** that fills intricate reinforcement cages without external vibration, Prof. Victor C. Li and Dr. Hyun-Joon Kong (Univ. of Michigan, Ann Arbor) developed a **constitutive rheological control** framework. The system independently modulates electrostatic repulsion among cement particles via sulfonated melamine formaldehyde (MFS) superplasticizer while controlling steric hindrance and liquid-phase viscosity via hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC). Coupling micromechanical interface criteria with deformability indices ($\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2$), L-box passing ability, and uniaxial tensile tests demonstrated that combining 1.0 wt.% MFS with 0.15 wt.% HPMC maintained liquid viscosity above the critical particle settling threshold ($\eta_{crit}$), achieving zero bleeding (0.0 %), self-consolidating deformability ($\Gamma_2 = 2.6$), and **tensile strain capacity of 4.0 ~ 5.0 %** in unvibrated cast composite specimens.

## Key Contributions

1. **Two-stage constitutive rheological control framework decoupling electrostatic dispersion from steric viscosity**:
   - Formulated a methodology using strong polyelectrolyte MFS to maximize negative zeta potential and disperse cement flocs, paired with dissolved non-ionic HPMC chains to provide steric repulsion and viscous drag against sedimentation.
2. **Formulation of vibration-free self-consolidating PVA-ECC (SC-ECC)**:
   - Achieved high unvibrated slump flow (> 650 mm) and passing ability (L-Box ratio 1.0), enabling noise-free, vibrationless casting in heavily reinforced sections.
3. **Achievement of 5 % tensile ductility via 3D uniform PVA fiber dispersion**:
   - Completely eliminated hydrophilic PVA fiber clumping (100 %), matching the **4.5 ~ 5.0 % tensile elongation** and **4.5 MPa tensile strength** of conventionally vibrated ECC.
4. **Parallel integration of micromechanics and processing rheology**:
   - Established material design boundary charts (Charts I & II) identifying overlapping parameter windows satisfying both micromechanical bond criteria ($\tau_0, G_d$) and fresh rheological flowability.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture design (Section 2 & Charts I–II)**:
  - Binder: ASTM Type I ordinary Portland cement (specific gravity 3.15, $d_{50} = 15\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Aggregate: Fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, sand/cement ratio S/C = 0.60~0.80).
  - Admixture system:
    1. Superplasticizer (SP): MFS (sulfonated melamine formaldehyde, anionic dispersant, optimal dosage 1.0 wt.%).
    2. Viscosity modifier (VMA): HPMC (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, non-ionic modifier, molecular weight $10^5\ \text{g/mol}$, dosage 0.15 wt.%).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray surface-coated hydrophilic PVA fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elastic modulus 42 GPa, volume fraction $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - Water-to-cement ratio: W/C = 0.40~0.45.
- **Fresh state characterization (Section 2.2)**:
  - Mortar flow cone deformability index: $\Gamma_1 = (d_1 + d_2)/2d_0$ ($d_0 = 100\ \text{mm}$).
  - Large slump cone ECC deformability: $\Gamma_2 = (D_1 + D_2)/2D_0$ ($D_0 = 200\ \text{mm}$, measured immediately and after 10 min rest).
  - L-Box and U-Box tests: Passing ability through 35 mm rebar gaps under self-weight (no vibration).
- **Hardened uniaxial tensile testing (Section 2.3)**:
  - Specimen geometry: $150\ \text{mm} \times 75\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$ plates cured in water for 28 d.
  - Servohydraulic testing at $0.005\ \text{mm/s}$ displacement control (dual LVDTs, 50 mm gauge length).

### 2. Workability and strain-hardening mechanisms
- **Rheological compatibility**: MFS provides 100 % particle coverage to reduce yield stress, while HPMC maintains matrix viscosity above $\eta_{crit}$ to arrest particle settling, resolving the trade-off between fluidity and segregation resistance.
- **Saturated multiple microcracking**: Flow-induced fiber alignment along placement streamlines established uniform bridging stresses ($\sigma_0$), narrowing crack spacings to $1.5\sim 2.5\ \text{mm}$ and yielding $4.8\%$ tensile ductility (5 % level).

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that electrosteric and constitutive rheological control enables simultaneous achievement of self-consolidation and 5 % tensile strain-hardening in hydrophilic PVA-ECC.
