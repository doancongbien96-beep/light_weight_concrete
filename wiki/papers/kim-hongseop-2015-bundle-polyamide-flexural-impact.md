# Assessment of Flexural Toughness and Impact Resistance of Bundle-Type Polyamide Fiber-Reinforced Concrete

## Summary

This experimental study compares **bundle-type polyamide fibers**—composed of 544 fine nylon filaments (diameter 19.5 μm) assembled via air interlacing (30 mm×0.5 mm, AR 60, tensile strength 594 MPa, manufactured by Kolon)—with hooked-end steel fibers (identical dimensions, 1140 MPa) at equivalent volume fractions ($V_f = 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\%$, 0.5·0.75·1.0 %). Although steel fibers exhibited higher static flexural strength and fracture energy, polyamide fibers performed equally or better in mitigating scabbing under high-velocity projectile impact at identical fiber contents. The primary mechanism is the 2.46-fold higher fiber count per unit volume, which disperses impact stresses and promotes a uniform distribution of microcracks.

## Key Contributions

- **Trade-off between fiber count and bond strength**: At equal $V_f$, polyamide fibers provide 2.46-fold more individual fibers than steel fibers (3.8 million vs 1.56 million fibers/$\text{m}^3$ at 1 %), despite having a 3.35-fold lower interfacial pullout resistance. This creates a design dichotomy: steel fibers dominate static peak flexural capacity, whereas fiber count governs dynamic impact scabbing resistance.
- **Failure mode of bundle fibers**: Rather than simple pullout, the matrix penetrates the inter-filament gaps of the bundle, resulting in **elongation followed by fracture without pullout**, validating the bundle design's high interfacial contact area. Hooked steel fibers exhibited gradual softening via hook straightening and pullout.
- **Decoupling penetration and scabbing**: Compressive-strength-governed penetration depth was independent of fiber reinforcement (matching modified NDRC predictions), whereas reflected tensile-wave-driven rear scabbing and cratering were inversely proportional to fiber content.
- **Scabbing limit thickness data**: For $\varnothing 10\ \text{mm}$ (⌀10 mm) projectiles at 300 m/s, scabbing limit thickness was 40 mm for plain, SF-0.5, and SF-0.75, reducing to 30 mm for PA-0.75, PA-1.0, and SF-1.0. For $\varnothing 25\ \text{mm}$ (⌀25 mm, 66.4 g) at 170 m/s, limits were 80 mm (plain), 70 mm (PA and SF 0.5%), and 60 mm for higher contents.
- **Front crater diameter regression**: The coefficient $k$ in $C_d = k \cdot P_d$ decreased from 7.18 (plain) to 3.11 (PA-1.00) and 3.40 (SF-1.00), demonstrating suppression of frontal spalling.

## Methods

- Concrete matrix: OPC + fly ash, W/B 0.40, S/a 55 %, maximum coarse aggregate size 20 mm, compressive strength 50.2~56.6 MPa. Polyamide fibers were coated with a hydrophobic surfactant (polyhydric alcohol ester 40~50 % + anionic surfactant 30~40 % + antistatic agent 10~30 %) to eliminate fiber balling and bleeding.
- Pullout testing: Embedment length 15 mm, matrix cross section 25×25 mm, evaluating $\tau_{max} = P_{max}/(\pi D L)$.
- Flexural testing: ASTM C1609 unnotched prisms ($100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$, 100×100×400 mm) and RILEM 50-FMC notched beams (50 mm notch) for fracture energy $G_f$.
- Impact testing: Nitrogen gas gun firing spherical projectiles of $\varnothing 10\ \text{mm}$ (4.06 g, 170/300 m/s) and $\varnothing 25\ \text{mm}$ (66.4 g, 170 m/s) against plates of thickness 30~70 mm.

## Results

- Flexural strength $f_r$ (unnotched): Plain 5.44 MPa; PA 6.18, 7.42, 8.32 MPa (6.18·7.42·8.32); SF 8.45, 10.22, 11.52 MPa (8.45·10.22·11.52 MPa). Equivalent flexural strength $f'_r$: PA 3.11~5.84 MPa vs SF 6.93~10.31 MPa. Fracture energy $G_f$: Plain 857 N·m/$\text{m}^2$; PA 2705~6908 N·m/$\text{m}^2$; SF 5597~14642 N·m/$\text{m}^2$ (5597~14642 N·m/m²).
- PA-1.0 % exhibited a strain-hardening load-deflection curve where the secondary peak exceeded the first crack load.
- Fracture surface observations: PA showed uniform fiber distribution and multiple microcracking; SF exhibited localized wide cracks. Post-impact rear faces showed fiber fracture in PA vs pullout-induced spalling in SF.
- Strain rate effects: Static ($10^{-6}\sim 10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, 10⁻⁶~10⁻⁵ s⁻¹) vs impact ($10^1\sim 10^2\ \text{s}^{-1}$, 10¹~10² s⁻¹).
