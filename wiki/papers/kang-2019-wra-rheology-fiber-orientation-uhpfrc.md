# Quantitative Relationship Between Water-Reducing Admixture (WRA) Dosage, Rheological Parameters, 2D Fiber Orientation Coefficient ($\eta_\theta$), and Flexural Performance in 150 MPa UHPFRC

## Summary

To control the spatial orientation and dispersion of steel fibers during placement flow and maximize flexural performance in 150 MPa-grade ultra-high-performance fiber-reinforced concrete (UHPFRC), Professor Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author) at Chonnam National University, Professor Su-Tae Kang at Daegu University, and Professor Jae-Hong Kim at KAIST formulated four UHPFRC mixtures (W/B = 0.20, straight steel fibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) varying in high-range water-reducing admixture (WRA) dosage (30.8, 46.3, 61.7, 77.1 $\text{kg/m}^3$). The authors measured Bingham rheological parameters (plastic viscosity $\mu$, yield stress $\tau_0$) using a vane rotational rheometer, mini-slump flow, compressive strength (155~175 MPa) after $90\ ^\circ\text{C}$ steam curing, CMOD-controlled three-point notched beam bending, fiber dispersion coefficients ($\alpha_f$), and fiber orientation coefficients ($\eta_\theta = \int p(\theta)\cos^2\theta d\theta$). Increasing WRA dosage widened slump flow ($210 \rightarrow 260\ \text{mm}$) while sharply decreasing plastic viscosity ($38.5 \rightarrow 15.2\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). At an optimal plastic viscosity ($\mu \approx 28.5\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$), **Mix 2 (WRA $46.3\ \text{kg/m}^3$) maximized shear-flow-induced longitudinal fiber alignment, attaining an orientation coefficient $\eta_\theta = 0.82$, a peak flexural strength of $38.5\ \text{MPa}$, and a compressive strength of $172.5\ \text{MPa}$**. In contrast, excessive WRA (Mix 4) triggered fiber segregation, settling, and misalignment ($\eta_\theta = 0.68$), causing a 26.0 % decline in flexural strength.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of quantitative linkage between WRA dosage, plastic viscosity ($\mu$), orientation coefficient ($\eta_\theta$), and flexural strength in UHPFRC**:
   - Proved that matrix rheology governs fiber rotational dynamics during placement flow, shifting flexural tensile strength by up to 35 %.
2. **Identification of optimal fiber orientation coefficient ($\eta_\theta = 0.82$) and flexural strength ($38.5\ \text{MPa}$)**:
   - Demonstrated via cross-sectional image analysis that an optimal plastic viscosity ($28.5\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) forces steel fibers to align parallel to the principal tensile stress axis along velocity gradients.
3. **Clarification of rheological segregation and performance degradation at high WRA dosages**:
   - Showed that when viscosity drops below $15\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, fiber interference and gravitational settling reduce $\eta_\theta$, causing a 26 % drop in flexural capacity.
4. **Standardization of vane rheometer and CMOD-controlled notched beam testing**:
   - Eliminated fiber-induced wall slippage errors in rheological characterization using a vane spindle and standardized CMOD-load curves.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Table 1, $\text{kg/m}^3$)**:
  - Binders: Type I Portland cement (771 $\text{kg/m}^3$) + zirconium silica fume (193 $\text{kg/m}^3$) + quartz filler (231 $\text{kg/m}^3$) + silica sand (848 $\text{kg/m}^3$, $d_{50} = 250\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Water and additives: W/B = 0.20 (165 $\text{kg/m}^3$), expansive agent 57.8 $\text{kg/m}^3$, shrinkage reducing agent 7.7 $\text{kg/m}^3$, defoamer 1.4 $\text{kg/m}^3$.
  - Reinforcing fibers: Straight high-strength steel fibers (diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm, tensile strength 2500 MPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, 156 $\text{kg/m}^3$).
  - Four mixture series (WRA dosage):
    - Mix 1: WRA 30.8 $\text{kg/m}^3$ (low flow).
    - Mix 2: WRA 46.3 $\text{kg/m}^3$ (optimal viscosity).
    - Mix 3: WRA 61.7 $\text{kg/m}^3$ (high flow).
    - Mix 4: WRA 77.1 $\text{kg/m}^3$ (ultra-high flow / low viscosity).
- **Testing protocols (Sections 2.2–2.3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Rheology: Brookfield rotational rheometer with vane spindle (0~19 rpm steps, Bingham model $\tau = \tau_0 + \mu \dot{\gamma}$).
  - Flexural testing: Notched beams ($100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$, notch depth 10 mm, span 300 mm, clip-gauge CMOD, 0.3 mm/min).
  - Cross-section image analysis: Scanned cut sections at 35 mm and 220 mm to measure fiber dispersion ($\alpha_f$) and orientation ($\theta$).

### 2. Rheology-flow alignment coupling mechanism
- **Fiber rotation dynamics under shear flow**: At a plastic viscosity of $28.5\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, velocity gradients ($\dot{\gamma} = dv/dy$) across boundary surfaces impart rotational hydrodynamic torque, aligning fibers along the longitudinal beam axis.
- **Instability at low viscosity**: When WRA is excessive ($77.1\ \text{kg/m}^3$), loss of shear transfer causes 3D random tumbling and bottom settling, reducing effective bridging area and degrading flexural capacity to 28.5 MPa.

- **Overall conclusion**: This study demonstrated that regulating WRA dosage to achieve optimal plastic viscosity maximizes fiber orientation coefficients and raises UHPFRC flexural strength to 38.5 MPa.
