## Summary

Paper reporting the attainment of a 13.68 ± 0.85 % uniaxial direct-tensile strain capacity in fly ash single-precursor geopolymer composites (EGC) together with its micromechanical justification (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University; co-authored by Ranade and Victor C. Li; Cement and Concrete Composites, vol. 122). Varying the sodium metasilicate pentahydrate (SMP)/sodium hydroxide (SH) ratio of the activator from 0.5 to 2.5 served as the sole mix variable, with SMP/SH = 1.5 (UD-EGC15) optimal. All mixes were lightweight composites below a density of 1.83 g/cm³ that exceeded a strain capacity of 10 %, and UD-EGC15 achieved saturated multiple cracking thanks to the wide margin of its bridging capacity σ₀ of 7.71 MPa over its first-cracking strength of 3.70 MPa.

## Key Contributions

- **Attainment of a 13.7 % strain capacity in fly ash-based EGC**: the highest literature record at the time of publication. All four mixes exceeded ε_ts of 10 %.
- **Single-variable optimization of the SMP/SH ratio**: presented a route that simultaneously controls matrix stiffness (K_m, E_m) and interfacial friction (τ₀) through activator composition alone.
- **Precise derivation of micromechanics parameters**: applied ASTM E399 notched-beam K_m and single-fiber pullout G_d·τ₀·β formulations to explain the ductility differences via PSH energy/strength indices.
- **Lightweight and ultra-ductile in parallel**: a density below 1.83 g/cm³ delivered weight reduction while meeting the minimum structural-strength requirement of ACI 318.

## Methods

- Binder: Class F fly ash 100 % (SiO₂ 74.59 %, Al₂O₃ 14.38 %).
- Mixes (Table 1, mass ratios): FA 0.65~0.80, SH fixed at 0.10, SMP 0.10~0.25 (SMP/SH = 0.5~2.5), water 0.345, SP 0.003~0.009, VMA 0.001, antifoaming agent 0.001, PE fiber 1.75 vol.%.
- Fiber: PE fiber (diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, density 0.97 g/cm³, elastic modulus 88 GPa).
- Tests: compression (ASTM), JSCE uniaxial direct tension (gauge length 80 mm, displacement transducers attached on both sides), ASTM E399 notched prism K_m, single-fiber pullout (G_d = 2(P_a − P_b)²/(π²E_f d_f³), τ₀ = P_b/(π d_f L_e)).

## Results

| UD-EGC15 | **1.5** | 3.70 ± 0.11 | 6.79 ± 0.58 | **13.68 ± 0.85** | 0.71 |
| UD-EGC20 | 2.0 | 4.63 ± 0.26 | 8.10 ± 0.42 | 12.26 ± 0.71 | 0.78 |
| UD-EGC25 | 2.5 | 4.99 ± 0.34 | 7.18 ± 0.21 | 10.88 ± 0.93 | 0.66 |

- Cracking characteristics (Table 6): crack number for UD-EGC15 of 77.5 ± 4.7 (note the cautionary marking on a 5 cm gauge basis; see Fig. 8 of the paper), crack spacing of 1.00~1.21 mm (saturated state), mean crack width of 131~148 µm.
- Micromechanics (Tables 7~8): UD-EGC15 K_m of 0.07 MPa√m, E_m of 8.29 GPa, τ₀ of 0.55 MPa, σ₀ of 7.71 MPa, J'_b of 184.03 J/m². The combination of the low K_m and the sufficient bridging capacity is the direct cause of the ultra-high ductility.
- Interpretation: as SMP/SH increases, first-cracking strength rises while strain capacity decreases — an activator-composition-axis demonstration of the PSH logic ([[pseudo-strain-hardening-criteria]]) that matrix rigidification undermines saturated multiple cracking.
