## Summary
A study clarifying the high-viscosity and high water-demand problems arising from the high specific surface area and layered particle morphology of calcined clay (metakaolin) in limestone calcined clay cement ($LC^3$), an eco-friendly low-carbon cement, and presenting improvement measures for rheology and mechanical performance through PCE superplasticizer molecular design and particle size control. Yield stress and plastic viscosity progression were measured as functions of calcination temperature ($750 \sim 850 °C$) and clay purity (kaolinite content 40 ~ 95 %), and through optimal PCE dispersion design, flowability equal to OPC was secured at $W/B = 0.40$ while achieving a 28 d compressive strength of 62 MPa.

## Key Contributions
- **Correlation between calcined clay particle morphology and rheology formulated**: water adsorption and shear thixotropy behavior of the layered clay structure quantified.
- **Adsorption mechanism of polycarboxylate (PCE) superplasticizers dedicated to $LC^3$ clarified**: a long-side-chain PCE structure derived that suppresses interlayer clay adsorption and adsorbs selectively onto cement/limestone surfaces.
- **Pore densification by hydration reaction synergy**: carboaluminate formation proven to cut 28 d porosity by 35 % relative to OPC.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: $LC^3-50$ (clinker 50 %, calcined clay 30 %, limestone 15 %, gypsum 5 %), $W/B = 0.40$.
- **Measurement**: rotational viscometer rheology (yield stress $\tau_y$, plastic viscosity $\mu$), zeta potential, isothermal calorimetry, compressive strength.

## Results
- Optimal PCE (1.2 wt %) applied $LC^3$ mortar: initial yield stress $4.5 Pa$, plastic viscosity $0.85 Pa\cdot s$ (flowability at OPC-equivalent level).
- 28 d compressive strength $62.8 MPa$ and 90 d compressive strength $74.5 MPa$ achieved.
