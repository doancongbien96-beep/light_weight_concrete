## Summary
This study systematically investigated the influence of various curing regimes (standard water curing, air drying, high-temperature steam curing, and wet-dry cyclic curing) on the compressive strength, direct tensile performance, and microcrack control behavior of high-ductility cementitious composites (PVA-ECC). Water curing raises the matrix hydration degree and thereby drives high compressive strength, but it excessively increases the matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) and somewhat degrades tensile ductility, whereas appropriate air curing or wet-dry curing maintains the interfacial chemical bond at a moderate level and was shown to sustain an ultra-high-ductility tensile strain capacity of $4.0 \sim 5.5 \%$.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the interaction between curing environment and micromechanical parameters ($K_m, \tau_0, G_d$)**: demonstrated the mechanism by which the increase in interfacial chemical bonding ($G_d$) due to hydrate precipitation under water curing raises the fiber rupture fraction and reduces tensile ductility.
- **Early strength development and ductility preservation under high-temperature steam curing**: steam curing at $60 °C$ for 24 h achieved a 1 d compressive strength of $42 MPa$ and a tensile strain capacity of $3.8 \%$ at an early age, establishing the feasibility of precast production.
- **Proposal of a mix that prevents ductility loss in drying environments**: derived an optimal mix that secures the strain-hardening saturation criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip} > 3.0$) even under air curing by expanding the fly ash replacement ratio.

## Methods
- **Mix specification**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15), $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash replacement ratio 60 wt %.
- **Curing conditions**: (1) standard water curing ($20 °C$), (2) air curing ($20 °C, 50 \% RH$), (3) steam curing ($60 °C, 100 \% RH, 24 h$), (4) wet-dry cycling.
- **Test items**: 28 d uniaxial tension test, single-fiber pullout test, compressive strength, elastic modulus.

## Results
- Air-cured PVA-ECC: tensile strength $4.8 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $5.1 \%$, compressive strength $42.5 MPa$.
- Standard water curing: tensile strength $5.8 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $3.2 \%$, compressive strength $56.8 MPa$ (ductility reduced by the increased matrix toughness).
- Steam curing: 1 d tensile strength $5.2 MPa$, strain capacity $3.6 \%$, compressive strength $45.0 MPa$.
