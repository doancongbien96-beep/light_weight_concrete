# Engineering of PVA-ECC Based on Micromechanics

## Summary

This classic paper establishes the micromechanical interfacial engineering and mixture design principles required to develop high-performance, cost-effective PVA-ECC by overcoming the excessive chemical bond ($G_d$) and premature fiber rupture inherent to hydrophilic polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) fibers in cement matrices. By applying an organic silicone-based oiling agent (0.8 ~ 1.2 wt %) to PVA fiber surfaces, the authors reduced chemical debonding fracture energy ($G_d$) and optimized frictional bond strength ($\tau_0$), shifting the fiber failure mode from premature rupture to progressive multiple fiber pullout. Incorporating commercial PVA fibers at $V_f = 2.0\%$, this interface tailoring achieved an ideal multiple-cracking strain-hardening composite with a tensile strength of 4.5 ~ 6.0 MPa, a tensile strain capacity of $4.0 \sim 5.5\%$, and maximum crack widths under $60\ \mu\text{m}$.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of chemical bond ($G_d$) tailoring theory for PVA fibers**: Controlled the high hydrogen bonding ($G_d > 4.0\ \text{J/m}^2$) of uncoated PVA fibers down to an optimal $G_d \approx 1.5 \sim 2.0\ \text{J/m}^2$ range via surface oiling.
- **Formulation of PVA fiber pullout bridging constitutive relations**: Quantified how fiber cross-sectional reduction (slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$) and interfacial friction ($\tau_0 = 1.0 \sim 2.5\ \text{MPa}$, 1.0 ~ 2.5 MPa) govern the peak bridging stress ($\sigma_0$).
- **Validation of saturated multiple cracking criteria**: Established standard mixture proportions for silicone-oiled PVA-ECC that satisfy micromechanical PSH design criteria ($J_b'/J_{tip} > 3.0$, $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} > 1.4$).

## Methods

- **Fiber specifications**: Kuraray REC15 PVA fibers (diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm, elastic modulus 41 GPa, tensile strength 1600 MPa). Surface oiling content: 0.0 wt %, 0.5 wt %, 0.8 wt %, 1.2 wt %.
- **Single fiber pullout tests**: Directly measured interfacial parameters ($G_d$, $\tau_0$, $\beta$) across various matrix formulations.
- **Direct tension tests**: Quasi-static tensile tests on $V_f = 2.0\%$ plate dogbone specimens.

## Results

- Uncoated PVA fibers: Over 80 % of fibers ruptured during pullout, resulting in brittle failure with tensile strain capacity < 0.5 %.
- 0.8 ~ 1.2 wt % surface-oiled PVA fibers: Fiber rupture rate dropped below 20 %, promoting full pullout, achieving tensile strain capacities of $4.5 \sim 5.2\%$, and forming over 30 saturated microcracks.
