# UHPFRC flexural performance enhanced by an optimized layered-structure concept — closed-form governing equations and double-layer beam experiments

## Summary

Theory-plus-experiment study by the Brouwers·Yu group at TU Eindhoven. It proposes a double-layered UHPFRC (coarse-aggregate-bearing) concept that concentrates steel fibers only in the tension side of flexural beams. Using the layer elastic-modulus ratio α = E1/E2 and the thickness ratio β = h2/H, it builds deterministic governing equations that derive the neutral-axis position, first-cracking load, and debonding load in closed form; on that basis it designs and tests double-layered beams with β = 0.4~0.8 (100×100×500 mm, three-point bending on a 400 mm span). At an identical total fiber content (equivalent Vf 1.2 %), the arrangement of 0.6 % in the top layer plus 1.6 % in the bottom (U0.6(40)-1.6(60)) achieved a peak flexural load +24 % and flexural energy +14 % over the monolayer, and showed that fiber efficiency (flexural energy per unit mass) peaks at β = 0.6.

## Key Contributions

- Closed-form analysis framework for the first failure stage of layered beams: equilibrium conditions yield the neutral axis h0 (Eq. 3), the critical thickness ratio βc = √α/(1+√α) (Eq. 4), and the critical tensile-strength ratio γc (Eq. 10), discriminating whether first cracking starts in the top or the bottom layer. It covers the shear-debonding Fd,shear (Eq. 22) and tension-debonding Fd,tension (Eq. 24) load equations plus the "bond-strength conditions for preventing debonding" (Eqs. 25~27) — computable from tensile strength and elastic modulus alone, without stress-strain relations, securing design practicality.
- Experimental verification: computed first-cracking load deviates <7 % from measured values (β = 0.4~1.0). For β = 0.4~0.8 the failure stage always ends in bottom-layer cracking without debonding — consistent with the theoretical prediction.
- Three-stage behavior of the peak flexural load discovered: gradual rise over β 0~0.4 → jump at β 0.4 → gradual rise over β 0.6~1.0. The jump marks where the bridging mechanism switches from aggregate-dominated to fiber-dominated.
- New fiber-efficiency concept eff_f = E/m_f: maximal at β = 0.6. Flexural energy E improves by about 20×, 31×, and 32× over the plain monolayer (U0(100)) at β = 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, respectively.
- Quantified fiber-redistribution effect: at identical total fiber content, U0(40)-2(60) reaches Fmax 30.12 kN (+14 %) and U0.6(40)-1.6(60) reaches Fmax 32.79 kN (+24 %) versus U1.2(100). Energy is -10 % when the top layer carries no fiber and +14 % when 0.6 % is placed there — peak load is governed by the bottom-layer fibers, while both layers contribute to energy.
- Reinterpretation of the interface's role: a mere 45 min casting interval (before initial set) achieved a bond strength up to 9.28 MPa (surface treatment unnecessary). The interface becomes a fine-crack initiation site through stress concentration, yet this multiple fine cracking consumes fracture energy and instead raises the flexural energy.
- Crack-propagation observation: bottom-layer cracking (t ≈ 12 min) → small cracks in the top layer near the interface (t ≈ 13.5 min) → coalescence into a macrocrack (t ≈ 16.5 min) → complete failure (t ≈ 35 min), a far slower failure progression than the monolayer.

## Methods

- Mix (w/b = 0.20): CEM I 52.5R 588, silica fume 39.2, limestone powder 156.8, standard sand 839.9, basalt coarse aggregate 2–5 mm 413.2 and 5–8 mm 232.3 kg/m³ (Brouwers particle-size design), water 157 kg/m³, PCE superplasticizer 5.0~17.0 kg/m³ (flow ~560 mm), smooth straight steel fibers (length 13 mm, diameter 0.2 mm, tensile strength 2750 MPa) at Vf 0~2 %.
- Specimens: 100×100×500 mm beams; the top layer cast 45 min after the bottom (while the bottom layer is still before initial set — deformation prevention + strong bonding); water curing 28 d. Three-point bending over a 400 mm span, displacement control 0.2 mm/min, two LVDTs, no notch.
- Beam configurations: U0(60)-2(40), U0(40)-2(60), U0(20)-2(80) (β effect), U0.6(40)-1.6(60) (optimum), U0(40)-1.2(60) and U0.6(40)-1.2(60) (per-layer fiber effect), plus monolayer controls U0(100), U1.2(100), U2(100).
- Basic properties: compression per EN 12390-3, splitting tension per EN 12390-6 (interlayer bond strength also by the splitting test).

## Results

- Basic properties: compressive strength 140~153 MPa (negligible fiber-content effect); splitting tensile strength at 2 % fiber is +97.3 % over the plain mix. The 0.6 %-1.6 % interface bond strength peaks at 9.28 MPa.
- First-cracking load: insensitive from β 0.4→1.0, moving 25.67→26.69 kN (consistent with theory, α = 0.8~1 range).
- Fmax·E: basis for selecting β = 0.6 — entry into the post-jump gentle-rise region of Fmax plus maximum fiber efficiency.
- Redistribution comparison (common equivalent Vf 1.2 %): Fmax of U1.2(100) / U0(40)-2(60) / U0.6(40)-1.6(60) = approx 26.4 / 30.12 / 32.79 kN; E differs by -10 %/+14 %.
- Per-layer fibers: raising the bottom layer from 1.2→1.6 % (top fixed at 0.6 %) gives Fmax +19 % and E +38 %; adding top-layer fiber increases only E, nonlinearly.
- Conclusion: top layer 40 mm·Vf 0.6 % + bottom layer 60 mm·Vf 1.6 % is optimal. Multi-layering and dynamic (impact) loading are named as follow-up work.
