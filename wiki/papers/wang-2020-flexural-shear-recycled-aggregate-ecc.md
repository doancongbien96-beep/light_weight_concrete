## Summary
A study evaluating the four-point flexural performance, direct shear behavior, and multiple-cracking control characteristics of eco-friendly recycled-aggregate ECC (RA-ECC) in which natural silica sand was replaced with construction-waste-derived recycled fine aggregates (RFA, particle size $< 1.18 mm$) at 30 %, 50 %, 70 %, and 100 %. The study showed that porous aged mortar attached to RFA surfaces lowers the matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$), thereby making the PSH strain-hardening criterion easier to satisfy. At 50 % RFA replacement, a 28 d flexural modulus of rupture of 12.8 MPa, a deflection ductility ratio of 8.5, and a shear strength of 5.4 MPa were maintained, demonstrating the feasibility of manufacturing structural ECC with recycled aggregates.

## Key Contributions
- **Micromechanics mechanism of recycled fine aggregate incorporation in ECC clarified**: the low elastic modulus and porosity of RFA were confirmed to reduce matrix toughness, instead improving the fiber-bridging energy performance ratio ($J_b'/J_{tip}$) by 15 ~ 25 %.
- **Flexural and direct shear strength correlation models derived for each RFA replacement ratio**: mechanical performance of at least 90 % relative to natural silica-sand ECC was maintained up to 50 % replacement.
- **Eco-friendly circular-economy model established**: maximization of construction-waste recycling rates and reduction of environmental damage from natural sand extraction.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: cement + fly ash (FA/C = 2.2), RFA (density $2.32 g/cm3$, absorption 5.8 %), PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15).
- **Test program**: four-point flexural test, Iosipescu direct shear test, 28 d compressive strength, and electron-microscopy interface analysis.

## Results
- 50 % RFA mix: compressive strength $45.2 MPa$, flexural strength $12.8 MPa$, direct shear strength $5.4 MPa$, and mean crack width $38 \mu m$.
- Even at 100 % full replacement, a flexural deflection ductility index of 7.2 and tensile strain-hardening behavior were stably exhibited.
