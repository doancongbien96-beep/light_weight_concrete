# Critical Review on Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC) (Elmesalami & Celik 2022)

## Summary

Addressing the carbon footprint issue where the OPC content of standard ECC is 2~3 times that of conventional concrete, this review compiles 55 papers published from the inaugural 2012 Lee et al. study through December 2021 on Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC / SHGC). After mapping the scholarly landscape via VOSviewer scientometric analysis, micromechanical parameters and mechanical performances are quantitatively benchmarked against ECC M45, evaluating matrix, fiber, curing parameters, durability, self-healing, and environmental impacts. The review concludes that EGC generally exhibits higher tensile strain capacity and lower compressive strength than ECC, with advantageous PSH indices and 50~76 % lower CO₂ emissions.

## Key Contributions

1. **Scientometric mapping**: VOSviewer bibliometric mapping based on Scopus (53 journal articles excluding 2 PhD dissertations from 55 collected papers). Most influential journal = Construction and Building Materials; core productive authors = Nematollahi B., Sanjayan J., Li V., Lee B.; leading publishing countries = China and USA (followed by Australia, Denmark, Brazil). High-frequency keywords included fly ash, slag, PVA fibers, tensile strength/strain, compressive strength, and micromechanics. Research gaps were identified in durability, self-healing, and dynamic impact behavior.
2. **Quantitative micromechanical parameter comparison (EGC vs ECC M45)**: Matrix elastic modulus $E_m$ is ~53 % lower, and matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ is ~45 % lower (reflecting lower matrix stiffness/toughness rather than brittleness). Chemical bond $G_d$ is ~29 % higher (1.5~5 times ECC in certain literature), while slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$ is ~67 % lower (1/2~1/3 of ECC). Interfacial frictional bond $\tau_0$ is on average 45 % lower with conflicting literature reports (ECC 1.5~2.5 times higher vs EGC 2~3 times higher, likely due to reaction product chemistry). $\text{PSH}_{stress}$ averages 1.5 times higher, and $\text{PSH}_{energy}$ averages 5 times higher, confirming that lower matrix toughness benefits energy criterion satisfaction under the Yang et al. (2008) micromechanics framework.
3. **Matrix design parameters synthesis**: (a) Slag substitution: Above 40 %, workability drops, setting accelerates, and tensile strength increases via hybrid C-S-H / C-A-S-H gel formation; slag types (with 2 % gypsum vs gypsum-free) alter $\tau_0$ and $K_m$ (Nematollahi et al.). (b) Activator: Sodium-based ($\text{NaOH} + \text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$) outperforms potassium and lime systems in compression, tension, strain capacity, and PSH indices. Silicate modulus $M_s$: $\tau_0$ and $E_m$ peak at 1.2, whereas $G_d, \beta, K_m$ peak at 1.0 (inverted U-shaped optimum). Higher NaOH molarity increases strength up to a threshold, while strain capacity favors lower molarities (8 M optimizes $K_m$ and $\text{PSH}_{energy}$, Zahid et al.). Sodium metasilicate pentahydrate (SMP) activator achieved 13.7 % strain capacity ($\text{SMP}/\text{NaOH} = 1.5$). (c) Fine aggregate: Adding sand raises $E_m$ and $K_m$ ($s/b = 0.3 \rightarrow 0.6$ raises $E_m$ +14~36 %, $K_m$ +16~33 %) while reducing tensile strain; coarse sand (600 μm–1.18 mm) lowers $K_m$ by 7~22 %, favoring fine silica sand ($\le 212\ \mu\text{m}$, 212 µm). (d) W/GP ratio: Decreasing W/GP raises strength, strain, and $\text{PSH}_{energy}$ analogously to w/c.
4. **Fiber parameters**: Increasing PVA from 1 to 2 vol % raises tensile strength by +30~43 % and tensile strain capacity up to 1.4 times (flow −22 %). PVA has higher $\tau_0$ and $G_d$ than PE due to hydrophilicity, but higher $\beta$ reduces $J'_b$, lowering $\text{PSH}_{stress}$ by −25 % and $\text{PSH}_{energy}$ by −6 %. PBO fiber interfacial friction is 3 times that of UHMWPE and 2.5 times that of PVA. Hybridization: PVA + recycled tire steel (RTS) increases compressive strength by +14~25 % and reduces shrinkage; PVA + recycled tire polymer (RTP) degrades tensile capacity (RTP 1 % drops strain by −99 %); MWCNT 0.1 % + PVA 0.2 % achieved 38.43 MPa compressive and 7.75 MPa flexural strength.
5. **Durability and self-healing**: In sulfuric acid exposure (6 days immersion + 1 day drying $\times 15$ cycles), EGC mass loss was less than half that of ECC, where surface PVA fibers devitrified and degraded flexural strength, demonstrating superior chemical resistance at EGC interfaces (Ohno & Li). In POFA-EGC sulfate exposure, damage severity followed $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 > \text{mixed} > \text{MgSO}_4$, accelerated when molarity increased from 10 to 14 M. Self-healing products are predominantly amorphous aluminosilicates; PE-PVA hybrids showed superior self-healing evaluated by UPV at 7 days.
6. **Environmental impact (MSI-based)**: Embodied energy of heat-cured EGC is up to 17 % lower than ECC M45, and 35 % lower under ambient curing. CO₂ emissions are 50~76 % lower (and 25~30 % lower than HVFA-ECC). Contribution breakdown: for embodied energy, PVA fiber (38~72 %) > activator (28~44 %) > thermal curing (3~13 %); for CO₂, activator (38~56 %) > PVA fiber (22~61 %) > thermal curing (10~14 %); in ECC, OPC accounts for 34~48 % of CO₂. Reducing PVA from 2 to 1 % cuts embodied energy by −11 % and CO₂ by −7 %.

## Methods

Literature was compiled from Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar through December 2021, and Scopus bibliographic records were mapped in VOSviewer for co-citation and keyword co-occurrence. Benchmark reference is ECC M45 (Wang & Li 2007, FA/OPC 1.2, PVA 2 vol %). Compositions across all 55 collected papers (raw materials, activator, molarity, modulus, sand, fibers, curing) are tabulated in Table 2, and micromechanical parameters ($\tau_0, G_d, \beta, E_m, K_m, J_{tip}$, PSH indices) and fresh/hardened properties (density, flow, compression, tension, flexure) are synthesized in Figs. 8–9.

## Results

- Collected EGC literature averages vs ECC M45 (Fig. 9 synthesis): tensile strength −21 %, compressive strength −39 %, tensile strain capacity +41 %, density comparable (lower than 2500 kg/m³ and above ACI 213 lightweight criteria).
- Extreme performance cases: GGBFS + $\text{Ca(OH)}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ activator + PE 1.75 % achieved tensile strength 13 MPa and strain capacity 7.5 % (more than twice PE-ECC); SMP activator EGC achieved 13.7 % strain; MWCNT-functionalized EGC achieved $\text{PSH}_{energy}$ of 43~45 and SMP-based systems 151~322.
- Dynamic behavior: Under impact tension, EGC strain capacity decreased relative to static (PVA 2.5 $\rightarrow$ 1.8 %, PE 3.6 $\rightarrow$ 2.6 %) but remained superior to ECC; PE fiber is preferred for impact; under drop-weight and elevated temperature, NaOH molarity of 10~12 mol/L is recommended.
- Structural retrofitting: Concrete prisms retrofitted with PVA-EGC exhibited 19 times the peak deflection and 3 times the flexural capacity of unreinforced controls (Al-Majidi et al.).
- Future directions: Expanding non-PVA fibers, accumulating durability and self-healing data, reducing activator/PVA footprints, and expanding from MSI to full life-cycle assessment (LCA) and economic analysis.
