## Summary
This is a study by the research team of Professor Y. Shaheen at Menoufia University, Egypt, which precisely identified impact resistance, residual fracture energy absorption ratio, and failure mode recovery behavior by performing emergency rehabilitation repairs on ferrocement thin-plate slabs ($500 \times 500 \times 25 mm$) cracked and penetrated by drop-weight impact loads (4.5 kg steel weight, impact velocity $6.5 m/s$) with various repair materials (high-strength epoxy mortar, ultra-fine wire mesh overlay, carbon fiber composite CFRP wrapping), followed by secondary re-impact loading tests. It demonstrated that applying the wire mesh-strengthened epoxy mortar repair method restored the impact energy absorption ratio beyond the **140 %** level compared with the initial intact specimens.

## Key Contributions
- **Performance comparison of four major repair/strengthening methods for impact-damaged ferrocement plates**: (1) epoxy mortar filling, (2) double welded wire mesh overlay, (3) CFRP sheet strengthening, (4) hybrid repair.
- **Quantification of the secondary energy absorption mechanism under re-impact loading**: wire mesh layers disperse the impact penetration energy through in-plane tensile membrane tension.
- **Demonstration of post-repair failure mode transition**: transition from localized punching shear penetration failure to ductile flexural deformation failure.
- **Presentation of rapid restoration guidelines for protective facilities and marine thin-plate structures**: establishment of restoration methods for military and civilian protective panels.

## Methods
- Prototype specimen: $500 \times 500 \times 25 mm$ ferrocement plate (stacked 4-layer welded steel wire mesh, mortar $f_c = 45 MPa$).
- Introduction of primary impact damage: instrumented drop-weight impact tester (drop weight 4.5 kg, $h = 2.0 m$, inducing primary cracking).
- Execution of repair methods: clearing of damaged-area debris $\rightarrow$ epoxy primer $\rightarrow$ wire mesh stacking and high-strength epoxy grouting ($f_c = 75 MPa$).
- Secondary impact test: measurement of the number of continuous drop-weight impacts ($N_2$) and fracture energy up to complete failure.

## Results
- Secondary impact resistance and fracture energy recovery data by repair method:

| Specimen | Repair method details | Impacts at secondary final failure ($N_2$) | Impact fracture energy ($J$) | Performance ratio vs original intact specimen (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Control (intact) | Undamaged reference specimen | 68 | 5980 | 100.0 % (reference) |
| Damaged (unrepaired) | Re-impacted as-is after damage | 14 | 1230 | 20.5 % (early failure) |
| Repaired-Epoxy | Epoxy mortar filling alone | 52 | 4570 | 76.4 % |
| **Repaired-Mesh** | **Wire mesh 2 layers + epoxy** | **95** | **8360** | **139.8 % (over-recovery of performance)** |
| Repaired-CFRP | Bidirectional CFRP wrapping | 110 | 9680 | 161.8 % |
