# Micromechanical Design of 165 MPa-Class High Strength High Ductility Concrete (HSHDC) and the First Development of Uniaxial Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\sigma_{tu} = 14.5\ \text{MPa}, \varepsilon_u = 4.5\%$)

## Summary

To break through simultaneously the severe brittle fracture of ultra-high strength concrete (UHPC) and the low compressive strength limit ($40\sim 60\ \text{MPa}$) of standard ECC, this immortal landmark founding paper of RILEM SHCC2 2011 by **Prof. Victor C. Li, endowed chair professor at the University of Michigan, Dr. Ravi Ranade, and the US Army Engineer Research and Development Center (ERDC) research team** developed the world's first innovative high strength high ductility concrete (HSHDC: High Strength-High Ductility Concrete), combining an ultra-high strength cement matrix (28-day compressive strength $165\ \text{MPa}$) with ultra-high strength polyethylene (Spectra PE) fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$) based on micromechanical PSH (Pseudo Strain-Hardening) theory — and through single-fiber pullout tests and uniaxial direct tension tests fully demonstrated a **28-day compressive strength of $165.0\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial ultimate tensile strength of $14.5\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strain of $4.50\%$, and dozens of multiple fine cracks**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First simultaneous realization of ultra-high strength (165 MPa) and ultra-high tensile ductility (4.5%)**:
   - Overcame the traditional inverse trade-off between strength and ductility, achieving a compressive strength of 165 MPa, a tensile strength of 14.5 MPa, and a tensile strain of 4.5% simultaneously in a single composite.
2. **Establishment of micromechanical PSH design criteria tailored to the ultra-high strength matrix**:
   - To overcome the high fracture toughness ($K_m \approx 1.5\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$) of the ultra-high strength matrix, adopted ultra-high strength ($2600\ \text{MPa}$) and high-stiffness ($73\ \text{GPa}$) Spectra PE fibers to establish frictional pullout conditions without fiber rupture.
3. **Quantification of single PE fiber pullout friction mechanics ($\tau_0 \approx 2.0\ \text{MPa}$)**:
   - Induced slip-hardening behavior ($\beta > 0$) of PE fibers in the dense nano matrix to maximize the complementary bridging energy $J_b'$.
4. **Establishment of an innovative material for next-generation military protective structures and super-tall seismic structures**:
   - Completed the core source-material technology for energy-absorbing structural members that withstand blast, impact, ballistic, and extreme seismic loads.

## Methods

- **Materials and HSHDC mix composition (Sections 2~3 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Matrix: Type I OPC + silica fume (SF/C = 0.30) + micro silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/C = 0.80, W/B = 0.22, high-range water reducer).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Spectra 1000 PE microfibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 2600\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 73\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Curing: steam curing at $90\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 48 hours, then room-temperature storage.
- **Mechanical test program (Section 4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone-shaped tensile specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, 0.1 mm/min displacement control).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (28-day age).
  - Single fiber pullout: micro-pullout tester ($L_e = 1.0\sim 3.0\ \text{mm}$).

### 2. Interpretation of the micromechanical PSH mechanism in the high strength matrix
- **Energy criterion $J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 2.5$ satisfied**: despite the rise in $J_{tip}$ of the ultra-high strength matrix, the huge tensile rupture strength ($2600\ \text{MPa}$) and slip frictional resistance of Spectra PE fibers form massive fiber bridging energy $J_b'$, completely blocking brittle crack propagation.
- **Ultra-tight crack-width confinement at $40\ \mu\text{m}$**: even under $4.5\%$ tensile elongation, 35 multiple fine cracks form with the mean crack width strictly limited to $40\ \mu\text{m}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a monumental landmark founding paper in which Prof. Victor C. Li and Prof. Ravi Ranade developed the world's first ultra-high strength, ultra-high ductility composite HSHDC carrying a 165 MPa compressive strength, a 14.5 MPa tensile strength, and a 4.5% tensile elongation simultaneously, and established its micromechanical design.
