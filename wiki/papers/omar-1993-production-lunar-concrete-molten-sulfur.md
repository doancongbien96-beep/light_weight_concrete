## Summary

To realize anhydrous (water-free) construction technology on the water-scarce lunar surface, this LPSC origin paper experimentally validated a Lunar Sulfur Concrete (LSC) process — hot-melt mixing at $130 \sim 150 °C$, strength development through solidification mechanics, and high-vacuum/thermal-cycle durability — using elemental sulfur, thermochemically extractable from sulfide minerals (troilite $FeS$) abundant in lunar soil (regolith), as the binder. By melt-mixing and molding 25 wt % sulfur binder with 75 wt % lunar simulant (MLS-1) aggregate at $140 °C$, it achieved an ultra-rapid-setting anhydrous space concrete developing a compressive strength of **42.5 MPa** and a flexural strength of **6.8 MPa** within one hour of casting.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a water-free anhydrous lunar concrete manufacturing process**: complete strength development within one hour via $140 °C$ low-temperature melting and cooling solidification.
- **Demonstration of early-age high strength in MLS-1 lunar simulant sulfur concrete**: compressive strength 42.5 MPa and flexural strength 6.8 MPa achieved.
- **Establishment of measures against vacuum sublimation in the lunar environment**: vacuum chamber ($10^{-6} torr$) tests confirmed a sulfur evaporation rate of $0.02 \%$/year or less below $80 °C$.
- **100 % in-situ resource circulation and remelt recyclability**: a sustainable space construction material that can be 100 % remolded by heating broken members.

## Methods
- Raw materials: elemental sulfur (purity 99.5 %, melting point $119 °C$), Minnesota Lunar Simulant MLS-1 ($SiO_2 43.8 \%$, $Al_2O_3 13.5 \%$, $CaO 10.2 \%$, $FeO 14.5 \%$).
- Manufacturing process: sulfur and preheated simulant mixed in a $140 °C$ constant-temperature oven, then cast into heated molds and compacted by vibration.
- Testing: ASTM C39 uniaxial compressive strength, 3-point flexural test, $10^{-6} torr$ vacuum outgassing test, and thermal cycling test.
