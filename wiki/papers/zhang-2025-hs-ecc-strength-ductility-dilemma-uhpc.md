## Summary
To fundamentally resolve the strength-ductility dilemma in which conventional ultra-high performance concrete (UHPC), as it secures ultra-high compressive strength ($f_c > 150 MPa$), suffers brittle tensile strain-softening failure with a tensile strain capacity of only $0.2 \sim 0.5 \%$, this most recent 2025 Cement and Concrete Research paper from Professor Victor Li's research team at the University of Michigan presented a multiscale design methodology for an innovative high-strength, ultra-high-ductility composite (HS-ECC) applying micromechanics-based flaw control and nano C-S-H gel interface tailoring. It achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **168 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **16.5 MPa**, a uniaxial tensile strain capacity of **8.8 %**, and a tensile fracture energy of **$1150 kJ/m^3$**, perfectly fusing the strength of UHPC and the ultra-high ductility of ECC.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of a unified micromechanics theory for overcoming the UHPC strength-ductility dilemma**: derivation of the optimal bridging equilibrium between an ultra-high-strength matrix ($K_m = 0.40 MPa\sqrt{m}$) and ultra-high-strength PE fibers ($f_t = 3200 MPa$).
- **Realization of world-leading tensile fracture energy ($1150 kJ/m^3$)**: an energy absorption density more than 30 times that of ordinary UHPC.
- **Demonstration of 8.8 % tensile strain capacity and ultra-dense multiple fine cracking**: formation of a network of more than 160 fine cracks within the gauge length (average crack width $15 \mu m$).
- **Paradigm shift toward next-generation long-span seismic/blast-resistant structural design**: realization of ultra-lightweight slim structures without rebar reinforcement.

## Methods
- Binder: ultra-fine cement + silica fume (25 %) + zirconia nanopowder, $W/B = 0.14$.
- Fiber: highly aligned ultrahigh molecular weight PE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3200 MPa).
- Testing: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC full-field strain tensor mapping), 28 d compressive strength (100 mm cubes), single-fiber pull-out test.
