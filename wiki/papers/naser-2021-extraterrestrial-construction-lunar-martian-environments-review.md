# Landmark Review of Extraterrestrial Construction Technologies for Lunar and Martian Extreme Environments: In-Situ Resources (ISRU Lunar/Martian Regolith), Waterless Binders (Sulfur Concrete, Sintering), and Autonomous 3D-Printing Construction

## Summary

To overcome the enormous launch cost from Earth ($\$10,000\sim \$100,000/\text{kg}$) and the extreme space environment (high vacuum, $-150\ ^\circ\text{C}\leftrightarrow +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$ severe thermal cycling, space radiation, micrometeorite impacts, and the absolute scarcity of water) — the core challenges of building permanent extraterrestrial habitat infrastructure for the Artemis program and crewed Mars exploration — this ASCE 2021 landmark review by **Prof. M. Z. Naser and Prof. Qiushi Chen of Clemson University, USA** comprehensively consolidates **the mineralogical and mechanical properties of lunar and martian surface soils (Lunar/Martian Regolith), ISRU (In-Situ Resource Utilization)-based waterless binder systems (molten sulfur concrete, geopolymers, magnesium phosphate cements, microwave and laser high-temperature sintered bodies, polymer binders), 3D concrete/sinter printing autonomous additive construction technology, and structural integrity design standards** — the highest-authority landmark review paper in the field of extraterrestrial construction engineering.

## Key Contributions

1. **Systematized physicochemical and mechanical performance classification of lunar/martian in-situ binder systems**:
   - Comparative evaluation of compressive strength, heat resistance, and constructability of completely water-free sulfur concrete (Sulfur Concrete), microwave sintering (Sintering), and alkali-activated geopolymers.
2. **Establishment of the extraterrestrial 3D printing (Extraterrestrial 3D Printing) additive manufacturing mechanism**:
   - Clarified the gravity and vacuum suitability of continuous extrusion (Contour Crafting), laser sintering layering (SLS), and powder-bed binder jetting (Binder Jetting) processes.
3. **Analysis of extreme space radiation (GCR/SPE) and micrometeorite shielding thickness ($0.5\sim 2.0\ \text{m}$)**:
   - Proposed a structural design that shields astronauts' annual radiation dose below terrestrial safety standards through dense sintered regolith blocks and regolith backfill structures.
4. **Establishment of a standard framework for future space civil engineering and planetary infrastructure construction**:
   - Completed an integrated research roadmap for building multi-planetary civilization infrastructure extending from Earth to the Moon and Mars.

## Methods

- **Extraterrestrial soil characterization and binder chemistry analysis (Sections 2~4 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Lunar regolith: anorthosite and basaltic silicates ($\text{SiO}_2\ 45\%, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\ 25\%, \text{CaO}\ 15\%, \text{FeO}\ 10\%$).
  - Martian regolith: iron-oxide-rich clayey material ($\text{SiO}_2\ 44\%, \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\ 18\%, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\ 10\%, \text{SO}_3\ 6\%$).
  - Binders: molten sulfur ($130\sim 140\ ^\circ\text{C}$ melt bonding), geopolymer, high-temperature sintering ($1100\sim 1250\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Construction process and structural performance evaluation (Sections 4~6 & Figs. 5~8)**:
  - 3D printing extrudability and nozzle layer-interface bond strength.
  - Residual strength and thermal expansion behavior under vacuum and thermal cycling.

## Results

### 1. Comparison table of bonding mechanism, water consumption, compressive strength, energy demand, and space-environment suitability by major extraterrestrial construction material technology (Section 4 & Tables 2~4 & Figs. 9~13)

### 2. Radiation shielding and 3D additively constructed structure analysis
- **Shielding performance of sintered blocks**: demonstrated that a $1.0\ \text{m}$ thick sintered-regolith structure attenuates solar proton event (SPE) radiation by more than $99\%$.
- **Continuous 3D printing autonomous construction**: established a construction process that completes domes and landing pads unmanned within 10 days using gantry or robotic-arm-based 3D printers without human intervention.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review comprehensively consolidates ISRU waterless construction materials and 3D-printing autonomous construction technologies for lunar and martian environments, establishing the core theoretical and technical guidance for humanity's planetary habitation infrastructure — a top-level landmark original review in space civil materials engineering.
