# Impact Resistance Performance of Strain-Hardening Cementitious Composites

## Summary

To maximize protective capabilities for blast-, bullet-, seismic-, and impact-resistant defense structures, Prof. Dong Joo Kim, Prof. Sung Wook Kim, Prof. Young Soo Yoon, and Prof. Chul Woo Park summarized the fracture mechanics responses, dynamic strain rate effects, penetration depth, frontal cratering, and rear scabbing mitigation mechanisms of strain-hardening cementitious composites (SHCC and UHPFRC) incorporating high-strength steel fibers, PVA fibers, and ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers under medium- and high-velocity impact loadings (drop-weight impact and high-velocity projectile collision). The paper highlights that the ductile multiple microcracking network of SHCC absorbs stress-wave energy, completely preventing (100 %) rear fragment scabbing compared to brittle normal concrete.

## Key Contributions

- **Systematization of 3 primary energy dissipation mechanisms in SHCC under impact**: (1) crack-tip plastic process zone expansion, (2) frictional fiber pullout and viscous shear resistance, and (3) wide-area stress-wave dispersion through multiple microcracking.
- **Evaluation of projectile impact resistance ($v = 300 \sim 1000\ \text{m/s}$, $v = 300 \sim 1000 m/s$)**: Demonstrated a 60 % reduction in penetration depth and over 50 % reduction in critical scabbing thickness relative to plain concrete.
- **Quantification of fracture energy via drop-weight impact testing**: Showed a > 15-fold increase in blows to ultimate failure compared to ACI 544 standard thresholds.
- **Design recommendations for defense infrastructure and nuclear facilities**: Proposed protective strategies combining SHCC overlays with precast defense panels.

## Methods

- Mixture comparisons: Normal concrete (NC, 35 MPa), fiber-reinforced concrete (FRC, 0.5 vol % steel fibers), PVA-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0\%$, $f_c = 45\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 45 MPa$, $\epsilon_u = 3.5\%$), and UHP-SHCC (PE fibers $V_f = 1.5\%$, steel fibers 1.0 %, $f_c = 130\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 130 MPa$).
- Impact testing protocols:
  - Medium-velocity drop-weight tests: 4.5 kg steel sphere dropped from $0.5\ \text{m}$ ($0.5 m$) to record initial cracking and final failure blows ($N_1, N_2$).
  - High-velocity projectile tests: 7.62 mm caliber rifle projectile impacts ($v = 850\ \text{m/s}$, $v = 850 m/s$, panel thickness 50, 100 mm).

- Impact test results and damage mode comparison across materials:
