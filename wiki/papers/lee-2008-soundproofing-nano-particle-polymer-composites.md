# Soundproofing Effect of Nano Particle Reinforced Polymer Composites

## Summary

To simultaneously achieve weight reduction and enhanced sound transmission loss (STL) in automotive and architectural acoustic panels, Prof. Yeon June Kang's team at Seoul National University investigated nanocomposite panels. Organoclay (Cloisite 20A) and carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) were dispersed (1 ~ 5 wt %) into polypropylene (PP) and epoxy polymer matrices. The authors measured sound transmission loss, viscoelastic loss factor ($\tan\delta$), and tensile mechanical properties using a 4-microphone acoustic impedance tube setup. Due to the high specific surface area of nanoparticles and interfacial frictional nano-damping mechanisms, the nanocomposites improved sound transmission loss by up to **7.2 dB** in the audible frequency range of $500 \sim 2000\ \text{Hz}$ ($500 \sim 2000 Hz$) without increasing panel mass (Journal of Mechanical Science and Technology, 2008).

## Key Contributions

- **Identification of non-mass-law acoustic insulation in nanocomposites**: Demonstrated interfacial micro-friction damping (loss factor $\eta$) between polymer matrices and organoclay exceeding classical mass law predictions.
- **Precision acoustic impedance tube STL measurement system**: Characterized broadband transmission loss across $100 \sim 6400\ \text{Hz}$ ($100 \sim 6400 Hz$) using a 4-microphone impedance tube.
- **Identification of optimal organoclay dosage (3 wt %)**: A 3 wt % addition delivered a 5.2 dB ($5.2 dB$) increase in STL along with a 28 % improvement in tensile elastic modulus.
- **Ultra-lightweight high-damping composite panel design**: Established technological viability for automotive interiors and architectural floor impact sound isolation.

## Methods

- Matrix: Polypropylene (PP) and bisphenol-A epoxy resin.
- Nano-fillers: Organically modified montmorillonite organoclay (1, 3, 5 wt %) and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNT 0.5, 1.0 wt %).
- Testing equipment: ASTM E2611 4-microphone acoustic impedance tube, dynamic mechanical analyzer (DMA), universal testing machine (UTM) for tensile properties.
