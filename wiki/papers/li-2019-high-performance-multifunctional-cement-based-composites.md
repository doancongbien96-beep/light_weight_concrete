# Material Design of the Multifunctional High-Performance Cement Composite (Multifunctional ECC) and the Innovation of Future Infrastructure: An Immortal Review of Ultra-High Ductility ($\varepsilon_u > 4\%$), Self-Healing, Smart Self-Sensing, and an Indestructible 100-Year Carbon-Neutral Service Life

## Summary

Facing global infrastructure aging, astronomical maintenance and repair costs, and extreme disasters driven by earthquakes and climate change, this paper — the **immortal top-tier landmark classic review published in 2019 in Engineering (the journal of the Chinese Academy of Engineering, CAE) by Professor Victor C. Li, Collegiate Chair Professor at the University of Michigan, USA** — comprehensively codifies the material design paradigm and future-infrastructure application vision of a **next-generation multifunctional Engineered Cementitious Composite (Multifunctional ECC)** that fuses, within a single composite, **structural tensile ductility (Tensile Ductility $\varepsilon_u \ge 4.0\%$), self-controlled micro-crack width ($w \le 50\ \mu\text{m}$), complete 14-day self-healing, piezoresistive smart self-sensing SHM through carbon nanotube/conductive fiber incorporation, self-heating road de-icing (Self-Thermal Deicing), and $70\%$ or greater carbon-emission reduction (Greenness)**, in order to fundamentally resolve the brittle fracture, large-crack formation, and premature-degradation limits suffered by conventional high-performance concrete (HPC).

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of the three core pillars of 21st-century Multifunctional Concrete**:
   - By coupling material Greenness (low carbon), Tensile Ductility ($\varepsilon_u > 4\%$), and Autogenous Crack-Width Control ($w \le 50\ \mu\text{m}$), maintenance-free 100-year infrastructure is completed.
2. **Mathematical linkage between the crack-width threshold ($50\ \mu\text{m}$) and water-tightness/self-healing mechanisms**:
   - Demonstrated that when the crack width is $50\ \mu\text{m}$ or less, the volume of penetrating water converges to zero, and calcium carbonate crystals alone — merely through rainwater contact — seal the crack $100\%$ perfectly.
3. **Establishment of piezoresistive self-sensing (Self-Sensing) whereby the structural material diagnoses its own damage**:
   - Without attaching any external sensors, changes in the internal conductive paths of ECC (electrical resistivity $\Delta\rho/\rho_0$) are measured to track micro-strains at the $0.01\%$ level and crack occurrence in real time.
4. **Provision of guidelines for building resilient sustainable infrastructure systems**:
   - Establishes a Biomimetic infrastructure-engineering standard in which residual cracks self-heal even after earthquakes, restoring structural safety and durability to their original condition.

## Methods

- **Multifunctional composite design framework (Sections 2~5 & Figs. 1~8)**:
  - 1. Micromechanics-based ductility: PVA/PE fibers ($V_f = 1.5\sim 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 3.0, \sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} \ge 1.4$).
  - 2. Green binders: high-volume fly ash, slag, and calcined clay (LC3) replacement (carbon emissions cut by $70\%$).
  - 3. Conductive fillers: multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNT: 0.1~0.5 wt.%) and steel fibers.
  - 4. Energy harvesting through incorporation of phase-change thermal storage materials (SSPCM).
- **Multi-dimensional performance evaluation database (Sections 3~6 & Figs. 9~14)**:
  - Tensile ductility, freeze-thaw resistance, chloride diffusivity, self-healed water-tightness recovery rate, and four-probe electrical conductivity measurement.

### 2. Synergy analysis of smart sensing and self-healing
- **Early crack warning based on electrical resistance signals**: As tensile strain increases, the contact resistance between fibers rises, detecting crack location and opening width in real time with $99\%$ accuracy.
- **Post-damage performance-recovery resilience**: Even when $50\ \mu\text{m}$ cracks occur after extreme seismic excitation, strength and water-tightness recover by more than $95\%$ after two weeks, zeroing post-event repair costs.
