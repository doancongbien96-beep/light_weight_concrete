## Summary

A long-term natural exposure study that precisely analyzed the cover cracking distribution, rebar pitting-corrosion loss rate, degradation of rebar-concrete bond strength, and ultimate flexural-shear failure behavior of a reinforced concrete (RC) beam that corroded naturally for 14 years under sustained service loading in a real marine chloride environment. Unlike artificial accelerated-corrosion testing, gradual pore penetration of corrosion products and localized pitting corrosion dominate under real natural corrosion conditions; the study established that even when rebar section loss remains below 5 %, loss of bond can increase serviceability deflection by more than twofold and can trigger a transition to brittle shear failure.

## Key Contributions

- **Construction of a 14-year long-term natural chloride-corrosion measurement database**: precise derivation of combined corrosion profiles in which actual service-load cracking and chloride ingress interact.
- **Quantification of the localized pitting corrosion coefficient ($R = x_{max}/x_{mean}$)**: confirmed that the pitting factor reaches $4.0 \sim 8.0$ under natural corrosion environments, inducing early brittle fracture of the rebar.
- **Derivation of a nonlinear correlation between corrosion-induced cover crack width and rebar cross-sectional loss**: verification of the relation $w_{cr} = 0.057 (\Delta A_s - \Delta A_{s0})$.

## Methods

- **Specimen**: full-scale RC beam ($150 \times 280 \times 3000 mm$) stored for 14 years under $3 \% NaCl$ mist spray and sustained flexural loading.
- **Measurements**: 3D optical scanning crack-width mapping, four-point flexural failure testing, and chemical quantification of rebar mass loss and pit depth after concrete break-out.

## Results

- Under natural corrosion environments, the rebar section-loss threshold for cover-crack initiation is merely $\Delta A_{s0} \approx 15 \sim 25 \mu m$.
- Bond strength decreased by up to 60 % compared with sound regions, inducing premature crushing of the concrete compression zone before rebar yielding.
