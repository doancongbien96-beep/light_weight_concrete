# Building-envelope-integrated phase-change material (PCM) latent heat thermal energy storage technology: wall/slab incorporation schemes, energy peak load reduction of $40\%$, and a comprehensive review of indoor thermal comfort mechanisms

## Summary

To dramatically reduce buildings' heating and cooling energy consumption and curb greenhouse-gas emissions, this review consolidates **the principles, material classification, impregnation/encapsulation techniques, and building energy-reduction performance of latent heat thermal energy storage (LHTES: Latent Heat Thermal Energy Storage) technology that incorporates phase-change materials (PCM: organic paraffins, fatty acids, inorganic salt hydrates, eutectic mixtures) into building envelopes (walls, floor slabs, ceiling gypsum boards, Trombe walls)** and presents **thermodynamic design guidelines that stabilize indoor temperature fluctuation bands to within $3\sim 5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and shift electrical peak loads by up to $40\%$ (Peak Load Shifting) by applying direct immersion (Direct Immersion), microencapsulation (Microencapsulation), and shape-stabilized (Shape-stabilized PCM) technologies** — a Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews 2007 highest-authority landmark review by **Dr. V. V. Tyagi and Prof. D. Buddhi of Devi Ahilya University, India**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Complete establishment of a classification system and thermophysical database for building PCMs**:
   - Systematically classified the phase-change temperature bands ($18\sim 28\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and melting latent heats ($120\sim 200\ \text{J/g}$) of the organic family (paraffins, non-paraffin fatty acids), the inorganic family (salt hydrates), and eutectics (Eutectics).
2. **Establishment of the four major incorporation methods (Incorporation Methods) of PCMs into building materials**:
   - Specifies the merits and drawbacks and the leakage-prevention technologies of (1) direct incorporation (Direct Incorporation), (2) immersion impregnation into porous aggregates (Immersion), (3) polymer-shell microencapsulation, and (4) shape-stabilized composites (Shape-stabilized).
3. **Demonstration of improved indoor thermal comfort and 40 % peak-load reduction**:
   - Shortens air-conditioner operating time by $30\sim 50\%$ by absorbing daytime solar heat and internal heat gains as PCM latent heat and releasing them during nighttime natural ventilation.
4. **Presentation of long-term thermal-cycling stability and supercooling control technologies**:
   - Derives nucleating-agent (Nucleating Agents) addition to overcome the phase-segregation and supercooling defects of inorganic salt hydrates, together with fire-safety flame-retardant measures for organic PCMs.

## Methods

- **Scope of analyzed literature and technical domains (Sections 2~6 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Comprehensive synthesis of some 80 core latent heat storage / building energy primary sources worldwide.
  - Building application sites covered:
    1. PCM gypsum wallboards (PCM Wallboards).
    2. PCM concrete floor slabs and walls (PCM Concrete Blocks).
    3. Trombe walls and solar collector walls (Trombe Walls).
    4. Underfloor heating systems (Underfloor Heating).
- **Performance evaluation parameters**:
  - Phase-change temperature ($T_m$), melting latent heat ($\Delta H$), indoor peak-temperature reduction ($\Delta T$), electricity savings rate.

### 2. Interpretation of heat-flux damping and delay mechanisms under building-envelope application
- **Thermal-wave time lag (Time Lag) and amplitude damping (Decremental Factor)**: owing to the endothermic phase change of the PCM, the abrupt exterior solar-driven temperature rise of a wall reaches the interior side 4~6 hours late, synchronizing with the nighttime cooling window.
- **Heating/cooling peak-power shifting**: transferring cooling loads in the maximum power-demand window to late-night off-peak hours cuts building energy operating costs by up to $40\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the highest-authority landmark review in which Dr. V. V. Tyagi and Prof. D. Buddhi systematized the materials physics, incorporation methods, and energy-saving performance of PCM latent heat thermal storage technology for building envelopes, establishing textbook-grade guidance for the design of eco-friendly zero-energy buildings.
