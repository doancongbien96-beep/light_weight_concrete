# Strain Rate Dependent Tensile Behavior of Engineered Cementitious Composites

## Summary

To evaluate the direct tensile behavior of PVA-ECC and PE-ECC under dynamic actions such as earthquakes and impact events, this study conducted direct tension tests across a wide range of strain rates, from quasi-static ($\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4} s^{-1}$) to seismic ($10^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $10^{-2} s^{-1}$) and impact rates ($10^{0}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $10^{0} s^{-1}$). Evolution of first cracking strength, ultimate tensile strength, tensile strain capacity, crack count, and crack width were characterized using a high-speed hydraulic servo testing system. Hydrophilic hydrogen bonding in PVA fibers causes chemical debonding fracture toughness to rise sharply at high strain rates, triggering fiber rupture and reducing tensile strain capacity; conversely, hydrophobic PE fibers maintain high strain capacity even under impact loading rates.

## Key Contributions

- **Formulation of dynamic tensile DIF models**: Quantified that tensile strength increases by 1.4 to 1.8-fold ($DIF = 1.4 \sim 1.8$) across a 4-order-of-magnitude increase in strain rate.
- **Mechanistic rate sensitivity differentiation between PVA and PE fibers**:
  - PVA fibers: Rate-dependent increase in chemical debonding energy ($G_d$) promotes fiber rupture at high rates, reducing tensile strain capacity from 4.5 % to 2.2 % ($4.5 \%$ to $2.2 \%$).
  - PE fibers: Governed by frictional slip ($\tau_0$), sustaining high strain capacities of $3.5 \sim 4.0\%$ without fiber rupture even at impact velocities.
- **Observation of dynamic crack branching**: Dynamic crack branching narrowed crack spacings and increased the number of multiple microcracks by over 1.5-fold at higher strain rates.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions**:
  - PVA-ECC: $W/B = 0.25$, cement + fly ash (FA/C = 2.2), PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\%$, REC15).
  - PE-ECC: $W/B = 0.22$, high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 2800 MPa).
- **Testing apparatus**: Fast hydraulic servo testing system with non-contact optical displacement tracking.
- **Strain rate regimes**: $10^{-4}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ ($10^{-4} s^{-1}$, static), $10^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ ($10^{-2} s^{-1}$, seismic), $1.0\ \text{s}^{-1}$ ($1.0 s^{-1}$, impact).

## Results

- **PVA-ECC tensile performance**:
  - Quasi-static ($10^{-4}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $10^{-4} s^{-1}$): Tensile strength 4.8 MPa ($4.8 MPa$), strain capacity 4.6 % ($4.6\%$).
  - High speed ($1.0\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $1.0 s^{-1}$): Tensile strength 7.8 MPa ($7.8 MPa$, $DIF = 1.63$), strain capacity 2.4 % ($2.4\%$).
- **PE-ECC tensile performance**:
  - Quasi-static ($10^{-4}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $10^{-4} s^{-1}$): Tensile strength 6.2 MPa ($6.2 MPa$), strain capacity 5.2 % ($5.2\%$).
  - High speed ($1.0\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $1.0 s^{-1}$): Tensile strength 10.5 MPa ($10.5 MPa$, $DIF = 1.69$), strain capacity 4.8 % ($4.8\%$).
