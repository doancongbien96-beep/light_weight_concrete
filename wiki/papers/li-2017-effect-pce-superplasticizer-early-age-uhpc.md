# Effect of polycarboxylate superplasticizer (PCE) molecular structure (backbone · side-chain density) on the early-age rheological behavior and hydration reaction of UHPC: zeta potential, steric hindrance, and the mechanism underlying the development of compressive strength $160\ \text{MPa}$

## Summary

To optimize the workability and hydration kinetics of ultra-high performance concrete (UHPC), which suffers extreme viscous resistance and early-age flow loss due to its ultra-low water-to-binder ratio ($W/B \le 0.18$) and its massive nano/micro powder content, **four PCE superplasticizers (SP1~SP4: molecular weight $15,000\sim 45,000\ \text{g/mol}$) systematically differing in backbone charge density (carboxyl density) and side-chain (PEO side chain) length and density were designed**, and the zeta potential of cement–silica fume suspensions, TOC-based solid-phase adsorption amount, isothermal calorimetry hydration heat-release rate, time-dependent slump-flow retention, and 28-day compressive strength were precisely evaluated. This demonstrated **that applying the short-backbone, high-side-chain-density PCE (SP3), with its outstanding steric-hindrance repulsive force, achieves an initial slump flow of $780\ \text{mm}$ and loss-free retention over 120 minutes at a dosage of only $1.2\ \text{wt.}\%$, while minimizing hydration delay and securing a 28-day compressive strength of $162.5\ \text{MPa}$** — the highest-authority landmark original paper (Construction and Building Materials, 2017) by the research team of Dr. P. P. Li, Prof. Q. L. Yu (corresponding author), and Prof. H. J. H. Brouwers of Eindhoven University of Technology (TU Eindhoven), the Netherlands.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the molecular mechanics mechanism linking PCE molecular structure to dispersion efficiency in ultra-low-water-ratio UHPC**:
   - Proved that spatial steric hindrance from polymer side chains, rather than electrostatic repulsion (zeta potential $-15\sim -25\ \text{mV}$), is the key driving force behind the dispersion of UHPC ultrafine particles.
2. **Complete control of the 120-minute early-age slump-flow evolution of UHPC**:
   - Suppressed the rapid early-age consumption of superplasticizer and the rise in viscosity using a high-density-side-chain PCE, preserving high fluidity above $750\ \text{mm}$ for 2 hours.
3. **Establishment of the balance between the PCE-induced hydration induction-period delay and final compressive strength development**:
   - Achieved a 28-day strength of $160\ \text{MPa}$ while controlling alite ($\text{C}_3\text{S}$) hydration delay to within $4\sim 6$ hours and inducing dense particle packing.
4. **Completion of molecular design guidelines for next-generation smart concrete admixtures**:
   - Proposed a tailored PCE molecular structure standard optimized for future eco-friendly low-carbon UHPC with high substitution levels of silica fume and limestone powder.

## Methods

- **PCE superplasticizer synthesis and UHPC mixing (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Four PCEs: SP1 (long backbone, low side-chain density), SP2 (intermediate), SP3 (short backbone, high side-chain density, molecular weight 22,000 g/mol), SP4 (ultra-high molecular weight).
  - UHPC mix: Type 1 cement + micro silica fume + ground quartz sand powder ($W/B = 0.17$, PCE dosage 0.8~2.0 wt.%).
- **Physicochemical and rheological analysis (Sections 2.3~2.5 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Zeta potential: Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS (cement–silica fume suspension).
  - Adsorption analysis: Shimadzu TOC-L total organic carbon analyzer (residual PCE measured in the centrifuged supernatant).
  - Hydration heat measurement: TAM Air isothermal conduction calorimeter ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$, continuous heat release over 7 days).
  - Fluidity: Hägermann cone slump flow without compaction (0, 30, 60, 90, 120 min).

### 2. Steric hindrance and microstructure densification interpretation
- **Brush layer formation by PEO side chains**: the dense PEO side chains of SP3 trap water molecules and form a thick organic protective layer of $5\sim 10\ \text{nm}$, physically separating particle-to-particle contact at its origin.
- **Porosity minimization**: uniform particle packing is maintained without early water evaporation, reducing the capillary porosity after curing to below $2.8\%$ and achieving a compressive strength of $162.5\ \text{MPa}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a top-level landmark original paper in the field of chemical admixture engineering for high-performance concrete, in which the TU Eindhoven team elucidated the effects of the molecular structure of PCE superplasticizers on the rheology, zeta potential, adsorption, and hydration kinetics of ultra-low-water-ratio UHPC, demonstrating 120-minute loss-free slump retention and a compressive strength of 162.5 MPa.
