## Summary
A study in which Professor K.Q. Yu's research team at Tongji University and Professor Christopher Leung's team at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology clarified the explosive spalling mechanism, residual compressive strength, elastic modulus, envelope stress-strain curves, and energy absorption behavior of ductile strain-hardening composites (PVA-SHCC and PE-SHCC) exposed to fire and high-temperature heating environments ($200 \sim 800 °C$), through high-temperature electric furnace heating experiments and SEM microstructure analysis. It was demonstrated that the melting-vaporization action of PVA fibers at $230 °C$ forms micro-depressurization channels that vent internal steam pressure in the matrix, blocking spalling 100 % completely.

## Key Contributions
- **Anti-spalling mechanism of SHCC under high-temperature exposure quantified**: PVA fibers melt at $230 °C$ forming continuous capillary venting pathways.
- **Residual compressive strength-elastic modulus decay model established by temperature ($200 \sim 800 °C$)**: strength retention of 65 % at $400 °C$, with shape maintained even at $800 °C$.
- **Fire resistance of PVA versus PE fiber SHCC compared**: PVA fibers outperform PE fibers in steam devolatilization performance under high-temperature fire resistance.
- **Standard formula presented for evaluating post-fire residual capacity of tunnels and buildings**: a Eurocode 4 standard adoption model established.

## Methods
- Mixes: PVA-SHCC (REC15, $V_f = 2.0 \%$, $f_c = 48.5 MPa$), PE-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $f_c = 52.0 MPa$), $W/B = 0.40$.
- Heating conditions: electric furnace ramping at $10 °C/min$ to target temperatures of 200, 400, 600, and 800 °C, held 2 hours then naturally cooled at room temperature.
- Tests: uniaxial compression tests of $100 \times 200 mm$ cylinders (displacement control), SEM and thermogravimetric TGA analysis.

| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
