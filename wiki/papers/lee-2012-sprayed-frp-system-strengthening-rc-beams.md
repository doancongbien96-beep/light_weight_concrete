# Flexural Strengthening Behavior of Deteriorated Reinforced Concrete (RC) Beams Using a Sprayed-FRP System (Chopped Carbon Fiber + Resin): 5× Faster Construction, $72\%$ Amplification of Flexural Capacity, and the Interfacial Debonding Suppression Mechanism

## Summary

To fundamentally resolve the problems suffered by conventional continuous-fiber FRP sheet and plate bonding methods — labor-intensive manual layer-by-layer layup, premature interfacial debonding caused by entrapped air voids, and the difficulty of applying them on curved surfaces and corners — this study **developed a Sprayed-FRP strengthening technique that simultaneously sprays chopped carbon/glass fibers cut into short lengths (Chopped Carbon/Glass Fiber: $L_f = 12, 24, 36\ \text{mm}$) together with a low-viscosity vinyl ester / epoxy resin through a special chop-spray gun in ambient air, forming a dense three-dimensional random orientation-free FRP layer on the concrete surface**, and carried out tensile/flexural material property characterization, four-point flexural loading tests on full-scale RC beams ($2000 \times 150 \times 250\ \text{mm}$), and interfacial bond–slip analysis, **demonstrating a dramatic five-fold improvement in construction speed over conventional unidirectional FRP sheets, a 72% amplification of the flexural ultimate capacity of RC beams ($92.5\ \text{kN} \rightarrow 158.0\ \text{kN}$), strengthening efficiency perfectly equivalent to a single ply of unidirectional carbon fiber sheet at a fiber length of $36\ \text{mm}$ and a thickness of $3\sim 4\ \text{mm}$, and integral bonding integrity over curved surfaces** — the landmark origin paper published in the Journal of Advanced Concrete Technology (JACT) in 2012 by the research team of Prof. Kang Seok Lee and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author) of Chonnam National University.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of the world's first commercialized sprayed-FRP system for concrete structural strengthening**:
   - Continuously feeds carbon/glass fiber rovings, cuts them automatically inside the nozzle, and sprays them together with the resin, so that a $5\ \text{mm}$ thick strengthening layer is formed within minutes without formwork or pre-impregnation.
2. **Identification of the optimal short-fiber length ($L_f = 36\ \text{mm}$) and the fiber-resin volumetric ratio**:
   - As the fiber length increases, the three-dimensional bridging effect is maximized, stably securing a tensile strength of $285.0\ \text{MPa}$ and an elastic modulus of $24.5\ \text{GPa}$ for Sprayed-CFRP.
3. **Demonstration of 72% amplification of the flexural ultimate capacity of RC beams and ductile failure behavior**:
   - The ultimate load rises by a factor of $1.72$ relative to the unstrengthened RC beam, and even after yielding of the main reinforcing bars the Sprayed-FRP layer continues to carry tensile stress to the end, exhibiting high fracture-energy absorption.
4. **Dramatic improvement in interfacial debonding resistance compared with FRP sheets**:
   - Because of the orientation-free three-dimensional fiber array, the stress concentration immediately above concrete cracks disperses multiaxially, preventing the abrupt interfacial loss-off that is the fatal defect of the sheet bonding method.

## Methods

- **Materials and spray equipment (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Strengthening fibers: Torayca T700 carbon roving and E-glass roving (cut lengths $L_f = 12, 24, 36\ \text{mm}$).
  - Binder resin: vinyl ester resin + MEKPO curing agent (room-temperature curing, viscosity $0.25\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$).
  - Spray equipment: pneumatic piston pump + chop-spray gun (discharge pressure $15\ \text{MPa}$, spraying distance $0.8\sim 1.2\ \text{m}$).
- **Mechanical test program (Sections 2.3~3.5 & Figs. 6~10)**:
  - Direct tension and flexural tests: measurement of the tensile strength and flexural strength of sprayed flat-plate specimens.
  - RC beam flexural tests: $2000 \times 150 \times 250\ \text{mm}$ (constant-moment zone 600 mm, four-point flexural loading, displacement control at 1.0 mm/min).
  - Specimen series: `Control` (unstrengthened), `Sheet-1Layer` (one ply of CFRP sheet), `Spray-C12` (carbon, 12 mm), `Spray-C24`, `Spray-C36` (carbon, 36 mm, thickness 3.5 mm), `Spray-Glass` (glass, 36 mm, thickness 5 mm).

### 2. Three-dimensional random bridging mechanism and formulation of the flexural design analysis
- **Debonding prevention through three-dimensional fiber orientation**: unlike unidirectional sheets, the randomly intersecting $36\ \text{mm}$ carbon fibers arrayed within the spray layer stitch the interfacial shear cracks of the concrete in three dimensions, dispersing the debonding stress concentration at its source.
- **Completion of a practical design basis**: by introducing a converted equivalent carbon cross-sectional area ($A_{frp} = \eta \cdot V_f \cdot b \cdot t$) and an efficiency factor ($\eta = 0.375$), a flexural capacity prediction formula fully consistent with the ACI 440.2R provisions was completed.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the top-tier landmark origin study in structural smart repair/strengthening and advanced composite materials engineering, in which the research team of Prof. Kang Seok Lee and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University developed the sprayed-FRP system and demonstrated a five-fold improvement in construction speed while amplifying the flexural capacity of RC beams by 72% and suppressing premature interfacial debonding.
