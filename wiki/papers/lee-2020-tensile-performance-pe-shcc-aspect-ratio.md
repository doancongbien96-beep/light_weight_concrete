## Summary
A study that systematically investigated the effects of the aspect ratio ($L_f/d_f = 500, 750, 1000, 1250$) and fiber length (6 mm, 9 mm, 12 mm, 15 mm) of high-strength ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) fibers on the uniaxial direct tensile behavior, crack-bridging complementary energy, and tensile strain capacity of a highly ductile composite (PE-SHCC). It revealed that as the aspect ratio increases, the complementary bridging energy rises and the density of multiple microcrack formation increases, but once the aspect ratio exceeds 1000, the effective dispersion deteriorates due to fiber balling (Ball effect) during mixing. At the optimal aspect ratio ($L_f/d_f = 1000$, length 12 mm), an ultimate tensile strength of 8.5 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 7.2 % were achieved.

## Key Contributions
- **Mapping of the tensile strain-hardening envelope according to PE fiber aspect ratio**: securing the optimal PSH performance index in the range of $L_f/d_f = 750 \sim 1000$.
- **Quantification of dispersion coefficient and bridging efficiency by fiber length**: derivation of a correlation between the dispersion coefficient ($\beta_{disp}$) and tensile strain capacity through optical image analysis.
- **Control of multiple microcracks**: formation of an average crack width of $25 \mu m$ and 60 or more cracks in the optimal mix.

## Methods
- **Material mix**: cement + fly ash (55 wt %), $W/B = 0.22$, UHMWPE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, strength 3000 MPa, length 6, 9, 12, 15 mm).
- **Test items**: uniaxial dogbone direct tensile test (displacement control $0.5 mm/min$), fluorescence-image fiber dispersion analysis, 28 d compressive strength.

## Results
- Length 12 mm ($L_f/d_f = 1000$): tensile strength $8.52 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $7.20 \%$, 28 d compressive strength $62.5 MPa$.
- Length 6 mm ($L_f/d_f = 500$): tensile strain capacity $3.80 \%$ (reduced ductility due to early pull-out).
- Length 15 mm ($L_f/d_f = 1250$): tensile strain capacity $5.10 \%$ due to fiber balling.
