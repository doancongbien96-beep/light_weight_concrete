## Summary
A full-scale structural experiment applying a high-performance fiber-reinforced cementitious composite (HPFRCC / Slurry Infiltrated Mat Concrete) jacketing technique for the seismic strengthening and repair of reinforced concrete (RC) beam-column joints with deficient shear reinforcement or earthquake damage, and of flexure–shear-vulnerable beam members. A high-toughness HPFRCC layer (thickness 25 ~ 40 mm) was cast around the joints, which were then subjected to cyclic lateral loading, elucidating the behavior whereby the shear failure mode transitions into a ductile flexural-hinge mode. The HPFRCC retrofit was demonstrated to increase joint shear capacity by up to 65 % and to enhance energy dissipation capacity 2.5-fold.

## Key Contributions
- **Transition of shear-deficient RC joints to a ductile flexural failure mode**: blocked the onset of brittle shear cracks and induced stable energy dissipation at plastic hinges in the beam end regions.
- **Suppression of pinching in cyclic hysteretic behavior**: high-performance fiber bridging restrained shear crack opening, greatly enlarging the stiffness and energy-absorption area of the hysteresis loops.
- **Establishment of a simplified cast-in-place jacketing retrofit method**: verified a technique that restores seismic performance simply by chipping the surface cover and casting HPFRCC, without complex internal shear reinforcement detailing.

## Methods
- **Materials**: HPFRCC matrix ($W/B = 0.32$, natural pozzolan + silica fume), high-strength steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 0.16 mm, length 6 mm, strength 2000 MPa).
- **Specimens**: 3/4-scale RC interior beam-column joints (shear vulnerability induced by excessively wide spacing of the shear reinforcement).
- **Loading test**: quasi-static cyclic lateral loading test (interstory drift angle $\theta = 0.5 \% \sim 5.0 \%$).

## Results
- Unretrofitted control specimen: at an interstory drift angle of $1.5 \%$, X-shaped diagonal shear cracking developed in the joint panel zone, followed by rapid capacity degradation and brittle failure.
- HPFRCC-retrofitted specimen: stable hysteretic behavior maintained without capacity degradation up to an interstory drift angle of $4.0 \%$, with the ultimate shear load increased by 62 %.
