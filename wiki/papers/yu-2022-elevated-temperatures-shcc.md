## Summary
This study comparatively evaluated changes in residual compressive strength, elastic modulus, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, and crack patterns of ductile strain-hardening cementitious composites (PVA-SHCC and PE-SHCC) after exposure to elevated temperatures ($100 \sim 600 °C$). High-temperature microstructural changes arising from the melting temperature difference between hydrophilic PVA fibers ($T_m \approx 230 °C$) and hydrophobic PE fibers ($T_m \approx 150 °C$) were analyzed. It was found that upon exposure to $200 °C$ the PE fibers melted early and ductility dropped sharply, whereas the PVA fibers maintained tensile strain-hardening ($\epsilon_u > 3.0 \%$) even at $200 °C$, establishing design guidelines for elevated-temperature composite materials by application.

## Key Contributions
- **Comparative mapping of elevated-temperature mechanical degradation paths by fiber type (PVA vs PE)**: identification of tensile behavior transition points governed by fiber melting point differences.
- **Analysis of single-fiber pull-out interfacial changes after elevated-temperature exposure**: confirmation that micro hollow channels formed after fiber melting release vapor pressure and prevent spalling.
- **Establishment of a residual compressive strength prediction model at high temperatures**: derivation of the temperature-dependent reduction coefficient curve of $f_{c,T}/f_{c,0}$.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: PVA-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$) and PE-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash 50 wt %).
- **Heating test**: exposure in an electric furnace to $100, 200, 300, 400, 600 °C$, followed by slow cooling to room temperature.
- **Test items**: uniaxial dog-bone direct tensile test, compressive strength, dynamic elastic modulus, and TG-DTA thermal analysis.

## Results
- PVA-SHCC: tensile strength $4.5 MPa$ and strain capacity $3.2 \%$ maintained after exposure to $200 °C$.
- PE-SHCC: melting of PE fibers at $200 °C$ reduced tensile strength to $2.8 MPa$ with failure transitioning to softening (however, compressive strength was maintained at $55 MPa$).
- Both mixes achieved zero spalling up to $600 °C$.
