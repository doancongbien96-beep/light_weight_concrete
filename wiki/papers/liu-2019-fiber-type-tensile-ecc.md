## Summary

A study that systematically compared, under identical matrix conditions, the effect of fiber type (PVA, high-strength PE, steel fiber, and hybrid combinations) on the direct tensile performance of engineered cementitious composites (ECC). The interfacial bond characteristics, pullout mechanisms, and fiber bridging stress curves of hydrophilic PVA fiber, hydrophobic high-strength PE fiber, and high-modulus steel fiber were analyzed from a micromechanics perspective, and the tensile stress-strain responses and crack patterns were evaluated. It was established that PE fiber delivers the highest ductility ($\epsilon_u > 7.0 \%$) and high tensile strength, PVA fiber provides economy and fine crack control ($w < 40 \mu m$), and the PVA-steel hybrid offers synergistic improvement of first-crack strength and tensile strength.

## Key Contributions

- **Construction of a mechanical-behavior comparison matrix by fiber type**:
  - PE-ECC: highest tensile strength ($8.5 MPa$) and highest ductility ($7.2 \%$).
  - PVA-ECC: excellent crack distribution (mean crack width $35 \mu m$, strain $4.2 \%$).
  - PVA-Steel hybrid: 50 % increase in first-crack strength and improved flexural capacity.
- **Analysis of the correlation between single-fiber pullout characteristics and composite behavior**: demonstrated the influence of the difference between chemical bond ($G_d$) and frictional bond ($\tau_0$) on the tensile failure mode (fiber rupture vs complete pullout).

## Methods

- **Fiber specifications**: PVA (REC15, strength 1600 MPa), UHMWPE (strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa), micro steel fiber (diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm).
- **Test items**: uniaxial direct tension test, single-fiber pullout test, crack count and crack-width measurement with a digital microscope.

## Results

- PE-ECC: tensile strength $8.45 MPa$, strain capacity $7.20 \%$, 62 cracks.
- PVA-ECC: tensile strength $5.12 MPa$, strain capacity $4.25 \%$, 48 cracks.
- Hybrid (PVA 1.5 % + Steel 0.5 %): tensile strength $6.80 MPa$, strain capacity $2.85 \%$, first-crack strength $4.85 MPa$.
