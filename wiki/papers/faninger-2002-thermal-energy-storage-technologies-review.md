# Physical Principles and System Engineering of Thermal Energy Storage (TES): Sensible, Latent (PCM), and Thermochemical Storage, Volumetric Energy Density, and Solar-Building Integration

## Summary

To resolve the spatial and temporal mismatch between intermittent solar/renewable energy supply and building heating/cooling demands, Professor Gerhard Faninger at the University of Klagenfurt synthesized the thermodynamic principles, volumetric energy storage densities ($\text{kWh/m}^3$), system efficiencies, and economic feasibility of three primary thermal energy storage (TES) technologies under the International Energy Agency (IEA) Solar Heating and Cooling Programme: 1) water/rock-bed sensible heat storage, 2) phase change material (PCM) latent heat storage, and 3) reversible thermochemical/sorption storage. The review established thermal engineering design criteria demonstrating that PCM latent storage reduces storage vessel volume to 1/5 of conventional water tanks and achieves high-density thermal storage of 50~100 kWh/m³ across narrow operating temperature differentials of $\Delta T = 10\sim 15\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## Key Contributions

1. **Systematization of principles and volumetric energy densities for three TES technologies**:
   - Compiled a comparative performance database for sensible storage (10~30 kWh/m³), latent PCM storage (50~100 kWh/m³), and thermochemical sorption (150~300 kWh/m³).
2. **Elucidation of space-saving mechanisms in building-integrated PCM latent storage**:
   - Outlined designs where building structural elements act as thermal batteries, increasing effective thermal storage capacity more than 5 times compared to standard concrete walls without requiring separate mechanical storage rooms.
3. **Integration of solar collectors with centralized large-scale seasonal storage**:
   - Established efficiency models for long-term underground thermal energy storage (UTES: aquifer and borehole systems) utilizing surplus summer solar thermal energy for winter district heating.
4. **Establishment of global design guidelines under the IEA Solar Heating and Cooling Programme**:
   - Provided standard engineering benchmarks for solar thermal heating and building energy conservation policies across Europe and worldwide.

## Methods

- **Technical scope and classification (Sections 1–6 & Tables 1–4)**:
  - Synthesis of core TES systems from global literature and IEA Tasks.
  - Covered technology sectors:
    1. Sensible heat storage: Hot water tanks, underground aquifer/borehole thermal storage.
    2. Latent heat storage (PCM): Inorganic salt hydrates, organic paraffins, eutectic mixtures.
    3. Thermochemical storage: Zeolite-water vapor sorption, silica gel adsorption reactions ($AB + \text{Heat} \leftrightarrow A + B$).
- **Evaluation parameters**:
  - Volumetric storage capacity ($Q_v$, $\text{kWh/m}^3$), storage efficiency ($\eta$), operating temperature differential ($\Delta T$), and long-term heat loss rates.

## Results

### 1. Comparison of physical principles, storage densities, operating characteristics, and tradeoffs across TES technologies (Section 2 & Tables 1–3)

| Storage classification | Representative media | Thermodynamic storage principle | Volumetric energy density ($Q_v$, $\text{kWh/m}^3$) | Operating temperature differential ($\Delta T$, $^\circ\text{C}$) | Key advantages | Primary constraints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sensible Heat** | Water, rock beds, gravel | $Q = m C_p \Delta T$ (Temperature rise) | **10 ~ 30 kWh/m³ (Low)** | 30 ~ 60 °C | **Lowest cost, mature technology, non-toxic** | **Large installation volume, continuous heat loss** |
| **Latent Heat (PCM)** | **Paraffin, salt hydrates** | **$Q = m \Delta H_m$ (Phase change latent heat)** | **50 ~ 100 kWh/m³ ($3\sim 5\times$)** | **5 ~ 15 °C (Small $\Delta T$)** | **Isothermal storage, compact, building-integrable** | Low thermal conductivity, encapsulation cost |
| **Thermochemical** | Zeolite-water, metal hydroxides | $A + B \leftrightarrow AB + \Delta H_{rxn}$ | **150 ~ 300 kWh/m³ (Highest)** | 80 ~ 200 °C | **Lossless long-term seasonal storage at ambient temp** | Complex reactor design, high initial capital |

### 2. Isothermal operation and water tank replacement efficiency in PCM latent storage
- **Low-temperature differential operation**: Under low heat source temperatures ($< 35\ ^\circ\text{C}$) from heat pumps or solar collectors, PCMs absorb majority enthalpy within narrow phase-change ranges ($\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$), maximizing system Coefficient of Performance (COP).
- **Distributed thermal buffering in building envelopes**: Embedding PCMs into wallboards and floor slabs attenuates peak indoor temperatures by 3~5 °C without sacrificing usable architectural floor space.

- **Overall conclusion**: This IEA landmark review by Gerhard Faninger established the thermodynamic limits and system integration efficiencies of sensible, latent (PCM), and thermochemical thermal energy storage for solar-integrated zero-energy buildings.
