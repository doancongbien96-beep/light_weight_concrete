## Summary

A study evaluating the surface void distribution and micro-hardness characteristics of concrete according to formwork type (plywood, coated plywood, permeable, and steel formwork), which decisively affect the surface appearance and near-surface durability (resistance to water and chloride penetration) of concrete structures. It quantitatively established that applying a permeable sheet locally lowers the water-binder ratio ($W/B$) through drainage of surface excess water and air bubbles, suppresses the area fraction of bleeding bug holes by more than 90 %, and markedly improves surface rebound hardness and carbonation resistance.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of surface defects (void fraction) by formwork type**: optical image analysis proved that the area fraction of surface bubbles with diameters of 1 ~ 5 mm falls to 0.2 % or less with permeable formwork, a drastic reduction from ordinary plywood (3.8 %).
- **Improved near-surface micro-hardness and watertightness**: the drainage action of the permeable sheet raises the compressive strength (rebound hardness) of the 5 mm near-surface zone by 25 % and cuts the permeability coefficient to about one-third.
- **Suppression of long-term carbonation depth**: formation of a dense surface cover layer was shown to reduce accelerated carbonation depth by more than 40 %.

## Methods

- **Formwork types**: ordinary plywood, Euroform-coated plywood, steel formwork, and permeable nonwoven sheet (Permeable Formliner).
- **Concrete mix**: $W/B = 0.45$, slump $150 mm$, 28 d specified design strength $30 MPa$.
- **Evaluation techniques**: digital-image surface void analysis (binarization), Schmidt hammer rebound, carbonation depth (spraying of a 1 % phenolphthalein solution), and air permeability (Autoclam).

## Results

- Permeable-formwork specimen: surface bubble area fraction $0.15 \%$, 28 d surface rebound hardness $38.5 MPa$, 8-week accelerated carbonation depth $2.1 mm$.
- Ordinary plywood specimen: surface bubble area fraction $3.75 \%$, surface rebound hardness $30.2 MPa$, accelerated carbonation depth $4.8 mm$.
