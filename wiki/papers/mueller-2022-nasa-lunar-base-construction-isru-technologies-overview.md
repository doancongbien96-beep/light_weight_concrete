# NASA Kennedy Space Center (Swamp Works) Artemis lunar base construction technology overview: ISRU earthworks, rocket-plume-proof landing pads, 3D-printed shielding domes, and autonomous robotic construction architecture

## Summary

To build humanity's permanent settlement base (Artemis Base Camp) safely and economically at the lunar south pole in the 2030s under NASA's Artemis program, this top-authority landmark NASA technical report of 2022 by **Dr. Robert P. Mueller — founder of NASA Swamp Works and chief space civil engineer** comprehensively consolidates **the extraterrestrial civil construction systems developed at the NASA Kennedy Space Center (KSC) Swamp Works laboratory (autonomous space construction), local-resource-utilization (ISRU) excavation and ground clearing (Excavation & Regolith Conveyance), high-strength landing/launch pads and blast berms for protection against ultra-hot rocket plumes ($>2000\ \text{K}$) and supersonic regolith ejecta (PSI), extra-large 3D-printed autonomously layered habitat domes (Habitats), and dust mitigation infrastructure technologies**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Systematization of the four core infrastructure technologies for NASA's permanent Artemis lunar base construction**:
   - 1) landing/launch pads (L/L Pads) and blast berms; 2) unpaved/paved roads and traverse routes; 3) radiation-shielded habitat domes (Habitats); 4) protective structures for power generation and ISRU plants.
2. **Establishment of the protection mechanism against rocket exhaust plumes (PSI: Plume Surface Interaction)**:
   - Completed sintered paving materials and an interlocking tile system to prevent the phenomenon whereby the descent thrust jets of large crewed landers (HLS, mass 40~50 t) hurl regolith at thousands of km per hour and destroy the surrounding base.
3. **Demonstration of NASA's bootstrapping lightweight autonomous construction robot architecture**:
   - Presents an exponential construction model in which only an initial 12 t of lightweight robotic equipment is transported from Earth, which then self-expands its equipment fleet and infrastructure out of local materials.
4. **Establishment of civil engineering standards for the Global Exploration Roadmap architecture**:
   - Completed lunar surface construction specification guidelines to be jointly observed by NASA, ESA, JAXA, and commercial space companies (SpaceX, Blue Origin, ICON).

## Methods

- **Swamp Works space construction technology system (Sections 2~5 & Figs. 1~8)**:
  - Excavation robot: RASSOR (Regolith Advanced Surface Systems Operations Robot), a counter-rotating drum excavator (reactive-force cancellation at low gravity, $1/6g$).
  - Binding/forming processes: microwave sintering, solar-concentrator lens melting, polymer-mixed 3D extrusion.
  - Landing pad design: sintered tile paving 50~100 m in diameter + sloped blast berms (Berms) 3~5 m high.
- **Construction scenario and economic evaluation (Sections 6~8 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 9~12)**:
  - Estimation of per-phase equipment mass, power demand, and timeline for uncrewed pre-cursor construction.

## Results

### 1. Comparison table of construction methods, ISRU material consumption, required strength, and construction duration by lunar base infrastructure element (Section 5 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 13~18)

| Lunar base infrastructure element | Applied construction method and technology | Required ISRU material quantity (t) | Required structural strength ($f_c$, MPa) | Required power and duration | Primary function and protection target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rocket landing pad (L/L Pad)** | **microwave/laser high-temperature sintered tile paving** | **$150 \sim 300$ t** | **$\ge 80.0\ \text{MPa}$ (heat-resistant sintering)** | **$50\ \text{kW}$ / 45 days** | **complete protection against rocket plumes ($>2000\ \text{K}$) and PSI ejecta** |
| **Blast berms (Blast Berms)** | **autonomous robotic excavated-soil embankment compaction** | **$500 \sim 1000$ t** | shear strength $\phi \ge 38^\circ$ | $15\ \text{kW}$ / 30 days | blocking direct impact of landing-spray debris |
| **Shielded habitat dome (Habitats)** | **3D-printed concrete/shell layering** | **$350 \sim 600$ t** | **$\ge 40.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **$30\ \text{kW}$ / 60 days** | **shielding against space radiation, cryogenic cold, and micrometeorites** |
| **Connecting roads (Roads/Pavements)** | ground compaction and partial high-temperature sintering | $100 \sim 200$ t/km | $\ge 25.0\ \text{MPa}$ | $20\ \text{kW}$ / 20 days | securing rover trafficability and preventing dust ejection |

### 2. Dust mitigation and autonomous construction reliability analysis
- **Electrostatic and mechanical dust sealing**: penetration of fine, sharp regolith dust into machine seals and spacesuits is blocked $95\%$ through construction of paved roads and airlock clean zones.
- **Remote autonomous operability**: even under communication latency with Earth, robots avoid obstacles using autonomous LiDAR sensing and SLAM navigation, completing 24-hour continuous construction.

- **Overall conclusion**: This report is a top-tier landmark origin document of space civil engineering, in which Dr. Robert Mueller of NASA Swamp Works directed the ISRU earthworks, sintered landing pads, 3D-printed habitat domes, and autonomous robotic construction technologies for the Artemis lunar base, demonstrating the feasibility of humanity's permanent lunar surface infrastructure in the 21st century.
