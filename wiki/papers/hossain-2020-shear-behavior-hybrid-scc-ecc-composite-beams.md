# Shear Behavior of Self-Consolidating Concrete (SCC) and ECC Layered Hybrid Composite Beams: 110 % Stirrup-Free Shear Capacity Enhancement and Ductile Failure Transition

## Summary

To reduce material costs associated with high-volume fibers and binders in full-depth ECC while preventing brittle shear failures, Professor K. M. Anwar Hossain and co-workers at Ryerson University designed SCC-ECC layered hybrid composite beams ($1200 \times 150 \times 200\ \text{mm}$) placing a 2.0 vol.% PVA-ECC layer (thickness 50, 100 mm) only in the bottom tension/shear-critical web zone, monolithic with economical self-consolidating concrete (SCC: compressive strength 45.0 MPa) in the compression zone. Evaluating shear span-to-depth ratios ($a/d = 1.5, 2.5$), transverse stirrup reinforcement, ECC layer depth ratios, and DIC shear strain fields, the authors demonstrated that **even without any shear stirrups (stirrup-free), the hybrid beam achieved a 110 % increase in ultimate shear capacity ($78.5\ \text{kN} \rightarrow 165.0\ \text{kN}$), transitioned brittle diagonal tension shear failure into ductile flexure-shear, enhanced shear deformation energy absorption by 3.8-fold, and reduced ECC material costs by 50 %** compared to plain SCC beams.

## Key Contributions

1. **Stirrup-free shear resistance via strategic SCC-ECC hybrid layering**:
   - Eliminated rebar congestion, poor consolidation, and material segregation in narrow webs by relying entirely on ECC fiber bridging capacity for shear resistance.
2. **Transition from brittle diagonal tension failure to ductile flexure-shear**:
   - Converted sudden explosive cracking along single large diagonal cracks into distributed multiple diagonal microcracks within the ECC layer, sustaining substantial deflection ductility before failure.
3. **Consistent shear amplification across deep ($a/d = 1.5$) and slender ($a/d = 2.5$) beam regimes**:
   - Demonstrated over 2-fold shear capacity gains in both arch-action-dominated deep beams and beam-action-dominated slender members.
4. **Full composite action via wet-on-wet interface casting**:
   - Established complete sectional continuity without interlayer shear slip using 30 min delayed wet-on-wet casting combined with interface scratching.

## Methods

- **Specimen design and mix configurations (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Beam dimensions: $1200 \times 150 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ (span 1000 mm, effective depth $d = 160\ \text{mm}$, longitudinal rebar 3-D20).
  - 12 full-scale beam series:
    1. `SCC-Pure`: Unreinforced SCC control beam.
    2. `ECC-Pure`: Full-depth ECC upper benchmark.
    3. `Hybrid-50`: Bottom 50 mm ECC + top 150 mm SCC.
    4. `Hybrid-100`: Bottom 100 mm ECC + top 100 mm SCC.
  - Shear span ratios: $a/d = 1.5, 2.5$; Stirrups: Stirrup-free vs reinforced (D10@100mm).
- **Shear testing program (Sections 2.4–2.5 & Figs. 5–8)**:
  - 3-point/4-point shear loading (1.0 mm/min displacement control).
  - Instrumentation: 5 LVDTs, diagonal crack opening gauges, and DIC shear strain measurement.

### 2. Shear truss tensile zone bridging mechanics
- **Uniform dispersion of web diagonal tension**: PVA fibers in the ECC layer sustained principal tensile stresses, restricting diagonal crack widths below $0.08\ \text{mm}$.
- **Cost-performance optimization**: Replacing only half the beam depth with ECC (`Hybrid-100`) achieved 94.3 % of the shear capacity of full-depth ECC while cutting material cost by 50 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that strategic SCC-ECC hybrid layering achieves a 110 % increase in stirrup-free shear capacity and converts brittle shear failure into ductile flexural behavior.
