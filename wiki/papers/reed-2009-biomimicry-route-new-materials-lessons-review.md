# Biomimicry-Based Design Principles for Next-Generation High-Performance Eco-Friendly Sustainable Materials: Multiscale Hierarchical Structures in Nature (Nacre Brick-and-Mortar), Low-Energy Ambient Synthesis, and an Immortal Composite Mechanics Review

## Summary

To overcome the enormous energy consumption (high-temperature firing, high-pressure forming) and environmental pollution limits facing humanity's traditional material manufacturing processes and to apply the ultra-tough lightweight biomineral structures perfected by living organisms over hundreds of millions of years of evolution to engineering, this paper comprehensively consolidated **the multiscale hierarchical structuring of nacre (brick-and-mortar layered structure amplifying the fracture toughness of brittle calcium carbonate 3000-fold), bone (hierarchical nano-composite of collagen fibers and hydroxyapatite), and spider silk (tensile strength rivaling steel with extreme energy absorption), together with ambient aqueous self-assembly synthesis kinetics and energy-efficient adaptation mechanisms**, critically examined the error of superficial imitation, and established **four biomimetic design guidelines directly applicable to modern engineered composites (ECC/ceramic composites/self-healing smart materials)**. This is the highest-authority landmark classic review by Prof. Christopher Viney's materials science team at UC Merced, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A, 2009.

## Key Contributions

1. **Consolidation of four core engineering principles of biomimetic new-material design**:
   - 1) hierarchical multiscale structure, 2) ambient-pressure aqueous self-assembly synthesis, 3) local compositional gradients and interfacial shear energy dissipation, 4) functionally integrated multi-objective optimization.
2. **Quantitative fracture-mechanics analysis of the nacre brick-and-mortar mechanism**:
   - Established the mechanics by which $95\%$ brittle aragonite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and $5\%$ organic protein polymer undergo interlocking shear slip, amplifying the mineral's fracture toughness $3000$-fold.
3. **Critique of simple shape copying and presentation of the 'principle transfer' paradigm**:
   - Clearly distinguished biological constraints of living materials (regeneration, metabolism) from engineering demands (mass producibility, extreme durability), providing practical materials-engineering guidelines.
4. **Theoretical foundation for global biomimetic cement composites and smart self-healing materials**:
   - Provided the fundamental inspiration for artificial nacre-mimetic ECC, crack-induced shear dispersion, and biomimetic self-healing encapsulation research.

## Methods

- **Comparative analysis of biominerals and natural composites (Sections 2~5 & Figs. 1~6)**:
  - Nacre: $0.5\ \mu\text{m}$ thick aragonite platelets + $20\ \text{nm}$ organic protein interfaces.
  - Bone: hydroxyapatite nanoparticles ($2\sim 4\ \text{nm}$) + triple-helix collagen fibrils.
  - Silk: $\beta$-sheet nanocrystal domains + amorphous chain networks.
- **Comparative evaluation against engineering manufacturing processes (Sections 5~7 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 7~10)**:
  - Computed energy input (firing temperature, pressure), raw material efficiency, recyclability, and mechanical toughness indices.

### 2. Interpretation of interfacial slip shear dissipation and ambient self-assembly
- **Crack deflection by shear restraint**: the organic interfaces of nacre deflect and divert crack tips, lengthening the failure path thousands of times to dissipate energy.
- **Internalization of self-healing functionality**: just as living materials heal their own microcracks through material circulation, this presents the rationale for self-healing design using reactions between unhydrated minerals and water within cement composites.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is the top-level landmark classic review in biomimetic materials science and sustainability engineering, in which Prof. Christopher Viney's team systematized the multiscale hierarchical structures and low-energy ambient synthesis principles of natural biominerals, establishing scientific guidance for future ultra-tough eco-friendly sustainable composite design.
