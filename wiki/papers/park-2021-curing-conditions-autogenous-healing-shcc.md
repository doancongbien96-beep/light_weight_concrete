## Summary
An experimental study investigating the effects of various curing environment conditions (water immersion, wet-dry cycling, temperature $5 \sim 40 °C$, $CO_2$ concentration) on the autogenous healing efficiency of multiple microcracks ($w < 60 \mu m$) introduced into strain-high-ductility strain-hardening cementitious composites (SHCC/ECC). Specimens preloaded to $1.5 \%$ tensile strain to induce multiple microcracks were healed for 28 d under different environments, and air/moisture permeability tests, crack re-tensile mechanical recovery tests, and microscopic crystal analysis were performed. It was demonstrated that not only underwater immersion but also a wet-dry cyclic environment ($12 h$ immersion + $12 h$ standing exposure) maximizes atmospheric $CO_2$ uptake, so that 98 % of microcracks below $50 \mu m$ are completely closed with calcium carbonate and tensile stiffness is recovered by more than 85 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Crystallization pathways of autogenous healing products identified by curing environment**: XRD and SEM confirmed that delayed C-S-H hydration dominates under water-immersed conditions, while $CaCO_3$ precipitation dominates under wet-dry conditions.
- **Temperature-dependent healing kinetics derived**: quantified that the reaction rate accelerates by more than a factor of 2 in the $20 \sim 40 °C$ range, restoring water-tightness completely within 7 d.
- **Recovery of mechanical tensile stiffness and re-tensile strain capacity evaluated**: upon re-tensioning after completion of autogenous healing, cracks formed at new locations rather than existing cracks, proving complete healing (self-healing efficiency $\eta_E > 85 \%$).

## Methods
- **Materials and specimens**: PVA-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash 60 wt %).
- **Pre-damage introduction**: uniaxial tensile load applied to dogbone specimens to introduce $1.5 \%$ strain (about 25 ~ 30 cracks, mean crack width $35 \mu m$).
- **Healing conditions**: (1) room-temperature water immersion ($20 °C$), (2) high-temperature water immersion ($40 °C$), (3) wet-dry cycling ($20 °C$, 60 % RH), (4) air exposure ($20 °C$, 60 % RH).
- **Evaluation**: differential-head water permeability test, optical microscopy crack closure measurement, re-tensile test.

## Results
- Wet-dry cycling and $20 °C$ water immersion conditions: after 28 d of healing, mean crack closure ratio $96.5 \%$ and water-tightness recovery ratio $99.1 \%$.
- Simple air exposure condition: due to moisture shortage the healing ratio remained below $25 \%$, proving the essential role of liquid water.
- Re-tensile test results showed initial elastic modulus recovered to $88 \%$ relative to the undamaged state and secondary strain-hardening behavior perfectly reproduced.
