## Summary
A study by Dr. H. Zhu's research team at the University of Western Australia (UWA), Australia clarifying the uniaxial direct tensile behavior, four-point flexural strain-hardening envelopes, single fiber pullout bridging characteristics, and fine crack formation mechanisms of eco-friendly strain-hardening geopolymer composites (PVA-EGC) reinforced with micro PVA fibers at different volume fractions ($V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$) in an ambient-cured ($23 °C$) alkali-activated slag-fly ash geopolymer matrix. The mix with PVA fibers at 2.0 vol % achieved a room-temperature 28 d compressive strength of **52.5 MPa**, a uniaxial direct tensile strength of **5.6 MPa**, a direct tensile strain capacity of **4.8 %**, and a flexural modulus of rupture of **16.5 MPa**.

## Key Contributions
- **Simultaneous manifestation of tensile and flexural strain hardening in ambient-cured PVA-EGC demonstrated**: ductility rivaling ordinary cement-based PVA-ECC without heat curing.
- **Single PVA fiber interfacial frictional stress ($\tau_0 = 1.68 MPa$) quantified**: premature fiber rupture suppressed by appropriate retention of chemical bonding.
- **PSH energy index $J_b'/J_{tip} = 5.6$ derived**: more than 42 fine cracks dispersed within the gauge.
- **Eco-friendly structural material design criteria with 72 % embodied carbon reduction established**: cement-zero construction realized.

## Methods
- Binder: GGBS 60 % + Class F fly ash 40 %, alkali activator sodium silicate solution ($Ms = 1.5, W/B = 0.32$).
- Fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm).
- Tests: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), four-point bending test ($100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$), 28 d compressive strength.
