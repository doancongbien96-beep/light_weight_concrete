# Mechanical properties of structural concrete with fine recycled ceramic aggregates

## Summary

An experimental study evaluating the compressive and tensile strengths, elastic modulus, and abrasion resistance of structural concrete targeting C25/30, in which natural fine aggregate was replaced at 20·50·100 % by ceramic recycled **fine aggregates** (4 mm or smaller) crushed from waste brick and sanitary ware. Its differentiator against prior research is that both workability (slump 125±10 mm) and aggregate grading were fixed to isolate the replacement-ratio effect (a regular research paper upon body inspection, despite the reference-style filename). Conclusion: concrete with brick-derived recycled fine aggregate shows performance sufficient for structural use, whereas sanitary-ware aggregate, with its glassy surfaces, raises the effective w/c through agglutination problems and degrades performance markedly; recovery is possible, however, when a superplasticizer is used.

## Key Contributions

- **Simultaneous fixing of grading and workability**: controlling the grading changes and slump changes that most prior studies conflate, with mixes designed by the Faury method; isolating the pure effect of the replacement ratio itself.
- **Contrast between brick vs sanitary-ware fine recycled aggregate**: water absorption 12.2 % (brick) vs 0.2 % (sanitary ware); densities 1948 vs 2969 kg/m³. Brick could be handled by adding mixing water (effective w/c held at 0.53), but sanitary ware, despite its low absorption, suffered particle agglutination from glassy surfaces plus liquid bridging, driving the effective w/c up to 0.76–0.86.
- **Long-term strength development (pozzolanic effect)**: the compressive-strength loss of brick-aggregate concrete shrank with age (7 days −24.9 % → 56 days −7.1 %), attributed to the pozzolanic reaction of SiO₂·Al₂O₃ in the brick (consistent with prior work such as Khatib and Wild).
- **Quantified superplasticizer recovery effect**: adding SP at 1 % of cement mass to SWC100 (w/c kept identical to RC) raised compressive strength +28 % and tensile strength +17 %; even sanitary-ware fine aggregate has scope for structural use if agglutination alone is suppressed.

## Methods

- Cement CEM II A-L 42.5 R (350 kg/m³), target compressive strength ~37 MPa (cube), slump 125±10 mm. Seven mixes: RC + BC20/50/100 (waste brick) + SWC20/50/100 (sanitary ware).
- The additional absorption water of the brick fine aggregate was estimated by Rodrigues et al.'s method (increase in absorption of immersed samples weighed on an underwater balance; 84 % of maximum absorption occurs within 10 minutes) and added at the mixer; the effective w/c was held constant at 0.53. Pre-saturation was excluded as negative for durability (Ferreira et al.).
- Tests: compression (EN 12390-3, 7·28·56 days, 15 cm cubes), splitting tension (EN 12390-6, ⌀15×30 cm cylinders), elastic modulus (LNEC E397), abrasion resistance (DIN 52108, tested on cut faces after curing to remove the surface-finish variable).

## Results

- Compressive strength (28 days): RC 46.2 MPa vs BC20 −7.0 %, BC50 −9.5 %, BC100 −9.6 %; SWC20 −32.5 %, SWC50 −33.5 %, SWC100 −42.5 %. SWC20 (31.2 MPa) fell below BC100 (41.7 MPa): sanitary-ware replacement is more unfavorable than full brick replacement.
- Splitting tensile strength (28 days): RC 3.60 MPa vs BC max −5.7 % (less sensitive than compression), SWC max −33.8 %.
- Elastic modulus (28 days): RC 38.3 GPa vs BC100 −29 %, SWC100 −26 %. Mortar stiffness governs (fine-aggregate replacement acts directly on mortar stiffness).
- Abrasion loss (DIN 52108): RC 4.0 mm vs BC100 5.3 mm (+31 %), SWC100 6.0 mm (+50 %); since fine aggregate becomes part of the paste, its porosity and glass flaking translate directly into abrasion loss (the opposite trend to coarse-aggregate replacement studies).
- Literature benchmarking: this study's BC results are higher than those of other studies because of (1) the added-water approach and (2) fine rather than coarse replacement (avoiding coarse-aggregate-fracture control). The SWC strength drop is unique to this study (other studies fixed apparent w/c, so their effective w/c actually dropped).
