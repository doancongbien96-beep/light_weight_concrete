# Flexural behavior of ultra-thin FRP grid (CFRP/BFRP) reinforced UHPC composite plates: flexural strength of $42.5\ \text{MPa}$, deflection ductility ratio $\mu = 6.20$, and the grid anchorage mechanism

## Summary

To realize ultra-lightweight building facade panels, tunnel linings, and rebar-free corrosion-free bridge decks, **ultra-thin composite plates (thickness $20$ and $30\ \text{mm}$, dimensions $1000 \times 300\ \text{mm}$)** were designed by embedding carbon (CFRP) and basalt (BFRP) 2D continuous grids in a base of straight steel fiber UHPC at 2.0 vol.% (28-day compressive strength $135.0\ \text{MPa}$). Through four-point bending tests with various grid mesh sizes ($30 \times 30\ \text{mm}$ and $50 \times 50\ \text{mm}$) and reinforcement layer counts, DIC full-surface strain analysis, and precise evaluation of the mechanical interlocking anchorage effect of grid cross nodes, **the two-layer CFRP-grid-reinforced UHPC thin plate demonstrated a flexural modulus of rupture (MOR) of $42.5\ \text{MPa}$, a deflection ductility ratio $\mu = 6.20$, complete restraint of service-load crack widths to at most $0.05\ \text{mm}$, and stable deflection-hardening without interfacial delamination** in this 2022 Engineering Structures landmark origin paper by Professor J. J. Zeng's team at Guangdong University of Technology and Professor W. Y. Gao's team at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, China.

## Key Contributions

1. **Two-stage multi-scale bridging mechanism of continuous FRP grids and steel fiber UHPC clarified**:
   - Steel fibers restrain the opening of initial micro cracks while the continuous FRP grid takes charge of large flexural tensile stress, completing full flexural deflection-hardening without tensile softening.
2. **Mechanical interlocking anchorage of grid cross nodes demonstrated**:
   - The crossing nodal points of the FRP grid were firmly fixed within the ultra-dense UHPC matrix, proving that 100 % of the material tensile strength is expressed without pullout slip.
3. **One-to-one comparison of flexural reinforcement efficiency between CFRP and BFRP grids**:
   - The CFRP grid maximized flexural stiffness and first-crack load through its high elastic modulus ($240\ \text{GPa}$), while the BFRP grid was clarified to absorb greater deflection deformation energy based on its rupture elongation ($2.5\%$).
4. **Standard design proposal for 20 mm ultra-thin highly durable rebar-free composite panels**:
   - A precast panel system presented that eliminates corrosion concerns from insufficient cover thickness at the source and cuts self-weight by 70 % relative to ordinary RC slabs.

## Methods

- **Specimen design and material properties (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Composite plate dimensions: $1000 \times 300 \times 20\ \text{mm}$ and $1000 \times 300 \times 30\ \text{mm}$ (span $800\ \text{mm}$).
  - UHPC mix: cement + silica fume + silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, W/B = 0.18, 2 % brass-coated straight steel fibers $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$; compressive strength $135.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Two FRP grid types:
    1. `CFRP grid`: tensile strength $2100\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $240\ \text{GPa}$, mesh size $50 \times 50\ \text{mm}$.
    2. `BFRP grid`: tensile strength $1250\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $50\ \text{GPa}$, mesh size $30 \times 30\ \text{mm}$.
- **Flexural test program (Sections 2.3~2.5 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Four-point bending load test (displacement control at 1.0 mm/min).
  - Instrumentation: non-contact DIC 2D strain field measurement, five LVDT deflection gauges.

### 2. Interpretation of FRP grid nodal interlocking and energy dissipation mechanisms
- **Wedge-shaped anchorage effect of crossing nodes**: under tensile load, grid cross nodes act as mechanical wedges inside the UHPC matrix, blocking bond slip at the source.
- **Multiple fine flexural cracks induced**: cracks did not localize to specific sections but distributed uniformly at intervals of the grid mesh ($50\ \text{mm}$), more than 20 in number, dissipating large flexural plastic deformation energy.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark origin paper that established the next-generation lightweight high-strength facade and deck design standard by achieving a flexural strength of 42.5 MPa and a deflection ductility ratio of 6.20 in 20 mm ultra-thin plates through the combination of continuous FRP grids and ultra-high performance concrete.
