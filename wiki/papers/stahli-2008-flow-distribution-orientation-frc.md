## Summary
A pioneering study that visualized and quantified, using high-resolution three-dimensional X-ray computed tomography (X-ray CT), the influence of the flow characteristics, rheology, and mold filling paths of self-compacting fiber-reinforced concrete (SC-FRC) on the three-dimensional spatial orientation of steel fibers and on flexural mechanical behavior. Analyzing the rotational dynamics of fibers subjected to the viscous yield stress of the matrix and to flow velocity gradients, it demonstrated through three-dimensional single-fiber tracking that fibers aligned with the flow direction near the tensile edge of flexural specimens ($\theta < 20°$) govern both the peak flexural strength and the post-cracking residual tensile capacity.

## Key Contributions
- **Nondestructive determination of 3D fiber orientation tensors via X-ray micro CT**: individual identification of the positions, inclination angles, and azimuth angles of thousands of steel fibers inside concrete in a three-dimensional coordinate system.
- **Elucidation of the law of flow-induced orientation**: demonstrated that the fiber orientation coefficient surges to $\eta = 0.85$ or above under extensional flow passing through narrow flow channels.
- **Direct mapping of local fiber density onto flexural crack opening**: established a precise 1:1 correlation between the measured count of fiber bridges and orientation angles at crack locations and the load-deflection response.

## Methods
- **Materials**: self-compacting mortar ($W/B = 0.35$, silica fume 8 %), straight micro steel fibers (diameter 0.15 mm, length 6 mm, $V_f = 1.0, 2.0 \%$).
- **CT scanning**: industrial X-ray $\mu CT$ ($50 \mu m$ spatial resolution), with 3D volume rendering and fiber segmentation algorithms.
- **Flexural test**: three-point bending tests on $40 \times 40 \times 160 mm$ prisms.

## Results
- Specimens cut parallel to the flow direction: flexural modulus of rupture $MOR = 18.5 MPa$, fracture energy $14.2 kJ/m2$.
- Specimens cut perpendicular to the flow direction: $MOR = 8.2 MPa$ (a 55 % loss of strength), brittle fracture behavior.
