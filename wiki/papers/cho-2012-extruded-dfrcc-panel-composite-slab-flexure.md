# Flexural Strength ($37.0\ \text{MPa}$) of Extruded Ductile Composite (DFRCC) Thin Panels and the $35\%$ Flexural Capacity Amplification Mechanism of RC Composite Slabs

## Summary

To eliminate the formwork-removal process in building floor slab construction and control bottom tensile cracking and deflection at the source, this landmark 2012 Advances in Materials Science and Engineering paper by the team of Prof. Chang-Geun Cho (Chosun University), Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University, joint research), and Prof. Yun-Yong Kim (Chungnam National University) designed **permanent formwork panels of 15 mm ultra-thin highly ductile fiber-reinforced composite (DFRCC: PVA fiber $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, 28-day compressive strength $56.0\ \text{MPa}$) fabricated by an industrial vacuum extrusion process**, together with **RC-DFRCC composite slab members ($2400 \times 600 \times 150\ \text{mm}$) cast integrally with the DFRCC panel at the bottom of the concrete slab**, and carried out a precise evaluation of four-point flexural load–deflection tests and crack pattern analysis, demonstrating **flexural first-cracking strength of $29.1\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate flexural strength of $37.0\ \text{MPa}$, and a deflection ductility ratio $\delta/\delta_i = 9.01$ (12 multiple fine cracks) for the extruded DFRCC panel alone, and $35\%$ amplification of flexural ultimate capacity over ordinary RC plus strict confinement of bottom crack width to $0.1\ \text{mm}$ or below for the RC composite slab**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of mass-production technology for DFRCC thin panels based on the industrial extrusion process**:
   - The powerful flow shear forces generated while passing through the high-pressure vacuum extrusion nozzle align PVA fibers $100\%$ along the extrusion length direction, realizing an ultra-high-strength flexural member with a flexural strength of $37.0\ \text{MPa}$.
2. **Demonstration of the tensile-reinforcement role doubling as permanent formwork (Stay-in-Place Formwork)**:
   - Temporary-formwork dismantling labor and schedule cut by $50\%$, while the DFRCC panel shares the stress of the bottom tensile rebars, greatly improving slab cracking stiffness.
3. **$35\%$ improvement in flexural ultimate capacity and secured deflection ductility of RC-DFRCC composite slabs**:
   - Whereas an ordinary concrete slab exhibits abrupt stiffness degradation after tensile cracking, the composite slab increased its flexural capacity by $35\%$ through the strain-hardening tensile bridging of DFRCC.
4. **Strict control of bottom tensile fine crack width to $0.1\ \text{mm}$ or below**:
   - Ultra-tight multiple fine crack control that blocks harmful ion penetration under service load, dramatically improving the long-term durability of floor structures.

## Methods

- **Materials and DFRCC extrusion mix (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: Type I ordinary Portland cement + silica powder ($\text{SiO}_2 = 95.5\%$, Blaine $379\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 100\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.35, W/B = 0.24).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA micro fiber ($L_f = 8\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1700\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 29.4\ \text{GPa}$, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, 1.2% oiling coating).
  - Forming: two-axis vacuum extrusion machine ($15\ \text{mm}$ thickness, $100\ \text{mm}$ width, $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$ steam curing for 3 days).
- **Flexural test program (Section 3 & Figs. 1~6)**:
  - DFRCC panel-alone flexural test: $400 \times 100 \times 15\ \text{mm}$ (span 300 mm, four-point flexural loading).
  - RC-DFRCC composite slab test: $2400 \times 600 \times 150\ \text{mm}$ full-scale slab (span 2000 mm, simply supported four-point loading).

### 2. Interpretation of the bridging and interfacial shear mechanisms of extrusion-oriented PVA fibers
- **Maximization of flexural strength by one-dimensional extruded fiber orientation**: passing through the extrusion nozzle, the 2% PVA fibers are arrayed one-dimensionally in perfect alignment with the tensile stress axis, raising the flexural modulus of rupture abruptly to $37.0\ \text{MPa}$.
- **Slab interface bonding and crack dispersion**: the ridged surface geometry on the back of the DFRCC panel forms mechanical interlock with the upper concrete so the section behaves as fully composite, dispersing the bottom tensile cracks evenly into 18 cracks.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark origin paper in the field of high-performance precast composite structures, in which Prof. Chang-Geun Cho and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee fabricated DFRCC permanent formwork panels with a flexural strength of 37.0 MPa via the extrusion process and demonstrated 35% amplification of flexural capacity and crack-width control to 0.1 mm or below in the RC composite slabs applying them.
