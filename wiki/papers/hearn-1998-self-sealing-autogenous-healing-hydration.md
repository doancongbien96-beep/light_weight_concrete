# Distinction and Permeability Mechanics Among Self-Sealing, Autogenous Healing, and Continued Hydration in Cementitious Concrete

## Summary

To resolve persistent ambiguities in durability modeling and water tightness evaluations where three distinct permeability-reducing phenomena are often conflated, Professor Nataliya Hearn at the University of Toronto formulated rigorous definitions and physical-chemical distinctions among **self-sealing (SSE)**, **autogenous healing**, and **continued hydration**. The study quantitatively analyzed their relative contributions under different hydraulic exposure regimes (flowing vs stagnant water) and matrix damage states (uncracked pore networks vs cracked fracture faces). The author established that **self-sealing** is a rapid, short-term reduction in water permeability (by 1~2 orders of magnitude within hours) in uncracked concrete driven by dissolution-redeposition of hydration products and C-S-H swelling; **autogenous healing** is the physical bridging of crack openings by calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) precipitation resulting from carbonation of leached $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ with dissolved $\text{CO}_2/\text{HCO}_3^-$; and **continued hydration** is the long-term, bulk microstructure densification driven by the progressive reaction of unhydrated clinker grains.

## Key Contributions

1. **Rigorous classification of three distinct permeability-reducing mechanisms**:
   - **Self-Sealing**: Dissolution and transport of hydration products within uncracked matrices, causing physical pore neck blocking, particle entrapment, and mechanical swelling.
   - **Autogenous Healing**: Precipitation of calcium carbonate crystals and new C-S-H along crack surfaces, restoring both impermeability and mechanical load-bearing capacity.
   - **Continued Hydration**: Ongoing chemical hydration of residual clinker particles converting capillary porosity into dense gel porosity.
2. **Clarification of dissolution-redeposition mechanisms in uncracked concrete**:
   - Used solvent replacement and SEM to demonstrate that the steep reduction in water flow across uncracked concrete within initial hours is driven primarily by dissolution and redeposition of mobile hydration species.
3. **Formulation of carbonation-driven crack healing**:
   - Quantified that under flowing water containing bicarbonate ions, calcite crystal growth ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CaCO}_3 \downarrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$) after 7 d produces permanent crack sealing.
4. **Distinction between short-term initial and steady-state permeability**:
   - Demonstrated that relying on short-term initial permeabilities underestimates the inherent self-sealing and healing capacities of concrete, necessitating time-decay permeability modeling.

## Methods

- **Analytical evaluation framework (Sections 2–4)**:
  - Permeability testing: Steady-state pressure permeability ($0.5\sim 2.0\ \text{MPa}$, tracking inflow $Q_{in}$ and outflow $Q_{out}$).
  - Damage conditions: Induced cracks with nominal widths $w = 10\sim 300\ \mu\text{m}$.
  - Exposure regimes: Stagnant non-flowing water vs continuous flowing water.
- **Analytical techniques**:
  - Solvent exchange (isopropanol/methanol) for immediate hydration stoppage and porosity determination.
  - SEM-EDS and petrographic analysis identifying crystalline phases within healed cracks.

## Results

### 1. Comparative matrix: Self-sealing vs autogenous healing vs continued hydration

| Feature | Self-Sealing | Autogenous Healing | Continued Hydration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Target medium** | Uncracked saturated pore network | Fractured crack faces ($w = 10\sim 300\ \mu\text{m}$) | Bulk matrix around unhydrated clinker |
| **Primary mechanism** | Dissolution and pore neck redeposition, swelling | Calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) precipitation, C-S-H growth | Long-term hydration of $\text{C}_3\text{S}$ and $\text{C}_2\text{S}$ |
| **Timescale** | **Hours to days (Rapid decay)** | **Days to months** | **Months to years (Long-term)** |
| **Water condition** | Inflow pressure flow required | Operates in flowing and stagnant water | Requires internal moisture (RH > 80 %) |
| **Main precipitate** | Rearranged C-S-H, ettringite, portlandite | Calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$), aragonite | Secondary C-S-H gel, $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ |
| **Strength recovery** | Negligible (Permeability reduction only) | **Partial tensile/flexural recovery (20~80 %)** | **Overall compressive and modulus gain** |

### 2. Engineering implications for service life prediction
- **Time dependence of permeability**: Apparent permeability drops by 10~50-fold within 24 h due to self-sealing effects, requiring distinct separation between transient initial flows and stabilized long-term permeabilities.
- **Crack-width thresholds**: Complete 100 % autogenous healing occurs reliably when initial crack widths are below $50\ \mu\text{m}$, whereas cracks exceeding $200\ \mu\text{m}$ experience precipitate wash-out under flow.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the foundational physical and chemical distinctions among self-sealing, autogenous healing, and continued hydration, underpinning modern research on smart self-healing concrete and durability modeling.
