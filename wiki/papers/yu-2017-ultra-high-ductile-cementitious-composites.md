# Micromechanics design of an ultra-high ductile cementitious composite (UHDCC) exhibiting uniaxial ultimate tensile strain of $8.5\sim 12.0\%$ and its saturated multiple fine cracking control mechanism with more than 60 cracks

## Summary

To overcome the tensile elongation limit of standard ECC ($3\sim 5\%$) and prevent complete structural collapse under extreme deformation environments such as earthquakes and blasts while absorbing enormous plastic energy, **ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (PE) micro fibers ($V_f = 1.5\sim 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, tensile strength $3000\ \text{MPa}$) were combined with a precisely particle-size-controlled cement-fly ash matrix** to develop an ultra-high ductile cementitious composite (UHDCC) maximizing the micromechanical PSH energy/strength margins. Through precise evaluation by uniaxial direct tension testing, digital image correlation (DIC), and single fiber pullout interface mechanics, **a 28-day compressive strength of $52.5\ \text{MPa}$, a uniaxial ultimate tensile strength of $8.2\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strain of $8.5\sim 12.0\%$ (mean $8.8\%$), and more than 60 ultra-dense multiple fine cracks within the gauge section (mean crack width $45\ \mu\text{m}$)** were achieved in this 2017 Construction and Building Materials landmark origin paper by **Professor Jiangtao Yu of Tongji University, China, and Professor Victor C. Li, Chair Professor at the University of Michigan, USA**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Highest uniaxial tensile strain capacity in the history of cementitious composites achieved ($\varepsilon_u \approx 8.8\%$, up to $12.0\%$)**:
   - A giant tensile strain exceeding the yield elongation of steel by more than 40 times was realized merely by incorporating PE fibers at 2.0 %.
2. **Micromechanics design maximizing the PSH energy index ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 5.0$) established**:
   - The matrix fracture toughness $J_{tip}$ was lowered to at most $15\ \text{J/m}^2$ and the slip-hardening bridging energy $J_b'$ of the PE fiber amplified to at least $85\ \text{J/m}^2$, inducing extreme multiple crack saturation.
3. **More than 60 ultra-dense fine cracks and ultra-tight crack width restraint at $45\ \mu\text{m}$**:
   - Even at ultimate tensile strains above $8\%$, crack widths remained at most $50\ \mu\text{m}$, perfectly maintaining waterproofing and self-healing performance.
4. **Dramatic improvement of tensile energy absorption capacity ($g \approx 650\ \text{kJ/m}^3$)**:
   - Complete strain-hardening behavior was completed in which stress kept rising to $8.2\ \text{MPa}$ throughout the large-deformation plastic regime.

## Methods

- **Materials and UHDCC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Binder: Type I ordinary Portland cement + low-calcium Class F fly ash (FA/C = 2.2~3.0, ultra-high-content replacement).
  - Aggregate: refined fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 100\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.20, W/B = 0.24, polycarboxylate water reducer).
  - Fiber: ultra-high molecular weight PE micro fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 100\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Test program (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, displacement control at 0.2 mm/min).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (28 days).
  - Crack pattern analysis: 2D-DIC strain field mapping and optical microscope measurement of crack number/width.

### 2. Interpretation of the micromechanics bridging mechanisms underlying ultra-high ductility
- **$J_{tip}$ reduction effect of high-content fly ash**: massive fly ash incorporation (FA/B = 75 %) lowers matrix elastic modulus and initial fracture toughness, minimizing the crack-tip energy release rate $J_{tip}$.
- **Continuous slip hardening of ultra-high-strength PE fibers**: the $3000\ \text{MPa}$ high-strength PE fibers do not rupture during pullout and keep exhibiting slip hardening with $\beta > 0$ through micro asperity deformation on the fiber surface, driving saturation of more than 60 fine cracks across the entire gauge length.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the landmark origin paper in which Professor Victor C. Li and Professor Jiangtao Yu completed the design theory of an ultra-high ductile composite UHDCC achieving a uniaxial tensile elongation of 8.8 % (up to 12.0 %) and a tensile strength of 8.2 MPa by combining ultra-high-strength PE fibers with a high-content fly ash matrix.
