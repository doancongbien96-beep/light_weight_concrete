## Summary
A study in which Professor K.Q. Yu's research team at Tongji University clarified through high-speed hydraulic servo tension tests the uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening envelopes, dynamic increase factor ($DIF$), tensile energy absorption capacity, and multiple crack dispersion behavior of ultra-high performance strain-hardening composites (UHP-ECC, $f_c = 125 MPa$, PE fiber $V_f = 2.0 \%$) over a broad loading-rate range from quasi-static strain rates ($\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4} s^{-1}$) to seismic/impact-region strain rates ($\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-1} s^{-1}$), for precise protective design under extreme dynamic loading environments such as earthquakes, blasts, and high-speed collisions. As the strain rate increased to $10^{-1} s^{-1}$, the direct tensile strength surged from **14.5 MPa to 21.8 MPa** ($DIF = 1.50$) while the ultra-high ductility of a tensile strain capacity of **6.8 %** was fully maintained.

## Key Contributions
- **Dynamic tensile envelope of UHP-ECC completed over a broad strain-rate range ($10^{-4} \sim 10^{-1} s^{-1}$)**: first-cracking strength and ultimate tensile strength rise simultaneously with increasing dynamic strain rate.
- **Tensile DIF constitutive equation established**: empirical model $DIF_t = 1.0 + 0.12 \log_{10}(\dot{\epsilon}/\dot{\epsilon}_0)$ formulated ($R^2 = 0.98$).
- **Increase in dynamic multiple fine crack density demonstrated**: under high-rate deformation, inertial confinement effects increase the fine crack count from 85 to 140, a rise of 65 %.
- **Tensile energy dissipation density of $950 kJ/m^3$ achieved**: blast shock wave absorption capability of protective structures maximized.

## Methods
- Matrix: $f_c = 125 MPa$, PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa).
- Test equipment: high-speed hydraulic servo actuator (MTS high-rate testing machine), laser Doppler displacement meter, high-speed video camera (5000 fps DIC).
- Test variable: strain rates $\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1} s^{-1}$.
