# Optimization of Space Structural-Material Fabrication via the Self-Propagating High-Temperature Synthesis (SHS) Thermite Reaction of Ilmenite and Aluminum from Lunar In Situ Resources (ISRU), with Microgravity Verification

## Summary

To minimize the cost of transporting construction materials from Earth (tens of thousands of dollars per kg) for building crewed lunar surface bases, landing pads, and space protective structures, this study developed a self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS: Self-propagating High-temperature Synthesis) process based on the highly exothermic aluminothermic reduction reaction between ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$), abundant in lunar regolith, and aluminum (Al) as reductant, and systematically characterized ignition conditions, combustion wave propagation velocity ($1\sim 5\ \text{mm/s}$), adiabatic combustion temperature ($1600\sim 2100\ ^\circ\text{C}$), phase analysis (XRD), and the compressive strength of the resulting $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$-Fe-Ti metal-ceramic composite under both terrestrial (1g) and Airbus A300 zero-gravity parabolic flight (0g) environments. This is the landmark space-construction origin paper by Prof. Giacomo Cao's research team at the University of Cagliari, Italy. The study established that the critical stoichiometric threshold for sustaining the self-sustaining SHS reaction is $\text{Al}/\text{FeTiO}_3 \ge 0.9$, and confirmed that spontaneous combustion synthesis completes successfully even with lunar regolith simulant (JSC-1A) blended in at up to 40 wt %. The synthesized ceramic-metal composite develops into a high-strength structural body with a room-temperature compressive strength of **80–140 MPa**, and in a microgravity environment it maintained combustion behavior identical to terrestrial conditions, completely demonstrating the feasibility of space construction processes based on in situ resource utilization (ISRU).

## Key Contributions

1. **Thermodynamic optimization of the ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$)-Al thermite SHS reaction**:
   - Quantitatively proved that at an aluminum reductant mixing ratio of $\text{Al}/\text{FeTiO}_3 \ge 0.9$, the strong heat release ($\Delta H_r^\circ = -330.0\ \text{kJ/mol}$) allows the combustion wave to self-propagate from local ignition alone, without external heating.
   - As the reductant molar ratio increases ($x = 0.9 \rightarrow 2.0$), the combustion temperature rises from $1600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to **$2100\ ^\circ\text{C}$**, and the reaction products become progressively more deeply reduced, from $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Fe} + \text{Ti}_x\text{O}_y$ to a $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Fe-Ti}$ intermetallic composite.
2. **Determination of the 40 % dilution limit for local lunar soil (JSC-1A) resources**:
   - Even when non-reactive surface-soil minerals (Anorthite, Pyroxene, Olivine) make up as much as 40 wt.% of the ilmenite-enriched lunar soil, the SHS combustion front propagates stably without extinction, enabling block forming.
3. **Zero-gravity microgravity verification via parabolic flight (Parabolic Flight, Airbus A300)**:
   - Performed more than 30 ignition experiments under extreme weightlessness of $2 \times 10^{-2}\ g$ or below, demonstrating that combustion wave velocities and crystalline phase synthesis proceed identically to terrestrial conditions even in the space environment, where gravitational buoyancy-driven convection is absent.
4. **Securing high-strength space structural material properties (80–140 MPa)**:
   - Completed the technical foundation for locally producing dense refractory and impact-resistant ceramic-metal composite tiles with 100 MPa-class compressive strength while minimizing energy consumption.

## Methods

- **Starting materials and formulation**:
  - Ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$, purity >99 %, mean particle size 45 µm).
  - Metallic aluminum powder (Al, purity 99.5 %, particle size 30 µm).
  - Lunar regolith simulant (JSC-1A Regolith: containing anorthite, pyroxene, olivine).
  - Reactant pellets: cylindrical pressed compacts, 12 mm diameter and 15–20 mm height.
- **SHS apparatus and conditions (Fig. 2)**:
  - Vacuum reaction chamber (vacuum level $10^{-2}\ \text{mbar}$ or Ar inert atmosphere).
  - Local electric spark ignition via a tungsten heating filament.
  - Real-time measurement of combustion propagation velocity and temperature profiles with W-Re high-temperature thermocouples (Thermocouple) and a high-speed optical camera.
- **Microgravity (0g) test environment**:
  - 30 parabolic maneuvers aboard the Novespace Airbus A300 Zero-G aircraft, Bordeaux, France.
- **Property evaluation**:
  - XRD crystalline phase analysis, SEM/EDS microstructure observation, room-temperature uniaxial compressive strength (per ASTM C39).

## Results

### 1. SHS Combustion Parameters and Product Phases vs $\text{Al}/\text{FeTiO}_3$ Molar Ratio ($x$)

| Molar ratio ($x$) | Reaction system heat release | Combustion propagation velocity (mm/s) | Peak combustion temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$) | Primary synthesized crystalline phases (XRD) | SHS feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **$x < 0.9$** | Insufficient heat release | – | < 1200 | Unreacted residue remains | **Not possible (extinguished)** |
| **$x = 0.9$** | $-330\ \text{kJ/mol}$ | 1.2 ± 0.2 | 1620 ± 50 | $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Fe} + \text{Ti}_3\text{O}_5$ | **Self-propagation successful** |
| **$x = 1.0$** | $-365\ \text{kJ/mol}$ | 1.8 ± 0.3 | 1750 ± 40 | $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Fe} + \text{Ti}_2\text{O}_3$ | Good self-propagation |
| **$x = 1.33$** | $-480\ \text{kJ/mol}$ | 3.1 ± 0.4 | 1920 ± 60 | $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Fe} + \text{TiO}$ | High-temperature combustion |
| **$x = 2.0$** | **$-620\ \text{kJ/mol}$** | **4.8 ± 0.5** | **2100 ± 80** | **$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{FeTi} + \text{Fe}_3\text{Al}$** | **Ultra-high-temperature complete reduction** |

### 2. Terrestrial (1g) vs Zero-Gravity (0g) Comparison and Mechanical Compressive Strength

- **Microgravity reaction equivalence**: the combustion velocity under parabolic-flight 0g conditions ($1.9\ \text{mm/s}$ at $x=1.0$) matched the terrestrial 1g velocity ($1.8\ \text{mm/s}$) within experimental error, proving that there is no gravity dependence.
- **Compressive strength**: the synthesized $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\text{-Fe}$ composite exhibited a room-temperature compressive strength of **85–138 MPa** despite residual porosity, comfortably exceeding the requirements for lunar surface landing impact and shielding structures.

- **Overall conclusion**: this study established that the SHS thermite process of lunar surface ilmenite mineral and aluminum is the optimal space ISRU construction solution, capable of producing 100 MPa-class high-strength ceramic composite structural materials at ultra-high speed from lunar local resources alone, without transporting water or binder from Earth.
