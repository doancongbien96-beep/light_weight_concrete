# Compressive Strength ($20\sim 140\ \text{MPa}$), Elastic Modulus ($15\sim 45\ \text{GPa}$), and Stress-Strain Constitutive Mechanisms of Alkali-Activated Binders (AAB): A Comprehensive Review

## Summary

To systematically synthesize the mechanical properties and elastic deformation behavior of alkali-activated materials (AAMs) and geopolymers (blast furnace slag AAS, fly ash AAF, metakaolin AAMK), Dr. Miroslav Komljenović (University of Belgrade) compiled global research data in the *Handbook of Alkali-Activated Cements, Mortars and Concretes* (Elsevier, 2015). The review details how **precursor chemical composition (amorphous glass content, $\text{CaO}, \text{SiO}_2/\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$), activator chemistry ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3, \text{NaOH}, \text{KOH}$) and modulus ($M_s = \text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O}$), and curing conditions (ambient vs thermal $60\sim 85\ ^\circ\text{C}$) govern 28 d compressive strength ($f_c$), flexural strength ($f_{flex}$), static elastic modulus (Young's Modulus $E$), Poisson's ratio ($\nu$), and nonlinear stress-strain relations**. The author elucidated the underlying mechanisms connecting 3D C-A-S-H and N-A-S-H nanogel network structures with macroscopic mechanical stiffness.

## Key Contributions

1. **Governing principles for strength vs elastic modulus across C-A-S-H (AAS) and N-A-S-H (AAF) nanogels**:
   - High-calcium slag systems (AAS) form dense, layered C-A-S-H gels that develop high elastic moduli ($30\sim 45\ \text{GPa}$) under ambient curing. Low-calcium fly ash systems (AAF) form 3D zeolite-like N-A-S-H networks with $15\sim 25\%$ lower elastic moduli than equivalent-strength OPC concrete, providing higher strain compliance.
2. **Activator modulus ($M_s$) and dosage optimization curves for peak strength**:
   - Quantified peak compressive strength regimes ($80\sim 140\ \text{MPa}$) for liquid sodium silicate activators at silicate moduli $M_s = 1.0\sim 1.5$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ dosages of $4\sim 8\ \text{wt.\%}$.
3. **Characterization of nonlinear stress-strain ($\sigma-\varepsilon$) curves and Poisson's ratio ($\nu = 0.13\sim 0.22$)**:
   - Demonstrated that standard concrete constitutive equations (Hognestad and Popovics models) predict geopolymer concrete compressive behavior within $\pm 5\%$ error.
4. **Integration of precursor amorphous glass content with reaction kinetics**:
   - Established quantitative relationships between precursor specific surface area/vitreous content and initial dissolution/polycondensation rates.

## Methods

- **AAB binder classification and database structure (Sections 7.2–7.4 & Tables 7.1–7.8)**:
  - Group 1: High-calcium binders (Alkali-activated slag AAS $\rightarrow$ C-A-S-H gel, ambient $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ curing).
  - Group 2: Low-calcium binders (Alkali-activated fly ash/metakaolin AAF/AAMK $\rightarrow$ N-A-S-H gel, thermal $60\sim 85\ ^\circ\text{C}$ curing).
  - Group 3: Hybrid binders (Slag-fly ash blends $\rightarrow$ C-(N)-A-S-H co-existing gels).
- **Evaluated mechanical parameters**:
  - Compressive strength ($f_c$), flexural strength ($f_{flex}$), splitting tensile strength ($f_{st}$).
  - Static elastic modulus ($E_c = \alpha \sqrt{f_c}$ models), ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), and Poisson's ratio ($\nu$).

### 2. Microstructural nanogel mechanics and stiffness development
- **Nanostructural stiffness in C-A-S-H vs N-A-S-H**: The tobermorite-like sheets of high-calcium C-A-S-H gels, cross-linked by interlayer $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ cations, generate high elastic moduli, whereas the open 3D cage topology of N-A-S-H gels exhibits greater compliance under load.
- **Structural compliance and thermal shock resistance**: The lower elastic modulus of AAF systems ($E \approx 22\ \text{GPa}$) reduces restraint stresses, mitigating drying shrinkage cracking risks and thermal shock stress concentrations.

- **Overall conclusion**: This handbook review synthesized mechanical property relationships and constitutive models for alkali-activated binders, establishing nanostructure-to-macroscopic mechanical links for cementless construction materials.
