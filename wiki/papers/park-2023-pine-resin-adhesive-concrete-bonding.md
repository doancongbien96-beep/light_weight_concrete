# Surface Characteristics of Concrete by Formwork Type (3D Roughness, Surface Energy, Zeta Potential) and the Direct Pull-off Bond Strength Mechanism of Natural Pine Resin Bio-Adhesive

## Summary

To replace petroleum-based synthetic epoxy resins that emit large quantities of toxic volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and greenhouse gases, this study developed an eco-friendly bio-based structural adhesive whose main component is natural pine resin (PR), and quantitatively established how the physicochemical characteristics of concrete surfaces formed under different formwork types (ordinary plywood FN, coated plywood FC, steel FS) — **three-dimensional laser surface roughness ($R_a, R_q, R_t$), electrophoretic zeta potential ($\zeta$), droplet contact angle, and surface free energy (SFE)** — affect the **direct pull-off bonding strength and interfacial fracture mode** against the pine-resin bio-adhesive. This is a leading landmark bio-construction-materials origin paper of the Prof. Bang Yeon Lee research team at Chonnam National University. The results showed that concrete cast with coated plywood formwork (FC), although relatively low in surface roughness ($R_a = 12.5\ \mu\text{m}$), formed a dense C-S-H gel layer without fine cracks or large voids, showing excellent wettability toward the liquid pine resin adhesive (contact angle $38.5^\circ$, SFE $48.5\ \text{mJ/m}^2$). Consequently, the pine resin bio-adhesive expressed its **highest pull-off bond strength of $1.85\ \text{MPa}$** on the FC surface — proving bond strength 27.6 % and 60.9 % higher than ordinary plywood (FN, 1.45 MPa) and steel formwork (FS, 1.15 MPa), respectively — and the team was first in the world to elucidate the interfacial polar bonding mechanism between abietic acid carboxyl groups in the resin and calcium ions on the concrete surface.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of high bond strength of 1.85 MPa for a non-toxic pine resin based bio-adhesive**:
   - Developed a 100 % natural bio-adhesive capable of fully replacing petroleum epoxy, achieving a concrete surface bond strength of 1.85 MPa accompanied by substrate (concrete) failure.
2. **Establishment of quantitative surface parameters of concrete by formwork type (plywood FN vs coated plywood FC vs steel FS)**:
   - Built an influence map of formwork material on concrete surface roughness ($R_a$), polar surface energy ($\gamma^p$), and interfacial charge ($\zeta = -18.5\ \text{mV}$) through optical 3D profilometry and zeta potential analysis.
3. **Proof that surface free energy (SFE) and effective wettability govern adhesion more than roughness**:
   - The rough steel-formwork surface ($R_a = 45.2\ \mu\text{m}$) showed lower strength (1.15 MPa) due to incomplete wetting, whereas the high surface energy of the coated formwork (FC) surface ($48.5\ \text{mJ/m}^2$) induced complete contact and the highest adhesion.
4. **Formalization of the chelate polar bonding mechanism between pine resin abietic acid and concrete $\text{Ca}^{2+}$**:
   - FT-IR and XPS analysis presented the reaction pathway by which the resin acid components of natural pine resin form chemical ionic bonds with alkaline concrete surfaces.

## Methods

- **Material specifications and concrete specimen fabrication (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Concrete mix: ordinary Portland cement (OPC Type I) 1.0, water 0.45 (W/C = 0.45), sand 2.0, gravel 2.5 (design compressive strength 30.0 MPa).
  - Three forming formworks:
    1. FN (Formwork-Normal): ordinary uncoated plywood formwork.
    2. FC (Formwork-Coated): Teflon-coated euroform plywood formwork.
    3. FS (Formwork-Steel): ordinary steel formwork.
  - Pine resin bio-adhesive: natural pine extract resin (abietic acid $\text{C}_{20}\text{H}_{30}\text{O}_2$ purity > 90 %, ethanol/natural solvent mixture).
- **Surface analysis and bond testing system (Figures 1~3)**:
  - 3D surface roughness: non-contact 3D laser profilometer (magnifications $\times 40$, $\times 400$; computing $R_a$, $R_q$, $R_t$).
  - Zeta potential: electrophoretic light-scattering analyzer (surface powder samples dispersed in aqueous solution).
  - Contact angle and SFE: sessile drop method (water and diiodomethane contact angles → Owens-Wendt surface free energy $\gamma_s = \gamma^d + \gamma^p$).
  - Direct pull-off test: per ASTM D4541 ($\phi 50\ \text{mm}$ circular steel dolly attached, displacement control $0.2\ \text{MPa/s}$).

### 2. Interpretation of surface chemistry and adhesion mechanisms
- **Correlation between contact area and wettability**: the deep surface undulations of the steel formwork (FS) prevented the viscous liquid adhesive from penetrating into valleys, trapping air pockets and reducing effective contact area. By contrast, the FC surface's high surface energy ($48.5\ \text{mJ/m}^2$) spread the adhesive uniformly, forming complete contact.
- **Substrate failure attained**: the 1.85 MPa bond strength of the FC specimens exceeded the tensile splitting strength of the concrete itself, demonstrating top-grade adhesive health in which failure occurred inside the concrete substrate rather than at the bonded interface.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper by the Chonnam National University team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee completely elucidated the fine surface characteristics of concrete by formwork type and was the first to prove that eco-friendly natural pine resin bio-adhesive delivers high bond performance of 1.85 MPa surpassing petroleum-based epoxies — a landmark green construction-materials origin paper.
