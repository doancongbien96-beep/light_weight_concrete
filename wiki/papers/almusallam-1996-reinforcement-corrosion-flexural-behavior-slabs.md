# Mechanism of the sharp flexural-capacity drop ($-60\%$) of RC slabs with reinforcement corrosion level ($0\sim 25\%$) and of the threshold ($5\%$) for transition to brittle bond-splitting failure

## Summary

To quantitatively predict the residual flexural capacity and safety of reinforced concrete (RC) structures exposed to chloride-laden environments, the team of **Prof. Abdullah A. Almusallam and distinguished professor Rasheeduzzafar at King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), Saudi Arabia** tested **one-way full-scale concrete slab specimens ($711 \times 305 \times 63.5\ \text{mm}$) with precisely controlled reinforcement corrosion levels ($0, 1.5, 5.0, 15.0, 25.0\%$ mass loss ratio)** by **simply supported uniform-load flexural testing together with close evaluation of rebar-concrete interfacial bond behavior**. This most-cited landmark classic in ASCE Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering (1996) demonstrated for the first time worldwide that **at corrosion levels up to $5\%$, frictional bond is preserved by the slight volumetric expansion of corrosion products and flexural capacity rises marginally ($5\%$), whereas the moment corrosion exceeds $5\%$, longitudinal corrosion cracks of the cover concrete collapse flexural capacity by up to $60\%$ and the failure mode shifts abruptly from ductile flexural yielding to brittle interfacial bond splitting (Bond Splitting)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First-ever establishment of the correlation curve between reinforcement corrosion level and residual flexural capacity of RC slabs**:
   - Completed a two-stage corrosion-flexural behavior model consisting of a mild strength-gain segment at early corrosion ($0\sim 5\%$) and a steep capacity-collapse segment beyond $5\%$.
2. **Identification of the critical corrosion level for structural performance collapse ($\text{Critical Corrosion Level} = 5.0\%$)**:
   - Proved that loss of bond through longitudinal splitting cracks of the cover concrete, rather than rebar section loss itself, is the dominant factor degrading flexural capacity.
3. **Demonstration of the abrupt transition to brittle failure mechanisms**:
   - Showed that the ductile rebar-yielding behavior of sound slabs ($\mu = 5.5$) degenerates into instantaneous brittle collapse with deflection ductility ratio $\mu = 1.2$ once corrosion reaches $15\%$ or more.
4. **Standard basis for global RC structure repair and strengthening criteria**:
   - Cited as the core textbook basis for setting reinforcement corrosion serviceability limit states in structural maintenance guidelines worldwide, including ACI and CEB-FIP.

## Methods

- **Specimen dimensions and concrete mix (Section 2 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Slab dimensions: $711 \times 305 \times 63.5\ \text{mm}$ (main tension reinforcement 5-No.2 $\phi 6\ \text{mm}$ deformed bars @57 mm, cover thickness 19 mm).
  - Concrete mix: Type I OPC (cement content $370\ \text{kg/m}^3$, W/C = 0.45, coarse-to-fine aggregate ratio = 2.0, 28-day compressive strength $35.0\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Accelerated corrosion and flexural test program (Section 2.2 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Electrochemical accelerated corrosion: DC constant current ($i = 2.0\ \text{mA/cm}^2$) applied under immersion in $5\%\ \text{NaCl}$ aqueous solution.
  - Measured corrosion level: after flexural failure, all rebars extracted and acid-cleaned (ASTM G1) to measure mass loss ratio ($\%$).
  - Flexural test: simply supported one-way flexural loading test (0.5 mm/min displacement control).

### 2. Interpretation of the interfacial expansion-pressure and bond-loss mechanism
- **Bond friction gain in the $0\sim 5\%$ region**: the thin rust layer formed on the rebar surface increases friction against the concrete cover, temporarily maintaining flexural capacity.
- **Cover splitting (spalling) and bond collapse beyond $5\%$**: when corrosion expansion pressure exceeds the tensile strength of the cover and longitudinal splitting cracks penetrate through it, the rib-interlock effect of the rebar vanishes, producing a bond-splitting failure in which the bar slips out inside the concrete before it can even yield.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark classic of the highest authority in global concrete durability and structural safety diagnosis; Prof. Abdullah A. Almusallam systematically measured the flexural behavior of RC slabs against reinforcement corrosion level, identified the 5 % corrosion threshold, and elucidated the mechanism of abrupt capacity collapse caused by bond loss.
