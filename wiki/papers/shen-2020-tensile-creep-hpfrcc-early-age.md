## Summary
This study precisely measured the tensile creep and stress relaxation behavior of high performance fiber reinforced cementitious composites (HPFRCC / ECC) at early ages (1 d, 3 d, 7 d) using a specially designed temperature-stress coupled tensile creep frame. It analyzed the mechanism by which early-age tensile creep acts as a stress relief buffer against the tensile stress concentrations that arise under restrained shrinkage, greatly mitigating the risk of early-age cracking. A viscoelastic constitutive model (Burger-Kelvin model) of early-age tensile creep was established that integrates the sustained tensile stress ratio ($\sigma_{load}/f_t = 0.3 \sim 0.7$) with fiber-matrix interfacial slip creep.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of early-age tensile creep and stress relaxation mechanisms**: demonstrated that under sustained tensile loading at 3 d age the specific tensile creep reaches $75 \sim 120 \mu\epsilon/MPa$, relaxing and dissipating more than 40 % of the restrained tensile stress.
- **Separation of the fiber bridging contribution to tensile creep**: demonstrated that beyond the viscous flow of the matrix itself, fiber-matrix interfacial shear frictional slip creep accounts for 30 ~ 45 % of the total tensile creep.
- **Establishment of an early-age cracking resistance index**: improved the safety margin estimation method for early shrinkage cracking by applying an effective elastic modulus that accounts for tensile creep ($E_{eff} = E_c / (1 + \phi)$).

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-HPFRCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash 50 wt %).
- **Creep test apparatus**: hydraulic spring-type precision tensile creep frame installed inside a temperature-humidity chamber ($20 °C, 60 \% RH$).
- **Loading conditions**: sustained loading at 30 %, 50 %, and 70 % of the tensile strength at ages of 1 d, 3 d, and 7 d, with continuous strain measurement.

## Results
- The earlier the age, the steeper the tensile creep compliance rises: the creep strain at 1 d age is 2.8 times larger than at 7 d age.
- Even at a sustained load ratio of 70 %, no tertiary creep rupture occurred and stable steady-state secondary creep behavior was maintained, demonstrating high resistance to tensile creep rupture.
