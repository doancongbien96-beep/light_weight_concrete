## Summary
To maximize the economy and constructability of high-strength ECC, this study completed the micromechanics-based mix design of a Low-Fiber High-Strength ECC that cuts fiber content from the conventional 2.0 vol % to 1.0 ~ 1.2 vol % (close to half) while realizing compressive strength of 80 ~ 100 MPa and tensile strain capacity above 5.0 %. Matrix flaws were minimized through continuous particle packing theory, and the interfacial slip-hardening characteristics of ultra-high-strength PE fibers (diameter 12 µm, tensile strength 3200 MPa) were optimized so that the PSH energy criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip} > 4.5$) was satisfied even with a small amount of fiber.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a micromechanics design theory for low-fiber-content ($V_f = 1.0 \sim 1.2 \%$) high-strength ECC**: compensation of reduced bridging stress at lower fiber volume fraction through matrix flaw refinement and precise fracture toughness control.
- **Dramatic improvement in manufacturing cost and rheological constructability**: a 45 % reduction in fiber usage greatly lowered material cost, and reduced mixing viscosity enabled self-consolidating placement.
- **Demonstration of combined high strength and high ductility performance**: at $V_f = 1.2 \%$, 28 d compressive strength $92.5 MPa$, tensile strength $7.8 MPa$, and tensile strain capacity $5.4 \%$ were achieved.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: cement + silica fume (12 %) + slag (35 %), $W/B = 0.20$, high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm).
- **Test items**: uniaxial dogbone direct tensile test, single-fiber pull-out test, 28 d compressive strength, slump flow.

## Results
- Optimal mix at $V_f = 1.2 \%$: compressive strength $92.5 MPa$, tensile strength $7.85 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $5.42 \%$, slump flow $680 mm$ (self-consolidation secured).
- Compared with the conventional $V_f = 2.0 \%$ mix, equivalent tensile ductility was maintained while maximizing economy.
