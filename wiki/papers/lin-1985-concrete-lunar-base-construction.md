## Summary

The seminal original paper proposing humanity's first comprehensive engineering concept for concrete construction of a lunar base, presented by the world-renowned structural engineering master Prof. T. Y. Lin at the 1985 NASA Lunar Base Symposium. Using local lunar rock and soil (regolith) as aggregate and applying prestressed concrete shell structures, it mechanically demonstrates the design feasibility of a 100 m-class large habitat dome (Lunar Base Dome) and underground silos that fully resist the three extreme environmental loads of the lunar surface: internal pressurization ($1 atm = 101 kPa$), extreme thermal cycling ($-150 \sim +120 °C$), and cosmic radiation and micrometeorite impacts.

## Key Contributions

- **World-first proposal of a three-dimensional prestressed shell design concept for lunar concrete habitat structures**: three-dimensional post-tensioning tendons offset 100 % of the tensile stress caused by the 1 atm internal air pressure.
- **Establishment of the physicochemical advantages of concrete in the lunar environment**: high-density radiation shielding capability, interior temperature buffering via high heat capacity (maintained within $\pm 2 °C$), non-flammability and gas-free emission.
- **Engineering solution for concrete curing under vacuum**: sealed steam curing performed inside inflatable pneumatic formwork to block moisture evaporation.
- **Strategy for minimizing Earth-launched mass**: 90 % local procurement of regolith aggregate restricts materials transported from Earth to hydrogen and special tendons, within 10 %.

## Methods

- Structural mechanics models: membrane theory of thin-walled spherical domes and the load balancing method for prestress.
- Cement clinkering and concrete mix analysis based on the chemical compositions ($CaO, SiO_2, Al_2O_3, FeO$) of Apollo 11, 12, and 14 lunar soil samples.

## Results

- Design parameters and required specifications of the lunar base concrete dome structure:

| Item | Design specification | Mechanical basis and function |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| Habitat dome diameter / height | diameter 100 m / height 30 m | large community space housing 400 people |
| Concrete shell thickness | 0.5 m (2m regolith overburden on top) | blocks radiation to an annual 50 mSv or less |
| Required concrete compressive strength | $f_c = 40.0 MPa$ | supports internal pressurization and bending moments |
| Circumferential/meridional PT applied stress | $\sigma_p = 3.5 MPa$ | complete cancellation of tension from internal pressurization ($101 kPa$) |
| Interior temperature stabilization | constant maintenance at $20 \pm 2 °C$ | heat capacity of the 2.5 m thick concrete-regolith layer |
