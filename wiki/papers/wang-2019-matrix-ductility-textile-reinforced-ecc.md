## Summary
A study that elucidated, in the direct tensile behavior of textile reinforced ECC (TR-ECC), a composite combining a two-dimensional carbon fiber textile grid with short-fiber-reinforced high-ductility ECC, how matrix ductility (ordinary mortar vs high-ductility ECC) affects multi-stage tensile strain-hardening of the composite, textile-matrix interfacial stress transfer, and failure mode. Unlike TRC using a brittle mortar matrix, which undergoes premature textile rupture or debonding through early crack localization, TR-ECC was proven to achieve an ultimate tensile strength of 18.5 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 6.2 % through the dual bridging synergy of short-fiber bridging and the continuous textile mesh.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a dual-scale bridging mechanism**: elucidation of the complete interaction between first-order microcrack bridging by short fibers within the matrix and second-order macroscopic tensile support by the continuous carbon textile.
- **Relief of interfacial shear lag in the textile fiber bundles (rovings)**: the high deformation capacity of the ECC matrix buffers stress concentrations at the textile grid crossover points, improving the textile tensile efficiency factor from 0.62 to 0.94.
- **Establishment of design criteria for ultra-thin ductile claddings and strengthening panels**: realization of ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility structural performance with plates 12 ~ 20 mm thick.

## Methods
- **Material composition**: carbon fiber textile (grid size $15 \times 15 mm$, cross-sectional area $1.8 mm2/yarn$, strength 3200 MPa) + PVA-ECC matrices ($V_f = 1.5 \%, 2.0 \%$).
- **Test items**: uniaxial direct tensile test (dog-bone and plate specimens), DIC optical strain distribution analysis, interfacial pull-out test.

## Results
- TR-ECC composite: first cracking strength $4.5 MPa$, ultimate tensile strength $18.5 MPa$, ultimate strain $6.2 \%$.
- Compared with ordinary TRC, an 85 % increase in tensile strength and a leapfrogging 4.5-fold improvement in fracture energy absorption capacity.
