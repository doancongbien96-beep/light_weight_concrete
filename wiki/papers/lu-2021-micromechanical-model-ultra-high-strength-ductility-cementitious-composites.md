# Development of a three-dimensional micromechanics analysis model for ultra-high-strength, ultra-high-ductility cementitious composites (UHS-UHDCC, $f_c = 160\ \text{MPa}$): uniaxial tensile strength of $21.5\ \text{MPa}$, tensile elongation $\varepsilon_u = 8.5\%$, and the mechanism suppressing premature fiber rupture

## Summary

To fundamentally solve the problem of premature fiber rupture and loss of ductility (brittle failure) caused by the inevitably high fracture toughness ($J_{tip}$) of ultra-high-strength cement matrices ($f_c > 150\ \text{MPa}$) and their strong interfacial bond, **an all-encompassing three-dimensional micromechanical bridging constitutive model integrating the fiber rupture probability ($P_{rup}$), slip-hardening coefficient ($\beta$), snubbing friction ($f$), and matrix flaw size distribution was established within an ultra-high-strength matrix ($f_c = 160.0\ \text{MPa}$)**, and through a hybrid combination of high-strength PE microfibers ($d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$) and micro steel fibers, **a uniaxial direct tensile strength of $21.5\ \text{MPa}$ (world-leading level), a uniaxial ultimate tensile elongation of $\varepsilon_u = 8.50\%$, more than 60 saturated multiple ultra-fine cracks ($w \le 25\ \mu\text{m}$), and a compressive strength of $160.0\ \text{MPa}$ were simultaneously realized in a single composite** — the supremely authoritative, immortal landmark original paper of Cement and Concrete Research (2021) by the team of **Christopher K. Y. Leung, Chair Professor at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST), and Victor C. Li, Chair Professor at the University of Michigan, USA**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first establishment of a three-dimensional micromechanics model of fiber rupture-pullout dedicated to ultra-high-strength matrices**:
   - Overcoming the conventional assumption of rupture-free complete pullout, the rupture fraction governed by local stress concentration and the fiber strength distribution (Weibull) in an ultra-high-strength matrix is quantified mathematically.
2. **Complete breakthrough of the physical limit of the strength-ductility trade-off**:
   - At the ultra-high-strength state with compressive strength $160\ \text{MPa}$, a uniaxial tensile strength of $21.5\ \text{MPa}$ and a tensile elongation of $8.50\%$ were achieved simultaneously, opening a new era in composite materials engineering.
3. **Maximization of the complementary bridging energy ($J_b'$) through control of the hydrophilic/hydrophobic balance of PE fiber surfaces**:
   - By suppressing chemical adhesion ($G_d$) while maintaining pure frictional slip ($\tau_0 = 1.8\sim 2.5\ \text{MPa}$), the fiber rupture fraction was confined to $15\%$ or less, thereby inducing fully saturated PSH.
4. **Presentation of extreme design criteria for seismic core walls of super-tall buildings, undersea tunnels, and protective infrastructure**:
   - Enables the design of rebar-free highly ductile structural members that dissipate enormous seismic energy through their own strength and ductility alone, without reinforcement detailing.

## Methods

- **Micromechanics constitutive equations (Section 2 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Single-fiber pullout-rupture bridging equation: $\sigma(\delta) = \frac{4 V_f}{\pi d_f^2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \int_0^{L_f/2} [1 - P_{rup}(\delta, z, \theta)] P(\delta, z, \theta) p(\theta) p(z) \, dz \, d\theta$.
  - Energy criterion: $J_b' / J_{tip} \ge 3.0$, where $J_{tip} = K_m^2 / E_m$.
  - Strength margin: $\sigma_0 / \sigma_{fc} \ge 1.4$.
- **Experimental validation program (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 6~11)**:
  - Mix: cement + silica fume + silica sand powder ($W/B = 0.15$, high-range water reducer SP).
  - Reinforcing fibers: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$) + brass steel fibers ($V_f = 0.5\ \text{vol.}\%$).
  - Tests: uniaxial direct tensile test (dogbone specimens, displacement control at 0.2 mm/min), compressive strength on 40 mm cubes.

### 2. Bridging stress vs. crack opening displacement ($\sigma-\delta$) curves and multiple fine-crack propagation analysis
- **Area under the ultra-high-energy bridging curve**: The peak bridging stress $\sigma_0 = 22.0\ \text{MPa}$ combined with the critical crack opening $\delta_0 = 0.85\ \text{mm}$ drives the complementary bridging energy ($J_b'$) up to $1250\ \text{J/m}^2$.
- **Ultra-tight crack width ($25\ \mu\text{m}$) and durability**: All 65 cracks open uniformly so that crack widths remain strictly controlled to $25\ \mu\text{m}$ or less even at ultimate load.

- **Overall conclusion**: In this paper Prof. Christopher Leung of HKUST and Prof. Victor C. Li established the micromechanics model for controlling fiber rupture in ultra-high-strength matrices and simultaneously achieved a compressive strength of 160 MPa, a tensile strength of 21.5 MPa, and a tensile elongation of 8.5% — the historic pinnacle landmark original paper in composite materials mechanics and ultra-high-performance cementitious composites.
