# Comprehensive Review of Phase Change Material (PCM) Thermal Energy Storage (TES) Technologies in Building Structures and Concrete, Encapsulation Methods, and Heating/Cooling Peak Load Reduction Mechanisms

## Summary

To drastically reduce building heating and cooling energy consumption and maximize indoor thermal comfort, this landmark review — the highest-authority Energy and Buildings 2013 survey by **Professor Michal Pomianowski and Professor Per Heiselberg of Aalborg University, Denmark, and Professor Yinping Zhang of Tsinghua University** — comprehensively covers **the entire category of phase change material (PCM) thermal energy storage (TES: Thermal Energy Storage) technologies integrated into building envelopes and structural elements (concrete core slabs, wall panels, gypsum boards) and building service systems**, along with PCM classification (organic paraffins/fatty acids vs. inorganic salt hydrates vs. eutectic mixtures), encapsulation methods (direct incorporation, immersion impregnation, micro/macroencapsulation, form-stabilization), thermal conductivity enhancement techniques (metal fins, carbonaceous nano-fillers), numerical simulation models, and **the indoor temperature peak reduction ($2\sim 5\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and cooling power peak shifting (Peak Shifting) mechanisms in real building applications**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of an integrated classification framework for building-integrated PCM thermal energy storage (TES) technologies**:
   - Complete classification of the thermodynamic behavior of passive systems (PCM-impregnated gypsum boards/concrete walls) and active systems (ventilated concrete core slabs, PCM-charged floor heating, HVAC duct units).
2. **Analysis of the advantages/disadvantages and long-term reliability of the four major PCM encapsulation approaches**:
   - Compares leakage risk, mechanical strength degradation, and thermal efficiency of direct incorporation (Direct Incorporation), immersion (Immersion), microencapsulation (Microencapsulation), and form-stabilization (Form-Stabilization).
3. **Elucidation of the thermal-mechanical trade-offs when PCM is incorporated into concrete structural elements**:
   - Elucidates the mechanism whereby increasing PCM incorporation enlarges the combined sensible-latent heat storage capacity while degrading compressive strength, and derives optimal design ratios.
4. **Quantification of building electrical peak load shifting (Peak Shifting) and energy savings**:
   - Demonstrates the cycle economics of reducing cooling loads during daytime peak-temperature hours by $25\sim 40\%$ and releasing the stored energy at night using off-peak electricity or natural outdoor air.

## Methods

- **Scope of analyzed literature and technical domains (Sections 2~5 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Synthesis of a total of 105 key primary studies and demonstration projects.
  - Material families:
    1. Organic: Paraffin wax, fatty acids (Fatty acids), Esters.
    2. Inorganic: salt hydrates ($\text{CaCl}_2\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4\cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
    3. Eutectics: organic-organic and inorganic-inorganic eutectic mixtures.
- **Evaluation by application location (Sections 4~6 & Figs. 1~8)**:
  - Concrete slabs/walls, mortar plasters, ceiling panels, window shutters, PCM ventilation ducts.

### 2. Interpretation of structural thermal transmittance reduction and indoor thermal comfort index (PMV) control mechanisms
- **Maximization of thermal inertia (Thermal Inertia)**: PCM-incorporated concrete slabs deliver 2~3 times the heat capacity of ordinary concrete of equal mass, attenuating the amplitude of the indoor temperature response to outdoor air temperature fluctuations to less than half.
- **Integration with nighttime free-cooling (Free-Cooling)**: PCM that melts to liquid during the day is re-solidified by cool nighttime ventilation ($<18\ ^\circ\text{C}$), regenerating 100% of the next day's daytime cooling-load absorption capacity.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is a landmark review paper that comprehensively consolidates the materials science, encapsulation, heat transfer modeling, and peak load shifting mechanisms of PCM-integrated thermal energy storage in building structures and concrete, providing core textbook-level guidelines for zero-energy building design and smart thermal-storage concrete.
