# Interfacial Bond Behavior and Repair Performance of Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC): Slant Shear Bond Strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$, Tensile Strain Capacity of $8.80\%$, and Monolithic Substrate Failure

## Summary

To prevent interfacial delamination and drying shrinkage cracking in concrete repair and retrofitting while minimizing construction carbon emissions, Professor Lili Kan at USST and Professor Fei Wang at Tongji University evaluated cementless Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC). The eco-friendly composite utilizes ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) and fly ash (FA) activated by an alkaline silicate solution, reinforced with 2.0 vol.% high-strength PE microfibers (**28 d compressive strength $55.0\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial direct tensile strain capacity $8.80\%$, tensile strength $6.50\ \text{MPa}$**). Through slant shear tests, splitting tensile bond tests, interfacial fracture toughness tests, and SEM-EDS elemental mapping, the authors demonstrated a **140 % increase in slant shear bond strength ($28.5\ \text{MPa}$) and a splitting bond strength of $3.45\ \text{MPa}$ over conventional mortar, achieving 100 % substrate failure and 3D aluminosilicate chemical bonding**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First comprehensive quantification of cementless high-ductility EGC concrete bonding**:
   - Characterized interfacial shear, splitting tensile, and flexural bond behavior across multi-axial stress states and ASTM C882 standards.
2. **Attainment of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ slant shear bond strength and 100 % substrate failure**:
   - Unlike conventional repair mortars that suffer adhesive joint failure, EGC developed superior interfacial adhesion that forced failure into the concrete substrate.
3. **Complete absorption of restrained drying shrinkage via 8.80 % tensile ductility**:
   - Dispersed curing shrinkage stresses into over 60 saturated microcracks within the EGC overlay, eliminating interfacial shear stress concentrations.
4. **Alkali chemical etching and nano-interlocking mechanism**:
   - Proved that highly alkaline geopolymer gel slightly etches siliceous aggregate surfaces in the existing concrete, forming strong covalent Si-O-Al bonds verified by EDS.

## Methods

- **Materials and EGC mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Binder: GGBS + FA (GGBS/FA = 1.0, liquid $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{NaOH}$, modulus 1.2, W/B = 0.32).
  - Fine aggregates: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fibers: High-strength PE microfibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, length 12 mm, diameter $24\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 3000 MPa, modulus 110 GPa).
  - Substrate concrete: 28 d compressive strength $42.0\ \text{MPa}$ (sandblasted roughness $R_a = 1.8\ \text{mm}$).
- **Bond testing program (Sections 2.3–2.5 & Figs. 5–8)**:
  - Slant shear test: $100 \times 100 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ prisms ($30^\circ$ interface angle, ASTM C882).
  - Splitting tensile bond: $\phi 100 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ composite cylinders (ASTM C496).
  - Direct tension: Dogbone specimens (0.5 mm/min).

## Results

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cement Mortar (CM)** | **$45.0 \pm 2.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **<0.02 % (Brittle)** | **$11.8 \pm 0.8\ \text{MPa}$** | **$1.20 \pm 0.10\ \text{MPa}$** | **$2.10 \pm 0.15\ \text{MPa}$** | **Smooth interface debonding** |
| **PVA-ECC (Standard)** | $48.5 \pm 2.2\ \text{MPa}$ | $3.50 \pm 0.30\%$ | $21.5 \pm 1.2\ \text{MPa}$ | $2.65 \pm 0.15\ \text{MPa}$ | $4.85 \pm 0.25\ \text{MPa}$ | Mixed interface/substrate failure |

### 2. Interfacial nano-chemical interlocking and shrinkage stress relaxation
- **Geopolymer nano-gel penetration**: Alkaline silicate solution penetrated up to $200\ \mu\text{m}$ into microcracks and pores of the substrate concrete, creating interlocking chemical anchors.
- **Strain-hardening shrinkage relaxation**: The extreme tensile elongation (8.80 %) of EGC completely accommodated restrained shrinkage deformation, eliminating shear debonding stresses.

- **Overall conclusion**: This study demonstrated that high-ductility cementless EGC achieves a slant shear bond strength of 28.5 MPa and 100 % monolithic substrate failure, providing an ideal solution for concrete retrofitting.
