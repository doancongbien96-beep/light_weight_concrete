# Interfacial Bond Mechanisms of Steel and PP Fibers in Geopolymer Composites

## Summary

An experimental study elucidating the interfacial bond mechanisms when micro steel fiber (MSF) and polypropylene fiber (PPF) are added to fly ash geopolymer at 0.5~4 vol %. It shows that the combined effects of fiber contact angle (wettability), nanoscale surface roughness (AFM), and the shrinkage of the geopolymer matrix govern the fiber-matrix interaction and the mechanical properties. Key finding: MSF, with its hydrophilic surface, forms a strong bond that greatly improves energy absorption and flexural strength, whereas hydrophobic PPF couples with matrix shrinkage to cause interfacial debonding, actually degrading the mechanical properties.

## Key Contributions

1. Contact angle measurements: PPF is hydrophobic, going from 98° (30 s) to 91.1° (240 s); MSF is hydrophilic, going from 85.5° (30 s) to 69.3° (240 s). Since geopolymer is a water-based matrix, wettability is the primary factor of bond quality.
2. AFM nanoscale roughness: PPF has grooves 50~75 nm wide and 2 nm high (uniform and smooth); MSF has grooves 200~800 nm wide and 7 nm high (rough, about 3 times as rough). This roughness difference translates into a difference in mechanical anchoring.
3. Shrinkage-bond interaction model (Fig. 10): hydrophobic PPF traps air at the interface in the fresh state, so when the geopolymer shrinks, internal stress enlarges the debonding. For hydrophilic MSF, shrinkage instead acts as clamping pressure and confinement, strengthening the bond over time.
4. Reinforcement efficiency data: maximum flexural strength at 3 % MSF (27 MPa at 7 days, 35 MPa at 56 days); toughness is 25 times that of plain mortar on the d3 basis and 46 times on the d5.5 basis (PPF: 2.5 times and 5 times, respectively). PPF rather reduces the 56-day compressive and flexural strengths.
5. Reverse effect above 3 %: casting and compaction become difficult, increasing porosity and forming weak interfacial contact.

## Methods

Low-calcium FA activated with 16 M NaOH + sodium silicate (2.5:1:0.7 mass ratio, solution/solid 0.5). Oven curing at 65 °C for 24 h, then room temperature. Drying shrinkage (25×25×300 mm prisms, up to 56 days), three-point bending (ASTM C293), compression, toughness (ASTM C1018, d3·d5.5), FESEM-EDS, AFM, static contact angle (30~240 s).

## Results

- MSF: shrinkage almost eliminated (2 % or more), large improvements in flexure and energy absorption, no effect on compressive strength. Failure mechanism: crack branching, deflection, and debonding added on top of bridging and pullout.
- PPF: reduced early workability, reduced density (entrapped air), reduced 56-day strength. Only bridging and pullout operate as failure mechanisms.
- Cause of strength reduction (PPF): low elastic modulus (3.5 GPa) makes local failure near pores easy — behavior similar to air-entrained (foamed) concrete.
