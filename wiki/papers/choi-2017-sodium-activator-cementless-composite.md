# Compressive and Tensile Behavior of Fiber-Reinforced Cementless Composites as Functions of Sodium-Based Activator Combination and Curing Condition

## Summary

This study experimentally compared and evaluated the effects of the combination scheme of sodium-based alkali activators (M1: a combination of caustic alkali and a non-silicate acid salt, $\text{NaOH}+\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ vs M2: sodium silicate $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ 10 % alone) and of the 28-day curing environment (water curing W vs air curing A, 23 °C 60 % RH) on the density, compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, tensile strain capacity, and energy-absorption toughness of highly ductile composites obtained by incorporating high-strength polyethylene (PE) monofilament fibers (1.75 vol.%) into a 100 % ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) cementless binder (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University). The experiments showed that the M2 mix using 10 % sodium silicate promoted the dense formation of hydration products (C-S-H / C-A-S-H gels), exhibiting higher compressive strength (49.6–50.3 MPa) and maximum tensile strength (**8.2 MPa**) than the M1 mix, and that the M2-A (air-cured) specimens achieved an ultra-high tensile strain capacity of up to **5.7 %**. With respect to the curing environment, opposite curing dependencies were identified: the M1 combination improved strength and ductility under air curing, whereas the M2 combination maximized tensile strength (8.2 MPa) and tensile toughness ($0.31\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{mm/mm}$) under water curing.

## Key Contributions

1. **Identification of differences in hydration structure and tensile performance by sodium-based activator type**:
   - **10 % sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$) mix (M2)**: supplying active silica anions maximized the condensation degree of the C-S-H gel, recording a tensile strength of 8.2 MPa and a tensile toughness of $0.31\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{mm/mm}$ — a 37.8 % improvement in tensile performance and 2.3× toughness over M1.
   - **$\text{NaOH}(3 \%) + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(4 \%)$ mix (M1)**: initial reactivity is excellent, but owing to drying-shrinkage effects from the formation of the $\text{C}_4\text{AH}_{13}$ solid phase, tensile strength remained at 4.8–5.8 MPa.
2. **First demonstration of opposite curing dependencies by activator type**:
   - The M1 mix under air curing increased compressive strength (+11.5 %) and tensile strength (+20.8 %) relative to water curing through the rise of internal surface energy accompanying moisture evaporation.
   - The M2 mix under water curing increased tensile strength by 17.1 % through continuous silica polymerization and gel densification, reaching its maximum of 8.2 MPa.
3. **Maximization of the tensile-to-compressive strength ratio ($f_t/f'_c$) (up to 16.4 %)**:
   - Exhibiting a high tensile/compressive strength ratio reaching 1.6 times that of ordinary concrete (about 10 %), all mixes stably maintained saturated multiple fine-crack strain-hardening behavior.

## Methods

- **Materials and binder (Table 1)**:
  - Ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, fineness $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, density $2.92\ \text{g/cm}^3$, $\text{CaO}$ 44.6 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 31.5 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 13.0 %, $\text{MgO}$ 4.9 %, $\text{SO}_3$ 3.4 %).
  - Alkali activators:
    - M1 mix: powder-form $\text{NaOH}$ 3 % + $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ 4 % (replacement on GGBS mass basis).
    - M2 mix: powder-form sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$) 10 % (sole replacement).
- **Reinforcing fiber**: high-strength PE fiber (diameter 31 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa, elongation 3.0 %). Incorporation ratio 1.75 vol.%.
- **Mix design (4 mixes, Table 2)**:
  - **M1-W / M1-A**: GGBS 0.93 + $\text{NaOH}$ 0.03 + $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ 0.04, W/B 0.40, SP 0.007, VMA 0.0003, AF (defoamer) 0.001, PE 1.75 vol.%.
  - **M2-W / M2-A**: GGBS 0.90 + $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ 0.10, W/B 0.40, SP 0.006, VMA 0.0003, AF 0.001, PE 1.75 vol.%.
- **Curing conditions**: after casting, 2 days of air curing (23±3 °C, 60±5 % RH) before demolding.
  - Water curing (W): immersion in a water bath at 23±2 °C for 28 days.
  - Air curing (A): constant temperature/humidity chamber at 23±3 °C, 60±5 % RH for 28 days.
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: KS L 5105 (average of three $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens).
  - Uniaxial direct tension test: JSCE-standard dumbbell specimens (central section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm), 20 kN universal testing machine (displacement control at 0.1 mm/min), two LVDT displacement transducers installed on both sides at 25 mm.

## Results

### 1. 28-day compressive strength and uniaxial tensile behavior

| Mix ID | Activator composition | Curing condition | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | Maximum tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | Tensile/compressive strength ratio ($f_t/f'_c$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M1-W** | $\text{NaOH}+\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ | Water curing | 44.5 ± 2.1 | 4.8 ± 0.4 | 3.2 ± 0.5 | 10.8 % |
| **M1-A** | $\text{NaOH}+\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ | Air curing | 49.6 ± 1.8 | 5.8 ± 0.5 | 4.8 ± 0.6 | 11.7 % |
| **M2-W** | $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ 10 % | Water curing | 49.6 ± 1.5 | **8.2 ± 0.7** | 4.7 ± 0.4 | **16.4 %** |
| **M2-A** | $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ 10 % | Air curing | **50.3 ± 1.4** | 7.0 ± 0.6 | **5.7 ± 0.5** | 13.9 % |

### 2. Energy-absorption toughness under uniaxial tension (Table 3)

| Mix ID | Tensile toughness ($\text{MPa}\cdot\text{mm/mm}$) | Relative toughness index | Failure behavior assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1-W** | 0.13 | 0.43 | Few multiple cracks, early localization |
| **M1-A** | 0.23 | 0.75 | Good ductility at 4.8 % strain |
| **M2-W** | **0.31** | **1.00 (reference)** | **Highest tensile strength and maximum energy dissipation** |
| **M2-A** | 0.29 | 0.94 | Highest elongation (5.7 %) exhibited |

- **Overall analysis**: it was established that adopting sodium silicate as the alkali activator together with appropriate moist/water curing is most advantageous for maximizing tensile strength (8.2 MPa) and toughness in manufacturing cementless composites.
