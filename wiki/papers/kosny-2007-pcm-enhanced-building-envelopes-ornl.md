# ORNL Full-Scale Building Envelope Phase Change Material (PCM) Thermal Insulation System: Guarded Hot-Box Thermal Transmittance, $35\%$ Peak Heat Flux Reduction, and 6-Hour Thermal Time Lag

## Summary

To enhance the thermal storage capacity of lightweight wood-frame and concrete building envelopes and dramatically reduce summer cooling loads, researchers at the Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL, U.S. Department of Energy) conducted experimental evaluations (2003~2006) on building envelopes enhanced with microencapsulated organic phase change materials (paraffin core, melting point $T_m = 25.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat capacity $\Delta H_m = 140.0\ \text{J/g}$). The authors fabricated a **Dynamic Reflective Insulation (DRI, PCM surface density $0.49\ \text{kg/m}^2$) wall system integrating PCM into open-cell polyurethane foam and blown cellulose insulation**. Tested under a large-scale Rotatable Guarded Hot Box (ASTM C1363) and outdoor weather exposure in Tennessee, the system achieved a **$35\%$ reduction in daytime peak heat flux across walls, a 6-hour thermal time lag ($\Delta t_{lag}$), and a $25\%$ reduction in daily cooling energy consumption**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First accredited full-scale validation of PCM-enhanced building envelopes led by ORNL**:
   - Extended beyond bench-scale testing to generate standardized Guarded Hot Box (ASTM C1363) datasets for full-scale $2\times 4$ wood stud and concrete wall assemblies.
2. **Development of hybrid Dynamic Reflective Insulation (DRI) with PCM**:
   - Integrated low-emissivity aluminum foils (reflecting radiant heat) with PCM foams (absorbing conductive/convective heat), shifting static R-value paradigms toward dynamic thermal mass insulation.
3. **Verification of $35\%$ peak heat flux reduction and 6-hour thermal time lag**:
   - Attenuated interior peak heat fluxes by $35\%$ under extreme solar irradiance ($45\ ^\circ\text{C}$ exterior surface temperatures) while shifting peak cooling loads into evening hours.
4. **Field installation techniques for blown cellulose and loose-fill insulation**:
   - Proved practical constructability and long-term thermal cycling stability of blending microencapsulated PCM powders into dry blown insulation.

## Methods

- **Wall configurations and PCM parameters (Sections 2–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Wall dimensions: $2.44 \times 2.44\ \text{m}$ (6-cavity $2\times 4$ wood studs at 16 in. o.c., R-13 fiberglass/cellulose insulation).
  - PCM properties: Organic paraffin microcapsules ($T_m = 25.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $\Delta H_m = 140.0\ \text{J/g}$, PCM application density $0.49\ \text{kg/m}^2$).
  - Layer sequence: Exterior OSB sheathing ($12.7\ \text{mm}$) + DRI-PCM layer + fiberglass batt + interior gypsum board ($12.7\ \text{mm}$).
- **Guarded hot box and outdoor testing (Sections 3–4 & Figs. 4–7)**:
  - Large guarded hot box: ASTM C1363 steady-state and dynamic thermal cycling ($20\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$ daily profiles).
  - Instrumentation: Heat flux transducers (error $<1\%$) and 48-channel Type-T thermocouples.

### 2. Dynamic thermal transmittance and latent heat buffering mechanisms
- **Isothermal phase transition plateau**: As the wall temperature passes $25.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the PCM absorbs latent heat, buffering interior temperature rises and suppressing heat flux spikes.
- **Cooling load shifting**: Shifted the peak cooling demand from 12:00–14:00 to after 19:00, allowing natural night ventilation to dissipate accumulated heat.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark ORNL study provided authoritative full-scale hot box evidence demonstrating a 35% peak heat flux reduction and 6-hour thermal delay in PCM-enhanced building envelopes.
