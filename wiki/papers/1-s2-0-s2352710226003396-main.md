# Durability and environmental evaluation of high-strength lightweight concrete incorporating fly ash cenospheres

## Abstract from PDF

This study investigates the durability and sustainability performance of high-strength lightweight concrete (HSLWC) incorporating fly ash cenospheres (FAC) and supplementary cementitious materials, including silica fume (SF), ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBFS), and nano- silica (nS). Twelve mix designs, with FAC content ranging from 0% to 100%, and varying SCM proportions, were evaluated over 12 months for water absorption, rapid chloride permeability (RCPT), bulk electrical resistivity (BERT), and sulfate resistance. The findings revealed that higher FAC content enhances resistance to chloride and sulfate attacks but increases water ab - sorption due to its intrinsic porosity. Notably, incorporating 10% SF with 60% GGBFS (SF10GS60) or 0.5% nS (SF10nS0.5) significantly improved the durability of the FAC-HSLWC. The SF10GS60 sample reduced the RCPT value by 59.5% and sulfate expansion by 48.8%, while the SF10nS0.5 sample achieved the highest reduction in water absorption (38.2%). SEM indicated ITZ densification and additional C – S – H formation, explaining the improved transport resistance. This study demonstrates that SCM combinations can effectively mitigate porosity in FAC-HSLWC, resulting in a practical, eco-friendly, and resilient material suitable for harsh environmental conditions.
