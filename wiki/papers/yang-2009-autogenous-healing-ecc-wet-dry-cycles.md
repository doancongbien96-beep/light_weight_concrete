## Summary
This landmark original paper by Victor Li's research team at the University of Michigan systematically established the autogenous self-healing behavior of fine cracks in high-ductility composites (PVA-ECC). ECC specimens subjected to pre-tensile strain damage ($\epsilon = 1.0 \sim 3.0 \%$, mean crack width $w < 50 \mu m$) were exposed to repeated wet-dry cycles simulating the actual atmospheric environment ($20 °C$ underwater immersion $24 h$ + $50 \% RH$ ambient drying $24 h$), and the recovery ratios of stiffness and mechanical tensile strain capacity were precisely evaluated through resonant frequency (RF) nondestructive testing and retension testing. It was quantitatively demonstrated for the first time worldwide that fine cracks of $50 \mu m$ or less were completely closed by calcium carbonate crystals, restoring 100 % of the initial elastic modulus and perfectly recovering the retensile strain-hardening envelope.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a resonant frequency (RF) nondestructive recovery index for quantitative evaluation of ECC self-healing**: healing kinetics analysis through continuous nondestructive measurement.
- **Mechanistic establishment of the crack width threshold ($w_{crit} \le 50 \mu m$)**: demonstrated that only cracks of $50 \mu m$ or less restore structural retensile performance perfectly (100 %).
- **Elucidation of the chemical mechanism of self-healing precipitates**: ESEM, XRD, and Raman spectroscopy analyses identified $CaCO_3$ crystallization as the dominant healing mechanism.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash replacement 55 wt %).
- **Test conditions**: imposition of $1.0 \% \sim 3.0 \%$ pre-tensile strain, exposure to 4 to 10 wet-dry cycles.
- **Evaluation**: ASTM C215 resonant frequency, ESEM optical crack observation, uniaxial dogbone retension tests.

## Results
- After 10 wet-dry cycles: dynamic modulus of elasticity recovery ratio of $100 \%$ and perfect restoration of tensile strain capacity to $3.5 \%$.
- Retensioning after healing demonstrated complete interface rebonding, with cracks forming at new locations rather than at existing cracks.
