## Summary
An experimental study that quantitatively evaluated the improvements in compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, flexural modulus of rupture (MOR), flexural toughness, and impact resistance when hooked-end steel fibers are added at volume fractions of 0.5 vol %, 1.0 vol %, 1.5 vol %, and 2.0 vol % to 80 MPa-class high-strength concrete. It was shown that the inherent brittleness of the high-strength matrix is effectively mitigated by the steel fiber bridging action, with flexural strength improved by up to 65 % and splitting tensile strength by up to 95 %, while the number of blows to impact fracture increased more than 8-fold.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of steel fiber reinforcement ratios by volume fraction in a high-strength matrix**: derivation of linear/nonlinear mechanical property increase correlations with increasing steel fiber content.
- **Flexural toughness index evaluation (ASTM C1018 $I_5, I_{10}, I_{20}$)**: confirmed that $V_f = 1.5 \%$ exhibits energy-absorbing toughness beyond fully elasto-plastic behavior.
- **Repeated impact resistance evaluation (ACI 544.2R drop-weight test)**: demonstrated that the initial-crack blow count and final-fracture blow count increased 4.5-fold and 8.2-fold, respectively.

## Methods
- **Materials and mix**: $W/B = 0.30$, cement + silica fume (10 wt %), high-range water reducer, maximum coarse aggregate size 13 mm.
- **Steel fiber**: hooked-end steel fiber (length 30 mm, diameter 0.5 mm, aspect ratio 60, tensile strength 1100 MPa).
- **Test items**: compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, four-point bending test (span 300 mm), drop-weight repeated impact test.

## Results
- Compressive strength: $V_f = 0 \%: 82.5 MPa \rightarrow V_f = 1.5 \%: 95.2 MPa$ (15.4 % increase).
- Splitting tensile strength: $V_f = 0 \%: 4.8 MPa \rightarrow V_f = 1.5 \%: 9.4 MPa$ (95.8 % increase).
- Flexural modulus of rupture: $V_f = 0 \%: 7.2 MPa \rightarrow V_f = 1.5 \%: 11.9 MPa$ (65.3 % increase).
