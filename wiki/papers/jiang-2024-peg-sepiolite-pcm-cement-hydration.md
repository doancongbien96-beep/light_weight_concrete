# 24.14 % Reduction in 3 d Cement Hydration Heat and Carbon-Fiber-Reinforced 45.6 MPa Strength Recovery via Acid-Modified Sepiolite (HSEP)/Polyethylene Glycol (PEG) Composite Phase Change Materials (CPCM)

## Summary

To mitigate early-age thermal cracking from rapid hydration heat in mass concrete and tall buildings while maximizing building envelope latent thermal energy storage, Professor Jinyang Jiang and co-workers at Southeast University developed form-stable composite phase change materials (CPCM). Porous natural sepiolite clay (SEP) was acid-activated with $2.0\ \text{M}\ \text{HCl}$ to maximize specific surface area ($185.2\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$) and pore volume in modified sepiolite (HSEP), followed by vacuum impregnation with polyethylene glycol (PEG 1500), achieving **a melting latent heat enthalpy of $88.9\ \text{J/g}$ and leak-free stability over 100 thermal cycles**. Incorporating CPCM into cement paste (W/C = 0.40; 10 %, 20 %, 30 % replacement) with hybrid carbon fiber (CF: 0.3 %, 0.6 %, 0.9 %) reinforcement, the authors evaluated hydration kinetics via TAM Air isothermal calorimetry ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$), compressive strength, and thermal conductivity. The 30 % CPCM mix (CP30) absorbed hydration heat during the $42\sim 45\ ^\circ\text{C}$ solid-liquid PEG phase transition, **reducing 3 d cumulative hydration heat by 24.14 % from $318.5\ \text{J/g} \rightarrow 241.6\ \text{J/g}$** and delaying the exothermic peak by 3.8 h. Furthermore, adding 0.6 wt.% carbon fibers compensated for the strength loss from soft PCM inclusions, restoring the **28 d compressive strength to $45.6\ \text{MPa}$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of high-capacity CPCM ($88.9\ \text{J/g}$) via acid-modified sepiolite (HSEP)/PEG impregnation**:
   - $2.0\ \text{M}\ \text{HCl}$ leaching removed calcite and quartz impurities, expanding surface area by 4.2-fold and accommodating 60 wt.% PEG loading.
2. **24.14 % reduction in 3 d cumulative hydration heat and delayed exothermic peak**:
   - Isothermal calorimetry proved that reversible latent heat absorption buffers the rapid exothermic reaction of alite ($\text{C}_3\text{S}$).
3. **Restoration of 45.6 MPa compressive strength via 0.6 wt.% carbon fiber bridging**:
   - Reinforced weak matrix zones around soft PCM particles with a 3D carbon fiber network, satisfying 100 % of structural strength requirements.
4. **Leak-free thermal cycling durability (>99 % residual mass over 100 cycles)**:
   - Preserved chemical structure (FTIR) and latent heat capacity (DSC) after 50 h at $65\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and 100 repeated phase change cycles.

## Methods

- **Materials and CPCM synthesis (Section 2 & Tables 1–3)**:
  - Sepiolite carrier: Natural SEP $\rightarrow 2.0\ \text{M}\ \text{HCl}$ acid treatment ($80\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 4 h) $\rightarrow$ HSEP ($185.2\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, pore volume $0.32\ \text{cm}^3/\text{g}$).
  - Phase change material: PEG 1500 (molecular weight 1500, melting point $44.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $156.2\ \text{J/g}$).
  - Vacuum impregnation: Ethanol solution at HSEP : PEG = 40 : 60 mass ratio $\rightarrow$ filtration and drying $\rightarrow$ PHSEP-1 powder.
  - Cement mixes (W/C = 0.40):
    - Control (CP0): 100 % cement.
    - CP10, CP20, CP30: PHSEP-1 replacing cement at 10, 20, 30 wt.%.
    - CP20-0.3CF, CP20-0.6CF, CP20-0.9CF: CP20 with carbon fibers (length 6 mm, diameter $7\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 3500 MPa) added at 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 wt.%.
- **Testing protocols (Section 2.4)**:
  - Thermal properties: TA Q200 DSC ($20\sim 65\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$), NETZSCH STA 449F3 TGA ($30\sim 600\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Hydration calorimetry: TA TAM Air 8-channel microcalorimeter ($20.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 72 h continuous logging).
  - Mechanical and transport: Compression (ISO 679), Hot Disk TPS 2500S thermal conductivity.

### 2. Hydration heat suppression and carbon fiber bridging mechanics
- **Hydration damping**: Exothermic heat during the main acceleration period (12~24 h) triggered latent heat absorption as temperatures approached $44.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, depressing peak core temperatures by $4\sim 6\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Carbon fiber bridging**: Adding 0.6 % CF formed a 3D reinforcing network that redistributed stress concentrations around CPCM particles, recovering compressive strength to 45.6 MPa.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that acid-modified sepiolite/PEG composite PCMs reduce 3 d cement hydration heat by over 24 % while carbon fiber hybridization preserves 45 MPa structural compressive strength.
