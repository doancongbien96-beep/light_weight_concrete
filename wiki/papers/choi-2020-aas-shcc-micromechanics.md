# Micromechanics-Based Ductility Analysis of PE Fiber-Reinforced AAS-SHCC

## Summary

User-group paper (co-authored by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University). Identifies the ultra-high-ductility mechanism of PE fiber-reinforced alkali-activated slag (AAS) composites through experiments combined with micromechanics analysis. At a moderate compressive strength of 36.8 MPa, the composite achieved a tensile strength of 8.5 MPa, a strain capacity of up to 7.5 %, and saturated multiple cracking with a crack width of 59.8 µm; theoretical bridging curves incorporating matrix fracture toughness and interfacial property variability reproduced the measured tensile behavior well.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of AAS-SHCC ultra-high ductility**: tensile strength of 8.5 MPa and strain capacity of 7.5 % at a moderate compressive strength of 36.8 MPa. High ductility demonstrated in combination with the sustainability advantage of roughly 1/7 the CO₂ emissions of Portland-cement systems.
2. **Precise measurement of micromechanics parameters**: directly measured the matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) and fiber-matrix interfacial properties (bond strength, friction) and fed them into bridging-curve simulation.
3. **Saturated multiple cracking with crack-width control**: mean crack width held at 59.8 µm, within the self-healing threshold (100 µm) — demonstrating the suitability of AAS-SHCC from a durability standpoint.
4. **Theory-experiment cross-validation**: theoretical bridging curves accounting for variability in matrix properties and fiber dispersion reproduced the experimental stress-strain behavior, confirming the validity of the micromechanics-based design approach.

## Methods

- Materials: AAS binder (GGBS-based) + PE fibers.
- Micromechanics characterization: matrix fracture toughness; interfacial bond properties derived from fiber pull-out tests.
- Composite testing: compression (ASTM C109 family), direct tension (dumbbell specimens), crack pattern analysis (crack count and width).
- Analysis: bridging curve σ_B(δ) simulation incorporating matrix and interface parameter variability.

## Results

- Compressive strength 36.8 MPa / tensile strength 8.5 MPa / strain capacity up to 7.5 % / mean crack width 59.8 µm (saturated cracking).
- The theoretical bridging curve reproduced the experimental tensile behavior well.
