# Mechanical and Durability Characteristics of Ambient-Cured High-Strength Geopolymer Concrete

## Summary

This study evaluates the mechanical performance and durability of sustainable, high-strength geopolymer concrete cured at ambient temperatures ($20 \sim 25\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $20 \sim 25 °C$) without elevated heat curing. Using a binary precursor system of fly ash (FA) and ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) activated with combined sodium silicate ($Na_2SiO_3$) and sodium hydroxide ($NaOH$), the concrete achieved 28 d compressive strengths of $65 \sim 85\ \text{MPa}$ ($65 \sim 85 MPa$) under ambient curing. The co-formation of N-A-S-H and C-A-S-H gel networks was characterized via XRD, FTIR, and SEM-EDS across activator concentrations and $SiO_2/Na_2O$ molar ratios, alongside quantitative assessments of chloride penetration and acid resistance.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of optimal FA-GGBS replacement ratio for ambient high strength**: GGBS replacement levels of 40 ~ 60 wt % promoted early Ca2+ dissolution, achieving 3 d compressive strengths exceeding 40 MPa ($40 MPa$) under ambient curing.
- **Microstructural characterization of hybrid binder gel networks ($C-(A)-S-H + N-A-S-H$)**: Demonstrated matrix densification and microporosity reduction through dense cross-linked gel formation.
- **Verification of chemical durability**: Demonstrated over 50 % lower mass loss and compressive strength degradation compared to OPC concrete during immersion in 5 % sulfuric acid ($H_2SO_4$) and 5 % hydrochloric acid ($HCl$).

## Methods

- **Materials**: Low-calcium Class F fly ash and GGBS (blending ratios from 0:100 to 100:0), sodium silicate solution ($Ms = 2.0$), $NaOH$ concentrations of 12 M ~ 16 M.
- **Curing conditions**: Ambient air curing ($23 \pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $23 \pm 2 °C$, $60 \% RH$).
- **Test parameters**: Compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, elastic modulus, rapid chloride permeability test (RCPT, ASTM C1202), and drying shrinkage.

## Results

- Optimal mixture (FA 50 % + GGBS 50 %, $NaOH\ 14\ \text{M}$, $NaOH 14 M$): 28 d compressive strength of 78.4 MPa ($78.4 MPa$), flexural strength of 8.5 MPa ($8.5 MPa$), and RCPT charge passed of 650 C ($650 C$, "Very Low" rating).
- While higher GGBS contents accelerate early strength gain, they increase autogenous shrinkage; hence, the 50:50 ratio provides the optimal balance between mechanical strength and crack resistance.
