# Synergic Interaction between Fly Ash and Limestone Powder in Ternary Cements: Carboaluminate Formation and Ettringite Stabilization

## Summary

This foundational paper by De Weerdt et al. from SINTEF/NTNU in Norway investigated interactions between limestone powder and fly ash within ternary blended cements. Limestone powder reacts with AFm and AFt hydration phases, consuming monosulfate to form carboaluminate and stabilize ettringite. Because OPC alone produces limited aluminate hydrates, the effect of limestone alone is moderate; crucially, additional aluminates supplied by fly ash via pozzolanic reactions amplify this effect. Through 21 mix proportions (OPC replacement up to 35 %), mortar compressive and flexural strength testing up to 140 days, and paste TGA/XRD analyses, this synergy was demonstrated. Replacing 5 % fly ash with limestone increased 28-day compressive strength by approximately 8 %, with the enhancement persisting over long durations.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of long-term persistence of ternary (fly ash + limestone) synergy**: The 65 % OPC + 30 % FA + 5 % L mix achieved 41.2 MPa at 28 days, exhibiting highest strength compared to binary mixes with identical total replacement (FA 35 %: 38.3 MPa, L 35 %: 34.4 MPa).
2. **XRD confirmation of carboaluminate mechanism**: In the presence of limestone, formation of monocarboaluminate and hemicarboaluminate (Mc/Hc) instead of monosulfate (Ms) stabilized ettringite (AFt), as directly observed via low-angle XRD (8–13°2θ).
3. **Quantification of aluminate supply role of fly ash**: Elucidated the mechanism where fly ash pozzolanic reaction lowers sulfate-to-aluminate ratio, thereby amplifying limestone carboaluminate formation.
4. **TGA quantitative analysis of bound water (H) and calcium hydroxide (CH)**: Confirmed that 5 % limestone replacement caused reduction in CH per OPC mass (indicating carboaluminate conversion) without decreasing H per dry mass.

## Methods

- Materials: OPC clinker (Blaine 500 m²/kg) + Class F siliceous fly ash (470 m²/kg) + limestone powder (CaCO₃ 81 %, 810 m²/kg). Separately ground in laboratory ball mill; clinker was ground with 3.7 % gypsum.
- Mix matrix (Table 2): 100 % OPC control + limestone binary 5–35 % (mix 2–8) + fly ash binary 5–35 % (mix 9–15) + combination 35 % (mix 16–21).
- Mortar: EN 196-1 (W/B 0.50, binder:sand = 1:3), 40×40×160 mm prisms, cured in saturated Ca(OH)₂ water (20 °C).
- Paste: Vacuum mixer, W/B 0.50, sealed curing followed by hydration stoppage via isopropanol/ether solvent exchange at 1, 28, 90, and 140 days.
- Analysis: Mettler Toledo TGA/SDTA851 (50 mg, 30→980 °C @20 °C/min, N₂ 50 ml/min), PANalytical X'Pert Pro MPD XRD (Cu Kα).

## Results

- Compressive strength development by major mix proportions (Table 3 summary):

| Mix (OPC/FA/L) | 1 d | 28 d | 90 d | 140 d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100/0/0 | 22.9 | 47.5 | 56.5 | 58.7 |
| 65/0/35 | 14.2 | 34.4 | 37.5 | 37.2 |
| 65/35/0 | 12.3 | 38.3 | 54.4 | 60.2 |
| **65/30/5** | 13.2 | **41.2** | **56.9** | **61.1** |
| 70/30/0 | 14.1 | 41.4 | 55.1 | 59.9 |

- When replacing 5 % out of 35 % fly ash with limestone, compressive strength increased by +8 % at 28 days, +5 % at 90 days, and +2 % at 140 days. Significant improvement up to 90 days and equivalent performance at 140 days.
- The 65 % OPC + 30 % FA + 5 % L mix demonstrated equivalent strength across all ages compared to 70 % OPC + 30 % FA mix → an additional 5 % OPC can be replaced with limestone.
- Bound water (H): Ternary combination mixes showed higher H after 28 days than binary mixes. Maximum occurred at FA 25 % + L 10 %.
- CH content: FA 30 % + L 5 % exhibited lowest CH among ternary mixes compared to FA 35 % alone → confirmed consumption via carboaluminate conversion.
- DTG curves: In mixes containing limestone, the peak above 100 °C (AFt decomposition) enlarged and a single peak around 180 °C (AFm phase) became pronounced.
