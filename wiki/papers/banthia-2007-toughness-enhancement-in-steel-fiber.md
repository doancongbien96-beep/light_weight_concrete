# Maximizing flexural toughness of concrete through diameter-based steel fiber hybridization and post-crack strength (PCS) evaluation

## Summary

A landmark hybrid-fiber-composite origin paper by Prof. Nemkumar Banthia of the University of British Columbia (UBC), Canada, which systematically evaluated compressive strength, four-point flexural strength (MOR), the JSCE flexural toughness factor ($\sigma_b$), and post-crack equivalent flexural strength (PCS: Post-Crack Strength) in a 35 MPa-class structural concrete matrix. Large-diameter crimped steel fibers (Crimped Steel Fiber, diameter 0.80 mm, length 38 mm) are widely used in the field thanks to their excellent workability and mixing dispersion, but their low number density per unit volume makes them inefficient at developing flexural toughness. To overcome this limit, smaller-diameter crimped steel fibers of identical geometry (diameters 0.40 mm and 0.45 mm, length 38 mm) were substituted stepwise within constant total fiber volume fractions ($V_f = 0.50\ \%, 0.75\ \%$) — that is, hybridized. Hybrid composites replacing 25–50 % of the large-diameter fiber with small-diameter fiber fully preserved workability and uniform dispersion free of balling while raising the flexural toughness index and residual load-carrying capacity over the large-diameter-only mix by **up to 70–120 %**. The study demonstrated that this toughness enhancement arises from a multi-stage bridging mechanism combining delayed propagation of early microcracks, driven by the higher number density of the small fibers ($N_f \propto 1/d_f^2$), with large-deformation bridging provided by the stiff anchoring of the large-diameter fibers.

## Key Contributions

1. **Identification of the toughness synergy of large/small-diameter crimped steel fiber hybridization**:
   - At an identical fiber volume ($V_f = 0.50\ \%$), replacing 50 % of the 0.80 mm large-diameter fiber with 0.40 mm small-diameter fiber increased the number of bridging fibers per unit cross-sectional area by 2.5×, markedly reducing the post-initial-cracking load drop and improving residual flexural strength by more than 80 %.
2. **Establishment and quantification of the Post-Crack Strength (PCS) method**:
   - Established a standard material-design parameter framework that excludes pre-peak elastic energy from the structural load–deflection curve and converts the effective plastic energy absorbed in each deflection band ($L/150\sim L/3000$) into an equivalent flexural stress (PCS).
3. **Derivation of an optimal fiber combination considering workability and economy**:
   - Presented an optimal mixing guideline combining economical large-diameter fiber with high-performance small-diameter fiber at 50:50, preventing both the abrupt slump loss and the balling problems that arise in fine-fiber-only mixes.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and concrete matrix mix (unit contents $\text{kg/m}^3$, Table 1)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (Type I) $355\ \text{kg/m}^3$, mixing water $160\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (W/C = 0.45).
  - Coarse aggregate: 10 mm crushed stone $1070\ \text{kg/m}^3$; fine aggregate: river sand $815\ \text{kg/m}^3$; 28-day compressive strength 35 MPa.
  - Reinforcing fibers (three types of deformed crimped steel fiber, length $L_f = 38\ \text{mm}$, Table 2):
    - Large-diameter fiber: diameter $d_f = 0.80\ \text{mm}$ (aspect ratio 47.5, tensile strength 1050 MPa).
    - Small-diameter fiber A: diameter $d_f = 0.45\ \text{mm}$ (aspect ratio 84.4, tensile strength 1100 MPa).
    - Small-diameter fiber B: diameter $d_f = 0.40\ \text{mm}$ (aspect ratio 95.0, tensile strength 1150 MPa).
  - Mix matrix: at $V_f = 0.50\ \%$ and $0.75\ \%$, large/small-diameter blends of (100:0, 75:25, 50:50, 25:75, 0:100), 11 mixes in total.
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: $\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylindrical specimens (ASTM C39, 7-day water curing).
  - Four-point bending: $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 350\ \text{mm}$ prismatic beams (ASTM C1018, span 300 mm, closed-loop servo-controlled displacement rate $0.1\ \text{mm/min}$, LVDT deflection measurement).
  - JSCE-SF4 flexural toughness factor ($\sigma_b$) and PCS equivalent strength determined ($L/150 = 2.0\ \text{mm}$ deflection criterion).

### 2. Hybrid bridging synergy analysis
- **Multi-stage stress transfer**: small-diameter fibers ($d_f = 0.40\ \text{mm}$), with their high surface area and number density, take charge of rapid load transfer at early crack opening ($w < 0.5\ \text{mm}$), while large-diameter fibers ($d_f = 0.80\ \text{mm}$) maintain pull-out resistance at large deformation ($w > 1.0\ \text{mm}$) as the crimped anchors plastically deform.
- **Constructability secured**: the 50:50 hybrid stably secures 80–85 % of the toughness of the fine-fiber-only mix while eliminating the viscosity increase and balling risk seen when 0.40 mm fiber is used alone.

- **Overall conclusion**: demonstrated that diameter-based hybridization of steel fiber is the most efficient fiber-reinforcement engineering technique for dramatically improving the weak flexural toughness of large-diameter-fiber concrete — by more than 65 % — without sacrificing construction workability or economy.
