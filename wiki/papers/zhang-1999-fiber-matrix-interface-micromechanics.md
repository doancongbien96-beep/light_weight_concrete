## Summary
A core classic paper establishing the theoretical and experimental methodology for precisely measuring and back-calculating fiber-matrix interface characteristics (chemical fracture energy $G_d$, frictional shear bond stress $\tau_0$, slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$, and snubbing coefficient $f$), the basic inputs of micromechanical crack bridging analysis of short-fiber reinforced cementitious composites, through the single fiber pullout test. To prevent premature fiber rupture caused by the high chemical bonding of PVA fibers, an oil coating agent was applied to the fiber surface, and the variation behavior of $G_d$ and $\tau_0$ with oil content (0.1 ~ 1.2 wt %) was quantified, completing the optimal interface design criteria of ECC.

## Key Contributions
- **Computational back-calculation algorithm for single fiber pullout load-displacement curves developed**: $G_d$, $\tau_0$, and $\beta$ determined independently from three-stage curves of elastic debonding, chemical bond breakage, and frictional pullout.
- **Optimal thickness of PVA fiber surface oil coating derived**: applying a 0.8 wt % oil coating reduced the chemical bond $G_d$ from $4.5 J/m2$ to $1.2 J/m2$, preventing fiber rupture and inducing full pullout.
- **Snubbing coefficient ($f$) quantified through inclined pullout**: load amplification rates measured against inclination angle $\theta$, deriving $f = 0.5 \sim 0.8$.

## Methods
- Precision micro pullout testing machine (displacement control $0.01 mm/s$).
- Single PVA fibers (diameter 39 µm) embedded in a fine cement matrix at embedded lengths $L_e = 1 \sim 6 mm$.

## Results
- Optimal oil coating (0.8 wt %): $\tau_0 = 2.2 MPa$, $G_d = 1.5 J/m2$, $\beta = 0.05$, $f = 0.65$.
- Through these interface parameters, stable uniaxial tensile ductility above $4.5 \%$ was realized in PVA-ECC.
