# Variation sources when comparing concrete carbonation test results: CO₂ concentration, RH, preconditioning, and the case for measurement standardization

## Summary

A 2009 origin paper in the ASCE Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering by a joint team of
Prof. Paulo Helene (University of São Paulo, Brazil) and Prof. Pedro Castro-Borges (CINVESTAV,
Mexico): service-life prediction for carbonation-induced rebar corrosion in tropical concrete
structures is undermined by radically divergent accelerated carbonation test conditions across
institutions worldwide — CO₂ concentrations $3\sim 100\%$, relative humidity $50\sim 80\%$,
temperatures $20\sim 40\ ^\circ\text{C}$, preconditioning periods $7\sim 84$ days, and differing
depth-measurement methods make direct comparison impossible. The authors systematically analyzed
the problem, built Table 1 cataloguing conditions from over 25 published studies, demonstrated
that depth scatter reaches **over 300 %** across the CO₂ ($3\sim 100\%$) and RH ($50\sim 80\%$)
ranges, identified **ten core sources of variation**, and proposed a unified standard protocol
based on RILEM TC 116 preconditioning and EN 13295:2004.

## Key Contributions

1. **Meta-database of accelerated-carbonation conditions from 25+ studies (Table 1)**:
   seven items — CO₂ concentration, RH, temperature, specimen size, curing duration,
   preconditioning method, and measurement technique — compared side by side, quantitatively
   proving inter-study incomparability.
2. **Ten sources of variation systematically classified**: (1) CO₂ concentration, (2) RH,
   (3) temperature, (4) preconditioning/drying method, (5) curing period, (6) specimen
   size/shape, (7) sealed-face count, (8) phenolphthalein indicator concentration, (9) depth-
   measurement method, (10) correlation with natural carbonation.
3. **Product-transformation mechanism at high CO₂ (> 10 %) elucidated**: at $3\sim 5\%$ CO₂,
   calcite crystals resembling natural carbonation form; at $50\sim 100\%$, metastable polymorphs
   (vaterite, aragonite) appear, altering microstructure.
4. **Unified standard test protocol proposed with round-robin recommendation**:
   CO₂ = $3\sim 5\%$, RH = $65 \pm 5\%$, T = $23 \pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, RILEM TC 116-based
   preconditioning (equilibration at 75 % RH).

## Methods

- **Literature survey and meta-analysis (Table 1)**:
  - Conditions of 25+ accelerated carbonation studies published 1992–2006 compared on seven items.
  - CO₂ range: $3\%$ (Isaia 1995) to $100\%$ (Nepomuceno 1992; Kazmierczak 1995).
  - RH range: $50\%$ to $80\%$; temperatures $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Depth-measurement technique comparison (Fig. 3–4)**:
  - Phenolphthalein spray (0.1–1 % ethanol solution; color change at pH ≈ 9.0).
  - Need to report both mean depth ($\bar{e}$) and maximum depth ($e_{max}$).
  - XRD and TGA as complementary CaCO₃ quantification methods.
- **RILEM TC 116 preconditioning protocol**:
  - After water curing, pre-drying at $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$ → equilibrated drying at $75 \pm 2\%$
    RH, $20 \pm 1\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (uniform moisture distribution).

## Results

### 1. Worldwide variation ranges of test conditions (Table 1, excerpt)

| Study (year) | Specimen (mm) | CO₂ (%) | RH (%) | Temperature ($^\circ$C) | Curing | Preconditioning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nepomuceno (1992) | $20 \times 55 \times 80$ | **100 %** | 50~70 | 20 | 1~28 d water/air | 70 d lab drying |
| Isaia (1995) | $\phi 100 \times 100$ | **10 %** | 60~70 | 24~28 | 7 d water | 21 d lab drying |
| Seidler (1999) | $40 \times 40 \times 40$ | **5 %** | 68 | 21 | 28 d water | 7 d chamber drying |
| Heiyantuduwa (2006) | $100 \times 100 \times 400$ | **4 %** | 55~65 | 20 | 28 d water | 14 d drying |

### 2. Depth scatter by variation factor
- **CO₂ effect**: versus natural air (~0.04 %), 5 % CO₂ accelerates depth 10–15x preserving the
  $\sqrt{t}$ law; at 100 % microstructure transforms so linear extrapolation of acceleration fails.
- **RH effect**: optimum rate at RH = 50–70 % (balance between CO₂ diffusion and pore moisture);
  above 80 % pore blockage sharply cuts diffusion.
- **Preconditioning is decisive**: insufficient drying leaves internal moisture and underestimates
  depth; excessive drying (105 °C) distorts microstructure.

- **Overall conclusion**: this ASCE origin paper systematically identified the ten variation
  sources making worldwide accelerated carbonation results incomparable and proposed a unified
  standard protocol (CO₂ $3\sim 5\%$, RH $65 \pm 5\%$), advancing international
  standardization of concrete durability assessment.
