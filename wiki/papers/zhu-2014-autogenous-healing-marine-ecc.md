## Summary
A study evaluating the fine crack autogenous healing mechanisms and chloride penetration blocking efficiency of ductile composites (PVA-ECC) exposed to marine salt environments (artificial seawater immersion and intertidal wet-dry cycling). Magnesium ($Mg^{2+}$), sulfate ($SO_4^{2-}$), and carbonate ($CO_3^{2-}$) ions in seawater react with leached cement hydrates inside cracks, forming composite precipitate crystals of magnesium hydroxide (brucite, $Mg(OH)_2$), calcite ($CaCO_3$), and ettringite. This poorly soluble crystal membrane seals cracks completely; after 28 d of seawater healing the chloride diffusion rate was demonstrated to fall to the level of uncracked concrete.

## Key Contributions
- **Brucite-calcite dual self-healing precipitation membrane unique to marine environments clarified**: chemical mechanism interpreted whereby $Mg(OH)_2$ crystals rapidly block crack entrances under seawater immersion.
- **Complete recovery of water-tightness and salt resistance under marine intertidal wet-dry cycles**: chloride penetration blocked by 98 % within 14 d in specimens with crack widths of at most $40 \mu m$.
- **Retensile mechanical performance preserved**: stable behavior maintained without loss of tensile strength or ductility upon re-tensioning after seawater exposure.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash 55 wt %).
- **Exposure conditions**: ASTM D1141 standard artificial seawater immersion and intertidal wet-dry cycling ($12 h$ seawater + $12 h$ air).
- **Test program**: optical crack closure observation, SEM-EDS compositional analysis of crystal precipitates, silver nitrate chloride penetration depth measurement, re-tension tests.

## Results
- After 28 d seawater healing: crack closure rate $98.5 \%$, with chloride penetration depth suppressed below $10 mm$.
- $Mg^{2+}$ ions in seawater were proven to accelerate internal crack healing by a factor of 1.3 relative to fresh water.
