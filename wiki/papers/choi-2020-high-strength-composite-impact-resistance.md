# High-Velocity Projectile Impact Resistance and NDRC Model Verification of High-Strength Fiber-Reinforced Composites by Fiber Type and Content

## Summary

To protect protective structures against terrorism, explosion, and high-velocity projectile impact loads, three composites (M-S0.3, M-S1.5, M-H1.5) were fabricated by reinforcing a 140–150 MPa-class ultra-high-strength cement matrix (W/B 0.19, silica fume 20 % replacement) with straight steel fibers and highly ductile polyethylene (PE) fibers singly and in hybrid form, then subjected to uniaxial direct tension testing and gas-gun-based high-velocity projectile impact testing (projectile mass 66.1 g, caliber 25 mm, impact velocity 217 m/s, kinetic energy **1,556 J**) to quantify penetration depth, frontal spalling, rear-face scabbing, mass loss rate, and verification against the NDRC defense design formula (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University; co-authors Prof. Gyu-Yong Kim, Chungnam National University, and Sang-Gyu Lee, Agency for Defense Development). The specimen reinforced with 0.3 % steel fiber (M-S0.3) failed by complete perforation, whereas the specimens with 1.5 % steel fiber (M-S1.5) and the hybrid of 1.0 % steel + 0.5 % PE fiber (M-H1.5) fully captured the projectile without perforation at penetration depths of **9.60–9.61 mm** despite being thin panels only 40 mm thick; the hybrid composite minimized its major frontal damage area to 4.3 % and increased its tensile strain capacity by 85 % to **0.87 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of the synergistic effect of steel–PE fiber hybrid reinforcement (M-H1.5) under high-velocity impact**:
   - Combining the high local penetration resistance of steel fibers (high elastic modulus, 200 GPa) with the flexible tensile bridging energy of PE fibers (tensile strength 2700 MPa, elongation 3.0 %) increased the tensile strain capacity to **0.87 %, an 85 % improvement over M-S1.5 (0.47 %)**, and reduced the frontal impact damage area by **28 %** (4.3 % vs 6.0 %).
2. **Quantification of failure modes under high-velocity impact (1,556 J)**:
   - M-S0.3: complete perforation, rear-face crater formation, and radial perforation cracks; mass loss rate 5.28 %.
   - M-S1.5: completely free of perforation, penetration depth 9.61 mm, rear scabbing suppressed; mass loss rate **0.97 % (an 82 % reduction)**.
   - M-H1.5: completely free of perforation, penetration depth 9.60 mm, frontal spalling minimized.
3. **Comparative verification against the modified NDRC (National Defense Research Committee) protection formula**:
   - Against the perforation limit thickness ($h_e = 40.7\ \text{mm}$) and the rear-face spall limit thickness ($h_s = 72.5\ \text{mm}$) predicted by the concrete compressive-strength-based NDRC theoretical formula, perforation was fully blocked in the actual 40 mm panel, quantitatively proving the enhanced shock-energy dissipation provided by fiber bridging.

## Methods

- **Matrix mix proportion (mass ratios relative to binder, Table 1)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement 0.80, silica fume (SF, specific surface $7.05\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{SiO}_2 > 96\ \%$) 0.20 (W/B 0.19).
  - Silica flour filler ($d_{50} = 2.2\ \mu\text{m}$) 0.24, No. 7 silica sand ($d_{50} = 0.5\ \text{mm}$) 0.88.
  - Expansive admixture (EA) 0.075, shrinkage-reducing admixture (SRA) 0.010, superplasticizer (SP) 0.018, antifoaming agent (AF) 0.0007.
- **Reinforcing fiber specifications (Table 1)**:
  - Straight micro steel fiber: diameter 200 µm, length 16.5 mm, tensile strength 2500 MPa, elastic modulus 200 GPa.
  - High-strength PE fiber: diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa.
  - Mix group: M-S0.3 (steel fiber 0.3 vol.%), M-S1.5 (steel fiber 1.5 vol.%), M-H1.5 (steel fiber 1.0 % + PE 0.5 vol.%).
- **Test equipment and conditions**:
  - Compressive strength: KS L 5105 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens, 3 per mix).
  - Uniaxial direct tension: three JSCE dumbbell specimens (central section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gage length 80 mm), 20 kN universal testing machine.
  - High-velocity projectile impact test: gas-gun system fitted with a four-edge fully clamped jig, hemispherical metal projectile (mass 66.1 g, caliber 25 mm, impact velocity 217 m/s, $E_k = 1556\ \text{J}$), $200\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}$ panel specimens.
  - Damage assessment: frontal/rear damage area ratios (%) via high-resolution digital image processing, mass loss rate (%), penetration depth measured with a precision gauge.

## Results

### 1. 28-day compressive strength and uniaxial direct tensile properties

| Mix ID | Fiber composition | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | First-cracking strength (MPa) | Peak tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M-S0.3** | steel fiber 0.3 vol.% | 139 ± 4.2 | 8.7 ± 0.6 | 8.7 ± 0.6 | 0.01 % (strain-softening) |
| **M-S1.5** | steel fiber 1.5 vol.% | **146 ± 3.8** | 10.4 ± 0.8 | **17.0 ± 1.1** | 0.47 ± 0.05 % |
| **M-H1.5** | steel 1.0 % + PE 0.5 % | 145 ± 4.1 | 10.1 ± 0.7 | 16.5 ± 0.9 | **0.87 ± 0.08 % (+85 %)** |

### 2. Impact failure behavior and damage quantification under high-velocity projectile (1,556 J) (Table 3)

| Mix ID | Impact failure mode | Penetration depth (mm) | Frontal major damage area (%) | Rear major damage area (%) | Mass loss rate (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M-S0.3** | **complete perforation** | perforated (> 40 mm) | 6.0 % | 28.8 % | 5.28 % |
| **M-S1.5** | **perforation-free capture** | **9.61 mm** | 6.4 % | 25.1 % | **0.97 % (−82 %)** |
| **M-H1.5** | **perforation-free capture** | **9.60 mm** | **4.3 % (−28 %)** | 30.7 % (energy dissipation) | 2.92 % |

### 3. Comparison with NDRC defense formula predictions (Table 4)

| Assessment item | NDRC formula prediction | Measured values, M-S1.5 and M-H1.5 | Protection performance verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Penetration depth ($x$)** | 11.2 mm | **9.60–9.61 mm** | 14 % better penetration resistance than predicted |
| **Perforation limit thickness ($h_e$)** | 40.7 mm | **no perforation at 40.0 mm thickness** | perforation fully blocked below the theoretical limit thickness |
| **Rear-face spall limit thickness ($h_s$)** | 72.5 mm | **rear scabbing suppressed at 40.0 mm** | rear ejecta minimized by fiber bridging |

- **Overall conclusion**: even under ultra-high-velocity impact energies above 1,500 J, the 1.5 vol.% fiber-reinforced high-strength composites defended against perforation 100 % with only a 40 mm thin panel, and the steel–PE hybrid combination proved most effective at minimizing surface damage while absorbing energy ductilely.
