# PSH Design in Cementitious Composites (RILEM HPFRCC Workshop)

## Summary

A comprehensive literature compilation of the experimental tensile characteristics of continuous aligned (CA) and single random (SR) fiber-reinforced cementitious composites achieving PSH, compared against predictions of the University of Michigan U-M model (fracture mechanics plus a bridging stress–crack opening relation). It addresses issues specific to SR systems (orientation effects, two-way debonding at high V_f) and presents a design procedure based on the K-c fracture mechanism map. One of the first documents to formalize the design philosophy of the U-M ECC program.

## Key Contributions

1. Database of PSH achievement cases (Table 1): compiles σ_mu/σ_mc/σ_cu and ε_mu/ε_mc/ε_cu for steel fibers, carbon fibers, PP, glass strands, FRDSP, SIFCON, etc. CA systems reach strengths up to tens of times that of the matrix (V_f 12 % carbon fibers); SR is exceptionally strong in the FRDSP and SIFCON cases.
2. Strain capacity conclusions: failure strains are similar for CA and SR. In SR systems, ε_mc can reach 10–30 times that of the matrix and ε_cu 30–60 times — a one-to-two order-of-magnitude improvement when multiple cracking is well designed.
3. U-M model validation: compares ACK (CA) and U-M (SR) model predictions vs experiments, explicitly noting the uncertainty arising from unreported snubbing coefficients.
4. Identification of SR-specific issues:
   - Orientation angle effect (snubbing): pull-out resistance increases by up to 2×. However, under inclined pull-out the matrix may spall (plug pull-out, cone delamination), causing early load loss — Figs. 5–7.
   - Two-way debonding: dominant at high aspect ratio and high V_f; the bridging load can drop by up to 100 %. SIFCON (5–20 %) lies in the two-way-dominated regime (Fig. 10).
   - Fiber flexural rupture: for low-rupture-strain fibers (carbon, glass), the bridging stress decreases as the inclination angle increases (Fig. 8).
5. K-c mechanism map (Fig. 11) and critical fiber volume fraction design chart (Fig. 12): design so that the bonding-strength–aspect-ratio combination sits above the V_f,crit line and below the fiber rupture line. For Spectra fibers (τ ≈ 1 MPa, aspect ratio 334), predicts V_f = 3 % — multiple cracking confirmed experimentally.

## Methods

Comprehensive literature data compilation plus comparison with U-M model (ACK extension) predictions. Where parameters went unreported, the use of educational estimates is explicitly stated.

## Results

- High performance emerges from the combination of binder technology and fiber technology. A high-strength binder alone does not improve strain capacity.
- For crack-prohibited applications such as leakage-proof water structures, first-cracking strength and strain serve as the design indices; for crack-tolerant applications, ultimate strength and strain do.
