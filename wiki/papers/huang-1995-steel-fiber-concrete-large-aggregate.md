# Mechanical Properties, Flexural Toughness Indices, and Fatigue Behavior of Steel Fiber-Reinforced Concrete (SFRC) Containing Large Coarse Aggregate ($d_{max} = 40\text{ mm}$)

## Summary

To improve the construction economics of heavy-duty port wharves, container yards, and airport runway pavements utilizing large-aggregate mass concrete ($d_{max} = 40\ \text{mm}$ crushed stone), Professor Huang Chenkui and Academician Zhao Guofan at Dalian University of Technology investigated the effects of melt-extracted carbon steel fibers (lengths 25, 35, 45 mm, aspect ratios $L_f/d_f = 43, 60, 77$, dosages $V_f = 0.5\sim 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) and aggregate size gradings (medium stone $20\sim 40\ \text{mm}$ : small stone $5\sim 20\ \text{mm}$) on compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, ultimate flexural strength, ASTM C1018 toughness indices ($I_5, I_{10}, I_{20}, I_{30}$), and **cyclic flexural fatigue endurance ($S\text{-}N$ curves and $2 \times 10^6$ cycle fatigue limit)** across over 400 specimens and full-scale field casting at Dalian Harbour. When combining a 6:4 medium-to-small coarse aggregate ratio with fiber aspect ratios $\ge 60$ ($L_f \ge 35\ \text{mm}$), large-aggregate ($40\ \text{mm}$) SFRC achieved reinforcement efficiencies equivalent to standard $20\ \text{mm}$ small-aggregate SFRC. Incorporating $1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$ steel fibers increased splitting tensile strength by **58 %**, amplified ultimate flexural strength by **75 % (to 8.5 MPa)**, and increased the $2 \times 10^6$ cycle flexural fatigue endurance ratio by **54.5 % (from $0.55 \rightarrow 0.85$)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Optimal grading design for large-aggregate ($d_{max} = 40\ \text{mm}$) SFRC**:
   - Optimized the coarse aggregate ratio (medium stone $20\sim 40\ \text{mm}$ to small stone $5\sim 20\ \text{mm}$ at 6:4) to enable uniform fiber distribution without balling while preserving workability (slump 30~50 mm).
2. **Predictive flexural strength formulation based on fiber geometry**:
   - Established a regression formula ($f_{cf} = 0.97 f_r V_m + 3.4 V_f (L_f/d_f)$) incorporating fiber volume fraction and aspect ratio, calibrated with aggregate correction factors $\alpha_f$ for $40\ \text{mm}$ crushed stone matrices.
3. **Enhancement of ASTM C1018 flexural toughness indices ($I_{10}, I_{20}, I_{30}$)**:
   - Converted brittle matrix failure into plastic energy absorption, achieving $I_{10} = 8.5\sim 9.2$ and $I_{30} = 22.5\sim 25.0$ at $V_f = 1.5\%$.
4. **Field application at Dalian Harbour 100,000-ton container wharf and 85 % fatigue limit**:
   - For 40 mm aggregate SFRC, the $2 \times 10^6$ cycle repeated flexural fatigue limit ratio reached 0.85 (vs 0.55 in plain concrete), enabling a 35 % reduction in pavement thickness.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mix proportions (Section 2 & Tables)**:
  - Cement: Grade 425 Ordinary Portland Cement (W/C = 0.50).
  - Fine aggregate: Medium river sand (Fineness Modulus 2.6).
  - Coarse aggregate (Crushed limestone, $d_{max} = 40\ \text{mm}$):
    - Medium stone: $20\sim 40\ \text{mm}$ (fraction 40 %, 50 %, 60 %, 100 %).
    - Small stone: $5\sim 20\ \text{mm}$ (fraction 60 %, 50 %, 40 %, 0 %).
  - Steel fibers (QingAn melt-extracted carbon steel, density $7.8\ \text{g/cm}^3$):
    1. Type I: $L_f = 25\ \text{mm}$, aspect ratio $L_f/d_f = 43$.
    2. Type II: $L_f = 35\ \text{mm}$, aspect ratio $L_f/d_f = 60$.
    3. Type III: $L_f = 45\ \text{mm}$, aspect ratio $L_f/d_f = 77$.
  - Fiber volume fractions: $V_f = 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$.
- **Testing program (Figure 1)**:
  - Static flexure: $150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm} \times 550\ \text{mm}$ prisms (four-point loading, clip-gauge displacement control).
  - Splitting tension: $150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm}$ cubes.
  - Flexural fatigue: 250 kN hydraulic pulsating fatigue frame (frequency 5 Hz, stress ratio $R = 0.1$, run-out limit $2 \times 10^6$ cycles).

### 2. Bridging mechanics and fatigue damage attenuation
- **Overcoming aggregate size effects**: In $40\ \text{mm}$ coarse aggregate matrices, long fibers ($45\ \text{mm}$) form 3D bridging networks in paste layers surrounding aggregates, engaging immediately when cracks deviate around aggregate boundaries.
- **Repeated fatigue endurance**: Fiber bridging restrained progressive crack widening under cyclic loading, reducing the accumulation rate of residual plastic deformation by 70 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the aggregate grading design, mechanical properties, and fatigue endurance of steel fiber reinforced concrete containing $40\ \text{mm}$ large aggregates for port and pavement infrastructure.
