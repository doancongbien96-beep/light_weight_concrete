# Comparison of flexural behavior in the negative moment zone between steel-UHPC and steel-normal-concrete composite beams

## Summary

A study comparing four steel-concrete composite beam specimens under four-point loading with deck material (UHPC versus normal concrete C50) and stud arrangement (single row versus double row) as variables, in the negative moment region where the deck is in tension (bibliographic data confirmed from the paper body). It quantified that UHPC decks raise cracking loads by 102~135 % and greatly improve overall stiffness and ductility ratio, and proposed a simplified calculation formula for negative-moment flexural capacity that accounts for the UHPC tensile contribution through an equivalent tensile strength coefficient and additionally considers the degree of shear connection.

## Key Contributions

- Proposal of a flexural capacity formula (Mu,2) reflecting the UHPC tensile contribution reduced through an equivalent tensile strength coefficient k=20 lf/h (upper bound 0.8; lf is fiber length, h is beam depth), and a further formula (Mu,3) adding the shear connection degree γ=nNvc/(Art fsd+kAc fc) based on GB 50917. Final error within 5 %.
- Separated effect of increasing stud count: improvements in cracking load, stiffness, and ultimate capacity were marginal, but the ductility ratio rose sharply at NC +115 % and UHPC +62 % — demonstrating that shear connection degree is the governing factor for ductility.
- Quantification of interface slip behavior: slip onset load was about 20 % of the ultimate load in the UHPC composite beam versus about 5 % in the NC beam — the high tensile strength of UHPC and steel-UHPC friction dominate interface bonding.

## Methods

- Four specimens: NC-1 (C50 + single row), NC-2 (C50 + double row), UHPC-1 (UHPC + single row), UHPC-2 (UHPC + double row). Q235 H-section (flange 125×9 mm, web 107×6.5 mm), length 2 m, calculated span 1.8 m, studs 16 mm diameter × 70 mm height, deck section 300×90 mm × 1900 mm length.
- Deck reinforcement: four longitudinal HPB400 bars of 8 mm diameter (reinforcement ratio 0.67 %), stirrups of the same material @100 mm. HRB400 rebar yield 427.7 MPa, H-section yield 290 MPa.
- Materials: normal concrete compressive strength 52 MPa, UHPC compressive strength 123 MPa (96 h steam curing).
- Loading: four-point loading with deck facing down forming a 40 cm pure flexure zone at midspan, displacement control 0.5 mm/min (2 mm/min after the critical point). Instrumentation: deflection at 5 points, interface slip at 4 points, 15 strain gauges on the midspan section plus 2 rebar gauges.

## Results

- Cracking loads: UHPC-1 49.25 kN versus NC-1 21.1 kN (+135 %); UHPC-2 52.5 kN versus NC-2 26.0 kN (+102 %). Overall stiffness +108 % (single row)/+34 % (double row), yield stiffness +40 %/+44 %, ultimate capacity +9 %/+6 %, ductility ratio +133 %/+75 %.
- Doubling studs to two rows (NC): cracking load +23 %, overall stiffness +53 %, yield stiffness +13.2 %, ultimate capacity +12 %, ductility ratio +115 %. For UHPC: +6 %, +8 %, +16 %, +8 %, +62 % respectively.
- Ultimate loads: NC-1 143.7 kN, NC-2 161.0 kN, UHPC-1 156.5 kN, UHPC-2 170.8 kN. Failure mode was common to all: local buckling of the lower steel flange followed by load drop.
- Crack patterns: initial cracks formed in the pure flexure zone and near load points, developing into one or two dominant through cracks with symmetric transverse cracks — UHPC decks delay crack formation itself substantially.
- Capacity calculations: for NC the formula neglecting concrete tension (Mu,1) averaged 1.21 % error. For UHPC: Mu,1 averaged 4.11 %, Mu,2 averaged 0.16 %, Mu,3 averaged 2.48 % — the double-row specimen (UHPC-2) is underestimated by 10 % if concrete contribution is neglected, so Mu,3 is required. Elastic solutions of crack moments were within a few percent except NC-2 (-19.6 %).
