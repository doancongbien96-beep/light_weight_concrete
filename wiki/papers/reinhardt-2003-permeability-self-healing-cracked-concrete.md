# Hydraulic flow decay of cracked concrete as a function of temperature ($20\sim 80\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and crack width ($0.05\sim 0.20\ \text{mm}$), and the hydraulic–chemical mechanism of $100\%$ self-healing of a $50\ \mu\text{m}$ crack

## Summary

To prevent harmful moisture and ion ingress through cracks in concrete structures and extend service life, long-term steady-state constant-head water permeability tests (Water Permeability Test) were performed on high-performance concrete (HPC) into which finely controlled microcracks ($w = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20\ \text{mm}$) had been introduced via precisely controlled splitting tension tests, over a wide range of water temperatures ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}, 50\ ^\circ\text{C}, 80\ ^\circ\text{C}$), quantifying for the first time in the world the strongly time-dependent decay curve of the permeation flow ($Q$) and the phenomenon of autogenous self-healing (Autogenous Self-Healing). It conclusively demonstrated that at crack widths of $0.05\ \text{mm}$ ($50\ \mu\text{m}$) and below, the permeation flow drops to $0.0\ \text{ml/h}$ within days — a $100\%$ complete blockage — and that the calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystal precipitation rate accelerates exponentially as temperature rises: the immortal classic origin paper of chaired professor Hans-Wolf Reinhardt and Dr. Martin Jooss of the University of Stuttgart, Germany, published in Cement and Concrete Research (CCR) in 2003 and regarded as the greatest classic original paper in the history of concrete self-healing.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the cubic law relating crack width ($w$) to permeability and of its deviation mechanism**:
   - Quantified, for each temperature, the difference between the theoretical smooth parallel-plate value ($Q \propto w^3$) and the flow resistance of actual rough crack surfaces in concrete (roughness).
2. **World-first establishment of the critical self-healing crack width ($w_{crit} \le 50\ \mu\text{m}$)**:
   - Proved mathematically and experimentally that microcracks of $50\ \mu\text{m}$ and below recover watertightness perfectly under sustained water-flow exposure without any external intervention, providing the theoretical seed of modern ECC's crack-control criterion of $100\ \mu\text{m}$ and below.
3. **Demonstration that self-healing reaction kinetics accelerate with rising water temperature ($20 \rightarrow 80\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - Found that in hot environments the leaching of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions and the carbonation precipitation reactions speed up, so that even a 100 µm crack loses over 90% of its flow at 80 °C.
4. **Systematization of the four principal physicochemical self-healing mechanisms**:
   - Established the four principles of self-healing: 1) continued hydration of unhydrated cement particles (C-S-H gel formation), 2) precipitation of $\text{CaCO}_3$ crystals, 3) physical clogging of fine particles inside the crack, and 4) swelling of cement gel.

## Methods

- **Materials and fabrication of crack-induced specimens (Section 2 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Concrete: high-performance concrete (HPC, W/C = 0.38, 28-day compressive strength $85\ \text{MPa}$, CEM I 42.5R, silica sand/crushed-stone aggregate).
  - Specimen geometry: cylindrical specimens of diameter $\phi 100\ \text{mm}$ and height $100\ \text{mm}$ (laterally confined by four steel bars passing through the center).
  - Crack control: a single through-going crack was formed at the specimen center by splitting tensile (Splitting Tensile) loading $\rightarrow$ and the crack width was fixed precisely ($w = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20\ \text{mm}$) by tightening the nuts of the steel bars.
- **Constant-head permeability and self-healing testing (Section 2.3 & Fig. 2)**:
  - Water pressure: $0.1\ \text{bar}$ (constant water head of $1.0\ \text{m}$).
  - Test temperatures: $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (room temperature), $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (thermostatic water-bath chamber).
  - Permeating liquid: standard hardness water ($7\sim 14^\circ\text{dH}$).
  - Instrumentation: hourly permeation flow ($Q$, $\text{g/h}$) measured continuously and automatically with an electronic balance (for up to more than 100 hours).

### 2. Interpretation of the water temperature–flow interaction and the calcium carbonate crystal growth mechanism
- **Viscosity reduction vs. accelerated chemical reaction with increasing temperature**: as the water temperature rises, the viscosity coefficient of water decreases and the initial permeation $Q_0$ increases, but cement hydration and the $\text{Ca(OH)}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3$ carbonation crystal precipitation reactions accelerate more than fourfold, so the final rate of flow blockage is instead greatly improved.
- **Critical flow velocity and the crystal washout boundary**: in large cracks of $0.20\ \text{mm}$ and above, the shear force of the water flow washes out the precipitated crystals and self-healing remains incomplete, whereas at $0.05\ \text{mm}$ and below the crystals completely fill the crack gap under laminar flow conditions and permanent sealing is achieved.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the most celebrated landmark classic in the field, in which Professor Hans-Wolf Reinhardt formulated for the first time in the world the permeation flow decay and spontaneous self-healing behavior of cracked concrete as functions of crack width and temperature, establishing the 100% complete healing of cracks of $50\ \mu\text{m}$ and below and laying the cornerstone of self-healing-concrete and durability research worldwide.
