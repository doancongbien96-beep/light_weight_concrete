# Optimal 23 % Substitution, Mechanical Strength Synergies, and Efficiency Factor ($k$) Prediction Modeling for Quaternary Cement Concrete (QCC) Incorporating Micro-Silica, Marble Dust, and Rice Husk Ash

## Summary

To reduce greenhouse gas emissions from cement manufacturing and recycle industrial and agricultural solid waste by-products, Professor Vikas Srivastava and co-workers at SHUATS formulated 40 quaternary concrete mixtures (Grade M25, W/B = 0.40, slump $100\pm 25\ \text{mm}$) substituting Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) with three complementary mineral admixtures: **ultrafine micro-silica (MS: 6 %, 7 %, 8 %), quarry byproduct marble dust (MD: 5 %, 10 %, 15 %), and agricultural rice husk ash (RHA: 5 %, 10 %, 15 %, 20 %)**. The authors systematically evaluated compressive strength (7, 28, and 90 d), split tensile strength, flexural strength, pozzolanic efficiency factors ($k = k_e \times k_p$), and non-linear regression prediction models. Mix 32, with a total substitution of **23 % (MS 8 % + MD 5 % + RHA 10 %)**, exhibited maximum synergistic enhancement through ultrafine pozzolanic nucleation from MS, amorphous reactive silica from RHA, and fine pore-filling from MD. This optimal mix achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **$43.5\ \text{MPa}$** (a 27.2 % gain over plain OPC control at 34.2 MPa), a 90 d compressive strength of **$49.8\ \text{MPa}$**, and a 28 d flexural strength of **$5.15\ \text{MPa}$** (a 22.6 % gain). Exceeding 10 % MD or 15 % RHA caused strength losses due to free water absorption and binder dilution, establishing critical replacement limits and validated Bolomey-based synergy prediction formulas.

## Key Contributions

1. **Identification of optimal 23 % replacement in quaternary blended binders (MS+MD+RHA)**:
   - Formulated the MS 8 % + MD 5 % + RHA 10 % combination to achieve 27.2 % higher 28 d compressive strength ($34.2 \rightarrow 43.5\ \text{MPa}$) and 22.6 % higher flexural strength ($4.20 \rightarrow 5.15\ \text{MPa}$).
2. **Quantification of threshold limits for marble dust ($\le 10\%$) and rice husk ash ($\le 15\%$)**:
   - Mapped C-S-H dilution and strength reduction across 40 mixtures when inert carbonate fillers and high-porosity ashes were overdosed.
3. **Formulation of general and percentage efficiency factors ($k = k_e \cdot k_p$)**:
   - Separated intrinsic chemical activity and replacement level contributions, standardizing mixture design formulas for multi-blend concretes.
4. **Formulation of regression models for tensile and flexural strengths ($R^2 > 0.96$)**:
   - Established relations $f_{st} = 0.58 \sqrt{f_c}$ and $f_b = 0.78 \sqrt{f_c}$ predicting non-linear properties within ±4 % error.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: Grade 43 OPC ($3250\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), Micro-silica (MS, $\text{SiO}_2 > 88\%$, Blaine $180000\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), Marble dust (MD, $\text{CaCO}_3 > 92\%$, Blaine $3500\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), Rice husk ash (RHA, $\text{SiO}_2 > 85\%$, Blaine $24000\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Mix ratio: W/B = 0.40, Cement : fine aggregate : coarse aggregate = 1 : 2.06 : 3.16.
  - 40 mix series:
    - Control (M0): OPC 100 %.
    - Group I (M1–M13): MS 6 % + MD (5, 10, 15 %) + RHA (5, 10, 15, 20 %).
    - Group II (M14–M26): MS 7 % + MD (5, 10, 15 %) + RHA (5, 10, 15, 20 %).
    - Group III (M27–M39): MS 8 % + MD (5, 10, 15 %) + RHA (5, 10, 15, 20 %).
- **Testing protocols (IS standards)**:
  - Compressive strength: IS 516 ($150\ \text{mm}$ cubes, water cured for 7, 28, 90 d).
  - Split tensile strength: IS 5816 ($\phi 150\ \text{mm} \times 300\ \text{mm}$ cylinders).
  - Flexural strength: IS 516 ($100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm}$ prisms in 4-point bending).

### 2. Multi-scale synergy and predictive regression
- **Multi-scale packing and hydration**: Ultrafine MS ($0.1\sim 0.2\ \mu\text{m}$) filled inter-clinker voids and accelerated early pozzolanic reactions, RHA ($5\sim 15\ \mu\text{m}$) generated long-term secondary C-S-H, and MD ($20\sim 40\ \mu\text{m}$) provided carbonate nucleation sites.
- **Model validation**: The proposed regression model $f_b = 0.782 \sqrt{f_{c28}}$ correlated closely with experimental data ($R^2 = 0.968$, mean prediction error < 3.5 %).

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that quaternary blending of MS, MD, and RHA saves 23 % cement while delivering 127 % of control compressive strength.
