## Summary
A doctoral dissertation presented by Dr. Hong Zhu of the University of Western Australia, Australia, this monumental and enduring study consolidates the multiscale design theory and full-scale experimental validation of a next-generation ultra-high-strength, ultra-high-ductility composite (UHS-ECC: $f_c = 150 \sim 180 MPa$, direct tensile strength $16.5 MPa$, $\epsilon_u = 8.5 \%$) and of fused structural systems combining fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP/BFRP) reinforcement (FRP-UHS-ECC composite columns, beams, and seismic walls) that fundamentally blocks rebar corrosion in marine and extreme environments and resists seismic, impact, and blast loads. FRP-UHS-ECC composite columns were demonstrated to improve the seismic lateral load capacity **2.8-fold** and the plastic energy dissipation capacity **4.5-fold** over ordinary RC columns while reducing residual deformation by **80 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of the micromechanics of the ultra-high-strength, high-ductility composite (UHS-ECC, $f_c > 160 MPa$)**: optimization of the bridging equilibrium of nano-silica and ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers.
- **Establishment of the governing equations for the quasi-static cyclic behavior of FRP-UHS-ECC composite columns**: a complete fusion of elastic restoring force and ultra-high-ductility energy dissipation.
- **Attainment of a corrosion-free 100-year durability service life in extreme marine chloride and sulfate environments**: life-cycle cost cut by 60 % through complete exclusion of steel reinforcement.
- **Completion of design guidelines for blast- and impact-resistant ultra-high-energy-absorption composite members**: establishment of defense and protective engineering standards.

## Methods
- Material system: UHS-ECC ($f_c = 165 MPa$, PE fiber $V_f = 2.0 \%$, $\epsilon_u = 8.5 \%$) + high-strength BFRP/CFRP rebar ($f_u = 1450 MPa$).
- Full-scale structural testing: lateral reversed cyclic load test on $300 \times 300 \times 1800 mm$ composite columns (axial load ratio 0.30 $f_c A_g$).
- Multiaxial impact testing: integration of a high-speed drop-weight impactor and SHPB dynamic material property data.
