# Quantitative Evaluation of Self-Healing Performance of PE- and PVA-Fiber-Reinforced Fly Ash Concrete via 3-Stage Flexural Testing under CMOD Control ($0.05\ \text{mm}$) Across Pre-Cracking Ages and Healing Regimes

## Summary

To quantitatively evaluate the autonomous microcrack self-healing behavior of eco-friendly cementitious composites incorporating high-volume fly ash, Professor Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University, Professor Su-Tae Kang at Daegu University, and Professor Seung-Jun Kwon at Hannam University established a **3-stage three-point bending test protocol with clip-gauge crack mouth opening displacement (CMOD) precisely controlled to $0.05\ \text{mm}$ (1st pre-cracking loading $\rightarrow$ 2nd damage baseline loading $\rightarrow$ 21 d self-healing $\rightarrow$ 3rd failure reloading)**. The authors investigated the effects of reinforcing fiber type (hydrophilic PVA vs. hydrophobic PE, $V_f = 0.5\ \text{vol.\%}$), pre-cracking age (7 d, 28 d, 49 d, 91 d), and healing environment (water immersion vs. ambient air at 60 % RH, 21 d fixed period) on load-CMOD curves, flexural strength recovery ratio ($\text{SHR}_f$), initial flexural stiffness recovery ratio ($\text{SHR}_s$), and crack-face mineralization. Under water immersion, secondary pozzolanic reactions and $\text{CaCO}_3$ precipitation were maximized, allowing 7 d pre-cracked specimens to achieve **complete structural self-healing with a flexural strength recovery ratio of $128.5\pm 5.2\%$ and a flexural stiffness recovery ratio of $142.0\pm 6.5\%$**. Under air exposure, stiffness recovery was limited to $75\sim 85\%$, confirming the necessity of moisture, while hydrophilic PVA fibers accelerated early stiffness recovery by acting as interfacial moisture reservoirs.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of 3-stage flexural self-healing evaluation protocol under CMOD $0.05\ \text{mm}$ control**:
   - Developed a displacement-controlled testing protocol encompassing 1st pre-cracking $\rightarrow$ 2nd damage tangent recording $\rightarrow$ 3rd post-healing failure reloading.
2. **Demonstration of 128 % flexural strength and 142 % flexural stiffness recovery under water immersion**:
   - Proved that secondary hydration of unreacted fly ash within tight crack widths ($w \le 50\ \mu\text{m}$) bonds crack flanks, exceeding the original uncracked capacity.
3. **Quantification of healing efficiency decay across pre-cracking ages (7 d to 91 d)**:
   - Observed maximum healing at 7 d (128 %) due to abundant unhydrated binders, while long-term 91 d pre-cracked specimens maintained 102.5 % capacity restoration in water.
4. **Clarification of early stiffness recovery enhancement via hydrophilic PVA fiber surfaces**:
   - Demonstrated that PVA fibers facilitate interfacial moisture transport, boosting initial tangent stiffness recovery by over 15 % compared to hydrophobic PE fibers.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–4, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: Type I Portland cement (OPC) + Class F fly ash (FA 20 wt.% replacement).
  - Aggregates: Washed river sand (density $2.60\ \text{g/cm}^3$) + crushed gravel (maximum size 13 mm).
  - Proportions: Water-to-binder ratio W/B = 0.45, sand-to-aggregate ratio S/a = 48 %.
  - Reinforcing fibers ($V_f = 0.5\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    1. PVA fibers: Length 12 mm, diameter $40\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa, modulus 41 GPa (hydrophilic).
    2. PE fibers: Length 12 mm, diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 2700 MPa, modulus 88 GPa (hydrophobic).
- **Three-stage flexural testing protocols (Section 2.2 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Prisms: $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$ (300 mm span, 20 mm central notch).
  - Stage 1 (Pre-cracking): Loaded under three-point bending until reaching CMOD = 0.05 mm (86~88 % of peak load) and unloaded.
  - Stage 2 (Baseline re-loading): Reloaded to CMOD = 0.05 mm and unloaded to capture pre-healing damaged stiffness.
  - Self-healing period: 21 d under (1) water immersion ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Water) or (2) air drying ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 60 % RH Air).
  - Stage 3 (Failure reloading): Reloaded to complete flexural failure.

### 2. Healing mechanisms and microstructural evolution
- **Fly ash pozzolanic mineralization**: $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ reacted with reactive $\text{SiO}_2$ from fly ash in water to form dense C-S-H gels bridging crack faces.
- **Moisture affinity of PVA fibers**: Hydroxyl ($\text{-OH}$) groups on PVA fiber surfaces drew moisture into microcracks via capillary action, maintaining higher stiffness recovery under air exposure (85 % vs 82.5 %).

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the CMOD $0.05\ \text{mm}$ three-stage flexural protocol, demonstrating 128 % strength recovery in water-cured fly ash concrete.
