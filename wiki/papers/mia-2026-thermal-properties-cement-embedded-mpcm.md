## Summary

To cut heating and cooling energy demand in zero-energy buildings and improve indoor thermal comfort, the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University investigated composite heat-storage cement in which microencapsulated phase change material (MPCM, mean particle diameter $5 \sim 15 \mu m$) — octadecane (phase-change temperature $28 °C$, latent heat $\Delta H = 165 J/g$) encapsulated in a melamine-formaldehyde polymer nano-shell — substitutes cement paste volume at 0, 10, 20, 30 vol %, characterizing the effective latent heat storage capacity, transient thermal conductivity, specific heat, and compressive strength behavior through differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), Hot Disk TPS, and physical-model insulated chamber tests. Incorporating 20 vol % MPCM secured an effective latent heat of **28.5 J/g** and demonstrated mitigation of the indoor peak diurnal temperature by **3.8 °C**.

## Key Contributions

- **Unified envelope of latent heat capacity–thermal conductivity–strength as a function of MPCM dosage**: MPCM incorporation lowered thermal conductivity from $0.85 \rightarrow 0.42 W/(m\cdot K)$ — a 50 % reduction that doubles insulation performance.
- **Demonstrated 32 % reduction of the indoor peak heating/cooling load**: the latent-heat storage phase-transition mechanism extended the indoor temperature time lag by 2.8 hours.
- **Verified mechanical crush resistance of the porous nano-shell**: capsule breakage stayed below 2 % even under mixing agitation pressure.
- **Retention of 28 d compressive strength in MPCM-incorporated cement**: at 20 vol % dosage a compressive strength of 32.5 MPa was secured, meeting structural panel requirements.

## Methods

- Matrix: OPC ($W/C = 0.35$), high-range polycarboxylate superplasticizer (SP).
- PCM: microencapsulated paraffin octadecane (MPCM 0, 10, 20, 30 vol %).
- Tests: DSC latent heat and phase-change temperature analysis ($-10 \sim 50 °C$, $2 °C/min$), Hot Disk TPS 2500S transient thermal conductivity, compressive strength (50 mm cubes), and temperature history monitoring in a $300 \times 300 \times 300 mm$ scaled insulated chamber.
