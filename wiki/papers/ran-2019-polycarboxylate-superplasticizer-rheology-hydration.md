## Summary

This study elucidates how the molecular structural characteristics of polycarboxylate superplasticizers (PCE), namely the degree of polymerization of the main chain, the side-chain length (PEG molecular weight), and the density of carboxylate adsorption groups (charge density), affect the initial adsorption behavior, zeta potential, rheology (yield stress and plastic viscosity), and early-stage hydration kinetics of cement paste. It establishes that PCE molecules with long side chains and high adsorption density maximize electrostatic repulsion and steric hindrance effects, dramatically improving initial fluidity and inducing shear-thinning dispersion.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of the PCE molecular structure and steric-hindrance dispersion mechanism**: formulation of the correlations between side-chain density and length, adsorption layer thickness ($\delta_{layer} = 2.5 \sim 5.0 nm$), and yield stress reduction.
- **Control of early hydration retardation and adsorption competition**: analysis of the mechanism by which high-carboxylate-density PCE chelates strongly onto $C_3A$ and $C_3S$ surfaces and delays the induction period.
- **Presentation of a tailored PCE molecular design guide for low-water-binder, high-density ECC/UHPC**: proposes the optimal side-chain structure that lowers yield stress without impairing fiber dispersion.

## Methods

- **PCE synthesis**: free-radical copolymerization of controlled-molecular-weight MPEG and acrylic acid (AA) (side-chain molecular weights 1000, 2400, 5000 g/mol).
- **Measurements**: TOC adsorption analysis, zeta potential measurement, rotational rheometer rheology, and isothermal calorimetry hydration heat measurement.

## Results

- The PCE with side-chain molecular weight $2400 g/mol$ and a carboxylate molar ratio of 3.5 reduced the yield stress from $120 Pa$ to $2.5 Pa$ at a saturation adsorption amount on cement surfaces of $1.85 mg/g$, without excessively increasing plastic viscosity.
- This lays the foundation for optimal fluidity control that prevents fiber clumping and improves fiber orientation during mixing of fiber-reinforced composite materials.
