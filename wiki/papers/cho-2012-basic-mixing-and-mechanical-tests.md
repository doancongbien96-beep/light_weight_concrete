# Basic Mixing and Mechanical Tests on High Ductile Fiber Reinforced Cementless Composites

## Summary

An early domestic study examining the feasibility of producing ECC-class highly ductile composites without any cement, by incorporating 2 vol.% PVA fiber into a GGBS-based alkali-activated binder (a pioneering attempt at cementless ECC in Korea). Taking the activator combination as the variable, two mixes were selected (B1: Ca(OH)₂+Na₂SiO₃, B2: Ca(OH)₂+Na₂SO₄) and slump flow, compression, uniaxial tension, and flexure tests were performed. Both mixes achieved strain-hardening through multiple fine cracking and about 2 % tensile strain capacity, with the Na₂SO₄-based mix (B2) superior in every strength measure.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of cementless HD-FRCC**: mortar made only from GGBS + alkali activator + silica sand + water, with sole addition of 2 % PVA fiber (by volume), achieved uniaxial tensile strain-hardening behavior and multiple fine cracks (average width 30–40 µm) — proving that PSH-type highly ductile composites are possible even without cement.
2. **Activator type determines the flowability–strength balance**: the Na₂SO₄ mix (B2) secured adequate flowability and viscosity without fiber clustering even at a lower W/B (0.35 vs 0.55) in place of Na₂SiO₃ → the strength benefit of a low W/B can be exploited directly.
3. **Results interpreted against PSH theory criteria**: B2's superiority in deformation capacity is explained via the strength and energy conditions of Li & Leung, Li & Wu, and Leung, and Kanda & Li's performance-index framework — judged to stem from B2's larger increase of tensile strength over crack strength, which improved the strength index (σ₀/σ_fc).
4. **Interfacial interpretation of the tensile strength gain exceeding the crack strength gain**: based on fiber pull-out data, interpreted that in B2 the increments of chemical bond and frictional bond exceeded the increment of matrix crack strength, giving a larger bridging-performance improvement — suggesting that activator type changes even the fiber–matrix interfacial properties.
5. **Crack control confirmed**: flexural tests showed saturated multiple cracking at 2–3 mm spacing, and uniform fiber dispersion without clustering was observed on fracture surfaces.

## Methods

- **Binder**: KS F 2563 Type 3 ground granulated blast-furnace slag (Blaine fineness 4,204 cm²/g, specific gravity 2.93, basicity index 1.81; main oxides CaO 44.6 %, SiO₂ 34.7 %, Al₂O₃ 13.8 %).
- **Mixes (Table 3)**: B1 = GGBS + Ca(OH)₂/Na₂SiO₃, W/B 0.55, S/B 0.40; B2 = GGBS + Ca(OH)₂/Na₂SO₄, W/B 0.35, S/B 0.40. PVA fiber 2 vol.% in both.
- **Fine aggregate**: silica sand No. 7 with mean diameter 100 µm and specific gravity 2.65. Applies the ECC design logic of omitting or minimizing aggregate to lower matrix fracture toughness, plus purposes of shrinkage reduction and stiffness assurance.
- **PVA fiber** (ECC-grade, Japanese manufacturer K): diameter 0.04 mm (40 µm), length 12 mm, tensile strength 1,600 MPa, elongation 6 %, elastic modulus 37 GPa, oil agent content 0.8 %. The standard-ECC 2 vol.% was adopted as-is because matrix fracture toughness and fiber pull-out data were unavailable (micromechanics design stated as follow-up work).
- **Mixing**: dry mixing 1 min → mixing water added and mixed 1 min in a 30 L electric mixer while thickener and superplasticizer were added → fiber charged in split batches over 1 min, then mixed 4 min. Demolded at 3 days, followed by 28-day water curing.
- **Tests**: slump flow (KS F 2594), compression (KS L 5105, 50 mm cubes), uniaxial tension (specimen proposed by Yun-Yong Kim et al.; displacement control 0.1 mm/min, 2 LVDTs, gauge length 150 mm), flexure (400×100×10 mm panel, span 380 mm, two-point loading at ±50 mm from center, fb = 3Pl/(bd²)).

## Results

| Item | B1 | B2 | B2/B1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slump flow (mm) | 480 | 450 | − |
| Compressive strength 28 days (MPa) | 15.6 | 23.7 | +51.9 % |
| Crack strength (MPa) | 1.85 | 3.15 | +70.3 % |
| Tensile strength (MPa) | 2.29 | 4.04 | +86.4 % |
| Tensile strain capacity (%) | 1.80 | 2.04 | +16.7 % |
| Flexural crack strength (MPa) | 2.2 | 5.6 | +155 % |
| Flexural strength (MPa) | 3.1 | 8.5 | +174 % |
| Flexural deflection capacity (mm) | 17 | 40 | +135 % |

- Mean slump flow 465 mm — below the standard-specification high-flow criterion (600 mm) but higher than ordinary and fiber-reinforced concrete, with no fiber clustering or material segregation.
- Both uniaxial tension and flexure confirmed a strain-hardening regime through multiple cracking after first cracking; specimens retained their integrity by fiber bridging even after peak load.
- B2 flexural specimens showed saturated cracking at 2–3 mm spacing and tensile crack widths of 30–40 µm (within the range discussed for self-healing and durability).
