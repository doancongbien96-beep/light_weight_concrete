## Summary
To overcome the high logistics costs of standard imported silica sand and specially refined binders that hinder the worldwide dissemination of ECC, this FraMCoS-9 paper by the research team of Professor Victor Li at the University of Michigan elucidated the micromechanics-based mix design and uniaxial tensile behavior of a locally tailored sustainable composite (Local-ECC) utilizing locally procurable unprocessed river sand (Local River Sand, $d_{max} = 1.2 mm$), recycled concrete powder (RCP), and local fly ash. By controlling the increase in matrix fracture toughness caused by incorporation of coarse local river sand through chemical admixtures and slag fine-particle packing, the composite achieved a 28 d compressive strength of 45.2 MPa, a direct tensile strength of 5.1 MPa, and a tensile strain capacity of 4.2 %, while reducing material cost by 55 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a micromechanical PSH design model for Local-ECC containing coarse aggregate ($d_{max} = 1.2 mm$)**: overcoming the $J_{tip}$ increase caused by particle size non-uniformity through fiber-matrix interface optimization.
- **Manufacturing unit cost cut 55 % through 100 % utilization of local by-products**: complete elimination of dependence on imported standard silica sand.
- **Demonstration of a 4.2 % tensile strain capacity with a saturated fine multiple-cracking network**: mean crack width maintained below $40 \mu m$.
- **Securement of applicability to large-scale infrastructure in developing countries**: global diffusion of sustainable construction materials technology.

## Methods
- Materials: local river sand ($d_{max} = 1.2 mm$, $d_{50} = 450 \mu m$), local Class F fly ash ($FA/C = 1.5$), and Type I cement were used.
- Fibers: Kuraray REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm) were incorporated.
- Testing: dog-bone direct tensile tests ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain measurement), 28 d compressive strength tests, and single-fiber pullout tests were conducted.
