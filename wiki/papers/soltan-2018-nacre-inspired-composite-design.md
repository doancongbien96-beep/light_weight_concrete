# Nacre-Inspired Composite Design for Large-Scale Cementitious Members

## Summary

An experimental study that transferred the brick-and-mortar architecture and deformation mechanisms of nacre (tablet slip, damage compartmentalization) to civil-engineering scale. It breaks through the bottleneck in which prior artificial nacre research remained limited to thin-film, low-speed processes, via the insight that ECC's distributed multiple cracking behavior naturally creates tablet-like discrete units. High-strength ECC (HSHDC) was structured into precast stacked and polymer-mesh-inserted configurations and tested in direct tension, flexure, and compression; the PP geotextile mesh series showed about a 100 % increase in flexural strength and up to a 6-fold improvement in inelastic flexural toughness, identifying it as the most promising design.

## Key Contributions

1. **The structural analogy of ECC multiple cracking as a tablet generator**: when the matrix sequentially microcracks at weak spots under tension, the strong segments between the cracks act as macroscopic analogues of nacre tablets (corresponding to aragonite of diameter ~8 μm and thickness 0.5 μm), and the probabilistic offset of crack locations between layers reproduces the staggered tablet arrangement. The design principle is to scale up only the shaping effect, not the size effect.
2. **Quantified superiority of the mesh-laminate design**: the PP mesh series (aperture 6.25 mm², alkali-resistant geotextile grade rated for a 75-year service life) was the only series to simultaneously achieve an approximately +100 % increase in flexural modulus of rupture (MOR) and up to a 6-fold gain in inelastic flexural toughness. It outperformed the VCP mesh (small-aperture insect screen), and fractographic observation identified the few interlayer fibers passing through the apertures to connect adjacent layers as the cause.
3. **Damage compartmentalization**: early interlayer debonding induces crack arrest/bifurcation, confining damage within 1~3 layers. This contrasts with the through-crack bundling observed in monolithic controls, and resembles process-zone-like behavior of nacre.
4. **Sequential layer failure as failsafe**: progressive failure in which the remaining layers maintain load even after one layer's bridging capacity is exhausted was observed across all series. Even in the presence of through cracks, "through failure" was suppressed, presented as a safety-margin mechanism for seismic-, impact-, and blast-resistant applications.
5. **Compressive strength paradox**: a compressive strength gain not observed in nacre (PP mesh superior to monolithic in both layer orientations, perpendicular and parallel to loading) was confirmed — because the mesh dissipates energy by altering crack paths, debonding, and branching without carrying load itself. As reference, the monolithic HSHDC within the same study measured lower than previously reported values (~160 MPa), so comparisons were made on the basis of specimens from identical mixes.
6. **Limitations of the PU-bonded series clarified**: PU-bonded composites have large strain capacity but lower MOR than the monolithic, and the prestrained variant's elastic limit of ~2 MPa led to a judgment of structural unsuitability. The ligamental-type tensile resistance of the adhesive is evidenced by the tension-residual marks in Fig. 14, but it is classified as an energy-absorption-oriented solution permitting interlayer sliding up to 5 mm.

## Methods

The base material is the HSHDC of Ranade et al. (2011, 2013): Class H oilwell cement 72 %, silica fume 28 % (%CM basis), ground quartz 19.90 %, F-60 silica sand 50.40 %, water ~15 %, HRWRA (ADVA 190) 1.30 %, vinyl silane coupling agent 0.75 %, and UHMW-PE fibers (Spectra 900, diameter 38 μm, length 12.7 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa) at 2 vol %. After accelerated curing (room-temperature water immersion 7 days + 80 °C water 4 days + 70~80 °C drying for 24 h or more), testing was performed at ages of 14~19 days.

The design variants comprise two families and eight types: (i) stacked — PU adhesive-bonded 3·4 layers, prestrained PU-bonded (bonded after 2~3 % prestrain), and a hybrid alternating precast and cast-in-place layers; (ii) mesh — PP mesh 3·5 layers, VCP mesh 3·5 layers, and WD40 (form-release-coated) PP mesh 3 layers (isolating the structuring effect alone by excluding the mesh's reinforcement contribution). Controls were monolithic with normal casting and stage casting (inducing in-plane fiber alignment).

Testing: large dogbone direct tension (gauge section 76 × 76 mm, gauge length ~175 mm; Instron 490 kN, 1 mm/min, LVDT strain), four-point bending (MTS 97.9 kN, span 254 mm/loading span 89 mm, section 75 × 68 mm, sf = FL/(bd²)), and cubic compression (per ASTM C109, ~0.345 MPa/s).

## Results

| Item | Monolithic (cast) | Monolithic (layer-cast) | PU-bonded | Prestrained PU | PP-mesh (3 layers) | VCP-mesh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elastic limit stress | ~6 MPa | ~4 MPa | ~3 MPa | ~2 MPa | ~4.5 MPa | — |
| MOR change | baseline | baseline level | no increase (decrease) | — | about +100 % | inferior to PP |
| Inelastic flexural toughness | baseline | improved | superior energy absorption | — | up to 6-fold | improved (slight) |
| Initial flexural stiffness | 24.4 MPa/mm | — | 9.2 MPa/mm (4 layers) | — | 17.89 (WD40) | — |

- Direct tension: tensile strength was roughly equivalent across all series (dominated by matrix strength plus fiber bridging), while strain capacity improved significantly over cast monolithic in most series. The strength-strain trade-off is explained by ECC's characteristic bridging-exhaustion mechanism. Notably, it is pointed out that the literature value for JSCE dogbone specimens (Ranade: 11.8 MPa, ~3.5 %) is a value distorted favorably by specimen geometry in terms of fiber alignment and flaw statistics.
- Flexure: all series except PU-bonded achieved MOR ≥ +30 %. PP-mesh was simultaneously the highest in both strength and toughness. Increasing the number of layers correlated with reduced initial stiffness.
- Compression: PP-mesh outperformed the monolithic both when layer planes were perpendicular and parallel to the load axis.
- Mechanism observations: on longitudinal sections of PP/VCP specimens, crack arrest and bifurcation at layer boundaries; vertical cracks parallel to the load axis coinciding with mesh positions (evidence of interlayer shear); fiber pull-out traces at interlayers only in PP sections (absent in VCP owing to its small aperture); elongated residues of the PU adhesive and interlayer slip up to 5 mm.
