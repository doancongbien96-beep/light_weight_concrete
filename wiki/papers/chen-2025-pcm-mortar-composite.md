# A hydrated salt-expanded perlite cement mortar composite phase change material (PCM) with low subcooling and high latent heat for building energy conservation

## Summary

A multifunctional building-block development study that maximizes the insulation and thermal-storage performance of building envelopes by introducing a nucleating agent ($\text{SrCl}_2\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and $\text{TiO}_2$ nanoparticles into calcium chloride hexahydrate ($\text{CaCl}_2\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$) to suppress subcooling to 0.8 °C, vacuum-impregnating and encapsulating the salt into expanded perlite (EP), and then incorporating it into cement mortar to secure thermal reliability and mechanical performance simultaneously.

## Key Contributions

1. **Subcooling and phase-separation control of the hydrated salt PCM**: the synergistic nucleating effect of $\text{SrCl}_2\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{TiO}_2$ reduced the subcooling degree of the hydrated salt drastically, from the conventional 15–20 °C level down to 0.8 °C.
2. **Leak-proof encapsulation via porous expanded perlite**: manufacture of shape-stabilized PCM composite aggregates free of liquid leakage by exploiting capillary tension and surface tension.
3. **Indoor thermal environment improvement and durability verification**: the composite mortar achieved a phase-change temperature of 27.8 °C and a latent heat of 32.4 J/g, and after 1000 thermal cycles the latent heat loss was a mere 5.8 %, proving excellent thermal durability. Indoor model tests lowered the indoor peak temperature by 2.9 °C and extended the thermal lag time by 254 s.

## Methods

- **Material composition**: calcium chloride hexahydrate + strontium chloride hexahydrate + $\text{TiO}_2$ nanoparticles, porous carrier (expanded perlite EP), ordinary Portland cement (P.O 42.5), and standard sand.
- **Characterization**: DSC thermal analysis, SEM microstructure, 1000-cycle freeze–thaw thermal cycling reliability tests, 14-day compressive strength (0.9–2.0 MPa), and insulated thermal-storage model chamber tests.

## Results

- Adding the PCM capsules slightly reduces the compressive strength of the cement mortar, yet the composite still satisfies the criteria for non-load-bearing envelope insulation blocks while exhibiting outstanding thermal storage and indoor peak temperature reduction performance.
