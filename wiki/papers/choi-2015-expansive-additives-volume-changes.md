# Numerical Modeling of the Volume-Change Behavior of Concrete Mixed with Expansive Additives

## Summary

A paper that precisely predicted, through numerical analysis, the volume-change behavior of cementitious composites mixed with expansive additives to prevent drying shrinkage and autogenous shrinkage cracking of concrete, founded on the thermodynamic equilibrium between a capillary tension (Capillary Tension) based cement shrinkage model and an expansion model based on the volumetric increase rate of hydration products.

## Key Contributions

1. **Formulation of a thermodynamic capillary-tension-based shrinkage mechanism**: introduced an Effective Radius Factor considering the internal pore size distribution of hardened cement paste and the thermodynamic equilibrium state of moisture, quantitatively computing the capillary-tension shrinkage stress as relative humidity decreases.
2. **Modeling of the volumetric expansion rate of expansive hydration products**: mathematically modeled the reaction kinetics of expansive additives (ettringite- and calcium hydroxide-forming types) and the solid-phase volume-increase pressure accompanying hydration product formation.
3. **Concrete-scale volume-change prediction via a composite model**: combined paste-scale shrinkage–expansion water balance (balance) with the restraint effect of aggregates (extended Hobbs model) to precisely predict the expansion and shrinkage histories of concrete under various expansive-additive dosages and water-to-binder-ratio conditions.

## Methods

- **Experiments and model verification**: pastes and concretes at W/B 45 % and 55 % with expansive additive dosages (0 %, 3 %, 6 %, 9 %); pore-size distribution measurement (MIP), and long-term monitoring of length change ratio and relative humidity.
- **Numerical model**: Kelvin-equation-based capillary pressure analysis combined with an expansive-additive reaction evolution function.

## Results

- The proposed model predicted both the timing of the initial expansion peak and the subsequent residual shrinkage strain upon drying with high accuracy of determination coefficient $R^2 > 0.92$.
