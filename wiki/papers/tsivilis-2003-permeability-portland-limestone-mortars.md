## Summary

A study systematically evaluating the gas permeability, capillary absorption rate (sorptivity), total porosity, and particle size distribution of Portland limestone cement (PLC, EN 197-1 CEM II/A-L and CEM II/B-L) mortars in which finely ground limestone powder was interground with cement clinker at up to 35 wt %. Through the micro-filler effect of limestone and its acceleration of early-age $C_3S$ hydration nucleation, the study established that a gas permeability coefficient equal to or lower than that of plain OPC can be secured at 10 ~ 15 wt % limestone replacement, and formulated prediction equations for permeability as a function of $W/B$ and curing conditions.

## Key Contributions

- **Correlation between limestone replacement ratio and gas/liquid permeability established**: at limestone contents $\le 15 wt \%$, voids between clinker particles are filled, suppressing the gas permeability coefficient $k_{gas}$ to the $10^{-16} m2$ level.
- **Blaine fineness optimization of limestone cement**: demonstrated that grinding limestone finer than clinker (Blaine $> 4500 cm2/g$) reduces the capillary absorption coefficient by more than 30 %.
- **Balanced model of carbon emission reduction and durability presented**: replacing 15 % of the clinker immediately cuts cement production carbon emissions by 12 % while maintaining long-term watertightness.

## Methods

- **Materials**: CEM I (OPC), CEM II/A-L (limestone 10 %, 15 %), CEM II/B-L (limestone 20 %, 35 %).
- **Test items**: Cembureau gas permeability test, RILEM capillary absorption test, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), 28 d compressive strength.

## Results

- 15 wt % limestone cement ($W/B = 0.50$): 28 d compressive strength $48.2 MPa$, gas permeability coefficient $1.2 \times 10^{-16} m2$ (comparable or superior versus CEM I $1.5 \times 10^{-16} m2$).
- 35 wt % high-content limestone: insufficient hydration gel in the matrix tripled the permeability, deriving the necessity of lowering $W/B$ and increasing fineness.
