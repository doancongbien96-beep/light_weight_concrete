# Historical Evolution of Cement and Concrete, Hydration Degradation Mechanisms, and Forensic Analysis of Major Structural Collapse Disasters

## Summary

Covering materials that constitute over 70 % of global infrastructure, this 27-page landmark review by Professor Colin R. Gagg at The Open University synthesizes the historical origins of cement and concrete—from Roman volcanic ash pozzolanic hydraulic lime mortars to Joseph Aspdin's 1824 Portland cement patent—through the hydration kinetics of the four principal clinker minerals ($\text{C}_3\text{S}, \text{C}_2\text{S}, \text{C}_3\text{A}, \text{C}_4\text{AF}$), and provides forensic engineering failure analyses of major structural collapses worldwide. The paper examines the 1978 Willow Island cooling tower collapse (51 fatalities), phase conversion of high alumina cement (HAC), alkali-silica reaction (ASR), delayed ettringite formation (DEF), sulfate attack, and chloride-induced rebar corrosion, providing safety guidelines for lifecycle infrastructure integrity.

## Key Contributions

1. **Forensic investigation of the 1978 Willow Island cooling tower collapse (51 fatalities)**:
   - Overlooking the retardant effect of low overnight ambient temperatures ($1\sim 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$) on C-S-H hydration, crane jump-form anchor bolts were loaded into 1- and 3-day unhardened concrete, triggering punching shear failure and causing the tower crane and scaffolding to fall 50 m to the ground.
2. **Elucidation of phase conversion in high-alumina cement (HAC)**:
   - Metastable hexagonal hydrates ($\text{CAH}_{10}, \text{C}_2\text{AH}_8$) that provide early strength convert in warm/humid environments into stable cubic phases ($\text{C}_3\text{AH}_6 + \text{AH}_3$), producing a 53 % solid volume shrinkage and spiking capillary porosity to cause up to a **75 % plunge in compressive strength**.
3. **Synthesis of four primary chemical durability degradation mechanisms**:
   - Alkali-silica reaction (ASR: expansive silicate gel formation), sulfate attack (external gypsum and ettringite formation), delayed ettringite formation (DEF: secondary expansion following heat curing above 70 °C), and chloride-induced passivation film breakdown.

## Methods

- **Hydration chemistry and degradation systems (Figures 1–18)**:
  - Portland cement four-phase clinker hydration:
    - $\text{C}_3\text{S} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C-S-H} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (dominates 28 d strength).
    - $\text{C}_2\text{S} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C-S-H} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (dominates long-term strength).
    - $\text{C}_3\text{A} + \text{CaSO}_4\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Ettringite} \rightarrow \text{Monosulfate}$.
  - High-alumina cement phase conversion equation:
    $$3\text{CAH}_{10} \rightarrow \text{C}_3\text{AH}_6 + 2\text{Al(OH)}_3 + 18\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\Delta V_{solid} = -53.7\%)$$
  - Alkali-silica reaction (ASR):
    $$\text{SiO}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{expansion pressure } > 10\ \text{MPa})$$
- **Historical failure case studies**:
  - Willow Island Cooling Tower (1978, West Virginia, USA).
  - Stepney School Swimming Pool Roof (1974, London, HAC collapse).
  - Camden School for Girls Roof (1973, London, HAC collapse).
  - Montreal overpass and parking deck shear failures.

## Results

### 1. Concrete degradation mechanisms, structural outcomes, and case histories

| Failure mechanism | Primary chemical/physical process | Diagnostic indicators | Structural consequence | Representative case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Premature formwork removal**| Retarded C-S-H hydration at low temp | Microcracks around anchorages | Anchor cone pullout & progressive collapse | **1978 Willow Island (51 fatalities)** |
| **HAC conversion** | $\text{CAH}_{10} \rightarrow \text{C}_3\text{AH}_6$ solid shrinkage | Color shift to brown, porosity increase | **75 % strength plunge & brittle collapse** | 1974 Stepney School Roof |
| **Alkali-silica reaction (ASR)**| Alkalis ($\text{Na}^+,\text{K}^+$) + reactive silica | Map cracking, gel exudation | Member expansion & shear loss | Dams, piers, highway pavements |
| **Delayed ettringite (DEF)** | Reformation after steam curing > 70 °C | Rim microcracks around aggregates | Matrix expansion & crumbling | Precast railway ties |
| **Chloride rebar corrosion** | $\text{Cl}^-$ ingress $\rightarrow$ passivity loss | Rust staining, longitudinal cracking | **Cover spalling & steel section loss** | Marine bridges, parking structures |

### 2. Forensic investigation and design safety lessons
- **Limitations of 28 d standard curing**: Demonstrated disparities between laboratory water-cured specimens ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and in-situ cold-weather structures, leading to mandatory maturity monitoring for real-time strength verification.
- **HAC structural ban**: HAC was restricted strictly to refractory and emergency repair applications, leading to bans on its use in primary load-bearing structural members worldwide.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review bridges hydration chemistry and forensic structural engineering, demonstrating how material defects lead to catastrophic collapse and summarizing modern quality control and safety standards.
