# Development of a Strain-Hardening Cement Composite (3D-SHCC) for 3D Concrete Printing (3DCP): Nozzle Shear-Flow Alignment-Based Uniaxial Tensile Ductility of $2.55\%$ and Demonstration of Continuous 7-Layer Stacking

## Summary

To resolve the long-standing difficulty of placing reinforcement in the layered manufacturing of digital concrete 3D printing (3DCP) and to confer ultra-high ductility and crack-controlling ability on formless continuously stacked structural elements, this study developed a **3D-printing-specific strain-hardening cement composite (3D-SHCC) reinforced with high-density polyethylene (HDPE) micro fibers ($L_f = 6\ \text{mm}, d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$)**, and precisely evaluated across four mixtures (fiber contents $V_f = 0.3\%, 1.0\%, 1.5\%$) the nozzle extrudability, the buildability (7 layers stacked continuously), the interlayer stacking time interval (interlayer interval 1 min vs. 14 min), the uniaxial direct tensile behavior, and the fiber alignment mechanism — thereby demonstrating, for the `Mixture B` blend at 1.0 vol.% PE, perfect 7-layer stacking without shape collapse together with an extrusion-direction uniaxial tensile strength of $4.85\ \text{MPa}$ and an ultimate tensile strain of $2.55\%$, i.e. excellent strain-hardening with saturated multiple fine cracking, in a **Materials 2018 landmark origin paper** by the joint team of **Prof. Viktor Mechtcherine of TU Dresden (Germany) and Dr. Hiroki Ogura of Shimizu Corporation (Japan)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First establishment of a strain-hardening composite (3D-SHCC) system for 3D-printing (3DCP) layered manufacturing**:
   - The flowability during pumping and nozzle extrusion, together with the static yield stress and thixotropy required to retain shape after deposition, were optimized with a 1.0 vol.% HDPE fiber composite.
2. **Elucidation of the mechanism by which nozzle shear flow automatically aligns fibers with the printing direction**:
   - The wall shear stress gradient generated during nozzle extrusion aligns the micro HDPE fibers parallel to the filament extrusion direction, maximizing their bridging efficiency in the principal tensile stress direction.
3. **Demonstration of uniaxial tensile strain $2.55\%$ and multiple fine cracking in a formless 7-layer stack**:
   - Uniaxial direct tension tests on specimens cut from the 3D-printed stacked wall achieved dense multiple-crack strain-hardening spread over the bulk matrix without interlayer separation.
4. **Quantification of how the interlayer stacking delay time (interlayer interval) changes interlayer bond and ductility**:
   - Continuous stacking at a 1-minute interval exhibited fully monolithic behavior, while even 14-minute delayed stacking maintained an adequate moisture state and successfully preserved the strain-hardening characteristics.

## Methods

- **Materials and 3D-SHCC mixture composition (Section 2.1 & Tables 1–2)**:
  - Binder: CEM I 52.5R Portland cement + fly ash + silica fume.
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand ($d_{max} = 1.0\ \text{mm}$, S/B = 0.20–1.20, W/B = 0.22–0.24).
  - Reinforcing fiber: HDPE micro fiber ($L_f = 6\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, $\sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Four mixtures:
    1. `Mixture A`: W/B = 0.24, S/B = 1.20, $V_f = 0.3\%$.
    2. `Mixture B`: W/B = 0.22, S/B = 0.50, $V_f = 1.0\%$.
    3. `Mixture C`: W/B = 0.22, S/B = 0.20, $V_f = 1.5\%$.
    4. `Mixture D`: W/B = 0.24, S/B = 0.20, $V_f = 1.5\%$.
- **3D printing and test program (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 1–5)**:
  - 3D printing equipment: gantry-type concrete printer ($30 \times 15\ \text{mm}$ rectangular nozzle, travel speed $30\ \text{mm/s}$).
  - Buildability assessment: $400\ \text{mm}$ length, 7 layers stacked continuously (time intervals of 1 min and 14 min).
  - Uniaxial direct tension: $250 \times 40 \times 24\ \text{mm}$ specimens cut from the stacked wall (bolt clamps at both ends, 0.05 mm/s displacement control).

### 2. Interpretation of the nozzle-extrusion fiber alignment and interlayer interface behavior
- **1D fiber alignment effect through shear flow**: while passing through the nozzle interior, the micro fibers became highly aligned within $\pm 15^\circ$ of the extrusion direction, so that even at the low fiber content of 1.0 vol.% a high tensile elongation of $2.55\%$ was expressed compared with cast SHCC.
- **Stacking interval and interface bonding**: the 1-minute-interval stack proved that the upper and lower layer matrices fuse perfectly, so that intra-matrix crack bridging continues without interlayer separation (cold joint).

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a top-tier landmark origin paper in the fields of digital construction and advanced cement composites, in which Prof. Viktor Mechtcherine of TU Dresden and Shimizu Corporation introduced 1.0 vol.% micro HDPE fibers into the 3D concrete printing process and achieved, for the first time in the world, perfect extrusion/buildability together with nozzle-flow-alignment-based $2.55\%$ tensile strain-hardening.
