# Surface Coating Dispersion of Bundle-Type Polyamide (PA) Fibers and $5.2$-Fold Flexural Toughness and Drop-Weight Impact Energy Absorption Mechanisms in High-Strength Concrete (80 MPa)

## Summary

To eliminate fiber balling and bleeding defects during mixing of hydrophilic polyamide (PA / nylon) fibers and prevent brittle bursting and impact scabbing in high-strength concrete (compressive strength $80.0\ \text{MPa}$, 80 MPa), Prof. Gyu-Yong Kim (Chungnam National University), Prof. Nenad Gucunski (Rutgers University), and Dr. Jeong-Soo Nam developed high-strength fiber-reinforced concrete incorporating **bundle-type polyamide fibers coated with an amphiphilic dispersion system composed of polyhydric alcohol ester lubricants, anionic surfactants, and antistatic agents ($L_f = 30\ \text{mm}, d_f = 27\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 950\ \text{MPa}$, volume fractions $0.5\sim 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$) as well as hooked-end steel fibers**. ASTM C1609 flexural toughness testing, $100\ \text{kg}$ drop-weight impact testing (100 kg hammer, 1.0 m height, 980 J impact energy), and dynamic crack propagation analyses demonstrated **zero fiber balling ($0.0$), a $5.2$-fold amplification of flexural toughness ($T_{150}^{150} = 145.0\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$), a 12-fold increase in impact blows to failure, and complete suppression of rear-face concrete scabbing**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First development of amphiphilic dispersion coatings for polyamide fibers**:
   - The hydrophilic/lipophilic surfactant coating automatically deflocculates fiber bundles into individual filaments upon contact with water, eliminating fiber agglomeration.
2. **5.2-fold amplification of flexural toughness ($T_{150}^{150}$) in 80 MPa high-strength concrete**:
   - The ASTM C1609 toughness parameter $T_{150}^{150}$ increased by $5.2$-fold over plain concrete, preventing abrupt load drops and providing stable deflection hardening.
3. **Complete elimination of rear-face spalling under 100 kg drop-weight impact**:
   - Thousands of bridging PA fibers bound the rear tension face, preventing fragment scattering under dynamic stress-wave reflections.
4. **1:1 quantitative comparison between steel and polymeric fiber impact responses**:
   - Synthesized the initial crack-arresting stiffness of steel fibers with the large-deformation energy absorption of PA fibers to establish protective design rules.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Binders: Type I Portland cement + silica fume (SF/C = 0.10, W/B = 0.28, slump flow $650\ \text{mm}$).
  - Aggregates: Refined sand + coarse aggregate ($d_{max} = 13\ \text{mm}$).
  - Reinforcing fibers ($V_f = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$):
    1. Coated bundle PA fibers ($L_f = 30\ \text{mm}, d_f = 27\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 950\ \text{MPa}$, density $1.14\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
    2. Hooked steel fibers ($L_f = 30\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.5\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1100\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Mechanical and impact testing (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 5–8)**:
  - Flexural toughness: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ beams (ASTM C1609 4-point bending).
  - Heavy drop-weight impact: $300 \times 300 \times 100\ \text{mm}$ plates subjected to repetitive 100 kg steel hammer drops from 1.0 m.

### 2. Polyamide fiber dispersion and impact attenuation mechanics
- **Self-dispersion of amphiphilic coating**: Hydrophilic ends neutralize bundle surface tension upon mixing, maintaining high workability (slump flow $> 600\ \text{mm}$) even at $1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$ fiber content.
- **High-elongation plastic energy absorption**: High tensile elongation ($25\%$) in PA fibers dissipates reflective tensile wave energy, preventing concrete fragment spalling under 100 kg (100kg) impacts by $5.2$-fold.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark study proved that amphiphilic surface coating optimizes bundle PA fiber dispersion, boosting flexural toughness and 100 kg drop-weight impact resistance of high-strength concrete by over 5.2-fold.
