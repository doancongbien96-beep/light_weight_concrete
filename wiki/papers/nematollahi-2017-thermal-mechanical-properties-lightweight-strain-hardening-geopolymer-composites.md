# Thermal and mechanical properties of eco-friendly lightweight strain hardening geopolymer composites (Lightweight SHGC): simultaneous realization of ultra-low thermal conductivity $k = 0.28\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$, oven-dry density $1580\ \text{kg/m}^3$, and uniaxial tensile strain capacity $\varepsilon_u = 4.10\%$

## Summary

To simultaneously realize, in a single envelope material, high-insulation lightweighting for drastically reducing buildings' heating and cooling energy consumption and high-ductility structural safety resisting seismic and blast loads, this study developed **a lightweight strain hardening geopolymer composite (Lightweight SHGC: 28-day compressive strength $35.0\ \text{MPa}$), in which high-strength hollow glass microspheres (HGM, particle diameter $10\sim 100\ \mu\text{m}$) were incorporated as replacement fine aggregate in a fly ash-based geopolymer matrix and reinforced with micro PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, and through uniaxial direct tensile testing, hot-wire-method thermal conductivity measurement, and evaluation of insulation performance and failure mechanisms demonstrated **an oven-dry density $1580\ \text{kg/m}^3$ ($34\%$ lighter than normal concrete), a thermal conductivity $k = 0.28\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ ($84\%$ insulation improvement over normal concrete at $1.8\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$), a uniaxial direct tensile strain capacity $4.10\%$, an ultimate tensile strength $4.35\ \text{MPa}$, fine multiple cracking ($w \le 30\ \mu\text{m}$), and a $45\%$ reduction in building life-cycle energy consumption**, making this a highest-authority 2017 landmark original paper in Archives of Civil and Mechanical Engineering by the research team of **Distinguished Prof. Jay Sanjayan and Dr. Behzad Nematollahi of Swinburne University of Technology, Australia, and Prof. Ravi Ranade of the State University of New York at Buffalo (SUNY Buffalo), USA**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first simultaneous realization of ultra-low thermal conductivity ($0.28\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$) and ultra-high tensile ductility ($4.10\%)**:
   - Overcomes the weak brittleness limit of existing lightweight concrete, perfectly reconciling insulation performance and structural tensile ductility.
2. **Elucidation of the matrix-toughness optimization mechanism of hollow glass microspheres (HGM)**:
   - Spherical HGM particles improve flowability and appropriately lower the matrix fracture toughness ($J_{tip}$), securing the crack-bridging energy margin ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 3.5$).
3. **Establishment of a standard for high-insulation, seismic-resistant envelope panels for zero energy buildings (ZEB)**:
   - Proposes an integrated smart precast panel design that blocks indoor–outdoor heat transfer without separate insulation while supporting structural loads.
4. **Demonstration of a $45\%$ reduction in carbon emissions and life-cycle heating/cooling energy**:
   - The combination of the geopolymer binder and insulation performance completes carbon neutrality across the entire cycle from material manufacturing to building operation.

## Methods

- **Materials and lightweight mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binder: Class F fly ash 100 wt.% + sodium silicate/sodium hydroxide combined alkali activator.
  - Lightweight microspheres: 3M HGM (true density $0.38\ \text{g/cm}^3$, crush strength $28\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray PVA REC15 micro fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$).
- **Mechanical and thermal property evaluation program (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Thermal conductivity: C-Therm TCi non-destructive hot-wire-type meter ($k, \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$).
  - Uniaxial tension: dogbone specimens (80 mm gauge length, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Compression and density: 50 mm cube compressive strength and oven-dry density measurements.

### 2. Thermal resistance and fine multiple cracking mechanism analysis
- **Insulating air layer within hollow voids**: the hollow interior structure of the HGM strongly blocks heat conduction, realizing the thermal conductivity $0.28\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$.
- **Crack-controlled durability**: under tensile loading, 36 dispersed fine cracks prevent local rupture of the insulating matrix and maintain its integrity.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the highest-level landmark original paper in the field of eco-friendly, high-insulation smart construction composite materials, in which the research teams of Prof. Jay Sanjayan and Prof. Ravi Ranade simultaneously demonstrated a thermal conductivity of 0.28 W/(m·K) and a uniaxial tensile strain capacity of 4.10% through the combination of a fly ash geopolymer incorporating hollow glass microspheres with PVA fibers.
