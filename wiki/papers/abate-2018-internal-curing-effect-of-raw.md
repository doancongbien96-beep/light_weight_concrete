# Reducing autogenous shrinkage of high-strength slag-cement mortar through internal curing with carbonated recycled aggregate

## Summary

To suppress the severe autogenous and drying shrinkage cracking of a high-strength
slag-cement composite (OPC 60 % + GGBS 40 %, W/B 0.30), this joint study by Chosun
University and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University) systematically evaluated
shrinkage history, dry mass loss, non-evaporable water content (degree of hydration), and
compressive strength at 3, 28, and 91 days after substituting 25 vol.% and 50 vol.% of the
fine aggregate with raw recycled aggregate (RRA), recycled aggregate carbonated for 7 days
under accelerated 5 % CO_2 (CRA), or expanded shale lightweight aggregate (ES), all in SSD
condition. Carbonation treatment of the recycled aggregate transferred Ca(OH)_2 in the
attached mortar to CaCO_3, densifying fine capillary pores below 26 nm and stabilizing water
absorption from 8.5 % to 7.95 %, which greatly improved the persistence of internal moisture
release. The CRA50 mix consequently reduced autogenous shrinkage by **52 %** and drying
shrinkage by **38 %** against the control, while the internal water supply raised 91-day
non-evaporable water by 7.4 %, sustaining a long-term compressive strength of **78.5 MPa**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Pore refinement and improved internal-curing efficiency through carbonation
   modification (CRA)**:
   - Accelerated CO_2 carbonation converts calcium hydroxide and C-S-H inside the RRA to
     calcite (CaCO_3), shrinking the volume of sub-26 nm pores and reducing total porosity
     from 0.123 to 0.107 ml/g.
   - This blocks early evaporation loss of absorbed water and secures ideal desorption
     behavior in which moisture is released gradually as internal relative humidity drops
     during cement hydration.
2. **Large reductions in autogenous and drying shrinkage of high-strength slag mortar**:
   - CRA at 50 % substitution blocks more than half of the rapid early-age autogenous
     shrinkage over the first 7 days, preventing early fine cracking at its source.
3. **Improved long-term binder hydration through internal water supply**:
   - Sustained internal supply of the water needed for latent-hydraulic slag reaction
     raises 91-day non-evaporable water by about 7 % over the control
     ($11.2\ \%\rightarrow 12.0\ \%$).
4. **Minimal strength penalty and an eco-friendly high-strength mix**:
   - Unlike the sharp strength loss with lightweight aggregate (ES), the stiff carbonate
     coating of CRA preserves 28-day compressive strength of 74 MPa and 91-day strength of
     78.5 MPa.

## Methods

- **Binder and aggregate composition (unit contents $\text{kg/m}^3$, Tables 1, 2, 3)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (OPC: $\text{CaO}$ 64.07 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 22.00 %,
    fineness $341\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$) + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS:
    $\text{CaO}$ 41.5 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 33.2 %, fineness $435\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
  - Mix ratios: W/B = 0.30, aggregate/binder ratio = 1.20 (by weight),
    polycarboxylate SP 1.0 wt.%.
  - Internal-curing aggregates:
    - RRA (raw recycled aggregate, crushed 2–12.5 mm, 24 h absorption 8.5 %, SG 2.32).
    - CRA (accelerated-carbonated recycled aggregate, 5 % CO_2, 20 °C, 50 % RH for
      7 days; absorption 7.95 %, SG 2.36).
    - ES (expanded shale lightweight aggregate, absorption 13.7 %, SG 1.92).
  - Substitution: 25 % and 50 % of fine-aggregate volume (RRA25/50, CRA25/50, ES25/50).
- **Equipment and test methods**:
  - Mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP: Micromeritics AutoPore IV); XRD/TGA phase analysis.
  - Autogenous shrinkage: sealed prisms ($40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}
    \times 160\ \text{mm}$) measured with non-contact eddy-current sensors and linear gauges.
  - Drying shrinkage and mass loss: ASTM C596 (20 °C, 50 % RH).
  - Non-evaporable water: ignition loss (LOI) at $1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ after drying at
    $105\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes, 3, 28, 91 days).

### 2. Microstructure and internal-curing mechanism analysis
- **Pore structure change**: carbonation densifies sub-26 nm pores in CRA, suppressing
  early rapid desorption so that water is released at the later hydration stage.
- **Shrinkage-strength balance**: ES lightweight aggregate cures well internally but its
  low intrinsic particle strength cuts compressive strength by more than 20 %, whereas CRA
  limits strength loss to under 5 % — optimal for structural high-strength concrete.

- **Overall conclusion**: CO_2-carbonated recycled aggregate (CRA) is established as a
  high-performance eco-friendly internal curing agent that simultaneously recycles
  industrial waste, permanently sequesters CO_2, and slashes the critical autogenous
  shrinkage of high-strength cement composites by more than 50 %.
