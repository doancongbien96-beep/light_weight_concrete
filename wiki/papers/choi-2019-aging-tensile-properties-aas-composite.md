# Long-Term Tensile Ductility and Multiple-Cracking Behavior of an Aging Polyethylene Fiber-Reinforced Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Composite

## Summary

This study examined whether cement-based high-ductility composites (PVA-ECC), whose tensile strain capacity drops sharply beyond 28 days of age as fiber-matrix interfacial chemical bonding grows through continued cement hydration accumulation and fibers consequently rupture, can overcome this aging-embrittlement limit. It carried out long-term tracking experiments, by age (3, 5, 7, 14, 28, and 90 days), of the density, compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, tensile fracture toughness, and surface crack characteristics of a composite obtained by reinforcing a ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS)-based cement-free matrix with high-strength polyethylene (PE) monofilament fibers (1.75 vol.%) (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University, with co-authors Se Eon Park and Su-Tae Kang of Daegu University). Although the compressive strength of the AAS-PE composite increased continuously from 34.0 MPa at 3 days to 53.4 MPa at 90 days, thanks to the stable frictional slip characteristics of the hydrophobic PE fibers it retained, even at the long-term age of 90 days, a tensile strength of **11.27 MPa**, a tensile strain capacity of **6.73 %**, and a tensile toughness of $0.54\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m/m}$, demonstrating for the first time worldwide that no long-term ductility degradation whatsoever occurs. In particular, the composite was confirmed to preserve over the long term a world-class extreme saturated fine multiple-cracking control capability at 90 days: **99.8** cracks within the 80 mm gauge section, a crack spacing of **0.81 mm**, and a mean residual crack width of **50.5 µm**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First clarification of the mechanism preserving the long-term tensile ductility of AAS-PE composites (aging immunity)**:
   - Cement-based composites see their strain capacity fall to less than half beyond 28 days as the fiber rupture fraction increases with age, whereas the AAS-PE system suppresses interfacial chemical bond ($G_d$) formation at its source, maintaining stable ultra-high ductility perfectly from 3 days (6.58 %) through 7 days (7.07 %), 28 days (6.81 %), and 90 days (**6.73 %**).
2. **Achievement of a crack spacing below 1 mm (0.81 mm) and a crack width of 50 µm under long-term ages**:
   - As the age advanced from 3 to 90 days, the fiber frictional parameters were optimized through matrix strength gain: the number of cracks rose from about 90 to around **100**, the crack spacing densified to **0.76–0.81 mm**, and the residual crack width shrank to **50.5 µm**.
3. **Long-term stability of the PSH stress performance index secured**:
   - Even at 90 days, a maximum tensile strength of 11.27 MPa was developed against an initial cracking strength of 4.84 MPa, giving a stress performance index ($f_t/f_{1st}$) of **2.33**, far above the strain-hardening criterion value of 1.0.
4. **Verification of the fib Model Code and ACI 209 strength development models**:
   - The AAS matrix exhibited faster early-age strength development than the standard prediction models up to 28 days and converged gently thereafter, confirming long-term structural stability.

## Methods

- **Mix composition (mass ratios, Table 1)**:
  - Ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, fineness $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, density $2.91\ \text{g/cm}^3$) 0.895.
  - Alkali activator: powdered $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ 0.075 + $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ 0.030.
  - Water 0.25 (W/B 0.25), superplasticizer (SP) 0.014, defoamer 0.001.
  - Reinforcing fiber: high-strength PE fiber (diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$) at **1.75 vol.%**.
- **Curing and test ages**:
  - One day of air curing after casting → underwater curing in a thermostatic water bath at 23 ± 3 °C after demolding.
  - Test ages: 3, 5, 7, 14, 28, and 90 days.
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 (three $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens per age).
  - Uniaxial direct tensile test: JSCE-standard dumbbell-type specimens (cross-section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm), displacement control (0.2 mm/min), two LVDTs installed.
  - Crack-pattern analysis: after the tensile test at each age, precise determination of the number of cracks within the gauge section (80 mm), the average crack spacing, and the crack width.

### 3. Multiple-cracking patterns and fine crack-width control by age (Table 6)

| Age (days) | Number of cracks within gauge section | Average crack spacing (mm) | Mean residual crack width (µm) | Crack-control characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3 days** | 89.9 ± 11.3 | 0.90 ± 0.10 | 60.3 ± 8.2 | Early high-density multiple cracking |
| **5 days** | 82.1 ± 3.1 | 0.98 ± 0.04 | 68.5 ± 2.5 | |
| **7 days** | 90.9 ± 7.0 | 0.89 ± 0.07 | 62.8 ± 8.3 | |
| **14 days** | 103.5 ± 2.3 | 0.76 ± 0.02 | 51.4 ± 2.7 | Crack spacing entering the 0.7 mm range |
| **28 days** | **101.7 ± 7.9** | **0.79 ± 0.06** | **53.9 ± 4.0** | **Saturated fine multiple cracking formed** |

- **Overall conclusion**: even at the long-term age of 90 days, the AAS-PE composite stably maintained a tensile strain capacity above 6.7 % and crack widths at or below 50 µm, proving an ideal permanent repair and strengthening solution for structures requiring long-term durability and ductility.
