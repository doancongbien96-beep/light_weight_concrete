# Capric-stearic acid eutectic PCM based on a carbonized sugar beet pulp (CSBP) support: latent heat ($108.5\ \text{J/g}$) in the indoor comfort temperature band ($22.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and thermal conductivity $175\%$ enhancement mechanism

## Summary

To prevent liquid-phase leakage of phase change materials (PCMs) used for maintaining indoor thermal comfort and saving heating and cooling energy in buildings, and to upgrade a waste biomass into a high-value material, **an eco-friendly form-stabilized composite PCM (CSBP/CSEM)** was developed by vacuum-impregnating a comfort-temperature-band eutectic fatty acid (Capric-Stearic Acid Eutectic Mixture, CSEM: melting point $22.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $185.0\ \text{J/g}$) at $60\ \text{wt.}\%$ into a porous carbonized support (CSBP) manufactured by carbonizing agricultural waste sugar beet pulp at $700\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and chemically activating it with $\text{KOH}$; rigorous evaluation through DSC thermal analysis, transient plane source (TPS) thermal conductivity measurement, FE-SEM, FTIR, and a **1000-cycle continuous thermal cycling reliability test** demonstrated **leakage rate $0.0\%$, latent heat capacity $108.5\ \text{J/g}$, and thermal conductivity $0.48\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ ($+175\%$ enhancement)** together with excellent thermochemical stability — a landmark 2022 Journal of Energy Storage paper by the research team of **Prof. Ahmet Sarı and Prof. Gökhan Hekimoğlu of Karadeniz Technical University, Türkiye**.

## Key Contributions

1. **High-performance porous carbon support developed from sugar beet pulp agricultural waste**:
   - A mesoporous carbon structure with a specific surface area of $950\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$ and a pore volume of $0.68\ \text{cm}^3/\text{g}$ was synthesized, enabling large-capacity capillary confinement of fatty acid molecules at $60\ \text{wt.}\%$ loading.
2. **Adoption of a eutectic mixture tailored to the indoor comfort temperature (CSEM, $T_m = 22.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - The eutectic ratio of capric acid and stearic acid (81.5:18.5 wt.%) was precisely controlled so that phase change occurs immediately at the human-comfortable indoor temperature without operating any dedicated equipment.
3. **Dramatic $175\%$ improvement in thermal conductivity ($0.18 \rightarrow 0.48\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$)**:
   - The carbonized three-dimensional graphitic carbon skeleton acts as a heat-transfer highway, drastically improving the chronically slow heat storage and release rates of organic PCMs.
4. **Reliability demonstrated over 1000 consecutive heating-cooling thermal cycles**:
   - Ultra-high durability was verified: even after 1000 thermal cycles, latent heat retention remained at $98.5\%$ and the phase change temperature deviation stayed within $\pm 0.2\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## Methods

- **Support synthesis and composite PCM fabrication (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Carbonized sugar beet pulp (CSBP): raw-material washing $\rightarrow 700\ ^\circ\text{C}$ nitrogen-atmosphere carbonization (2 h) $\rightarrow \text{KOH}$ chemical activation ($800\ ^\circ\text{C}$) $\rightarrow \text{HCl}$ washing and drying.
  - PCM: capric acid (CA, 81.5 wt.%) + stearic acid (SA, 18.5 wt.%) eutectic mixture (CSEM, $T_m = 22.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $\Delta H_m = 185.0\ \text{J/g}$).
  - Vacuum impregnation: 3 h of reduced-pressure impregnation in a $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$ vacuum oven (0.08 MPa) $\rightarrow$ filtration and drying.
- **Property characterization and thermal cycling (Sections 2.4~2.6 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - DSC thermal analysis: PerkinElmer DSC 4000 ($-10\sim 60\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $3\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$).
  - Thermal conductivity: Hot Disk TPS 2500S instrument.
  - Thermal reliability: 1000-cycle continuous Peltier thermal cycler and TGA thermogravimetric analysis.

### 2. Capillary confinement and chemical non-reactivity analysis
- **Leakage blocked by capillary pressure**: the uniform mesopore structure of CSBP exerts strong capillary tension on the liquid fatty acid molecules, maintaining an exudation rate of $0.0\%$ in the $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$ elevated-temperature filter-paper test at $60\ \text{wt.}\%$ loading.
- **FTIR non-reactivity verification**: no new chemical bonds formed between CSEM and CSBP, confirming purely physical encapsulation.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original study in the field of energy storage materials, developing a leak-free eco-friendly composite PCM from carbonized sugar beet pulp, an agricultural by-product, that delivers a high latent heat of $108.5\ \text{J/g}$ in the indoor comfort temperature band ($22.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$) together with a thermal conductivity of $0.48\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$.
