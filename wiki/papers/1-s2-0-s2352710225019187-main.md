# Advances in lightweight ultra-high performance concrete: Mixture requirements, strength characteristics, and durability properties

## Abstract from PDF

The development of lightweight ultra-high performance concrete (LW-UHPC) has been a signif - icant advancement in construction materials, combining the superior mechanical and durability properties of ultra-high performance concrete (UHPC) with the lightweight advantages offered by lightweight aggregates (LWAs). This study systematically investigates the characteristics of LW- UHPC, in terms of various fresh and hardened properties. Including pre-saturated LWAs is noted to help with internal curing, which mitigates autogenous shrinkage and enhances cement hydration, contributing to the improved performance of this type of UHPC. Additionally, the mixture design and use of supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs), such as silica fume and fly ash, are found to enhance the particle packing and optimize the microstructure of LW-UHPC, further improving both mechanical and durability properties. This study characterizes the in - fluence of LWAs and SCMs on LW-UHPC ’ s workability, hydration kinetics, and shrinkage. The shape, porosity, and water absorption capacity of LWAs are among the critical factors that directly impact the workability of fresh mixtures, requiring adjustments in high-range water reducer dosages. In terms of hardened properties, the compressive strength, flexural strength, elastic modulus, and thermal conductivity of LW-UHPC are evaluated, recognizing that while the use of LWAs can adversely affect mechanical performance measures, optimized mixtures can still meet the expected thresholds. In addition, LW-UHPC is noted to exhibit lower thermal conduc - tivity than normal-weight UHPC, offering the potential for energy-efficient construction appli - cations. The durability of LW-UHPC is systematically evaluated by investigating its resistance to chloride ion penetration, carbonation, acid solutions, and freeze-thaw cycles. The outcome sheds light on how LW-UHPC can be considered as a material of choice for harsh environmental con - ditions and long-lasting infrastructures. Further research is recommended to optimize mixture designs and expand the use of LW-UHPC in emerging domains, such as prefabrication, 3D printing, and energy-efficient applications.
