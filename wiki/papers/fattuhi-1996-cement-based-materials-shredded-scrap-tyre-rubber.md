# Mechanical Behavior and Impact Energy Absorption of Cement Composites Containing Shredded Scrap Truck Tire Rubber: 4.8-Fold Toughness Amplification, Shrinkage Mitigation, and Waste Recycling

## Summary

To safely recycle millions of tons of scrap truck tires discarded annually and overcome the explosive brittle failure modes of conventional concrete, Professors N. I. Fattuhi and L. A. Clark at the University of Birmingham developed rubberized cementitious composites (Rubcrete) replacing 0~50 vol.% of fine aggregate in paste, mortar, and concrete with shredded rubber chips ($2\sim 10\ \text{mm}$) and fine crumb rubber ($d \le 2\ \text{mm}$). Evaluating 28 d compressive strength, flexural tensile strength, drop-weight impact fracture toughness, unit weight reduction, and fly ash (PFA) incorporation, the study demonstrated a **4.8-fold amplification in impact fracture energy absorption, zero explosive shattering (0.0), a 15% reduction in unit weight**, and established design criteria for impact-absorbing crash barriers and railway slab buffers.

## Key Contributions

1. **First systematic framework for large-volume scrap truck tire replacement in cement composites**:
   - Compiled an engineering database on the mechanical properties of paste, mortar, and concrete across tire grinding grades (rubber chips vs crumb rubber).
2. **Demonstration of transition from brittle failure to ductile plastic deformation**:
   - Proved that unlike plain concrete which shatters explosively at ultimate load, rubberized concrete retains structural integrity post-failure without fragmentation.
3. **4.8-fold enhancement in drop-weight impact energy absorption**:
   - Viscoelastic rubber particles acted as internal dampers absorbing energy at crack tips, extending impact fatigue life.
4. **Interfacial bonding improvement via fly ash (PFA) blending**:
   - Mitigated compressive strength loss from rubber incorporation through pozzolanic C-S-H gel densification.

## Methods

- **Materials and Rubcrete mix proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binders: Ordinary Portland cement (OPC) + pulverized fuel ash (PFA, replacement 0~30%).
  - Rubber aggregates:
    1. Rubber Chip (Coarse shredded rubber: particle size $2\sim 10\ \text{mm}$).
    2. Rubber Crumb (Fine granulated rubber: particle size $0.5\sim 2\ \text{mm}$).
  - Aggregate replacement: 0%, 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50% by total aggregate volume (W/C = 0.40 ~ 0.55).
- **Testing program (Sections 2.3–3.5 & Figs. 4–7)**:
  - Compressive and flexural tests: 100 mm cube compressive strength, $100 \times 100 \times 500\ \text{mm}$ prism four-point bending.
  - Drop-weight impact testing: 4.5 kg steel hammer drop tests recording initial cracking and ultimate failure blows.

### 2. Viscoelastic buffering and crack arrest mechanisms of rubber particles
- **Stress relaxation at crack tips**: Propagating microcracks encountering compliant rubber particles experienced crack tip blunting, arresting further rapid extension.
- **Environmental benefits**: Consumed 4~6 scrap tires per $1\ \text{m}^3$ of concrete, sequestering waste tires permanently into civil infrastructure.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the impact energy dissipation (4.8x) and ductile non-shattering failure modes of shredded tire rubberized concrete (Rubcrete) for sustainable impact-resistant civil engineering.
