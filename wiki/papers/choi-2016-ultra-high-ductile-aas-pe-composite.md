# Strain-Hardening and Tensile Behavior of an Ultra-High-Ductile PE Fiber-Reinforced Non-Cement Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Composite

## Summary

A landmark origin paper that developed an ultra-high-ductility composite (M26) at the world's highest level, simultaneously exhibiting a tensile strength of **13.06 MPa** and a tensile strain capacity of **7.50 %**, by incorporating high-strength polyethylene (PE) monofilament fibers (1.75 vol.%, tensile strength 2700 MPa) into a cement-free matrix that uses ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) as a 100 % binder without any ordinary Portland cement, a greenhouse-gas emitter, and characterized the density, 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, and surface multiple-cracking characteristics across W/B ratios (0.26, 0.30, 0.34, 0.38) (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University, with co-authors Ravi Ranade of SUNY Buffalo, Victor C. Li of the University of Michigan, and Yun Lee of Daejeon University). The M26 mix reached a stress performance index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$) of **2.68**, fully satisfying the saturated strain-hardening (PSH) condition, and its tensile fracture toughness of $0.0067\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{mm/mm}$ demonstrated an overwhelming energy absorption capacity: **2.7×** that of standard PVA-ECC (Li et al., 2001) and **1.8×** that of high-strength high-ductility concrete (HSHD, Ranade et al., 2013).

## Key Contributions

1. **Simultaneous achievement of 7.50 % tensile strain capacity and 13.06 MPa tensile strength in a cement-free binder**:
   - While securing a compressive strength of 54.8 MPa in the W/B 0.26 matrix, realized UHPC-grade tensile strength above 13 MPa and an ultimate strain capacity of 7.5 % by optimizing the high-strength character of PE fibers together with the frictional slip behavior of the AAS interface.
2. **Clarification of the correlation between the PSH stress performance index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$) and multiple-cracking saturation**:
   - As W/B decreased from 0.38 to 0.26, the $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$ index rose 1.50 → 1.95 → 2.49 → **2.68**.
   - Proportional to the rise in the stress index, the number of cracks within the gauge section surged from 36.6 to **67.3**, while the crack spacing narrowed from 2.25 mm to **1.20 mm**.
3. **Maximization of the tensile-to-compressive strength ratio ($f_t/f'_c$) (23.8 %)**:
   - Exhibited a tensile/compressive strength ratio more than twice that of ordinary concrete (about 10 %) and ordinary FRC (average 19.8 %, M26 at **23.8 %**), maximizing the contribution to shear reinforcement reduction and ultimate flexural capacity of structures.
4. **Breakthrough increase in tensile energy absorption (toughness)**:
   - The tensile toughness, the integrated area of the uniaxial tensile stress-strain curve, reached $0.0067\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{mm/mm}$, quantitatively outperforming Prof. Li's standard PVA-ECC (0.0025) and Ranade's HSHDC (0.0038).

## Methods

- **Binder and alkali activator (Table 1 & 2)**:
  - Ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, Blaine fineness $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, density $2.91\ \text{g/cm}^3$, $\text{CaO}$ 44.5 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 33.4 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 15.3 %, $\text{MgO}$ 3.6 %).
  - Activator: powdered $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (8.38 mass % of GGBS) + $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ (3.35 mass %) used in combination to prevent flash setting and secure workability.
- **Reinforcing fiber (PE fiber, Table 3)**:
  - Diameter 31 µm, length 12 mm, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength 2700 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa, elongation 3.0 %. Volume fraction **1.75 vol.%**.
- **Mix design (4 mixes, Table 1)**:
  - **M26**: W/B 0.26, HRWRA (high-range water-reducing admixture) 0.024, VMA (viscosity-modifying agent) 0.0000, defoamer 0.001, PE fiber 1.75 vol.%.
  - **M30**: W/B 0.30, HRWRA 0.009, VMA 0.0000, defoamer 0.001, PE fiber 1.75 vol.%.
  - **M34**: W/B 0.34, HRWRA 0.004, VMA 0.0001, defoamer 0.001, PE fiber 1.75 vol.%.
  - **M38**: W/B 0.38, HRWRA 0.002, VMA 0.0003, defoamer 0.001, PE fiber 1.75 vol.% (aggregate-free paste composite).
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Density and compressive strength: three $50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (ASTM C109 after 28 days of air/moist curing).
  - Uniaxial direct tensile test: plate-type tension specimens (cross-section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm), displacement control (0.2 mm/min), two LVDTs.
  - Crack-pattern analysis: after the tensile tests, high-resolution optical microscopy in the unloaded state plus visual counting of cracks; crack spacing and crack width computed.

## Results

### 1. Density and 28-day compressive strength characteristics (Table 4 & 5)

| Mix ID | W/B | Measured density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | Theoretical density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M26** | 0.26 | 2.03 ± 0.01 | 2.04 | **54.8 ± 1.5** |
| **M30** | 0.30 | 1.99 ± 0.01 | 1.99 | 49.0 ± 3.2 |
| **M34** | 0.34 | 1.95 ± 0.02 | 1.94 | 42.3 ± 2.4 |
| **M38** | 0.38 | 1.89 ± 0.01 | 1.89 | 36.3 ± 1.0 |

### 2. Uniaxial direct tensile performance and stress index (Table 7)

| Mix ID | First cracking strength (MPa) | Maximum tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | Cracking/compressive strength ratio ($f_i/f'_c$) | Tensile/compressive strength ratio ($f_t/f'_c$) | PSH stress index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M26** | **4.87 ± 0.46** | **13.06 ± 2.40** | **7.50 ± 0.45** | 8.88 % | **23.8 %** | **2.68** |
| **M30** | 4.61 ± 0.26 | 11.46 ± 1.05 | **6.33 ± 0.38** | 9.41 % | 23.4 % | **2.49** |
| **M34** | 3.92 ± 0.37 | 7.64 ± 1.08 | 5.35 ± 0.41 | 9.26 % | 18.1 % | 1.95 |
| **M38** | 3.37 ± 0.34 | 5.06 ± 0.94 | 4.58 ± 1.31 | 9.27 % | 13.9 % | 1.50 |

### 3. Tensile toughness comparison (Table 6)

| Composite material | Tensile toughness ($\text{MPa}\cdot\text{mm/mm}$) | Relative toughness index | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PVA-ECC (Li et al., 2001)** | 0.0025 | 1.00 | Standard PVA-ECC |
| **AAS-PVA (Lee et al., 2012)** | 0.0019 | 0.76 | Early cement-free composite |
| **HSHDC (Ranade et al., 2013)** | 0.0038 | 1.52 | High-strength high-ductility concrete |
| **This study (M26)** | **0.0067** | **2.68** | **World's highest level achieved** |

### 4. Surface multiple-cracking control characteristics (Table 8)

| Mix ID | Average number of cracks | Average crack width (µm) | Average crack spacing (mm) | Multiple-cracking saturation behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M26** | **67.3 ± 5.1** | **88.9** | **1.20** | **Highest-density saturated multiple cracking** |
| **M30** | 47.7 ± 6.2 | 106.9 | 1.70 | High-density multiple cracking, strain capacity 6.3 % |
| **M34** | 40.3 ± 3.6 | 109.8 | 2.03 | Good strain hardening |
| **M38** | 36.6 ± 5.5 | 99.3 | 2.25 | Moderate crack count, strain 4.6 % |

- **Conclusion**: Through the perfect harmony between matrix densification and the bridging capacity of PE fibers achieved in the W/B 0.26 mix, it was established that even a cement-free binder can realize 13 MPa-class tensile strength and 7.5 % ultra-high ductility surpassing cement-based composites.
