# Ultimate strength of bamboo scrimber-concrete composite beams with UHPC and ECC

## Summary

A study that took bamboo scrimber (heat-compressed engineered bamboo lumber), a wood-substitute eco-friendly material, as the tension face and ordinary concrete (OC), ECC, or UHPC as the compression face in nine composite T-beams (3 concrete types × 3 shear connector types), carrying out static three-point bending tests, Abaqus finite element analysis, and formulation of a strut-and-tie-based ultimate strength prediction model. Failure of every beam was governed by tensile cracking of the scrimber, and a role division was confirmed in which UHPC raised ultimate strength +14.0 % and stiffness +17.2 % while ECC raised ductility +20.5 %. Notched connectors (bolt, steel mesh) achieved ultimate strength +27.1 % over the angle-steel connector but lost 15.0 % ductility. Although the LVDTs registered no interface slip, the strain distribution revealed internal debonding starting at about 40 % of peak load, splitting the section into two neutral axes (concrete and scrimber); a prediction formula incorporating this loss of composite action together with the fiber bridging contribution was proposed (verified mean ratio 1.04, COV 0.14).

## Key Contributions

1. **The 3×3 experimental matrix of concrete type × connector type**: the failure mode is governed by concrete type, not connector configuration. OC beams failed brittly (rapid propagation of scrimber cracking with abrupt loss of load capacity), whereas ECC and UHPC beams, thanks to strain-hardening, showed narrow, closely spaced cracks and gradual stiffness degradation. First cracking occurred at about 15 % of peak load for OC and ECC and about 40 % for UHPC (high tensile strength), and all three materials reached the peak after audible scrimber cracking at about 95 % of peak load.
2. **Quantification of the UHPC/ECC role division**: UHPC-S gained +14.0 % ultimate strength and +17.2 % initial stiffness over OC-S (high compressive strength supplies compression-layer capacity, high tensile strength delays crack initiation). ECC beams showed ultimate strength similar to OC beams with lower stiffness but a +20.5 % ductility coefficient (ECC-A vs OC-A) — strain-hardening driven by PE fiber bridging restrains crack widening.
3. **Separating zero apparent slip from internal debonding**: the LVDTs detected no interface slip over the entire process, yet the mid-section strain distribution split into double neutral axes after about 40 % of peak load — the practical implication is that internal debonding, invisible to visual inspection and LVDTs alike, has already degraded composite action and therefore must be reflected in ultimate strength prediction. In the angle-steel connector beams the neutral axis sat lowest after debonding, giving the lowest scrimber utilization (connector type affects the scrimber-side neutral axis, while concrete type affects the concrete side).
4. **FE-supplied quantitative compression-zone heights**: the concrete compression-zone height was about 60 mm for OC versus about 40 mm for UHPC/ECC (the higher-strength material carries the same stress over smaller area); the scrimber compression zone was about 60 mm under steel-mesh and bolt-notch connectors versus about 90 mm under the angle-steel connector. These serve directly as inputs to the ultimate strength prediction formula.
5. **A unified prediction model built on the composite-action coefficient η**: on top of a strut-and-tie model (concrete = compression strut, scrimber = tension tie, connector = connection node), a ratio η = ns/n₀ ≤ 1 of the actual connector count ns to the required minimum count n₀ was introduced. Interface shear resistance was decomposed into (i) the connector's own shear resistance F_s1 = f_u·A_s, (ii) the surrounding-concrete contribution F_s2 (connector perimeter × 20 mm area × f_t0, with notched types adding the shear contribution of the notch cross-section), and (iii) friction (μ₀ = 0.7, ACI 318-05). Reflecting fiber bridging, the effective tensile resistance of UHPC/ECC was augmented to f_t0 = 1.2·f_t. Final form V_uf = η·min(M_u/a, V_u); verification gave a calculated/experimental ratio averaging 1.04 with COV 0.14.
6. **Conservative treatment of scrimber tension**: because scrimber is brittle in tension with no plastic deformation, the rectangular stress block was confined to representing the equivalent tensile force of the actual triangular distribution; a fully plastic assumption was excluded as yielding unconservative predictions. Ultimate-state compressive stress was adopted as remaining at about 50 % of compressive strength per the simulation results.

## Methods

- **Bamboo scrimber**: density 1241.91 kg/m³, moisture content 2.28 %. Pure compressive strength (f_cb) 141 MPa, pure tensile strength (f_tb) 121 MPa (fiber direction, GB/T 50329-2012). Anisotropic constants for FE input: E₁/E₂/E₃ = 15 879/2878/2315 MPa, G₁₂/G₁₃/G₂₃ = 566/562/152 MPa, ν₁₂/ν₁₃/ν₂₃ = 0.44/0.43/0.47 (fiber direction aligned via a material-axis local coordinate system; failure simulated with a fiber-directional brittle cracking model).
- **Concretes**: OC compression 50.2 MPa · tension 2.8 MPa (body-text statement; recorded as 2.5 MPa in Table 1 — an internal inconsistency in the paper), ECC compression 55.2 MPa · tension 6.6 MPa · ultimate tensile strain 7 %, UHPC compression 125.4 MPa · tension 10.5 MPa. Fibers at 2 vol % in both: PE in ECC (diameter 0.13 mm, length 18 mm, 2600 MPa), steel fiber in UHPC (the source lists the same 0.13 mm × 18 mm · 2600 MPa as for PE). Mix mass ratios (cement basis): OC W/B 0.38 · fine aggregate 2.72 · coarse aggregate 1.11; UHPC W/B 0.25 · silica fume 0.25 · silica sand 0.612 (fine) + 0.488 (coarse); ECC W/B 0.70 · fly ash 1.43 · silica sand 0.94.
- **Specimens**: T-section width 300 mm × height 230 mm × length 1000 mm. A concrete layer 1000×300×80 mm sits atop a scrimber member 1000×100×150 mm. Shear span ratio 1.5 (simulating deep bridge-girder bottoms), clear span 690 mm. Five connectors per group (spacing 180 mm). An HRB400 Φ8@100 rebar mesh was placed 40 mm above the scrimber surface to prevent local spalling on the compression side — minimal contribution to overall flexural strength and stiffness (intended for local damage control).
- **Shear connectors**: (1) angle steel — welded from 40×100×5 mm + 40×40×5 mm plates, fastened with grade 8.8 bolts and nuts; (2) bolt-notch — a grade 8.8 hex bolt M10×120 mm epoxy-embedded into an 80×50×30 mm notch; (3) steel mesh-notch — a 50×130 mm mesh (10 mm holes, staggered layout) epoxy-embedded within the notch. Adhesive: two-component epoxy HRD-RE-10 (bond 2.7 MPa, compression 126.6 MPa, flexure 88.3 MPa, tension 38.4 MPa).
- **Loading and instrumentation**: three-point bending (PWS-30000 hydraulic servo), displacement control at 0.5 mm/min with 15 s observation every 0.5 mm. Nine LVDTs (supports L1-L2, interface slip L3-L5/L9, deflection L6-L8) and nine strain gauges along the height of the mid-section. Initial stiffness = slope of the load-deflection curve up to 75 % of peak load; ductility coefficient = ultimate deflection (point where load falls to 85 % of peak)/peak deflection.
- **FE (Abaqus)**: concrete, connectors, and scrimber as C3D8R solid elements, rebar as T3D2 truss elements (embedded in the concrete), loading and support points as rigid bodies. Tie constraints (concrete-connector, connector-scrimber, etc.) plus surface-to-surface contact only at the concrete-scrimber interface (friction coefficient 0.4). Steel connectors use the continuum damage model (CDM, necking initiation → fracture); cementitious materials use the concrete plastic damage model (CPDM; GB50010-2010, with UHPC on the Lin et al. 2021 experimental curves and ECC on in-house tension tests). Mesh size determined by convergence analysis.

## Results

| Specimen | Concrete | Connector | Ultimate strength (kN) | Initial stiffness (kN/mm) | Ductility coefficient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OC-A | OC | Angle steel | 164 | 68.1 | 1.12 |
| OC-B | OC | Bolt-notch | 241 | 100.4 | 1.05 |
| OC-S | OC | Steel mesh | 243 | 93.3 | 0.98 |
| ECC-A | ECC | Angle steel | 180 | 61.4 | 1.35 |
| ECC-B | ECC | Bolt-notch | 247 | 84.0 | 1.25 |
| ECC-S | ECC | Steel mesh | 250 | 91.3 | 1.14 |
| UHPC-A | UHPC | Angle steel | 207 | 64.5 | 1.30 |
| UHPC-B | UHPC | Bolt-notch | 263 | 109.4 | 1.12 |
| UHPC-S | UHPC | Steel mesh | 277 | 109.1 | 1.13 |

- Concrete-type effect (S-series comparison): UHPC-S gained +14.0 % ultimate strength and +17.2 % initial stiffness over OC-S. ECC matched OC in ultimate strength with ductility +20.5 % (A-series basis). Because elastic moduli are similar (OC 34 GPa to UHPC 44 GPa vs scrimber about 16 GPa), the stiffness difference is limited — the concrete layer's small section height (80 mm vs 150 mm) contributes inherently little flexural rigidity, and the scrimber governs stiffness and tension.
- Connector-type effect: the notched types (bolt, mesh) delivered ultimate strength +27.1 % over angle steel (UHPC basis) with ductility reduced 15.0 %. Angle steel blocks interface slip but provides weak composite action, leaving a double-neutral-axis state in which each material shares the load independently.
- Interface load-transfer mechanism (FE stress tracking): early in loading the concrete around the connectors resists first (principal tensile stress in the surrounding zone reaching tensile strength), after which the connector von Mises stress rises. Angle-steel connectors reach ultimate strength at peak but do not fracture thanks to their ductility; bolt-notch and steel-mesh connectors continue gaining stress past the peak (ultimate strength unreached) — meaning the design has sufficient connectors yet is not fully rigid (partial slip exists).
- Prediction model: flexural ultimate strength M_u = concrete compressive resultant × lever arm + scrimber compressive resultant × lever arm (effective width b_e = b₀ + 2b₁, b₁ = min(span/6, S₁, 6h_c), GB50017-2003 and Nie); shear ultimate strength V_u = V_c + V_bs (V_c = (0.0465 + 0.168e^(−λ^1.514/9.67))·f_c·b_e·h_c, V_bs = τ_tb·b₂·h_s); final V_uf = η·min(M_u/a, V_u). Verification: calculated/experimental average 1.04, COV 0.14.
