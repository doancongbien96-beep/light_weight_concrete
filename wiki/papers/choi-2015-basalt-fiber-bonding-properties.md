# Interfacial Bonding Characteristics of Basalt Fiber and the Strength-Reduction Mechanism with Fiber Inclination Angle

## Summary

To evaluate the suitability of basalt fiber, which offers excellent tensile strength (>1700 MPa), a density close to that of concrete matrices ($2.6\\sim 2.8\\ \\text{g/cm}^3$, no segregation or bleeding), non-corrosiveness, and elevated-temperature durability (90 % of room-temperature strength retained at 600 °C), as a cementitious reinforcement at the micromechanics level, this study with Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University) as corresponding author performed single-fiber pullout tests and inclined-fiber fracture-strength tests at inclination angles of 0°, 30°, 45°, and 67.5°, and identified the cause of strain-softening in basalt-fiber composites through quantitative comparison with PVA and PE fibers together with numerical analysis of a theoretical fiber-bridging constitutive law ($\\sigma-\\delta$). Owing to its inorganic-mineral nature, basalt fiber developed a chemical adhesion energy with cement/AAS matrices ($G_d = 2.59\\ \\text{J/m}^2$) as much as **87.6 %** higher than oil-coated PVA fiber ($1.38\\ \\text{J/m}^2$) along with excellent frictional bond strength ($\\tau_0 = 1.08\\ \\text{MPa}$); however, its slip-hardening coefficient ($\\beta = 0.0054$) was a mere 24.4 % of that of PVA (0.0221), and its fiber strength-reduction coefficient ($f' = 1.535$) reached **9 times** that of PVA (0.171) and **3.2 times** that of PE (0.475), proving that steeply inclined fibers undergo abrupt premature rupture (an 83 % strength loss at 67.5°).

## Key Contributions

1. **Identification of the ultra-high chemical bonding and brittle-fracture mechanism of basalt fiber**:
   - Because its main constituents are minerals such as silica and alumina, similar to the matrix itself, basalt fiber forms a powerful chemical bond of $G_d = 2.59\\ \\text{J/m}^2$ through interfacial hydration reactions.
   - However, it was clarified that this excessive chemical adhesion causes simultaneous fiber rupture rather than frictional slip pullout when cracks open, inducing embrittlement and strain-softening of the composite.
2. **First quantification of the strength-reduction coefficient ($f'$) with fiber inclination angle**:
   - Inclined tensile strengths were precisely measured from 0° up to 67.5° using a snubbing-effect test rig:
     $$\\sigma_{fu}(\\theta) = \\sigma_{fu}(0) e^{-f' \\theta}$$
   - Owing to its intrinsically brittle crystalline structure, basalt fiber proved extremely vulnerable to corner shear-flexural stress, recording $f' = 1.535$ (a sharp drop from 1773 MPa at 0° to 302 MPa at 67.5°, an 83 % loss).
3. **Theoretical fiber-bridging stress–crack opening displacement ($\\sigma-\\delta$) analysis and directions for improvement**:
   - Predicting the three-dimensional randomly oriented bridging stress curve from the derived micromechanics parameters showed that the basalt-fiber system exhibits softening behavior in which, despite a high initial cracking strength, the bridging stress collapses immediately as crack opening increases.
   - It was demonstrated that reducing $G_d$ through organic surface coating of the fibers together with toughness-enhancing surface treatment is essential to secure ductile (strain-hardening) behavior in basalt-fiber composites.

## Methods

- **Materials and fiber properties (Table 2 & 3)**:
  - **Basalt fiber**: diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, density $2.7\\ \\text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength 1773 MPa, elastic modulus 89 GPa; principal chemical composition ($\\text{SiO}_2$ 52.8 %, $\\text{Al}_2\\text{O}_3$ 17.5 %, $\\text{FeO}+\\text{Fe}_2\\text{O}_3$ 10.3 %, $\\text{CaO}$ 8.6 %, $\\text{MgO}$ 4.8 %).
  - **PVA fiber**: diameter 40 µm, length 12 mm, density $1.3\\ \\text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength 1202 MPa, elastic modulus 41 GPa (1.2 % oil coating).
  - **PE fiber**: diameter 31 µm, length 12 mm, density $0.97\\ \\text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength 2757 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa.
- **Matrix mix (mass ratio, Table 4)**: binder 1.0, water 0.34, silica sand 0.40, high-range water reducer 0.01, defoamer 0.0002 (42 MPa-class alkali-activated mortar).
- **Single-fiber pullout test**:
  - Fiber embedded length $l_e \\approx 1.0\\ \\text{mm}$ in an acrylic mold (inducing full debonding), demolded at 2 days and then water cured.
  - Electronic tensile testing machine fitted with a 5 N precision load cell, displacement rate 0.1 mm/min.
  - $G_d$, $\\tau_0$, and $\\beta$ determined from the debonding load $P_a$ and the frictional-slip onset load $P_b$:
    $$G_d = \\frac{2(P_a - P_b)^2}{\\pi^2 E_f d_f^3}, \\quad \\tau_0 = \\frac{P_b}{\\pi d_f l_e}, \\quad \\beta = \\left(\\frac{d_f}{l_f}\\right)\\left[\\left(\\frac{1}{\\tau_0 \\pi d_f}\\right)\\left.\\frac{dP}{ds}\\right|_{s\\to 0} + 1\\right]$$
- **Inclined-angle fiber strength test**:
  - Custom aluminum jigs and acrylic plates fabricated; tensile fracture loads measured at inclination angles of $\\theta = 0^\\circ, 30^\\circ, 45^\\circ, 67.5^\\circ$ (15 or more tests per angle).

## Results

### 1. Single fiber–matrix interfacial bond characteristics (Table 5)

| Fiber type | Chemical adhesion energy $G_d$ ($\\text{J/m}^2$) | Frictional bond strength $\\tau_0$ (MPa) | Slip-hardening coefficient $\\beta$ | Interfacial failure-mode characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basalt fiber** | **2.59 ± 0.20 (+87.6 %)** | **1.08 ± 0.19 (+2.9 %)** | **0.0054 ± 0.0005 (−75.6 %)** | Ultra-high chemical bonding, absent slip hardening, rupture-dominated |
| **PVA fiber (1.2 % oil)** | 1.38 ± 0.29 | 1.05 ± 0.30 | 0.0221 ± 0.0032 | Moderate chemical bonding + excellent slip hardening |

### 2. Tensile strength and strength-reduction coefficient by fiber inclination angle (Table 6)

| Fiber type | Tensile strength at 0° (MPa) | Strength at 30° (MPa) | Strength at 45° (MPa) | Strength at 67.5° (MPa) | Strength-reduction coefficient $f'$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basalt fiber** | 1773 ± 349 | 871 ± 247 (−50.9 %) | 715 ± 268 (−59.7 %) | **302 ± 219 (−83.0 %)** | **1.535 (9 times that of PVA)** |
| **PVA fiber** | 1202 ± 132 | 1114 ± 182 (−7.3 %) | 1025 ± 223 (−14.7 %) | 1003 ± 161 (−16.6 %) | **0.171** |
| **PE fiber** | 2757 ± 220 | 1962 ± 458 (−28.8 %) | 1867 ± 112 (−32.3 %) | 1697 ± 249 (−38.4 %) | **0.475** |

### 3. Micromechanics modeling and fiber-bridging behavior
- Analysis with model inputs ($E_m = 20\\ \\text{GPa}$, matrix spalling coefficient 500) showed that the basalt-fiber system exhibits **strain softening**, with fiber ruptures concentrated immediately after crack opening collapsing the bridging stress, whereas the PVA-fiber system satisfied **strain-hardening** conditions through a gradual rise in bridging stress.
