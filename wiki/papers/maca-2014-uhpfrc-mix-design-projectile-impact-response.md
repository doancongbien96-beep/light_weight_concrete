# Mix Design of 175 MPa-Class UHPFRC and Impact Penetration Resistance Against 7.62 mm Rifle Bullets ($710\sim 830\ \text{m/s}$): Zero Rear-Face Spalling and Demonstration of the Optimal Fiber Content (2 vol.%)

## Summary

To optimize the mix design of ultra-high-performance fiber-reinforced concrete (UHPFRC) for military protective structures and extreme ballistic facilities and to characterize its resistance to small-caliber high-velocity projectiles, **high-strength micro straight steel fibers ($V_f = 0\%, 1\%, 2\%, 3\%$) were incorporated into a high-density particle-packed matrix substituted with waste glass powder (GP) and silica fume (SF)** to produce 175 MPa-class UHPFRC, and uniaxial direct tension, flexural modulus-of-rupture, and elastic-modulus tests together with **live-fire tests on 50 mm thin slabs using 7.62 mm caliber rifle bullets (deformable lead-core and non-deformable steel-core tips, impact velocities $710\sim 830\ \text{m/s}$)** were carried out, demonstrating that **at a steel fiber content of 2.0 vol.% the direct tensile strength of $11.8\ \text{MPa}$ and flexural strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ are developed while, under live $710\ \text{m/s}$ bullet impact, penetration depth is locally suppressed to 15 mm and rear-face fragment scabbing is completely blocked** — a Materials & Design 2014 landmark paper by **Prof. Petr Máca and Prof. Radoslav Sovják of the Czech Technical University in Prague (CTU in Prague)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of a 175 MPa-class high-density packed UHPFRC mix utilizing waste glass powder (GP)**:
   - Finely ground waste glass powder was recycled as a micro filler, reducing the usage of expensive cement and silica fume while lowering hydration heat and achieving a 28-day compressive strength of $175.2\ \text{MPa}$.
2. **Establishment of the uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening and volumetric energy-absorption mechanism of UHPFRC**:
   - In mixes with 2–3 % steel fibers, tensile strain hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 0.45\%$) after first elastic cracking is realized through fine bridging, amplifying fracture energy by more than $800\%$.
3. **Reduction of penetration depth by $60\%$ and blocking of rear-face spalling under 7.62 mm hypervelocity live fire ($710\sim 830\ \text{m/s}$)**:
   - While unreinforced UHPC slabs failed by complete perforation and frontal comminution, the 50 mm thin UHPFRC slabs limited penetration depth to 15 mm and sealed rear-face damage completely at 0.0 mm.
4. **Identification of the optimal fiber volume fraction ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) balancing ballistic performance and economy**:
   - Even when fiber content was increased from 2 % to 3 %, changes in nose penetration depth ($15.2 \rightarrow 14.8\ \text{mm}$) and crater area were marginal, proving $V_f = 2.0\%$ to be the optimal structural ballistic threshold.

## Methods

- **UHPFRC mix design (Section 2.1 & Tables 1–2)**:
  - Binders: OPC CEM I 52.5R + silica fume (SF) + waste glass powder (GP, $d_{50} = 15\ \mu\text{m}$) (W/B = 0.18, HRWR added).
  - Aggregates: two quartz sands ($0.1\sim 0.6\ \text{mm}$ and $0.3\sim 0.8\ \text{mm}$).
  - Steel fibers: straight high-strength steel fibers ($d_f = 0.175\ \text{mm}$, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2200\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Four levels of fiber volume fraction: `UHPC` (0 %), `UHPFRC 3-1` (1 %), `UHPFRC 3-2` (2 %), `UHPFRC 3-3` (3 %).
- **Mechanical testing and live-fire impact testing (Sections 2.2–3.3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Compressive strength and elastic modulus: $\phi 100 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylinders (ground finish, displacement control).
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone-shaped specimens (closed-loop displacement control).
  - Live-fire test: $300 \times 400 \times 50\ \text{mm}$ flat slab panels (clamped in a fixed jig, firing distance 25 m).
  - Ammunition specifications: 7.62×39 mm rifle rounds (deformable lead-core tip at $710\ \text{m/s}$; non-deformable hardened steel-core tip at $830\ \text{m/s}$).

### 2. Analysis of High-Velocity Projectile Energy Absorption and Crater Suppression Mechanisms
- **Confinement of frontal comminution and induction of projectile plastic deformation**: at the instant of nose impact, the micro steel fibers confine the pulverization of concrete three-dimensionally in the ultra-high-pressure zone, shrinking the crater diameter by $50\%$, and the kinetic energy of the nose is absorbed by the flattening plastic deformation (mushrooming) of the core itself.
- **Capturing the rear-face tensile release wave**: the intense tensile fracturing wave generated when the shock wave reflects from the free rear surface is captured fully in tension by the dense fiber bridging network, completely blocking rear-face fragment scabbing despite the thin 50 mm slab.

- **Overall conclusion**: By optimally placing 2.0 vol.% micro steel fibers in ultra-high-density particle-packed UHPFRC, this paper completely defeats perforation of a 50 mm thin slab even under the hypervelocity impact of a 7.62 mm rifle bullet ($710\ \text{m/s}$) and achieves zero rear-face spalling — a top-level landmark paper in the field of protective ballistic structures.
