## Summary
To detect near-surface delamination and void defects in reinforced-concrete bridge decks and road pavement structures rapidly and without contact, this study evaluated the defect detection limits and mutual complementarity of an air-coupled impact-echo (AC-IE) system and infrared thermography (IRT) technology. On full-scale concrete slab specimens with artificial delamination defects embedded (depths of 20 ~ 75 mm, sizes of 50 ~ 300 mm), solar/halogen heating thermography and microphone acoustic scanning were performed. IRT proved excellent for areal detection of shallow delaminations ($d \le 40 mm$), while AC-IE excelled at quantifying the depth of deep defects ($d > 50 mm$); based on these findings, a combined non-destructive diagnosis framework is proposed.

## Key Contributions
- **Mapping of the AC-IE acoustic frequency response onto flexural-mode vibration models**: established an algorithm that back-calculates the delamination depth from the natural flexural vibration frequency ($f_{flex}$) of the thin concrete plate above the defect.
- **Sensitivity interpretation of the thermographic temperature difference ($\Delta T$)**: identified the defect diameter-to-depth ratio ($D/d \ge 2.0$) as the critical geometric condition for effective thermographic detection.
- **Creation of multi-sensor data-fusion diagnosis maps**: combined thermographic area scans with acoustic cross-section scans, reducing the false-positive rate by 80 %.

## Methods
- **Specimen**: concrete slab of $2000 \times 1500 \times 250 mm$ (with artificial delamination defects of various depths and sizes embedded).
- **Instrumentation system**: focused microphone-array AC-IE system, high-resolution thermal camera (FLIR, sensitivity $< 0.03 °C$), and halogen lamp heat source.

## Results
- Shallow defect ($d = 25 mm$): clearly detected in IRT with a surface temperature difference of $\Delta T = 2.8 °C$.
- Deep defect ($d = 65 mm$): not detectable by IRT, but AC-IE discriminated its depth with 95 % accuracy through an $8.2 kHz$ flexural vibration peak.
