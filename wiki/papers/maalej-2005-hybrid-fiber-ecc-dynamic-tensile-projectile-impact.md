# Dynamic Tensile Behavior (DIF = 1.45) of Steel-Fiber/PE-Fiber Hybrid ECC and Resistance to Ultra-High-Velocity Projectile ($300\sim 750\ \text{m/s}$) Impact: Demonstration of Zero Rear-Face Spalling and Repeated-Hit Protection

## Summary

To maximize the high-velocity impact-penetration resistance and rear-face scabbing-prevention performance of blast-resistant bunkers and military protective walls, **a hybrid-fiber ECC optimally combining stiff straight steel fibers ($V_f = 1.5\%$) with low-modulus ultra-high-ductility PE fibers ($V_f = 0.5\%$)** was developed, and dynamic uniaxial direct tensile tests across strain rates ($\dot{\varepsilon} = 2\times 10^{-6} \sim 0.2\ \text{s}^{-1}$) plus panel impact-penetration tests at each thickness (55–150 mm) and repeated-impact tests using a **compressed-helium gas gun firing high-velocity projectiles ($V_0 = 300\sim 750\ \text{m/s}$, 15 g ogive-nosed projectile)** demonstrated **a dynamic increase factor (DIF) of 1.45, retention of $2.5\%$ tensile ductility under high strain rates, and complete protection performance with no rear-face spalling fragment ejection whatsoever even under $750\ \text{m/s}$ ultra-high-velocity impact**: a 2005 landmark original paper by Professors Mohamed Maalej and Ser Tong Quek of the National University of Singapore (NUS), published in the ASCE Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering.

## Key Contributions

1. **Construction of the complementary steel-fiber/PE-fiber hybrid bridging system**:
   - Establishes the mutually complementary mechanism in which steel fibers take charge of the high-stress tensile strength ($> 6.5\ \text{MPa}$) while PE fibers supply long-distance pullout ductility without rupture at crack opening displacement ($\varepsilon_u > 2.5\%$).
2. **Quantification of the dynamic tensile strain-rate effect (DIF) and demonstration of ductility retention**:
   - Proves that even when the strain rate increases $10^5$-fold relative to the quasi-static state ($\dot{\varepsilon} = 0.2\ \text{s}^{-1}$), the tensile strength increases by $45\%$ (DIF = 1.45) without embrittlement while maintaining multiple fine cracking.
3. **Complete suppression of rear-face spalling under ultra-high-velocity projectile ($300\sim 750\ \text{m/s}$) impact**:
   - While normal and high-strength concrete panels shattered and broke apart into fragments, the 55 mm ultra-thin hybrid ECC panel showed only finely dispersed rear-face cracking with zero (0.0) fragment detachment, exhibiting extreme toughness.
4. **Demonstration of the protective reliability of multiple hits at the same location (Repeated Impact)**:
   - Even when a $465\ \text{m/s}$ high-velocity projectile was fired twice consecutively at the same aim point, the panel was not perforated and retained its residual thickness, completely preserving its secondary protection performance.

## Methods

- **Hybrid ECC mix design (Section 2.1 & Table 1)**:
  - Matrix: OPC + fly ash (FA/C = 1.2) + silica sand (S/B = 0.36) + SP.
  - Fiber hybrid:
    - Steel fiber: micro straight steel fiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 0.16\ \text{mm}$, $L_f = 6\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2000\ \text{MPa}$).
    - PE fiber: Spectra 900 ($V_f = 0.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2600\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Test methods (Sections 2.2–2.3 & Figs. 2–3)**:
  - Dynamic tensile test: servo-hydraulic Instron UTM (loading rates $0.02\sim 2000\ \text{mm/min}$, gauge length 140 mm).
  - Gas-gun high-velocity impact test: compressed helium gas gun, projectile mass 15 g, diameter 12.6 mm, CRH 2.5 ogive nose.
  - Panel dimensions: $200 \times 200\ \text{mm}$, thicknesses $t = 55, 75, 100, 120, 150\ \text{mm}$.

### 2. Mechanism analysis of high-strain-rate impact absorption and rear-face fragment-ejection blocking
- **Front-face tunneling restraint and projectile fragmentation**: the high-modulus steel fibers restrain the ultra-high-pressure crushing zone ahead of the nose in three dimensions, reducing the crater diameter to one quarter of that of normal concrete, while the kinetic energy of the nose is dissipated through plastic deformation of the projectile body itself.
- **Reflected tensile shock-wave capture**: the flexible PE fibers bridge and absorb the strong tensile shock wave reflected from the rear face, preventing rear-face tensile fracture and fragment ejection at their source.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the most authoritative landmark original paper in the fields of protective structures and extreme-load-resisting materials engineering, in which Prof. Mohamed Maalej achieved zero rear-face spalling and repeated-hit resistance even under ultra-high-velocity projectile impact ($\le 750\ \text{m/s}$) through the hybrid synergy of steel and PE fibers.
