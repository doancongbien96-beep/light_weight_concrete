# Fracture Toughness of Basalt Fiber-Reinforced Geopolymer Concrete: Critical Stress Intensity Factor ($K_{Ic} = 1.95\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$), 3.5-Fold Fracture Energy Amplification, and Early High Toughness Development

## Summary

To mitigate carbon emissions from Portland cement manufacturing and develop eco-friendly cementless structural materials exhibiting rapid early-strength and high thermal resistance, Professor Clelio Thaumaturgo and Dr. Dylmar P. Dias at the Instituto Militar de Engenharia (IME) in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, manufactured geopolymer concrete by reinforcing metakaolin-based poly(sialate-siloxo) (PSS) geopolymer binder with natural basalt chopped microfibers ($d_f = 13\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}$, volume fractions $V_f = 0.5, 1.0\ \text{vol.}\%$). Through single-edge notched beam three-point bending tests (span-to-depth ratio 4.0), RILEM two-parameter fracture model evaluation, and SEM-EDS interfacial microstructure analysis, the authors demonstrated rapid early (7-day) compressive strength development ($52.0\ \text{MPa}$), 28-day fracture toughness reaching $K_{Ic} = 1.95\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$, 3.5-fold amplification in fracture energy, and chemical erosion resistance of basalt fibers within the alkaline geopolymer matrix compared with ordinary Portland cement (OPC) concrete.

## Key Contributions

1. **First quantification of linear elastic and elastoplastic fracture toughness in geopolymer-basalt fiber composites**:
   - Overcame the brittle defects of geopolymer matrices through microfiber bridging, evaluating the critical stress intensity factor ($K_{Ic}$) and critical crack-tip opening displacement ($\text{CTOD}_c$).
2. **Verification of basalt fiber chemical stability in alkali-activated geopolymer matrices**:
   - SEM observations confirmed that basalt aluminosilicate mineral fibers suffered no chemical erosion and maintained strong interfacial bonding under high-alkalinity ($\text{pH} > 13.5$) geopolymer hydration environments.
3. **Rapid early strength and early fracture toughness development**:
   - Reached over 90 % of the 28-day fracture toughness within 7 days of ambient curing, verifying suitability for emergency repair and precast military protective structures.
4. **Demonstration of 3.5-fold fracture energy amplification relative to OPC concrete**:
   - A 1.0 vol.% dosage of basalt fibers dissipated stable fiber pullout energy in the post-peak softening regime, preventing catastrophic brittle collapse.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and geopolymer concrete mixture design (Section 2 & Tables 1–2)**:
  - Binder: Potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) + potassium silicate-activated metakaolin PSS geopolymer (Control: rapid-hardening Portland cement PC).
  - Aggregates: Silica sand fine aggregate + granite coarse aggregate ($d_{max} = 9.5\ \text{mm}$, binder:sand:gravel = 1:2.25:3.25, W/B = 0.80).
  - Fibers: Continuous filament basalt microfibers ($d_f = 13\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 2800\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 89\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Fracture mechanics testing program (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Three-point bend single-edge notched beam tests: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ (notch depth $a_0 = 40\ \text{mm}$, $a_0/W = 0.40$).
  - Instrumentation: Clip gage (CMOD), LVDT displacement transducer, total fracture energy ($G_F$) integrated from load-CMOD curves.

### 2. Geopolymer 3D aluminosilicate network structure and fiber bond analysis
- **Compatibility between nanoporous geopolymer matrix and basalt fibers**: Major constituents of basalt fibers ($\text{SiO}_2, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) share chemical affinity with the geopolymer gel, forming a homogeneous 3D Si-O-Al covalent network without weak calcium hydroxide crystals in the interfacial transition zone (ITZ).
- **Crack deflection and interfacial frictional pullout**: Propagating main cracks were deflected upon encountering basalt fibers, and multiple fiber frictional pullout amplified total fracture energy by 4.4-fold.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work quantified fracture mechanics parameters of basalt fiber-reinforced cementless geopolymer concrete, verifying $K_{Ic} = 1.95\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$ and a 4.4-fold improvement in fracture energy for sustainable composite design.
