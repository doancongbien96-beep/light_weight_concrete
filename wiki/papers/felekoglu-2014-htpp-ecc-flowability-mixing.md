# Matrix Rheological Flowability, High-Shear Mixing, and Water Curing to Achieve 3.9% Tensile Strain Capacity in High-Tenacity Polypropylene (HTPP) Fiber ECC

## Summary

To reduce the manufacturing cost of strain-hardening engineered cementitious composites (ECC) by replacing PVA fibers, Professors Victor C. Li at the University of Michigan and Burak Felekoglu at Dokuz Eylul University addressed the fiber balling/clumping issues of high-tenacity polypropylene (HTPP: diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 850 MPa, volume fraction $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) by systematically investigating matrix flowability (high flow HF vs low flow LF), three mixing procedures (P1 standard low-speed, P2 high-speed high-shear, P3 dry pre-mixing), and curing regimes (air drying A vs water immersion W). Fresh spread diameter, 28 d compressive strength (30~70 MPa), uniaxial direct tensile response (JSCE dogbone), and high-resolution digital image crack widths (CW) were analyzed. The results revealed that excessively fluid matrices fail to impart sufficient shear stress to disperse fiber bundles, whereas **low-flow (LF) matrices with adequate plastic viscosity combined with 1 min of high-shear mixing (P2) achieved 100 % individual fiber dispersion**. Under optimal mixing and water curing (P2-LF-W), HTPP-ECC achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **55.0 MPa**, uniaxial tensile strength of **4.2 MPa**, and tensile strain capacity of **3.91 %**, matching standard PVA-ECC performance at significantly lower cost.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of fiber dispersion mechanics in moderate-viscosity matrices with high-shear mixing (P2)**:
   - Demonstrated that dispersing $12\ \mu\text{m}$ micro-HTPP fibers requires a balance between slurry plastic viscosity ($\mu_{pl}$) and mixer blade rotational shear stress ($\tau = \mu_{pl} \cdot \dot{\gamma}$).
2. **Achievement of 3.9 % tensile strain capacity using low-cost HTPP fibers**:
   - Replaced surface-oiled PVA fibers with hydrophobic HTPP fibers while achieving stable pseudo strain-hardening (PSH) with 28 d tensile elongation of **1.91 % ~ 3.91 %**, reducing material costs by over 40 %.
3. **Maximization of saturated multiple microcracking via water curing**:
   - Water curing increased multiple crack counts from 4~6 in air to **25 ~ 35**, reducing mean crack width at 1 % strain from $258\ \mu\text{m}$ to **$74\ \mu\text{m}$**.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and HTPP-ECC mixture proportions (Table 1)**:
  - Binders: Type I ordinary Portland cement $360\ \text{kg/m}^3$ + Class F fly ash $1008\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (FA/C = 2.80, W/B = 0.23).
  - Fine aggregate: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, sand/binder = 0.36).
  - Reinforcing fibers: HTPP fibers (diameter $d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$, length $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 850 MPa, elastic modulus 4.0 GPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - Flowability grades: High flow (HF: SP/B = 0.50 %, mini-flow 190 mm) vs Low flow (LF: SP/B = 0.15 %, mini-flow 150 mm).
- **Mixing procedures (three series)**:
  - P1: Dry mix 1 min $\rightarrow$ water+SP at low speed (140 rpm) for 3 min $\rightarrow$ fibers added at low speed for 2 min.
  - P2 (Optimum): Following P1, **added 1 min of high-speed (420 rpm) intense shear mixing**.
  - P3: Dry pre-mixing of dry powders and fibers before adding water.
- **Testing program**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: JSCE standard dogbone specimens ($13\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 80\ \text{mm}$, displacement rate $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$, dual LVDTs).
  - Digital image crack analysis: High-resolution camera capturing crack widths ($20\sim 45\ \mu\text{m}$, Eq. 1).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cubes (ASTM C109, 28 d air/water curing).

### 2. Micromechanics and crack control analysis
- **Suppression of clumping**: P2 high-shear mixing homogenized fiber spatial number density, preventing single-crack softening and satisfying the PSH energy condition ($J'_b/J_{tip} \ge 3.0$).
- **Enhanced chemical bonding via water curing**: Continuous hydration increased ITZ interfacial friction ($\tau_0$), raising fiber pullout bridging stress to 4.2 MPa.

- **Overall conclusion**: Combining moderate-viscosity matrices with high-shear mixing and water curing achieved 3.9 % tensile strain capacity and 74 µm crack width control in low-cost HTPP-ECC.
