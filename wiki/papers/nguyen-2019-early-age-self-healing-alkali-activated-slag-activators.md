# Activator-Type-Dependent Self-Healing of $45\ \mu\text{m}$ Microcracks at $100\%$ and the Resonant Frequency Stiffness Recovery Mechanism ($85.2\%$) in Early-Age (7-Day) Damaged Alkali-Activated Slag Composites (PE-AAS)

## Summary

To spontaneously seal early cracks generated during initial construction of structures (early age) and secure long-term durability, **PE fiber-reinforced alkali-activated slag composites (PE-AAS, $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$)** in which uniaxial tensile crack damage was imposed at the early age of 7 days were subjected to **28 days of water-immersion self-healing curing under three types of alkaline activators (`AAS-Ca`: $\text{Ca(OH)}_2+\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$, `AAS-Na`: $\text{NaOH}$, `AAS-NaS`: $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3+\text{NaOH}$)**, while the optical-microscopy crack width reduction ratio ($W_c$), the impact resonant frequency (RF) dynamic elastic stiffness recovery rate, and SEM-EDS and XRD microchemical phase analysis were precisely evaluated, establishing **complete closure of early-age microcracks up to $45\ \mu\text{m}$ at $100\%$ with $85.2\%$ resonant frequency stiffness recovery, along with the combined calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and C-A-S-H gel healing precipitation mechanism, in the `AAS-Ca` mixture** — the 2019 landmark origin paper of the team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author) and Dr. Jung-Il Choi of Chonnam National University together with Prof. Hyung-Ki Kim of Chosun University**, published in Construction and Building Materials.

## Key Contributions

1. **First elucidation of the self-healing mechanism of alkali-activated slag composites against early-age (7-day) damage**:
   - The spontaneous healing performance of the cement-free slag system and the formation rate of its healing products fully demonstrated for shrinkage and externally forced cracks occurring at an early age.
2. **Quantification of the early-age self-healing critical crack width by alkaline activator type**:
   - Demonstrated that the $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$-based activator (`AAS-Ca`) completely seals cracks up to $45\ \mu\text{m}$ at $100\%$, exerting the most outstanding early-age healing capability compared with $\text{NaOH}$ ($30\ \mu\text{m}$) and sodium silicate ($20\ \mu\text{m}$).
3. **Confirmation of the dynamic elastic stiffness recovery rate ($85.2\%$) through the impact resonant frequency (RF) nondestructive test**:
   - Proved that the dynamic modulus of elasticity degraded by early cracking was restored to $85.2\%$ through 28 days of water-immersion healing, re-establishing structural integrity at an early stage.
4. **Crystallographic analysis of the healing products (calcite $\text{CaCO}_3$ and hydrotalcite/C-A-S-H gel)**:
   - Proved that the $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ activator releases abundant calcium ions at an early age, which combine with $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ in the water to rapidly block the crack entrances with dense calcite crystals.

## Methods

- **Materials and PE-AAS mixture composition (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS, specific surface area $435\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$, $\text{CaO} = 41.5\%$).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Fiber: Spectra PE fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2700\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 88\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Three alkaline activators:
    1. `AAS-Ca`: $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (10 wt.%) + $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (3 wt.%).
    2. `AAS-Na`: $\text{NaOH}$ aqueous solution (3 wt.% $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$).
    3. `AAS-NaS`: liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Ms} = 1.0$, 4 wt.% $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$).
- **Early-age damage and healing test procedure (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Early-age damage imposition: uniaxial tensile strain of $1.5\%$ applied to dumbbell-shaped specimens at 7 days of age and released $\rightarrow$ initial crack width ($W_i$) measurement.
  - Healing condition: underwater immersion at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 28 days (measurements at 7, 14, 21, and 28 days).
  - Resonant frequency (RF) measurement: FFT spectrum analysis of longitudinal impact vibration.
  - Microstructure analysis: SEM/EDS elemental analysis and XRD diffraction analysis.

### 2. Interpretation of the early-age healing reaction rate and carbonation mechanism
- **Abundant $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ supply from $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$**: at the early age of 7 days, slag hydration is still in progress, so unreacted $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ dissolves out immediately into the crack pore water and induces a rapid carbonation reaction with dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ in the water, completely sealing the $45\ \mu\text{m}$ cracks within a short period.
- **Synergy with secondary slag hydration**: residual slag particles undergo secondary hydration driven by infiltrating water, precipitating fine C-A-S-H gel on the inner walls of the cracks and achieving physical closure and chemical stiffness recovery simultaneously.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark origin paper in the field of eco-friendly early-age self-healing construction materials, in which the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University demonstrated, through the $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ activator, $100\%$ closure of cracks up to $45\ \mu\text{m}$ and $85.2\%$ dynamic elastic stiffness recovery in cement-free alkali-activated slag composites cracked at the early age of 7 days.
