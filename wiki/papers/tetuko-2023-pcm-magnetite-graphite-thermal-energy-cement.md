## Summary
To overcome the slow heat charging/discharging caused by the low thermal conductivity ($< 1.0 W/(m\cdot K)$) of cement-based thermal energy storage composites, this study investigated the thermodynamic behavior of a hybrid PCM cement composite in which a paraffin phase-change material was co-encapsulated with high-conductivity expanded graphite (EG) and magnetic magnetite ($Fe_3O_4$) nanoparticles. The expanded graphite forms a three-dimensional thermally conductive skeleton while the magnetite particles assist electromagnetic induction heating and heat conduction, increasing the thermal conductivity of the composite 2.8-fold and shortening its heat charging time by 60 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Fabrication of ternary hybrid PCM encapsulation (paraffin/EG/magnetite)**: capillary adsorption by the expanded graphite prevents liquid-paraffin leakage at the source ($\Delta H = 145 J/g$).
- **Dramatic enhancement of thermal conductivity**: the thermal conductivity of the cement composite increased by 182 %, from $0.75 W/(m\cdot K)$ to $2.12 W/(m\cdot K)$.
- **Demonstration of electromagnetic-thermal conversion and energy-storage multifunctionality**: realization of smart energy cement capable of rapid induction heating and latent heat storage under an alternating magnetic field.

## Methods
- **Material synthesis**: vacuum impregnation of paraffin wax with $Fe_3O_4$ nanoparticles (20 nm) and expanded graphite (5 wt %).
- **Cement mixing**: hybrid PCM particles incorporated into cement paste at 10–25 wt %.
- **Measurements**: laser-flash thermal diffusivity measurement, DSC latent-heat measurement, compressive strength.

## Results
- Cement incorporating 15 wt % hybrid PCM: latent heat capacity $21.5 J/g$, thermal conductivity $2.12 W/(m\cdot K)$, 28 d compressive strength $36.8 MPa$.
- Excellent thermal stability demonstrated, with latent heat loss below 1.2 % even after 500 thermal cycles.
