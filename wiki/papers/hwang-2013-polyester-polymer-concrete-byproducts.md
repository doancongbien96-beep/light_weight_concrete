# Unsaturated Polyester Polymer Concrete Using Spherical Industrial By-Products (Rapid-Cooled Steel Slag and Fly Ash): 21.3 % Binder Reduction, Ultra-High Strength, and Hot-Water Degradation Mechanics

## Summary

To improve the economic feasibility of polymer concrete (PC)—which exhibits superior mechanical strength and durability but suffers from high resin binder costs (>80 % of total material cost)—a collaborative research team from Kongju National University and UT Austin (Professor Jung Heon Yeon) substituted conventional fillers ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and fine aggregates (river sand) with spherical industrial by-products: **fly ash (20 vol.% filler replacement) and rapid-cooled steel slag (RCSS: $0\sim 100\ \text{vol.\%}$ fine aggregate replacement)**. Across unsaturated polyester (UP) resin binder dosages (7.5, 8.0, 8.5, 9.0 wt.%), the authors evaluated **28 d compressive strength (up to 105.0 MPa), flexural strength (up to 24.5 MPa), water absorption, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), hot water resistance ($80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ immersion for 3 d), and SEM microstructures**. The ball-bearing effect of spherical RCSS significantly improved mixture workability, enabling a **21.3 % reduction in polymer binder requirement ($10.8 \rightarrow 8.5\ \text{wt.\%}$)** and an **18.5 % reduction in total material cost** in the optimal mix (50 vol.% RCSS, 8.5 wt.% binder). This formulation achieved a compressive strength of **$102.5\ \text{MPa}$**, flexural strength of **$23.8\ \text{MPa}$**, and water absorption below **$0.05\%$**. Furthermore, $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ hot-water tests revealed that thermal expansion mismatches between resin and high-conductivity RCSS ($14.7\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$, $50 \times 10^{-6}/^\circ\text{C}$ vs $6 \times 10^{-6}/^\circ\text{C}$) induce interfacial micro-debonding under sustained hydrothermal exposure.

## Key Contributions

1. **21.3 % binder reduction and 18.5 % total material cost reduction**:
   - Maximized particle packing using spherical fly ash and RCSS, producing 100 MPa polymer concrete at a minimal resin content of 8.5 wt.%.
2. **Elimination of steel slag volumetric expansion defects**:
   - Resolved the $\text{CaO}$ hydration expansion instability of steel slag by incorporating it into a non-aqueous hydrophobic polymer matrix (100 % volume stability).
3. **Over 55 % reduction in MIP porosity and water absorption**:
   - Exploited the near-zero absorption (0.005 %) and dense packing of RCSS to eliminate capillary porosity, keeping absorption below 0.05 %.
4. **Hot-water ($80\ ^\circ\text{C}$) thermal expansion mismatch stress analysis**:
   - Quantified interfacial shear stresses and degradation mechanisms driven by thermal conductivity and expansion coefficient differentials between polymer resin and steel slag aggregates.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mix designs (Tables 1–4, mass proportions)**:
  - Polymer binder: Unsaturated polyester resin (UP, viscosity $0.8\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, density $1.12\ \text{g/cm}^3$) 80 wt.% + shrinkage-reducing agent 20 wt.% (initiator MEKPO 1.0 wt.%, promoter cobalt octoate 0.5 wt.%).
  - Filler: Calcium carbonate powder vs spherical fly ash (surface area $4100\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, 20 vol.% replacement).
  - Fine aggregates: River sand ($d \le 2.5\ \text{mm}$) vs rapid-cooled steel slag (RCSS beads, density $3.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$, absorption 0.005 %, 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 vol.% replacement).
  - Coarse aggregates: Crushed granite ($d_{max} = 13\ \text{mm}$, 51.0~52.5 wt.%).
  - Resin dosages: 7.5, 8.0, 8.5, 9.0 wt.% (20 mix series cured at $20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 14 d).
- **Testing program (Section 2)**:
  - Mechanical testing: Compression (ASTM C39, $\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$), flexure (ASTM C78, $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$).
  - Porosity: Micromeritics AutoPore IV 9500 MIP (contact angle $130^\circ$).
  - Hot water resistance: $80\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$ water bath immersion for 3 d measuring residual mechanical properties and mass changes.
  - Microstructure: JEOL JSM-6380 SEM.

### 2. Economic optimization and thermal degradation mechanics
- **Cost optimization**: Compared to commercial PC formulations (10.8 wt.% binder), the 50 % RCSS mix (8.5 wt.% binder) cut binder usage by 21.3 % and lowered unit production cost by 18.5 %.
- **Hydrothermal degradation**: Elevated thermal conductivity ($14.7\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) and thermal expansion mismatch ($\Delta \alpha \approx 44 \times 10^{-6}/^\circ\text{C}$) accelerated moisture ingress and induced localized debonding at aggregate interfaces under $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ immersion.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that spherical steel slag and fly ash aggregates reduce resin binder demand by 21 % while achieving 100 MPa compressive strength in cost-effective polymer concrete.
