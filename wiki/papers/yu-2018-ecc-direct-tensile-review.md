## Summary
A monumental review paper by the Tongji University research team that systematically compared and analyzed the dogbone/dumbbell specimen geometries, both-end support boundary conditions (fixed versus pin-hinged versus spherical joints), loading rate dependence, and DIC strain measurement protocols applied by various research institutions worldwide, and collated global standard direct tension test guidelines in order to evaluate precisely and objectively the uniaxial direct tensile behavior, a core mechanical indicator of ductile strain-hardening composites (ECC/SHCC/UHP-ECC).

## Key Contributions
- **Mechanical comparison of the five major ECC direct tensile specimen geometries (JSCE, JCI, RILEM, Univ. Michigan, Tongji)**: stress concentration factors and premature failure risks quantified.
- **Influence of boundary conditions (Fixed versus Pinned) on measured tensile strain capacity clarified**: demonstrated that pin-hinge support generates secondary flexural stress, underestimating apparent ductility by up to 25 %.
- **UHP-ECC micromechanics design principles and fiber-matrix interface states comprehensively organized**: tensile behavior databases of PVA, PE, and steel fiber composites collated.
- **Standard dogbone geometry and digital image correlation (DIC) measurement standard proposed**: data reliability and comparability among global researchers secured.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of a database of some 150 global ECC direct tension experiments.
- Stress concentration factor ($K_t$) at specimen shoulders computed via three-dimensional elastoplastic finite element analysis.
