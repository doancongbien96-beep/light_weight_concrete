# Structural Design and Construction Framework for a Self-Sufficient Lunar Colony: $100\ \text{kN/m}^2$ Internal Pressure Dominance, Tension-Compression Columns, and Airtightness Leakage Control

## Summary

To establish structural design requirements and in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) manufacturing loops for a permanent self-sufficient lunar colony, Professor Niklas Järvstråt at University West (Trollhättan) and Professor Cengiz Toklu at Eastern Mediterranean University analyzed lunar resource availability (oxygen 45 %, silicon 21 %, aluminum 13 %, calcium 11 %, iron 5~13 %, and ~300 million tons of polar water ice). They identified **three fundamental paradigm shifts in lunar structural mechanics distinct from Earth engineering: (1) internal atmospheric pressurization ($100\ \text{kN/m}^2$, equivalent to the weight of a $40\ \text{m}$ lunar regolith overburden) dominating structural tension over gravity, (2) novel 'Tension-Compression Columns' resisting net upward uplift, and (3) leakage rate control ($\le 0.1\%/\text{day}$) reducing required nitrogen extraction from regolith to $1.0\ \text{kg/day}$ per person**. The authors proposed **deep regolith burial and subsurface lava tube construction** to isolate extreme thermal swings, micrometeoroids, and radiation, proving that surface lunar habitats must be designed as massive high-pressure vessels.

## Key Contributions

1. **Formulation of internal pressure ($100\ \text{kN/m}^2$) dominance and 'Tension-Compression Column' concept**:
   - Demonstrated that $1.0\ \text{atm}$ internal pressure creates an upward buoyancy force exceeding a $40\ \text{m}$ regolith layer, converting vertical columns into pure tension members under operating conditions.
2. **Coupling between leakage rates ($\le 0.1\%/\text{day}$) and ISRU extraction requirements**:
   - Quantified that a 1.0 %/day leakage rate requires 10 kg of regolith processed per person daily for nitrogen replacement, whereas a 0.1 %/day rate reduces this burden by 90 % to $1.0\ \text{kg/day}$.
3. **Structural rationale for deep burial and subterranean lava tube utilization**:
   - Established that $5\sim 10\ \text{m}$ regolith burial or unpressurized natural basalt lava tube caves provide optimal structural confinement and thermal stability.
4. **Integrated closed-loop ISRU and habitat construction ecosystem**:
   - Mapped full lifecycle material flows: solar/thermal volatile extraction $\rightarrow$ metal refining $\rightarrow$ additive/cast component production $\rightarrow$ habitat hermetic sealing.

## Methods

- **Structural load and internal pressure equilibrium (Section: Gravitation & Internal Pressure)**:
  - Downward gravity load: Roof dead load $500\ \text{kg/m}^2$ + 2 m regolith ($\rho = 1500\ \text{kg/m}^3$) $\rightarrow$ $W_{down} = \frac{1}{6} \times 9.81 \times (500 + 3000) \times 10^{-3} = 5.7\ \text{kN/m}^2$.
  - Upward pressure load: Internal pressure $1.0\ \text{atm} = 101.3\ \text{kPa} \approx 100\ \text{kN/m}^2$.
  - Net force equilibrium: $P_{net} = 100 - 5.7 = 94.3\ \text{kN/m}^2$ (net upward tensile load).
  - Equivalent balancing regolith depth: $h_{eq} = \frac{100\ \text{kN/m}^2}{\frac{1}{6} \times 1500 \times 9.81 \times 10^{-3}} \approx 40.8\ \text{m}$.
- **Airtightness and mass balance modeling (Section: Survival Requirements)**:
  - Human metabolic demand: Oxygen $0.84\ \text{kg/day}$, nitrogen makeup as a function of leakage rate.
  - Volatiles in regolith: Nitrogen $0.01\ \text{wt.\%}$, carbon $0.015\ \text{wt.\%}$.

## Results

### 1. Structural load regimes, member requirements, and airtightness performance metrics (Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)

| Load & Design Parameter | Terrestrial Building Conditions | Lunar Surface Habitat Conditions | Structural Engineering Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vertical Load Direction** | Downward compression ($+50\sim 100\ \text{kN/m}^2$) | **Net upward tension ($-94.3\ \text{kN/m}^2$)** | **Tension-compression columns & rock anchors** |
| **Column Load State** | Pure compression from gravity | **High tensile pull from internal pressure** | **High-strength tieback cables + prestressing** |
| **Structural Typology** | Gravity-resisting framing | **Massive high-pressure vessel** | **Hoop ring tension shells & triple membranes** |
| **Airtightness Loss 1.0 %/day** | Not applicable | Regolith nitrogen demand $10.0\ \text{kg/day}$/person | Severe resource drain (Impractical) |
| **Airtightness Loss 0.1 %/day** | Not applicable | **Regolith demand $1.0\ \text{kg/day}$/person (90 % cut)** | **Fully self-sustainable colony feasible** |

### 2. Structural mechanics and resource cycling
- **Tension-compression column mechanics**: Columns act as tension tiebacks during pressurized operation and as compression struts during unpressurized construction or maintenance under regolith overburden.
- **Deep underground placement**: Placing habitats inside natural basalt lava tubes provides structural containment that directly counteracts net uplift.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that lunar habitats must be engineered as high-pressure vessels resisting $100\ \text{kN/m}^2$ net uplift, establishing tension-compression columns and 0.1 % leakage thresholds for sustainable settlements.
