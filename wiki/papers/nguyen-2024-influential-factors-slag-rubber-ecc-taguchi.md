# Analysis mechanism of the influential factors governing tensile ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 7.20\%$) and fine-crack control of a slag–rubber hybrid S-ECC based on Taguchi ($L_8$) robust design

## Summary

To optimally design a high-ductility, eco-friendly engineered cementitious composite (S-ECC) recycling the industrial by-products blast furnace slag (GGBS) and waste tire crumb rubber (Crumb Rubber, CR), this study applied **the Taguchi $L_8(2^7)$ orthogonal array design-of-experiments method and analysis of variance (ANOVA)** to precisely elucidate the statistical contributions of four key factors (slag replacement ratio $S = 10, 50\ \text{wt.}\%$, water–binder ratio $W = 0.25, 0.35$, crumb rubber replacement ratio $R = 0, 10\ \text{wt.}\%$, and PE fiber volume fraction $P = 1.0, 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) to 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile behavior (first-crack strength, ultimate tensile strength, tensile strain), number of multiple cracks, and average crack width, demonstrating **compressive strength $68.5\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strength $8.25\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strain $7.20\%$, and average crack-width $48\ \mu\text{m}$ constraint performance** — a 2024 landmark original paper in the Journal of Structural Integrity and Maintenance (JSIM) by the research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), Dr. Se-Eon Park, and Prof. Young-Sang Kim of Chonnam National University**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Decomposition of the influential factors of slag–rubber S-ECC via the Taguchi $L_8$ orthogonal array and ANOVA**:
   - Systematically separates the interactions of the four main variables to fully derive each factor's statistical contribution (Contribution Index %) to compressive strength, tensile strength, tensile strain, and crack pattern.
2. **Elucidation of the tensile-ductility enhancement mechanism of waste tire rubber powder ($\varepsilon_u = 7.20\%$)**:
   - Demonstrates that crumb rubber (CR) particles artificially lower the fracture toughness ($J_{tip}$) of the cement–slag matrix, raising the pseudo strain-hardening index ($J_b'/J_{tip}$) and thereby amplifying the tensile strain capacity up to $7.20\%$, with its contribution quantified ($28.5\%$).
3. **Establishment of the crack-bridging strength interaction between PE fiber volume fraction and slag replacement ratio**:
   - With 2.0 vol.% PE fiber incorporation the tensile strength reaches up to $8.25\ \text{MPa}$, and even at 50% slag replacement adequate fiber bond is maintained to exhibit saturated multiple cracking.
4. **Tight fine-crack control ($W_{avg} \le 55\ \mu\text{m}$)**:
   - Demonstrates that the average crack width remains strictly constrained at the $48\sim 55\ \mu\text{m}$ level even under combined rubber-powder and slag incorporation, guaranteeing self-healing and harmful-ion-blocking durability.

## Methods

- **Taguchi $L_8$ factor and level design (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Factor 1 ($S$, slag replacement ratio): $10\ \text{wt.}\%$ vs. $50\ \text{wt.}\%$.
  - Factor 2 ($W$, water–binder ratio W/B): $0.25$ vs. $0.35$.
  - Factor 3 ($R$, crumb rubber replacement ratio): $0\ \text{wt.}\%$ vs. $10\ \text{wt.}\%$ (particle size $0.5\sim 1.5\ \text{mm}$).
  - Factor 4 ($P$, PE fiber volume fraction): $1.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ vs. $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$.
  - Binder: Type I OPC + GGBS (specific surface area $435\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.50).
  - Fiber: Spectra PE fiber ($d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2700\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 88\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Testing and statistical analysis procedure (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, 0.2 mm/min displacement control).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (28-day age).
  - Crack pattern analysis: optical-microscopy-based measurement of the crack count ($N_c$) and average crack width ($W_{avg}$) within the gauge section.
  - ANOVA statistical processing: calculation of sum of squares (SS), mean squares (MS), F-tests, and contribution rates.

### 2. Statistical contribution by factor and mechanism interpretation
- **Contribution to tensile strain ($\varepsilon_{ts}$)**: the PE fiber fraction ($P$) accounts for $52.4\%$ and the rubber replacement ratio ($R$) for $28.5\%$. Rubber particles relieve the brittleness of the matrix and induce fine crack propagation, increasing the density of multiple cracking by more than twofold.
- **Contribution to compressive strength ($f_c$)**: the W/B ratio ($W$) at $44.2\%$ and the rubber replacement ratio ($R$) at $35.1\%$ exert the dominant negative influence.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the latest landmark original paper in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University clearly elucidated, via the Taguchi method, the factor contributions to the mechanical properties of an S-ECC that co-recycles slag and waste rubber, achieving ultra-high ductility of $7.20\%$ and fine-crack constraint performance of $52\ \mu\text{m}$, thereby presenting design guidelines essential for the practical deployment of eco-friendly high-ductility cement composites.
