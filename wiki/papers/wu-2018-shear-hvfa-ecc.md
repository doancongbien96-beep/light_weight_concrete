## Summary
A study evaluating the direct shear behavior, shear strain-hardening performance, and fine-crack shear transfer mechanisms of high-volume fly ash composites (HVFA-ECC with FA/C = 2.2 ~ 4.0) through Iosipescu and V-notch shear tests. High fly ash replacement was clarified to lower the matrix shear fracture toughness and improve fiber dispersion, so that shear strain hardening ($\gamma_u > 4.5 \%$) and multiple diagonal fine cracks ($w < 40 \mu m$) develop even under pure shear loading. An eco-friendly shear-resistant seismic material design was completed that secures a pure shear strength of 5.8 MPa while cutting binder carbon emissions by 65 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Ultra-ductile shear strain-hardening behavior of HVFA-ECC demonstrated**: ultimate shear strain $\gamma_u = 5.2 \%$ achieved at the FA/C = 3.0 mix (more than 50 times ordinary concrete).
- **Shear crack interface friction and fiber anchorage mechanism clarified**: spherical fine fly ash particles act as ball bearings, inducing stable fiber pullout without fiber damage.
- **Correlation equation between shear strength ($\tau_u$) and uniaxial tensile strength ($f_t$) established**: proportional relation $\tau_u \approx 1.15 \sim 1.30 f_t$ verified.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: cement + Class F fly ash (FA/C = 2.2, 3.0, 4.0), PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15).
- **Shear testing**: JSCE-standard Iosipescu V-notch shear specimens, displacement-controlled pure shear loading.
- **Instrumentation**: shear strain fields via rosette gauges and DIC, crack distribution analysis by optical microscopy.

## Results
- Optimal FA/C = 3.0 mix: 28 d compressive strength $42.5 MPa$, shear strength $5.85 MPa$, shear strain capacity $5.20 \%$, mean shear crack width $32 \mu m$.
- Under shear loading, diagonal tension brittle failure was fully suppressed and stable shear plastic deformation was maintained through a network of diagonal multiple cracks.
