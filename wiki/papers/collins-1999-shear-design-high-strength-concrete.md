# Workability, early strength, flexural strength, and long-term deformation (shrinkage/creep) of AAS concrete

## Summary

A classic monumental origin paper by Profs. Frank Collins and Jay Sanjayan (Monash University,
Australia): to develop AAS concrete achieving 1-day early strength equal to or better than OPC
concrete with excellent slump retention under standard ambient curing (23 °C), two activator
systems were designed — caustic-carbonate $\text{NaOH}+\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (H/C) versus
powdered sodium silicate-slaked lime $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3+\text{Ca(OH)}_2$
(AAS1/AAS2), W/B 0.50 — and quantitatively compared against an OPC control in fresh slump
evolution, air content, bleeding, and hardened compressive strength development, elastic
modulus, flexural strength, drying shrinkage, and 112-day creep. The AAS1 mix — powdered sodium
silicate and slaked lime pre-blended dry — showed outstanding constructability with almost no
slump loss over 2 hours, reaching 28-day compressive strength **47.8 MPa** and flexural
strength **7.18 MPa** (flexural/compressive ratio **0.15**, 29 % better than OPC). AAS concrete's
elastic modulus was 36.7 GPa — 12 % below OPC's 41.7 GPa — with higher 112-day creep strain
($42.0\ \mu\varepsilon/\text{MPa}$), contributing to restrained-drying-shrinkage stress relaxation.

## Key Contributions

1. **OPC-equivalent early strength and slump retention at ambient curing (23 °C)**:
   - 1-day compressive strength of 15–18 MPa without steam curing or heating, realizing formwork
     stripping cycles equal to OPC.
   - The powdered sodium-silicate/slaked-lime combined activator (AAS1) fully solved the rapid
     slump loss (false set) within 60 minutes seen with liquid activators, maintaining stable
     flow for 120 minutes.
2. **High flexural strength ($f_r$) and flexural/compressive ratio ($f_r/f'_c$) established**:
   - AAS1's 28-day flexural strength of **7.18 MPa** exceeded OPC's (5.57 MPa) by **28.9 %**,
     with a ratio of **0.15** proving superior tensile/flexural crack resistance.
3. **Restrained-shrinkage stress relief via lower modulus and higher creep first pointed out**:
   - Elastic modulus of 36.7 GPa (~12 % below OPC) and a 112-day creep coefficient of
     $42.0\ \mu\varepsilon/\text{MPa}$ above OPC's $36.7\ \mu\varepsilon/\text{MPa}$ effectively
     relax restrained tensile stresses despite high drying shrinkage.

## Methods

- **Binder and aggregate composition (unit contents $\text{kg/m}^3$, Tables 1 & 2)**:
  - OPC and GGBS slag ($\text{SiO}_2$ 35.04 %, $\text{CaO}$ 39.43 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$
    13.91 %, $\text{MgO}$ 6.13 %, fineness $420\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
  - Activator mixes:
    - **OPC control**: OPC $360\ \text{kg/m}^3$, water $180\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (W/C 0.50).
    - **H/C**: Slag $360\ \text{kg/m}^3$ + liquid NaOH (1.5 % $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$) +
      $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (4.5 % $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$), W/B 0.50.
    - **AAS1**: Slag $360\ \text{kg/m}^3$ + powdered sodium silicate ($4.25\ \%\ \text{Na}_2\text{O}$,
      $M_s = 0.9$) + hydrated lime slurry ($1.25\ \%\ \text{CaO}$), W/B 0.50.
    - **AAS2**: Slag $360\ \text{kg/m}^3$ + liquid sodium silicate + NaOH + hydrated lime.
  - Aggregates: 14 mm basalt coarse ($G_s = 2.95$) at $1095\ \text{kg/m}^3$; dune sand fine
    ($G_s = 2.65$, fineness modulus 2.19) at $730\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
- **Equipment and test methods**:
  - Fresh concrete: slump cone at 0/30/60/120 minutes, pressure-method air content, bleeding.
  - Compression: $100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylinders (1, 3, 7, 28, 90 days; 23 °C water curing).
  - Flexure: $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$ prisms, third-point loading.
  - Static modulus (ASTM C469), drying shrinkage (ASTM C157, 50 % RH), creep (ASTM C512, 112-day loaded).

## Results

### 1. Fresh flowability and slump evolution

| Mix | Initial slump (mm) | 30 min (mm) | 60 min (mm) | 120 min (mm) | Air (%) | Bleeding (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OPC** | 120 | 95 | 75 | 45 | 1.8 % | 1.2 % |
| **H/C** | 140 | 110 | 85 | 50 | 2.5 % | 0.8 % |
| **AAS1 (powdered)** | **135** | **125** | **110** | **90 (best retention)** | **3.2 %** | **0.4 %** |
| **AAS2 (liquid)** | 150 | 60 | 10 (flash set) | 0 | 3.5 % | 0.3 % |

### 2. 28-day mechanical properties and elastic modulus comparison (Table 3)

| Property | OPC control | H/C mix | AAS1 mix | AAS1 vs OPC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **28 d compression ($f'_c$)** | 46.4 ± 1.5 MPa | 34.1 ± 1.2 MPa | **47.8 ± 1.8 MPa** | **+3.0 % (equal or better)** |
| **28 d flexure ($f_r$)** | 5.57 ± 0.3 MPa | 4.77 ± 0.2 MPa | **7.18 ± 0.4 MPa** | **+28.9 % (superior)** |
| **Flexural/compressive ratio ($f_r/f'_c$)** | 0.12 | 0.14 | **0.15** | **+25.0 %** |
| **Static modulus ($E_c$)** | **41.7 GPa** | 36.5 GPa | **36.7 GPa** | **−12.0 % (compliant)** |
| **112 d creep strain** | $36.7\ \mu\varepsilon/\text{MPa}$ | – | **$42.0\ \mu\varepsilon/\text{MPa}$** | **+14.4 % (stress relaxation)** |

- **Overall conclusion**: AAS concrete combining powdered sodium silicate and slaked lime
  delivers 48 MPa-class compression and 7.2 MPa ultra-high flexural strength under ambient
  curing, exceeding OPC, with excellent slump retention and creep stress-relaxation behavior —
  proving optimal as a sustainable structural concrete.
