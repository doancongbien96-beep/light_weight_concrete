## Summary
To reduce the unit production cost of engineered cementitious composites (ECC) while simultaneously achieving high strength and ultra-high ductility, this study evaluated the four-point flexural behavior and multiple fine-cracking characteristics of composites in which high-strength hydrophobic PE fiber and hydrophilic PVA fiber were hybrid-mixed at various ratios (PE:PVA = 100:0, 75:25, 50:50, 25:75, 0:100, total $V_f = 2.0 \%$). It demonstrated that the high interfacial chemical bond strength of the PVA fiber induces early fine-crack formation while the outstanding pull-out slip resistance of the PE fiber carries extensive post-cracking strain hardening and deflection ductility, producing a synergy effect over single-fiber systems.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the PVA/PE fiber hybrid synergy mechanism**: optimal combination of PVA's crack-multiplying dispersion function with PE's support of high bridging stress ($\sigma_0$).
- **Derivation of the cost-performance-optimal hybrid ratio**: the PE 50 % + PVA 50 % mix cuts material cost by 40 % relative to pure PE-ECC while maintaining a flexural strength of $13.8 MPa$ and a deflection ductility index of 10.5.
- **Control of crack count and crack width**: hybrid mixes form a dense crack network with an average crack width of $35 \mu m$ and 30 or more cracks.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA fiber (REC15, strength 1600 MPa) and UHMWPE fiber (strength 2800 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa).
- **Matrix**: $W/B = 0.26$, fly ash 55 wt %.
- **Testing**: four-point bending test (dimensions $350 \times 75 \times 50 mm$, span 300 mm), digital image crack analysis.

## Results
- Pure PE-ECC: flexural strength $15.2 MPa$, maximum deflection $14.5 mm$.
- Hybrid (PE 1.0 % + PVA 1.0 %): flexural strength $13.8 MPa$, maximum deflection $12.8 mm$.
- Pure PVA-ECC: flexural strength $11.5 MPa$, maximum deflection $9.2 mm$.
