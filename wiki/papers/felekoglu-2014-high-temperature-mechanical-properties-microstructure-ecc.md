# Residual Mechanical Properties and Microstructural Evolution of Engineered Cementitious Composites (ECC) Under High-Temperature and Fire Exposure ($200\sim 800\ ^\circ\text{C}$): Explosive Spalling Mitigation via PVA Fiber Melting Channels and Post-Fire Tensile Ductility

## Summary

To prevent explosive spalling that causes catastrophic structural collapse during fire exposure and ensure post-fire residual mechanical safety, Professors Burak Felekoglu at Dokuz Eylul University, Mustafa Sahmaran at Hacettepe University, and Ismail Ozgur Yaman at METU subjected standard PVA-ECC (M45 mix, 2.0 vol.% PVA fibers, FA/C = 1.2) and PP-ECC specimens to electric furnace heating from room temperature ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$) up to $200\ ^\circ\text{C}, 400\ ^\circ\text{C}, 600\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Evaluating residual uniaxial direct tensile response, compressive strength, elastic modulus, ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), and SEM-EDS microstructure, the authors demonstrated **complete prevention of explosive spalling (100 %)** through the creation of 3D vapor escape channels upon PVA fiber melting at $230\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Specimens retained a uniaxial tensile strain capacity of **2.5 %** and **92 %** of compressive strength after $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ exposure, transitioning to dehydration decomposition of C-S-H gels above $400\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of zero-spalling (0.0) mechanisms in ECC under fire exposure**:
   - High internal vapor pressures generated within the dense matrix were vented rapidly through network pores left by billions of PVA fibers melting at $230\ ^\circ\text{C}$, eliminating explosive spalling.
2. **Quantification of temperature-dependent residual uniaxial tensile ductility**:
   - Maintained 2.5 % tensile strain capacity after $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ exposure due to preserved fiber-matrix interfaces, transitioning to tension-softening above $400\ ^\circ\text{C}$ following fiber carbonization.
3. **Establishment of residual compressive strength and modulus degradation curves**:
   - Compiled post-fire damage databases tracking compressive strength retention: $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (92 %) $\rightarrow$ $400\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (75 %) $\rightarrow$ $600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (45 %) $\rightarrow$ $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (18 %).
4. **Non-destructive evaluation via ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV)**:
   - Established diagnostic correlations between UPV reduction ($V_p = 4.2\ \text{km/s} \rightarrow 1.5\ \text{km/s}$) and internal thermal microcrack damage.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–2 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binders: Ordinary Portland cement + Class F fly ash (FA/C = 1.2, W/B = 0.27).
  - Aggregate: Micro-silica sand ($d_{max} = 200\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, melting point $230\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Heating regimes and testing (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 4–6)**:
  - Heating: Electric furnace heating rate $5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$, held at target temperatures (200, 400, 600, 800 °C) for 2 hours followed by slow air cooling.
  - Mechanical tests: Uniaxial direct tension (dogbone specimens at 0.2 mm/min), compressive strength (50 mm cubes), and UPV testing.
  - Microstructural analysis: SEM and EDS observation of fiber melting voids.

### 2. Fiber melting pore network and matrix dehydration mechanisms
- **Pressure relief via PVA fiber melting**: At $230\ ^\circ\text{C}$, PVA fibers liquefied and vaporized, leaving continuous $39\ \mu\text{m}$ capillary channels that vented steam pressure to the atmosphere.
- **High-temperature phase decomposition ($400\sim 800\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**: Dehydroxylation of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ at ~450 °C and C-S-H gel breakdown above 600 °C drove residual strength loss.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work by Felekoglu and Sahmaran elucidated the spalling prevention mechanisms and residual tensile ductility evolution of ECC under high-temperature fire exposure for fire-safe structural engineering.
