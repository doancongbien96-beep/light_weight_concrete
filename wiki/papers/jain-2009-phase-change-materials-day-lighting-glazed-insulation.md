# Smart Variable Solar Transmittance ($\tau = 0.22\leftrightarrow 0.78$) and $8.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Indoor Surface Temperature Reduction via PCM-Infilled Double-Glazed Windows

## Summary

To overcome the poor thermal insulation and excessive daytime solar heat gain of building glass facades while maintaining natural daylighting, Dr. Lokesh Jain and Dr. S. D. Sharma at the CSEM-UAE Innovation Center developed a smart phase-change material (PCM) glazing system. The system infills the 12 mm air cavity of a double-glazed unit (4 mm glass panes) with a translucent paraffin wax (Rubitherm RT27: melting point $T_m = 26\sim 28\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat of fusion $\Delta H_m = 180.0\ \text{J/g}$). Under extreme solar irradiance conditions in the United Arab Emirates (UAE), solar transmittance and window surface temperatures were continuously monitored using pyranometers and RTD sensors. The system demonstrated autonomous optical switching from an opaque solid state ($\tau = 0.22$) providing shading and thermal insulation to a transparent liquid state ($\tau = 0.78$) providing clear daylighting, **reducing the peak indoor window surface temperature by $8.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ compared to conventional double glazing**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Smart glazing with PCM cavity filling**:
   - Achieved passive, autonomous regulation of solar transmittance ($\tau = 0.22 \leftrightarrow 0.78$) driven solely by ambient temperature and solar radiation without mechanical blinds or electrical power.
2. **Latent heat absorption and enhanced insulation**:
   - Diffusely transmitted visible daylight into the interior while absorbing infrared solar thermal energy via the latent heat capacity of RT27 ($180\ \text{J/g}$), mitigating indoor thermal loading.
3. **$8.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ reduction in peak inner surface temperature**:
   - Whereas conventional double glazing overheated to an indoor surface temperature of $42.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the PCM-infilled window remained stable below $33.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
4. **Glare prevention and uniform diffuse daylighting**:
   - The polycrystalline microstructure of solid paraffin scattered direct solar beams into soft, uniform diffuse light, improving visual comfort.

## Methods

- **Specimen configuration and PCM properties (Section 2 & Figures 1–2)**:
  - Dimensions: $500 \times 500\ \text{mm}$ double-glazed unit (two 4 mm glass panes, 12 mm cavity gap, sealed aluminum spacer).
  - PCM: Rubitherm RT27 paraffin ($T_m = 26\sim 28\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $\Delta H_m = 180.0\ \text{J/g}$, solid density $0.88\ \text{g/cm}^3$, liquid density $0.77\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - Reference: Standard 12 mm air-filled double-glazed unit.
- **Optical and thermal instrumentation (Section 2.2 & Figure 3)**:
  - Pyranometers: Two Eppley precision pyranometers + one Kipp & Zonen pyranometer (error $<0.1\%$).
  - Temperature sensors: 4-wire PT100 RTD resistance temperature sensors (exterior surface, internal PCM layer, interior surface).
  - Data acquisition: Advantech ADAM unit logging at 1 s intervals.

## Results

### 1. Solar transmittance and surface temperature comparison between PCM glazing and standard double glazing (Section 3 & Figures 4–6)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard Double Glazing (Air)** | Midday solar peak (Noon) | **$0.76 \pm 0.02$** | **$45.5 \pm 1.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$** | $43.0 \pm 1.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (Air) | **$42.0 \pm 0.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$** | 0.0 $^\circ\text{C}$ (Baseline) |

### 2. Optical refractive index matching and latent storage mechanics
- **Light scattering in solid state**: Polycrystalline paraffin scattered incoming light rays, lowering transmittance to 0.22 and eliminating glare.
- **Refractive index matching upon melting**: Liquid paraffin closely matched the refractive index of glass, restoring transmittance to 0.78 for clear natural daylighting.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that infilling double-glazed cavities with RT27 PCM enables passive modulation of solar transmittance and lowers indoor window temperatures by $8.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
