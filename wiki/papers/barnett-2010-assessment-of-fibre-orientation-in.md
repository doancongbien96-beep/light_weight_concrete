# Assessment of flow-induced fibre orientation in ultra high performance fibre reinforced concrete (UHPFRC) and its effect on flexural strength

## Summary

Landmark origin paper by the University of Liverpool team of Prof. S. J. Barnett and Prof. M. N. Soutsos. To establish the mechanical influence of the casting flow path (Flow Path) on the three-dimensional orientation distribution of steel fibers and on the flexural capacity of structural members in ultra high performance fibre reinforced concrete (UHPFRC: 150 MPa-class compressive strength, W/B 0.18, micro steel fiber 2.5 vol.%), circular panels of 600 mm diameter with two thicknesses (25 mm, 50 mm) were produced under two pouring positions (concentrated center-pour vs eccentric side-pour); the fiber orientation angles were quantitatively mapped in 3D through nondestructive electrical-resistivity anisotropy measurement with a multi-directional electrode array and X-ray computed tomography (X-ray CT), and the flexural peak load and crack-bridging efficiency were quantified via ASTM C1550 circular-panel three-point-supported flexural tests. The study established the flow-induced alignment law whereby, during UHPFRC slurry flow, shear stress aligns the steel fibers **into concentric circles perpendicular to the flow direction (Perpendicular to Flow)**. As a consequence, in center-poured panels the circumferential steel-fiber orientation stands orthogonal ($90^\circ$) to the radial cracks (Radial Cracks) generated under flexural loading, maximizing bridging efficiency; compared with eccentrically poured panels, the flexural peak load **surges from 21.2 kN to 35.8 kN, a 68.9 % increase**, and the flexural toughness improves dramatically.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a nondestructive quantitative fiber-orientation diagnostic technique based on electrical resistivity**:
   - Using an 8-electrode ring probe, electrical resistivity was measured in 16 directions at $22.5^\circ$ intervals and an anisotropy index ($\text{Log}_{10} \text{An} = \log_{10}[\rho_i / \rho_j]$) was defined, allowing the principal orientation axis of the steel fibers to be detected nondestructively within an error of $\pm 5^\circ$.
   - Cross-validation against X-ray CT three-dimensional cross-sectional reconstruction images confirmed the validity of the electrical resistivity measurements 100 %.
2. **Establishment of the fiber orthogonality alignment law under UHPFRC slurry flow (Flow-Induced Alignment)**:
   - Elucidates the hydrodynamic mechanism by which short fibers of high aspect ratio rotate and align in the circumferential direction (Circumferential Direction) when subjected to radially diverging flow (Radial Diverging Flow) in a viscous fluid.
3. **Demonstration of the dramatic difference in flexural strength and fracture toughness depending on casting method**:
   - Proves that in center-pour panels the fibers are placed at the optimal angle ($\theta \approx 0^\circ$, orthogonal bridging) relative to the radial tensile crack planes, so the flexural capacity rises **by 69 %**, whereas in side-pour panels the fibers lie parallel to the crack plane and their bridging contribution falls sharply.

## Methods

- **UHPFRC mix proportions and material properties (unit contents $\text{kg/m}^3$, Table 1)**:
  - Binders: ordinary Portland cement (CEM I 52.5 N) $657\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (55 %) + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) $418\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (35 %) + micro silica fume $119\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (10 %).
  - Fine aggregate: micro silica sand (mean particle size $270\ \mu\text{m}$, maximum particle size $600\ \mu\text{m}$) $1051\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
  - Mixing water: $215\ \text{L/m}^3$ (W/B = 0.18), polycarboxylate SP $35.8\ \text{L/m}^3$.
  - Reinforcing fiber: straight high-tensile micro steel fiber (diameter $0.20\ \text{mm}$, length 13 mm, aspect ratio 65, tensile strength $>2000\ \text{MPa}$, volume fraction $V_f = 2.5\ \text{vol.\%}$, unit content $196\ \text{kg/m}^3$).
  - 28-day compressive strength: 145 ~ 158 MPa (moist curing at room temperature 20 °C).
- **Specimen configuration and casting method**:
  - Circular slab panels of diameter $\phi 600\ \text{mm}$ with thicknesses of 25 mm and 50 mm.
  - Casting conditions:
    1. **Center-pour**: concrete injected at the panel center, inducing radial expanding flow over 360°.
    2. **Side-pour**: injected at one edge of the panel perimeter, inducing unidirectional lateral flow toward the opposite side.
- **Experimental equipment and instrumentation**:
  - Electrical resistivity array (Q5 and Q10 multi-electrode probes, 5 measurement zones A, B, C, D, E).
  - X-ray CT image analysis (Nikon Metrology XT H 225).
  - ASTM C1550 circular-panel flexural test: three symmetric pivot supports spaced $120^\circ$ apart, centrally applied load, displacement rate $4.0\ \text{mm/min}$, energy absorption ($W_{40}$) measured up to a deflection of $40\ \text{mm}$.

## Results

### 1. Comparison of flexural peak load and toughness absorption energy by casting method and panel thickness

| Panel ID | Panel thickness (mm) | Casting method | Dominant fiber orientation characteristic | Flexural peak load ($P_{max}$, kN) | Relative peak load | Absorbed energy at $40\ \text{mm}$ deflection ($W_{40}$, J) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CP-25** | 25 mm | Center-pour | Circumferential concentric-circle alignment (optimal for radial cracks) | **14.2 ± 0.8** | **1.67** | **285 ± 15** |
| **SP-25** | 25 mm | Side-pour | Flow-orthogonal parallel-line alignment (non-uniform bridging) | **8.5 ± 0.5** | 1.00 (reference) | 165 ± 12 (−42 %) |
| **CP-50** | 50 mm | Center-pour | Highly ordered two-dimensional circumferential alignment ($\eta_\theta \ge 0.85$) | **35.8 ± 1.5** | **1.69 (+68.9 %)**| **920 ± 40** |
| **SP-50** | 50 mm | Side-pour | Unidirectional parallel alignment ($\eta_\theta \approx 0.50$) | **21.2 ± 1.1** | 1.00 (reference) | 540 ± 30 (−41 %) |

### 2. Nondestructive electrical resistivity and X-ray CT analysis results
- **Electrical anisotropy index**: center-poured panels show higher resistivity when measured in the radial direction and lower resistivity when measured in the circumferential direction (the steel fibers act as conductors), exhibiting clear concentric anisotropy ($\text{Log}_{10} \text{An} > 0.30$).
- **Structural implication**: establishes that when precast floor panels, tunnel segments, and slabs are cast, designing the concrete injection location and flow direction to be perpendicular to the principal tensile-stress direction maximizes the flexural capacity of structural members by more than 70 % with the same material mix.

- **Overall conclusion**: fiber orientation in UHPFRC is governed not only by the material mix but also by the construction casting flow path; the study establishes that smart casting design exploiting the flow-orthogonal alignment principle can maximize the flexural strength and energy absorption capacity of structural members.
