# Thermal and Mechanical Performance, Building Energy Reduction, and Life-Cycle Environmental Impact of Building Envelope Composites Combining Cementless Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) and Shape-Stabilized Phase Change Aggregates (TESA)

## Summary

To reduce building envelope heating and cooling (HVAC) energy consumption and eliminate greenhouse gas emissions from cement manufacturing, Professor Yong-Rak Kim and co-workers at Texas A&M University developed an eco-friendly building envelope cementitious composite. The composite integrates a 100 % cementless alkali-activated blast-furnace slag (AAS) binder (activated by solid anhydrous sodium metasilicate) with **shape-stabilized thermal energy storage aggregates (TESA, latent heat capacity $73.5\ \text{J/g}$)**. TESA was manufactured by vacuum-impregnating a bio-based phase-change material (PureTemp27, melting point $27\ ^\circ\text{C}$) into porous expanded glass aggregates (EGA, Poraver, particle size $1\sim 2\ \text{mm}$) followed by an epoxy/graphite dual encapsulation coating. TESA was substituted for natural river sand at 0 vol.%, 50 vol.%, and 100 vol.%. The authors evaluated 7 and 28 d compressive strengths, thermal conductivity (Hot Disk TPS), latent heat storage (DSC), **EnergyPlus building energy simulations, and life-cycle environmental assessments (LCA)**. Even at 100 % TESA replacement, the AAS composite attained a 28 d compressive strength of **$28.2\ \text{MPa}$** (44.6 % higher than the 19.5 MPa attained by the corresponding 100 % TESA OPC mix), exceeding structural wall requirements (>20 MPa). Furthermore, TESA replacement reduced thermal conductivity by **48.5 % from $1.85 \rightarrow 0.95\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$** and stored **$36.8\ \text{J/g}$ of latent heat**. Applied to a residential building in Houston, Texas, it delivered an **18.5 % reduction in annual HVAC cooling energy** and a **58.4 % reduction in life-cycle carbon footprint ($43.5 \rightarrow 18.1\ \text{kg CO}_2\text{-eq/m}^2$)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Structural strength preservation ($28.2\ \text{MPa}$) in cementless AAS-TESA composites**:
   - Defended against the typical strength degradation of porous PCM aggregates by utilizing a dense C-A-S-H gel forming AAS matrix and epoxy-graphite reinforced coatings to maintain 28.2 MPa structural strength.
2. **Zero liquid PCM leakage (0.0 %) via epoxy/graphite dual encapsulation**:
   - TGA and thermal cycling tests confirmed complete physical containment with zero mass loss or leakage over 500 heating-cooling cycles.
3. **Simultaneous thermal insulation (48.5 % lower conductivity) and latent storage ($36.8\ \text{J/g}$)**:
   - Maximized thermal inertia by coupling the low conductivity of expanded glass with the isothermal heat absorption of bio-PCM.
4. **Demonstration of 18.5 % annual energy savings and 58.4 % carbon reduction via EnergyPlus and LCA**:
   - Integrated measured composite thermophysical properties into full-scale building energy and Cradle-to-Gate LCA models with 100 % scientific consistency.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3, mass and volume ratios)**:
  - Binders: GGBS (Blaine $4250\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{CaO}$ 41.5 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 35.2 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 13.8 %) vs OPC Type I.
  - Solid activator: Anhydrous sodium metasilicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$, 99 % purity, 5.0 wt.% of binder).
  - Shape-stabilized PCM aggregate (TESA):
    - Carrier: Porous expanded glass aggregates (EGA, Poraver, $1\sim 2\ \text{mm}$, bulk density $230\ \text{kg/m}^3$).
    - PCM: Bio-based PCM (PureTemp27, melting point $27.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $180.0\ \text{J/g}$, vacuum loading 45 wt.%).
    - Encapsulation: Structural epoxy resin + fine graphite powder ($44\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Mixture parameters: W/B = 0.40, aggregate-to-binder ratio = 2.0.
  - 6 mix groups: OPC-0, OPC-50, OPC-100 vs AAS-0, AAS-50, AAS-100 (TESA replacing sand at 0, 50, 100 vol.%).
- **Testing and simulation protocols (Section 3)**:
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes, 7 and 28 d).
  - Thermal conductivity: Hot Disk TPS 2500S (ISO 22007-2, 6.4 mm sensor).
  - Thermal analysis: TGA 5500 ($500\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$), DSC 2500 ($0\sim 60\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $1.5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$).
  - Energy simulation: EnergyPlus v9.4 (ASHRAE 90.1 prototype residential building, Houston, TX climate data).
  - Life-cycle assessment: SimaPro 9.1 (TRACI 2.1 impact categories, Cradle-to-Gate).

### 2. Thermal energy dynamics and environmental benefits
- **AAS interfacial bonding**: Metasilicate-activated slag developed dense interfacial contact with the epoxy/graphite coating of TESA, achieving 44.6 % higher compressive strength than the corresponding OPC matrix.
- **Indoor temperature peak shaving**: 100 % TESA wall assemblies reduced peak summer daytime indoor temperatures by $3.2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, smoothing cooling loads.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that coupling cementless AAS binders with shape-stabilized PCM aggregates delivers structural strength (28.2 MPa), 48.5 % lower thermal conductivity, 18.5 % annual energy savings, and 58.4 % carbon reduction in zero-energy building envelopes.
