# Tensile strain-hardening evolution ($\varepsilon_u = 5.5\%$) of 86 MPa-class ultra-high-strength ECC (HS-ECC) under high-temperature exposure ($20\sim 200\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and the PE fiber melting anti-explosive-spalling mechanism

## Summary

To establish the fire safety and residual mechanical performance of high-rise buildings and underground structures exposed to fire and high-temperature thermal environments, **an ultra-high-strength strain-hardening cementitious composite of the 86 MPa class in 28-day compressive strength (HS-ECC: High-Strength ECC, PE fibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$)** was heated to various target temperatures ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}, 50\ ^\circ\text{C}, 100\ ^\circ\text{C}, 140\ ^\circ\text{C}, 200\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and subsequently cooled naturally, after which uniaxial direct tension, four-point flexural load-deflection response, uniaxial compressive strength, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), thermogravimetric analysis (TG/DTG), and SEM microstructure were precisely evaluated — a landmark original paper of Construction and Building Materials (2019) by the team of **Prof. Kequan Yu of Tongji University / University of Michigan and Prof. Wenjun Zhu of Beijing Jiaotong University** that demonstrated that **over the $50\sim 100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ range a secondary pozzolanic reaction further extended the tensile strain from $4.5\% \rightarrow 5.5\%$, and that at $147.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ the fine pore channels created upon PE fiber melting vented the internal water vapor pressure, preventing explosive spalling entirely even at $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the mechanism by which HS-ECC tensile ductility ($5.5\%$) is further increased under thermal exposure at $50\sim 100\ ^\circ\text{C}$**:
   - Revealed a unique thermomechanical behavior in which the heat-accelerated pozzolanic reaction of fly ash optimizes matrix toughness, improving tensile deformation performance by an additional $22\%$ relative to room temperature ($\varepsilon_u = 4.5\%$).
2. **Analysis of the mechanical behavior transition around the PE fiber melting point ($147.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - Quantified that strain hardening with tensile strength $8.5\ \text{MPa}$ and ductility $2.1\%$ is maintained up to $140\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (just before the melting point), whereas at $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ fiber melting causes a transition to brittle failure.
3. **Demonstration of zero explosive spalling in ultra-high-strength concrete via PE fiber melting pore channels**:
   - While ordinary high-strength concrete (HSC) explosively disintegrates near $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ owing to internal vapor pressure, a nano ventilation network formed by melted ultra-fine PE fibers of diameter $24\ \mu\text{m}$ safely vents the vapor pressure, blocking explosive spalling at its source.
4. **Precise quantification of temperature-dependent porosity (MIP) and C-S-H hydrate dehydration evolution**:
   - Confirmed that after exposure to $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ the total porosity increases from $12.5\% \rightarrow 24.8\%$ while a compressive strength retention of $49.5\%$ ($42.6\ \text{MPa}$) is maintained.

## Methods

- **HS-ECC mix design (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binders: OPC P.O 52.5 + fly ash (FA/C = 1.2) + silica fume (SF/C = 0.1) (W/B = 0.18, ultra-high-strength matrix).
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand (maximum size 0.3 mm).
  - Fibers: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$, $T_m = 147.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
- **High-temperature heating and mechanical testing procedure (Sections 2.2~2.3 & Figs. 2~3)**:
  - Heating conditions: electric furnace heating (heating rate $6.6\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$, held 2 hours at target temperatures of $20, 50, 100, 140, 200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ $\rightarrow$ natural cooling in ambient air).
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone specimens (displacement control at 0.5 mm/min).
  - Four-point bending test: $40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prismatic specimens (span 120 mm).
  - Thermal/microstructural analysis: Netzsch TG 209 F3 thermogravimetric analyzer, Micromeritics AutoPore IV MIP pore measurement.

### 2. Interpretation of the thermal enhancement and fiber melt-escape mechanisms
- **Pozzolanic reaction acceleration at or below $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$**: Residual moisture and the thermal environment activate secondary pozzolanic reactions of unreacted fly ash and silica fume, reinforcing the matrix-fiber interface and stretching the tensile strain-hardening elongation up to $5.5\%$.
- **PE fiber melting and formation of vapor-pressure release pathways**: PE fibers molten at $147.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ form continuous escape pore channels of $24\ \mu\text{m}$ that rapidly release the vapor pressure inside the matrix, preventing destructive explosive spalling $100\%$ despite being an ultra-high-strength concrete of 86 MPa.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper establishes the mechanical characteristics and microstructural evolution of 86 MPa-class ultra-high-strength ECC (HS-ECC) across the $20\sim 200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ temperature range, demonstrating ductility enhancement ($5.5\%$) at or below $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ together with completely spalling-free safety at $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ — a landmark original paper in the field of high-temperature fire-resistant construction materials.
