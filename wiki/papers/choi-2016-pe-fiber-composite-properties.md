# Mechanical properties and crack control of high-strength PE-fiber cement versus cement-free (AAS) composites

## Summary

A study by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's group (corresponding author) that manufactured four ductile
composites — high-strength PE short fiber (1.5 vol.%, 2700 MPa) in an eco-friendly cement-free
alkali-activated GGBS (AAS) matrix and in ordinary Portland cement (OPC) matrices (C34, C38,
S34, S38) — comparing density, compressive strength, uniaxial direct tension, and surface fine-
multiple-crack patterns. The AAS composites showed somewhat lower compression (31.3–43.0 MPa vs
60.8–75.9 MPa) and peak tension (5.96–7.89 MPa vs 9.53–9.80 MPa) at equal W/B, but their stress
ratio $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} = 3.15$ exceeded the cement system's 2.62 by 20.1 %, achieving far
higher multiple-cracking saturation: crack count averaged 58.1 versus 32.5 (**+78.7 %**),
spacing tightened to 1.41 mm (**−45.6 %**), and mean residual width was controlled to **77.4
µm** (−27.1 % versus 106.1 µm), with up to **5.92 %** ultra-high tensile strain capacity.

## Key Contributions

1. **Mechanism of AAS ultra-ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 5.92\ \%$)** established: low matrix
   fracture toughness ($K_m$) with appropriate frictional bond sustains stable slip pullout of
   bridging fibers after first cracking without rupture.
   With strength ratio $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$ reaching 3.15–3.31 the PSH strength criterion is
   fully met, forming over 58 dense fine cracks on average.
2. **Dramatic rise of the tension/compression ratio demonstrated**: ordinary concrete sits near
   10 %; here the cement systems reached 12.9–15.7 % while AAS systems recorded **18.3–19.1 %**
   — maximizing shear and tensile resistance efficiency.
3. **Harmful crack width ≤ 80 µm at 1.41 mm spacing achieved**: S34 and S38 attained crack
   spacing 1.41 mm and residual widths 74.5–80.2 µm, experimentally securing permeability near
   uncracked concrete and outstanding service life.

## Methods

- **Binder and activator (Table 2)**:
  - OPC (density $3.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$, fineness $330\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$,
    $\text{CaO}$ 63.3 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 20.8 %).
  - GGBS (density $2.91\ \text{g/cm}^3$, fineness $432\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$,
    $\text{CaO}$ 44.5 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 33.4 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 15.3 %).
  - Activators: powdered $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (8.38 mass% of GGBS) + $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$
    (3.35 mass%).
- **Fiber (PE, Table 3)**: diameter 31 µm, length 12 mm, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, tensile
  strength 2700 MPa, E 88 GPa, elongation 3.0 %. Dosage 1.5 vol.%.
- **Mix design (four mixes, Table 1)**:
  - **C34**: cement binder, W/B 0.34, SP 0.005, VMA 0.0003, defoamer 0.0002.
  - **C38**: cement binder, W/B 0.38, SP 0.004, VMA 0.0003, defoamer 0.0002.
  - **S34**: AAS binder (GGBS+activator), W/B 0.34, SP 0.006, VMA 0.0003, defoamer 0.0002.
  - **S38**: AAS binder, W/B 0.38, SP 0.005, VMA 0.0003, defoamer 0.0002.
    (aggregate-free paste composite).
- **Equipment and methods**:
  - Hobart mixing → mold casting → 1 day ambient cure → 28 days water curing (23±3 °C).
  - Density and compression: three $50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$
    cubes.
  - Uniaxial direct tension: plate specimens (gauge section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$,
    gauge length 80 mm), displacement-controlled machine (0.2 mm/min), two LVDTs.
  - Crack analysis: post-test visual counts on both faces; spacing and widths computed.

## Results

### 1. Density and 28-day compressive strength (Tables 4 & 5)

| Mix | Binder | W/B | Measured density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | 28 d compression (MPa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C34** | OPC | 0.34 | 2.07 ± 0.01 | 75.9 ± 4.1 |
| **C38** | OPC | 0.38 | 2.02 ± 0.01 | 60.8 ± 2.0 |
| **S34** | AAS | 0.34 | 1.95 ± 0.01 | 43.0 ± 1.3 |
| **S38** | AAS | 0.38 | 1.91 ± 0.01 | 31.3 ± 1.4 |

### 2. Direct tension behavior and strength ratios (Table 6)

| Mix | First cracking (MPa) | Max tension (MPa) | Strain capacity (%) | $f_i/f'_c$ | $f_t/f'_c$ | Strength ratio ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C34** | 3.97 ± 0.71 | **9.80 ± 0.85** | 4.88 ± 0.78 | 5.23 % | 12.9 % | 2.47 |
| **C38** | 3.43 ± 0.54 | 9.53 ± 0.71 | 3.91 ± 0.58 | 5.64 % | 15.7 % | 2.78 |
| **S34** | 2.64 ± 0.26 | 7.89 ± 0.91 | **5.32 ± 0.72** | 6.14 % | **18.3 %** | 2.99 |
| **S38** | 1.80 ± 0.46 | 5.96 ± 0.87 | **5.92 ± 0.63** | 5.75 % | **19.1 %** | **3.31** |

### 3. Multiple-crack pattern and fine-crack control characteristics (Table 7)

### 3. Multiple-crack pattern and fine-crack control characteristics (Table 7)

| Mix | Mean crack count | Mean width (µm) | Mean spacing (mm) | Multiple-cracking assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C34** | 34.5 ± 8.9 | 113.1 | 2.48 | ordinary hardening, widths over 100 µm |
| **C38** | 30.5 ± 5.5 | 99.1 | 2.71 | moderate count, 3.9 % strain |
| **S34** | 57.1 ± 5.3 (+65.5 %) | **74.5 (−34.1 %)** | **1.41 (−43.1 %)** | **dense fine multiple cracking** |
| **S38** | **59.0 ± 12.8 (+93.4 %)** | **80.2 (−19.1 %)** | **1.41 (−48.0 %)** | **best crack dispersion, ultra-high ductility** |

- **Overall assessment**: AAS-PE composites run ~45 % lower in compression than cement systems,
  but the enlarged $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$ margin halves crack spacing and raises tensile
  elongation by ~28 % — proving optimal for structural repair and strengthening where
  durability and deformation capacity are demanded.
