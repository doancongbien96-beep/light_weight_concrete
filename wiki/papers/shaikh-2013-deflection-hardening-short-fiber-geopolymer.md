## Summary
This is the original research paper in which Professor F.U.A. Shaikh of Curtin University, Australia precisely identified the four-point flexural deflection-hardening behavior, initial cracking flexural strength, modulus of rupture (MOR), flexural toughness indices ($I_{10}, I_{20}, I_{30}$), and multiple fine-crack formation mechanisms of short-fiber reinforced geopolymer composites, obtained by reinforcing micro PVA fibers of different lengths (6 mm, 8 mm, 12 mm) and volume fractions ($V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$) into an eco-friendly fly ash-slag based alkali-activated geopolymer matrix. Under the condition of incorporating 2.0 vol % of 12 mm PVA fibers, a distinct deflection-hardening behavior in which the load continued to rise after initial cracking was exhibited, achieving a flexural strength of **14.2 MPa**, an ultimate deflection of **8.5 mm**, and a flexural toughness index of $I_{30} = 38.5$.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of the geopolymer flexural deflection-hardening envelope according to PVA fiber length and incorporation ratio**: the modulus of rupture increased by 65 % as the fiber length increased from 6 mm to 12 mm.
- **Quantitative evaluation of flexural toughness according to ASTM C1018 and JSCE standards**: demonstration of deflection-hardening toughness surpassing ideal fully plastic behavior in the $V_f = 2.0 \%$ mix.
- **Quantification of multiple fine-crack formation density**: more than 28 fine cracks formed within the pure bending zone, controlling the maximum crack width at or below $40 \mu m$.
- **Presentation of an eco-friendly high-ductility structural member mix with a 70 % embodied carbon reduction**: laying the foundation for manufacturing high-ductility precast members without cement.

## Methods
- Binder: low-calcium Class F fly ash + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS 20 wt %), liquid sodium silicate and $NaOH 8 M$ activator ($W/B = 0.36$).
- Fiber: Kuraray PVA fiber (diameter 39 µm, lengths 6, 8, 12 mm, $V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$).
- Test: four-point bending test on $100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$ prism beams (span 300 mm, pure bending span 100 mm, $0.5 mm/min$, LVDT measurement), 28 d compressive strength.
