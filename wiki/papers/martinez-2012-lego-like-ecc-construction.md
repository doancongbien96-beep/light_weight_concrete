# Preliminary Feasibility Study of a LEGO-like ECC Block Construction Method

## Summary

A single-page academic poster. This study evaluated the feasibility of a LEGO-style construction method in which structures are assembled, disassembled, and reconfigured from male-female dovetail ECC blocks (motivation: reduced landfill waste, energy, and CO₂). In uniaxial tension tests on Green-ECC (G-ECC) block joints, the mean maximum load was 388 N for the unreinforced joint and 505 N for the joint reinforced with steel mesh, a +30 % improvement from the mesh. The failure mode was identical in both types — tensile cracking at the reentrant corner of the female element's dovetail inlet and compressive crushing at the lower corner of the male element's protrusion — while brittle fracture at the male neck was completely suppressed thanks to the geometry. However, cracking in the female element exhibited tensile-softening rather than multiple fine cracking, limiting both load capacity and reusability; accelerated water curing at 60 °C was identified as the cause of shortening ECC's strain hardening — the preliminary conclusion being that material processing and curing management decide the success or failure of LEGO-style joining.

## Key Contributions

1. **Proposal of the LEGO-like ECC construction concept**: presented a vision of demountable, reconfigurable block construction that reduces landfill waste, energy use, and CO₂ emissions, and organized an evaluation framework around four elements — joint integrity, durability, safety, and structural performance.
2. **Uniaxial tension data for the dovetail joint**: inserting steel mesh at the joint raised the maximum load from 388 to 505 N (+30 %) relative to unreinforced G-ECC, and the displacement range from 4.2–5.4 mm to 4.2–6.4 mm. The mesh-reinforced specimen showed more cracks and more crushing.
3. **Identification of failure modes**: the dovetail geometry completely suppressed brittle fracture of the male neck, indicating promise as a rapid joining/disassembly scheme, but the crack at the female element's reentrant corner propagated in tensile softening rather than strain hardening (multiple fine cracking), simultaneously limiting the joint's load capacity and the reusability upon reconfiguration.
4. **Demonstration that curing governs PSH expression**: owing to schedule constraints, thermally accelerated curing (water at 60 °C for 7 days) was adopted, under which ECC tensile strain reached only 1.0–1.8 % (low compared with typical ECC); the analysis attributed the opening of the two arms of the female joint without mesh to its inability to resist through true strain hardening — a case demonstrating the importance of proper ECC material processing and curing.
5. **Follow-up design improvements proposed**: removal/mitigation of stress concentration regions, reduction of the male neck cross-section to induce multiple cracking, bolted joining of male and female elements, lap joints for stabilization, and finite element analysis before the next round of testing.

## Methods

- **Two specimen types**: (a) G-ECC joint-through type, (b) type with steel mesh reinforcement in the joint region. Joint behavior under tension was evaluated.
- **Mix properties** (Table 1; water curing at 60 °C for 7 days): compressive strength 47.5 MPa, tensile strength 4.7 MPa (first cracking 3.9 MPa), tensile strain 1.0–1.8 %. The detailed G-ECC mix table is not included in the poster.
- **Evaluation items**: load-displacement curves (Fig. 3), and post-failure crack/crushing patterns in the joint region (Fig. 4).
- **Phasing**: Phase 1 design (single block, rapid assembly/disassembly, joint behavior under tension) → Phase 2 testing (the two specimen types). This poster covers only the results of this preliminary stage.

## Results

- Mean maximum load: G-ECC 388 N, G-ECC + steel mesh 505 N (+30 %).
- Displacement: unreinforced 4.2–5.4 mm, mesh-reinforced 4.2–6.4 mm.
- Failure mode (common to both types): tensile cracks at the inner reentrant corner of the female dovetail and concrete crushing at the lower corner of the male protrusion. Both cracks and crushing increased in the mesh-reinforced specimen.
- Brittle fracture of the male neck was completely suppressed — the dovetail geometry effectively avoided stress concentration.
- Limitation: the tensile-softening failure of the female element sets the ceiling on load capacity and makes block reuse (disassembly followed by reassembly) difficult; interpreted as ECC ductility shortened by the accelerated thermal curing.
