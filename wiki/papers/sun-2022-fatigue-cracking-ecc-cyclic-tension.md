## Summary
This study evaluated the fatigue life, strain progression, microcrack density, and crack-width progression behavior of high-ductility PVA-ECC under uniaxial direct tensile fatigue loading using a high-precision servo-hydraulic fatigue testing machine. Tensile $S-N$ curves were derived according to maximum stress level ($S = \sigma_{max}/f_t = 0.65 \sim 0.90$, load ratio $R = 0.1$, frequency 4 Hz), and the fracture probability was analyzed with a two-parameter Weibull distribution model. It was demonstrated that multiple microcracks form progressively while maintaining fine crack widths of $25 \sim 40 \mu m$ even under repeated tensile fatigue, achieving a high fatigue life exceeding $10^6$ cycles at $S = 0.70$.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a three-stage damage evolution model for ECC under direct tensile fatigue loading**: (1) initial surge of multiple cracking and stiffness degradation, (2) steady increase of fatigue strain (secondary steady state), (3) localization into a critical single crack and fracture.
- **Presentation of a Weibull statistics-based reliability model for tensile fatigue life**: derivation of a safe design fatigue limit line at fracture probability $P_f = 0.05$.
- **Demonstration of microcrack width control capability under repeated fatigue loading**: individual crack widths confirmed to be constrained below $50 \mu m$ even beyond $10^5$ cycles.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, static tensile strength $5.2 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $4.8 \%$).
- **Test technique**: uniaxial direct tensile fatigue test on plate-type dogbone specimens (load control, $R = 0.1$, frequency 4 Hz).
- **Instrumentation**: LVDT strain measurement on both sides, microscope crack counting, DIC surface strain analysis.

## Results
- At fatigue stress ratio $S = 0.70$: runout without failure achieved at $1.2 \times 10^6$ cycles.
- At $S = 0.85$: mean fatigue life $N_f = 14200$ cycles, cumulative tensile strain at failure reaching $3.2 \%$.
