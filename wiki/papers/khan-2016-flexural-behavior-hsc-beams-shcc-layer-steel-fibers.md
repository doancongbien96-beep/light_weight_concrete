# Flexural Behavior of High-Strength Concrete (HSC, 75 MPa) Beams Reinforced with Strain-Hardening Composite (SHCC) Layers and Steel Fibers: 48 % Flexural Capacity Amplification, Deflection Ductility $\mu = 4.85$, and Crack Restriction

## Summary

To overcome sudden compressive crushing and wide tensile crack openings in high-strength concrete (HSC, compressive strength $75.0\ \text{MPa}$) beams, Professor M. Iqbal Khan and colleagues at King Saud University developed full-scale composite beams ($1500 \times 150 \times 200\ \text{mm}$) combining a **bottom layer of 2.0 vol.% PVA strain-hardening cementitious composite (SHCC: thickness 25, 50 mm, tensile strain capacity $3.20\%$) with hooked-end steel fibers ($V_f = 1.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) distributed in the upper HSC matrix**. Four-point bending tests, load-deflection tracking, crack opening measurements, and interface analyses demonstrated an **85 % increase in first-crack flexural load, 48 % amplification of ultimate flexural capacity ($78.5\ \text{kN} \rightarrow 116.5\ \text{kN}$), a 2.2-fold boost in deflection ductility ($\mu = 2.20 \rightarrow 4.85$), tight serviceability crack width restriction below $0.06\ \text{mm}$, and perfect composite action without delamination** compared to plain HSC reference beams.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantification of flexural composite synergy between HSC and bottom SHCC layers**:
   - Integrated upper HSC compressive resistance with lower SHCC tensile strain-hardening and 3D steel fiber shear bridging to eliminate brittle failure modes.
2. **Deflection ductility ratio $\mu = 4.85$ (2.2-fold enhancement) and plastic energy dissipation**:
   - Sustained ductile plastic deformation without abrupt load drops beyond longitudinal rebar yielding due to SHCC multiple cracking.
3. **Strict serviceability crack width restriction below $0.06\ \text{mm}$**:
   - Prevented corrosive moisture and aggressive ion ingress in harsh marine and desert environments.
4. **Sectional design guidelines for combined steel fibers and SHCC overlays**:
   - Identified that $1.0\ \text{vol.\%}$ steel fibers in HSC arrest shear cracking while a 50 mm bottom SHCC layer carries tensile stresses effectively.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Base concrete: High-strength concrete HSC ($f_c = 75.0\ \text{MPa}$, silica fume 8 %, W/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fibers:
    1. PVA fibers (Kuraray REC15: $d_f = 40\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1300\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 38\ \text{GPa}$).
    2. Hooked-end steel fibers ($d_f = 0.62\ \text{mm}, L_f = 50\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1250\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 210\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Beam series (6 full-scale beams): `Plain-HSC`, `HSC-Steel1%`, `HSC-SHCC25mm`, `HSC-SHCC50mm`, `HSC-Steel1%-SHCC50mm`.
- **Flexural test protocols (Sections 2.3–2.5 & Figs. 5–8)**:
  - Four-point bending ($1500 \times 150 \times 200\ \text{mm}$, 1350 mm span, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Instrumentation: 3 LVDTs, optical crack measurement microscope, rebar strain gauges.

## Results

### 1. First cracking load, ultimate capacity, deflection ductility, and crack width comparisons (Section 3 & Tables 4–6 & Figs. 9–14)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain-HSC (Control)** | Monolithic HSC (Plain) | **$24.0 \pm 1.0\ \text{kN}$** | **$78.5 \pm 3.0\ \text{kN}$** | $14.5 \pm 0.6\ \text{mm}$ | **$2.20 \pm 0.12$ (Brittle)** | **$0.28 \pm 0.03\ \text{mm}$** | **Brittle top concrete crushing** |
| **HSC-Steel1%** | HSC + 1% Steel Fibers | $35.0 \pm 1.5\ \text{kN}$ | $95.0 \pm 3.5\ \text{kN}$ (+21 %) | $22.0 \pm 1.0\ \text{mm}$ | $3.25 \pm 0.18$ | $0.15 \pm 0.02\ \text{mm}$ | Ductile flexure |
| **HSC-SHCC50mm** | Bottom 50 mm SHCC layer | $41.0 \pm 1.8\ \text{kN}$ | $105.0 \pm 4.0\ \text{kN}$ (+34 %) | $28.5 \pm 1.2\ \text{mm}$ | $4.10 \pm 0.20$ | $0.08 \pm 0.01\ \text{mm}$ | SHCC multiple cracking |
| **HSC-Steel-SHCC50 (Optimal)** | **HSC (1% Steel) + 50 mm SHCC** | **$44.5 \pm 1.8\ \text{kN}$ (+85 %)** | **$116.5 \pm 4.5\ \text{kN}$ (+48 %)** | **$34.0 \pm 1.5\ \text{mm}$ ($2.3\times$)** | **$4.85 \pm 0.25$ ($2.2\times$)** | **$<0.06\ \text{mm}$ (Ultra-tight)** | **Fully ductile flexural plastic hinge** |

### 2. Multi-axial stress transfer and energy dissipation mechanisms
- **Complementary compression-tension synergy**: Compressive steel fibers confined the high-strength concrete, while the bottom SHCC layer developed over 30 microcracks, increasing fracture energy by 2.3-fold.
- **Interfacial composite integrity**: Wet-on-wet casting eliminated interlayer slip ($<0.05\ \text{mm}$), preserving linear strain distributions across the beam depth up to ultimate deflection.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that combining a bottom SHCC layer with steel fibers in high-strength concrete beams elevates flexural capacity by 48 %, enhances ductility to 4.85, and restricts crack widths below 0.06 mm.
