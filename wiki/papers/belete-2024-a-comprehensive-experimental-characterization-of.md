# Comprehensive experimental characterization of graphite-carrier shape-stable PCM mortar

## Summary

Experimental study of building PCM mortars by the PAUSTI (Pan African University, Kenya) group. The filename carried a truncated title, but the full title was confirmed from the paper body. A shape-stable PCM (SSPCM, melting point 28 °C, latent heat 210 kJ/kg, thermal conductivity 18 W/(m·°C)) made of sodium sulfate decahydrate 45 % + calcium chloride hexahydrate 44 % + surfactant 3 % + graphite 8 % was incorporated into plastering mortar at mass fractions of 3~10 %, and the physical, mechanical, thermal, and economic characteristics were evaluated in an integrated way. The core conclusion is that using the high-conductivity SSPCM at low contents (up to 7 wt%) can greatly raise the heat storage capacity while satisfying all property standards. Owing to the graphite carrier, the thermal conductivity increases rather than decreases — an inversion behavior unlike ordinary PCMs.

## Key Contributions

- Confirmation of the thermal-conductivity inversion behavior: as the PCM incorporation rate rises from 0→10 %, the thermal conductivity increases almost linearly from 2.81→3.86 W/(m·°C) — exactly opposite to ordinary microencapsulated PCMs, which lower thermal conductivity, because graphite (18 W/(m·°C)) forms a conduction network.
- Linear increase in specific heat capacity (R² = 0.9962): 0.92→1.33 kJ/(kg·°C), with both latent melting and the heat-transfer efficiency provided by graphite contributing.
- Increase in thermal diffusivity and shortening of equilibration time: 1.40→1.68×10⁻⁶ m²/s, with the time to reach thermal equilibrium falling from 130 min (no PCM) to below 85 min (10 %).
- Total heat storage 13.86→47.36 kJ (sensible 13.86~37.06 kJ, latent 0~10.3 kJ) — synergy observed in which sensible storage increases together with latent heat.
- Quantitative correlation modeling between thermal and physical/mechanical properties: negative correlation between dry density↓ and thermal properties↑ (R² 0.9676~0.9921), absorption ↔ diffusivity R² = 0.9833, and a negative correlation between compressive strength and thermal properties (R² up to 0.9981) — suggesting that thermal performance can be tuned with density and strength as design variables.
- Quantified economics: at a PCM raw-material cost of 220 USD/kg versus mortar (0.15 USD/kg), the composite unit price soars from 0.15 up to 22.14 USD/kg at 10 % incorporation — explicitly identifying price, not properties, as the practical-deployment bottleneck.

## Methods

- Mix: cement:sand = 1:3 (per dry 1 kg basis, cement 250 g and sand 750 g; 32.5R Portland cement + 3.5 mm river sand); water adjusted to maintain a flow of 110 mm (W/C 125~155 mL/kg).
- Specimens: molded and cured per EN 1015-11 (28 days); compression and tension (EN 1015-11), flow (ASTM C270 flow table), density (ASTM C948-81), absorption (24 h immersion).
- Thermal conductivity: self-built guarded hot plate (ASTM C177-13), 70×70×50 mm specimens dried at 120 °C for 48 h, k-type thermocouples + TDS-630 data logger, 5 repetitions.
- Specific heat capacity: heat-exchange system (water-based reference specific heat of 4.18 kJ/(kg·°C); cooling cycle in which the 80 °C specimen is dropped into water in an insulated jug).
- Thermal diffusivity: transient plane source method + derivation via α = k/(ρcp); temperature distribution observed with four axial-direction thermocouples.
- Heat storage: sensible and latent components separated and computed as Q_total = m_PCM·L_f + m_composite·cp·ΔT.

## Results

- Physical: density 2163→1709.73 kg/m³ (-5.25~-20.96 %), absorption 8.49→16.05 %. The 3~5 % mixes fall within the standard range; 7~10 % exceed it (graphite absorbency and surface porosification).
- Mechanical: 28-day compressive strength reductions of -1.3/-4.79/-6.38/-10.45 % (at 3/5/7/10 %) and tensile strength reductions of -3.93/-7.9/-10.8/-14.2 %. Even so, every mix satisfies the top grade CS IV (≥12.5 N/mm²) of BS EN 998-1.
- Verdict: compliance with all physical, mechanical, and thermal criteria holds up to 7 wt% — at 10 % the physical criterion (absorption) is exceeded, marking the limit.
- Thermal: thermal conductivity 2.81→3.86 W/(m·°C), specific heat 0.92→1.33 kJ/(kg·°C), thermal diffusivity 1.40→1.68×10⁻⁶ m²/s, total heat storage 13.86→47.36 kJ.
- Cost: per 1 kg of composite, 0.15 (Mortar-0)→6.75→11.14→15.54→22.14 USD (3/5/7/10 %).
