# In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) Manufacturing of Functional Materials, Continuous Basalt Fiber (CBF) Spinning, and 3D Braided Structures for the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS)

## Summary

To support the Chinese Lunar Exploration Program (Chang'e-5/6) and the construction of the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS, 2030~2040), Professor Zhang He and co-workers at Wuhan University of Technology and Wuhan Textile University synthesized in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) manufacturing frameworks to eliminate earth-to-moon transportation bottlenecks (costs exceeding $10^5\ \text{\$/kg}$) by fully exploiting lunar regolith and KREEP rocks. The authors established core manufacturing processes utilizing high-temperature melting ($1400\sim 1500\ ^\circ\text{C}$) of mare basalt regolith to spin **continuous basalt fibers (CBF, tensile strength $1800\sim 2500\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $85\sim 110\ \text{GPa}$)** and 3D braid them into lightweight, ultra-tough composite structures for cosmic radiation and micrometeorite protection. The review also examined rare-earth phosphate minerals such as Changesite-(Y) for sensor applications, mobile 3D printing robotics (e.g., Platypus Galacticus), and microgravity ($1/6\text{g}$) high-purity crystal growth for lunar infrastructure.

## Key Contributions

1. **In-situ melt spinning of continuous basalt fibers (CBF) from 100 % lunar regolith**:
   - Formulated unmanned processes to melt raw lunar surface basalt regolith at $1450\ ^\circ\text{C}$ using concentrated solar energy or induction furnaces, producing continuous high-performance structural fibers (tensile strength up to 2.5 GPa) without importing earth reinforcements.
2. **3D braiding for lunar shelters and inflatable composite skins**:
   - Utilized multi-axis 3D braiding of continuous basalt fibers to fabricate delamination-free lattice beams, trusses, and Whipple shields resisting hypervelocity micrometeorite impacts.
3. **Exploitation of lunar microgravity ($1/6\text{g}$) and vacuum for material processing**:
   - Demonstrated that suppressed thermal convection and buoyancy in lunar gravity favor defect-free crystal growth and high-purity inorganic fiber manufacturing compared to terrestrial conditions.
4. **Comparative synthesis of global lunar construction programs (NASA Artemis vs ESA Moon Village vs China ILRS)**:
   - Evaluated waterless binder 3D printing, geodesic shelters, and lava tube shielding concepts across international space roadmaps.

## Methods

- **Lunar mineral resources and chemical compositions (Tables 1–2 & Fig. 2)**:
  - Chang'e-5 young basalt regolith: $\text{SiO}_2$ 42.5 %, $\text{TiO}_2$ 5.5 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 11.2 %, $\text{FeO}$ 22.8 %, $\text{CaO}$ 10.8 %, $\text{MgO}$ 6.5 %.
  - Changesite-(Y) phosphate mineral ($(\text{Ca}_8\text{Y})\Box\text{Fe}^{2+}(\text{PO}_4)_7$), enriched in yttrium and rare-earth elements.
  - KREEP terrain rocks: Potassium (K), rare-earth elements (REE: 22.5~45 billion tons estimated), and phosphorus (P).
- **Core ISRU manufacturing processes (Figures 3–5)**:
  1. Basalt melt spinning (platinum-rhodium bushing nozzles $\phi 10\sim 20\ \mu\text{m}$, drawing speed $10\sim 30\ \text{m/s}$).
  2. 3D circular braiding and yarn composite weaving.
  3. Autonomous mobile robotic 3D printing (e.g., Platypus Galacticus).
  4. Thermal solar and microwave sintering of raw regolith.

## Results

### 1. Performance metrics of lunar in-situ manufactured materials and composite structures (Comprehensive Synthesis)

| Functional Material System | Raw Material & Manufacturing Process | Tensile / Compressive Properties | Radiation & Impact Shielding | Earth Up-mass Reliance | Primary Structural Applications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Continuous Basalt Fiber (CBF)** | Melt spinning of basalt regolith at $1450\ ^\circ\text{C}$ | **Tensile strength 1800~2500 MPa, modulus 85~110 GPa** | Inorganic non-flammable radiation barrier | **0.0 % (100 % ISRU)** | Composite rebar, cables, space suits |
| **3D Braided Shelter** | 3D multi-axis interlocking of CBF | **Impact fracture energy 3.5-fold over standard composites** | Hypervelocity micrometeorite energy dissipation | 0.0 % | Habitat domes, protective barriers |
| **Sintered Regolith Block** | Concentrated solar direct sintering ($1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$) | Compressive strength 150~260 MPa (Brittle) | Heavy-element radiation shielding (2.3 m thickness) | 0.0 % | Landing pads, unpressurized roads |
| **Regolith-Polymer Composite (RPC)** | 85 % Regolith + thermoplastic melt | Compressive strength 45~55 MPa, flexure 18 MPa | Hydrogen-rich neutron shielding | $15\sim 20\ \text{wt.\%}$ | 3D printed pressurized shelters, pipes |

### 2. Strategic engineering implications for lunar base construction
- **Strategic value of in-situ fiber spinning**: Unlike unreinforced sintered blocks that resist only compression, continuous fiber composites sustain tensile forces, bending moments, and dynamic impacts necessary for pressurized inflatable habitat shells.
- **2030~2040 ILRS roadmap**: Outlines a closed-loop progression from robotic exploration to unmanned in-situ precursor construction and eventual manned habitat operations.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review synthesized the advanced technologies for 100 % ISRU functional material manufacturing, continuous basalt fiber spinning, and 3D braiding for the construction of permanent lunar research bases.
