# Construction and Radiation Shielding of Lunar Structures Using In-Situ Materials: Centering-Free Domes, Vaults, and Monolithic In-Situ Magma Casting

## Summary

To completely eliminate the transport of construction materials, formwork, and falsework from Earth for lunar base construction, Professor E. Nader Khalili (founder of Cal-Earth Institute and NASA consultant) proposed the **'Archemy' (Architecture + Alchemy)** philosophy. This approach fuses millennia-old adobe and ceramic masonry traditions from resource-scarce desert environments with concentrated solar and microwave thermal energy technologies. Utilizing unprocessed or minimally processed lunar regolith and basalt rocks, the author established **centering-free (building without centering) corbeled/leaning vaults and compression domes, interlocking velcro adobe, solar/microwave-fused lunar adobe, and monolithic magma shell casting over temporary regolith mounds**. The study proved that the lunar 1/6 gravity environment reduces self-weight thrust lines, enabling vastly larger unreinforced masonry spans than on Earth. By casting a monolithic magma shell over an earthen mound and excavating the interior soil to backfill a $2.5\sim 3.0\ \text{m}$ radiation shielding overlay, this concept realizes a 100 % in-situ circular construction process.

## Key Contributions

1. **Pioneering of the 'Archemy' paradigm in extraterrestrial architecture**:
   - Proposed firing permanent monolithic shelters directly on-site using indigenous regolith and thermal energy rather than relying on imported mechanical prefabricated components.
2. **Standardization of centering-free lunar arches, domes, and vaults**:
   - Adapted corbeled arches, leaning vaults, and squinches to construct large clear spans using 100 % in-situ blocks without temporary timber or steel centering.
3. **Development of regolith mound forming and in-situ magma shell casting**:
   - Established a formwork-free method where solar/plasma torches fuse a monolithic basaltic skin over a bulldozed earthen mound, after which interior soil is excavated for radiation shielding.
4. **Standardization of Velcro Adobe and microwave-sintered structural blocks**:
   - Formulated block manufacturing protocols combining dry angular regolith frictional interlocking with rapid microwave volumetric sintering.

## Methods

- **Structural typology and centering-free construction principles (Section: Archemy & Rock Structures)**:
  - Mechanics: Pure compression catenary thrust line alignment for single- and double-curvature shells.
  - Construction methods:
    - Leaning Arches: Masonry rings laid at an incline against an end wall, advancing without falsework.
    - Fused Monolithic Vaults: Masonry assembled and sintered vertically, then tilted horizontally into place (Fig. 1).
- **In-situ magma shell casting workflow (Section: Magma Structures & Figs. 2–3)**:
  - Stage 1: Bulldoze lunar regolith into a temporary interior-form mound.
  - Stage 2: Fuse the mound surface using Fresnel lens concentrated solar heat ($>1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$), microwave arrays, or plasma torches to cast a monolithic magma shell with ribs.
  - Stage 3: After cooling, excavate (tunnel) interior regolith to create the pressurized habitat volume.
  - Stage 4: Place excavated regolith over the exterior shell to a thickness of $2.5\sim 3.0\ \text{m}$ for radiation, meteorite, and thermal shielding.

### 2. Structural mechanics in lunar 1/6 gravity
- **Structural advantages of 1/6 gravity**: Reducing gravity to 1/6 scales dead-load thrust down by 1/6, allowing stable spans to increase by 2.45-fold compared to equivalent terrestrial masonry arches.
- **Closed-loop construction cycle**: Using the earthen mound as both casting core and subsequent radiation shield eliminates construction waste and imported falsework entirely (100 % closed loop).

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper provided the foundational architectural and civil engineering blueprint for constructing and shielding permanent lunar habitats entirely from in-situ materials without falsework.
