## Summary

The highest-authority original paper of the global high-strength ECC field, in which the research team of Prof. Victor Li (University of Michigan) and Dr. Ravi Ranade (US Army Engineer Research and Development Center, ERDC) elucidated the micromechanics-based mix design, the static and dynamic uniaxial tensile and compressive stress-strain responses, and the fracture energy dissipation mechanisms of the high-strength, high-ductility cementitious composite (HSHDC / Super-ECC) developed to realize ultra-high strength (compressive strength above 160 MPa) and high ductility (uniaxial direct tensile strain capacity of 3.5 % or higher) simultaneously in a single composite. By combining untreated-surface high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, tensile strength 3000 MPa) with an optimally packed nano/micro-particle matrix, the composite achieved a 28-day uniaxial compressive strength of **165 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **14.8 MPa**, a tensile strain capacity of **3.65 %**, and a tensile fracture energy density of $540 kJ/m^3$.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of the PSH strain-hardening criterion in an ultra-high-strength matrix ($f_c > 160 MPa$)**: controlling the matrix fracture toughness to $K_m \le 0.75 MPa\sqrt{m}$ and securing an energy index of $J_b'/J_{tip} = 3.8$.
- **Simultaneous attainment of ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility**: ductility improved 18-fold over conventional UHPC (tensile strain capacity 0.2 %) while excluding brittle explosive fracture.
- **Demonstration of saturated multiple fine cracking**: formation of a dense crack network with mean crack width $25 \mu m$ and crack spacing $1.8 mm$ even at the ultimate tensile state.
- **Creation of optimal composite materials for defense blast/ballistic protection and extreme infrastructure shielding**: provides the highest level of resistance against high-velocity impact and blast pressure waves.

## Methods

- Matrix composition: Type I cement + silica fume (20 wt %) + fine silica flour ($d_{50} = 2.5 \mu m$, 30 wt %), $W/B = 0.16$, with a high-performance polycarboxylate superplasticizer (SP).
- Fiber: Spectra 900 ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 38 µm, length 12.7 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa).
- Test equipment: servo-hydraulic dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, LVDTs on both faces), compressive strength on $100 \times 200 mm$ cylindrical specimens (ASTM C39), and single-fiber pullout testing.
