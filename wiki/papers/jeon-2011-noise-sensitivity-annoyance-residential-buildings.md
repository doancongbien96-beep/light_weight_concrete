# Influence of Noise Sensitivity on Annoyance of Indoor and Outdoor Noises in Residential Buildings

## Summary

This study utilizes multivariate statistical analysis to investigate the impact of individual noise sensitivity among apartment residents on annoyance and behavioral responses toward various indoor and outdoor noise sources (heavyweight/lightweight floor impacts, road traffic noise, and water supply/drainage noise). Questionnaires combining Weinstein's Noise Sensitivity Scale with 11-point numerical annoyance ratings and acoustic field measurements were administered to over 600 households. Floor impact noise (particularly low-frequency heavyweight impact noise) induced significantly greater resident annoyance than road traffic noise, with highly noise-sensitive groups exhibiting steep increases in annoyance at identical sound pressure levels ($L_{i,Fmax,AW}$).

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of source-dependent annoyance contributions**: Proved statistically that floor impact sounds represent the dominant contributor to degraded residential satisfaction compared to other domestic or traffic noise sources.
- **Amplification of sensitivity to low-frequency impact sounds**: Demonstrated that low-frequency (63 ~ 125 Hz) components of heavyweight impacts (rubber ball, bang machine) induce severe psychological distress and annoyance in noise-sensitive residents.
- **Integration of psychoacoustic factors into acoustic design criteria**: Highlighted the necessity of establishing floor acoustic isolation standards based on subjective auditory annoyance rather than simple $L_{eq}$ decibel metrics.

## Methods

- **Target population**: 620 households in metropolitan apartment complexes.
- **Measurement and survey instruments**:
  - Weinstein's Noise Sensitivity Scale (21-item Likert scale).
  - Subjective annoyance ratings for indoor/outdoor noise sources (0 ~ 10 point ISO 15666 scale).
  - Heavyweight floor impact levels ($L_{i,Fmax,AW}$), lightweight impact levels ($L_{n,AW}$), and traffic noise ($L_{den}$).
- **Statistical analysis**: Multiple regression, structural equation modeling (SEM), and factor analysis.

## Results

- The highly sensitive group reported annoyance ratings averaging 1.8 ~ 2.4 points higher than the normal group at identical floor impact noise levels.
- When heavyweight floor impact levels exceeded $50\ \text{dB}$, the proportion of residents reporting severe annoyance increased sharply, underscoring the necessity of high-performance resilient interlayers and ductile, high-damping composite slabs.
