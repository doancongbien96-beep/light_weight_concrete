# Interfacial Transition Zone (ITZ) Densification by Defoamer Addition in Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Cementless Composites: Mechanism Simultaneously Enhancing Compressive Strength $62.5\ \text{MPa}$ and Tensile Strain Capacity $7.52\%$

## Summary

A landmark 2017 Composite Structures origin paper by the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), Yu Lee, Jeong-Il Choi, and Hyeong-Ki Kim at Chonnam National University, which systematically evaluated how a **defoamer (added at 0 %, 0.1 %, and 0.2 % by mass of binder)** affects hardened density, compressive strength, uniaxial direct-tension strain-hardening behavior (first-cracking strength, tensile strength, tensile strain capacity), crack width ($w$), and crack spacing ($x_d$) in an eco-friendly high-ductility composite that reinforces an alkali-activated slag (**AAS: Alkali-Activated Slag**) cementless binder with hydrophobic polyethylene (**PE fiber: $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, tensile strength $2900\ \text{MPa}$**), addressing the weakness of the interfacial transition zone (ITZ) caused by entrapped air voids inevitably generated during admixture mixing and by water-film formation on fiber surfaces. The study demonstrated that the **D2 mix with 0.2 % defoamer achieved a compressive strength of $62.5\ \text{MPa}$ (+23.0 %), a tensile strength of $13.3\ \text{MPa}$ (+30.4 %), and ultra-high-ductility multiple fine cracking with a tensile strain capacity of $7.52\%$**, while the average crack width was controlled down to **$38\ \mu\text{m}$** and the crack spacing to **0.42 mm**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of simultaneous maximization of compressive strength ($62.5\ \text{MPa}$) and tensile ductility ($7.52\%$) via defoamer addition in an AAS cementless composite**:
   - Broke the usual trade-off in which ductility degrades as strength increases, achieving a singular performance gain in which compressive strength ($+23\%$), tensile strength ($+30\%$), and tensile strain capacity ($+38\%$) all improve at once with only 0.2 % defoamer addition.
2. **World-first elucidation of the mechanism by which a defoamer densifies the fiber-matrix interfacial transition zone (ITZ)**:
   - By lowering the surface tension of the mixing water, the defoamer minimizes the thickness of the water film that forms on the hydrophobic PE fiber surface in the fresh state, blocking porous ITZ defects at the source after hardening and strengthening the frictional bond.
3. **Induction of saturated PSH strain-hardening through increased complementary bridge energy ($J_b'$)**:
   - ITZ densification raises the fiber frictional shear stress ($\tau_0)$ to an appropriate level, increasing the maximum bridging stress $\sigma_0$ and the complementary bridge energy, thereby forming stable, saturated multiple fine cracks.
4. **Control of ultra-fine crack width ($38\ \mu\text{m}$) and extremely dense crack spacing (0.42 mm)**:
   - Even at extreme deformation beyond $7\%$ strain, the average crack width is held below $40\ \mu\text{m}$, securing watertightness and durability equivalent to uncracked concrete.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix proportions (Tables 1~3 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBFS, specific surface area $4380\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Alkaline activator: combined activator of high-purity sodium silicate + sodium hydroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 4.0\%$, Ms = 1.0).
  - W/B = 0.35, silica sand (S/B = 0.40, $d_{max} = 0.3\ \text{mm}$).
  - PE fiber: diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length $15\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength $2900\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $88\ \text{GPa}$, $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$.
  - Polyether-based defoamer dosage: **D0 (0.0 %), D1 (0.1 %), D2 (0.2 %)**.
- **Test methods (Section 2.2 & Figs. 2~3)**:
  - Hardened density: 28-day water/submerged-dry weight method (ASTM C642).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (ASTM C109).
  - Uniaxial direct tension: JSCE dumbbell-shaped specimens ($30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, gauge length $80\ \text{mm}$), displacement-controlled at $0.2\ \text{mm/min}$.
  - Crack pattern analysis: precise measurement of crack number, average crack spacing ($x_d$), and average crack width ($w$) using an optical microscope.

### 2. Interpretation of the defoamer action and ITZ microstructure mechanism

- **Removal of entrapped large air voids**: the entrapped air voids of $100\sim 500\ \mu\text{m}$ observed without defoamer (D0) are completely eliminated by defoamer addition (D2), increasing the effective cross-sectional area of the matrix and raising the compressive strength to $62.5\ \text{MPa}$.

- **Frictional bond optimization by water-film suppression**: suppressing the excess water film that forms on the PE fiber surface during fresh-paste mixing allows a dense C-A-S-H gel free of voids to form around fibers after hardening $\rightarrow$ the slip frictional shear stress during fiber pull-out improves, simultaneously delivering the high tensile strength of $13.3\ \text{MPa}$ and the ultra-high ductility of $7.52\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark origin study in which the Chonnam National University team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee first identified a new mechanism whereby defoamer addition densifies the fiber-surface ITZ in alkali-activated cementless fiber-reinforced composites, achieving top-tier mechanical performance with a compressive strength of $62.5\ \text{MPa}$, a tensile strength of $13.3\ \text{MPa}$, and a tensile strain capacity of $7.52\%$.
