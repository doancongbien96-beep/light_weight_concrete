# Self-healing (Self-Healing) and mechanical performance recovery mechanisms of ECC by precracking age and curing environment (cyclic wet/dry · $\text{CO}_2$)

## Summary

To elucidate how the timing of structural damage and the conditions of external environmental exposure affect crack self-healing (Self-Healing) and mechanical performance recovery in strain-hardening cementitious composites with a fine multiple-cracking character (PVA-ECC, W/B 0.38, 2.0 vol.% PVA fiber, nanoclay addition), specimens were precracked by four-point bending to a midspan deflection of 1.5 mm (multiple fine cracks with crack widths below 50 µm) at three ages (14 days, 28 days, 56 days), then given 28-day healing exposure in one of four environments (air curing, 3 % $\text{CO}_2$ exposure, cyclic wet/dry under 3 % $\text{CO}_2$, full water immersion) before a second flexural reloading test was performed — an immortal self-healing origin paper by the research team of Prof. Shunzhi Qian (Southeast Univ) and Prof. Erik Schlangen (TU Delft). The results showed that ECC specimens cured through cyclic wet/dry cycles and water immersion fully recovered and exceeded the flexural deflection ductility of undamaged (virgin) control specimens at the **100 ~ 200 % level**. The study also demonstrated that specimens damaged at the early ages of 14 and 28 days recovered **70 ~ 95 %** of their initial elastic flexural stiffness through continued rehydration of residual unreacted binders and precipitation of $\text{CaCO}_3$ crystals within the cracks, with ultimate flexural strength increasing above the level of undamaged specimens.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstrated 100 % complete recovery of flexural deflection ductility in precracked damaged members**:
   - Specimens pre-flexured to develop cracks up to 1.5 mm, upon reloading after self-healing, generated new microcracks successively around the previously healed crack faces and re-manifested **80 ~ 200 % deflection capacity** relative to undamaged controls.
2. **Quantification of self-healing efficiency by damage age (14, 28, 56 days) and curing environment**:
   - **Cyclic Wet/Dry (with $\text{CO}_2$)**: repeated supply of moisture and dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ maximizes precipitation of calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals, giving the best stiffness recovery rate.
   - **Water Curing**: promotes C-S-H rehydration reactions, most favorable for ultimate strength recovery.
   - **Damage-age effect**: specimens damaged at 14 and 28 days, thanks to their high proportion of unreacted binder, show markedly higher strength and stiffness recovery rates than fully matured 56-day specimens.
3. **Elucidation of the internal water supply (Water Reservoir) mechanism of nanoclay**:
   - Interlayer-expansive nanoclay retains more than 95 % of its own mass as interlayer water, slowly releasing internal moisture to sustain self-healing even under air exposure where external moisture is limited.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mix design (Table 1)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (CEM I 42.5 N) $570\ \text{kg/m}^3$ + fly ash (Class F) $684\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (FA/C = 1.20).
  - Fine aggregate: micro silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, Sand/Binder = 0.36), W/B = 0.38.
  - Internal reservoir material: hydrophilic nanoclay (Nanoclay, 1.0 wt.% by cement mass).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber (diameter $39\ \mu\text{m}$, length 12 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elastic modulus 41 GPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
- **Precracking induction and healing curing conditions**:
  - Precracking: four-point bending loading to a midspan deflection of 1.5 mm (at 14, 28, and 56 days).
  - Healing environments (28 days):
    - Condition A: reference undamaged control (Reference Virgin).
    - Condition B: indoor air curing (Air, 20 °C, 50 % RH).
    - Condition C: accelerated air curing under 3 % $\text{CO}_2$.
    - Condition D: cyclic wet/dry curing (Cyclic Wet/Dry: 12 h air drying under 3 % $\text{CO}_2$ + 12 h water immersion).
    - Condition E: complete water immersion curing at 20 °C (Water Curing).
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Four-point flexural loading test: $40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 160\ \text{mm}$ beams (span 120 mm, displacement control $0.15\ \text{mm/min}$, MTS servo-controlled testing machine).
  - Microstructural analysis of crack sealing by environmental scanning electron microscopy (ESEM) and optical microscopy.

## Results

### 1. Mechanical recovery characteristics after self-healing by curing regime and damage age (Figure summary)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **14-day damage** | Air curing (Air) | 9.8 ± 0.4 | 1.05 | 65 ± 5 % | 95 ± 8 % | partial precipitation |
| **14-day damage** | Wet/Dry cycle (Wet/Dry) | **12.4 ± 0.6** | **1.33 (+33 %)** | **88 ± 4 %** | **145 ± 12 %** | **complete calcite sealing** |
| **14-day damage** | Water curing (Water) | **13.1 ± 0.5** | **1.41 (+41 %)** | **82 ± 5 %** | **160 ± 15 %** | **C-S-H + carbonate crystals** |
| **28-day damage** | Air curing (Air) | 10.2 ± 0.4 | 0.98 | 60 ± 4 % | 90 ± 7 % | partial precipitation |
| **28-day damage** | Wet/Dry cycle (Wet/Dry) | **12.8 ± 0.5** | **1.23 (+23 %)** | **92 ± 3 % (highest stiffness)**| **150 ± 10 %** | **crack width 50 µm fully healed**|

### 2. Self-healing mechanisms and ESEM analysis
- **Crack width limit**: multiple fine cracks with initial widths of 50 µm or less are sealed 100 % completely by calcium carbonate crystals within 4~5 cyclic wet/dry cycles.
- **Redamage resistance**: the bond strength of healed crack faces is sufficient that, on second loading, cracks form at new locations rather than reopening existing ones, and ductile behavior continues.

- **Overall conclusion**: the 50 µm fine-crack control capability of ECC realizes perfect mechanical ductility and stiffness self-healing under cyclic wet/dry regimes and moisture supply, establishing ECC as a smart self-healing material capable of maintaining semi-permanent structural durability without separate repair injection work.
