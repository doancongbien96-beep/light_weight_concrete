# Fiber Reinforcement Categories (Steel, Glass, Carbon, Synthetic, Recycled), Bridging Mechanics, Hybrid Synergies, and Mechanical Performance of FRC: A Review

## Summary

To restrain tensile crack opening, elevate flexural toughness, enhance impact resistance, and improve fatigue durability in brittle cementitious matrices, Dr. Wafa Abdelmajed Labib (Liverpool John Moores University) compiled a comprehensive review on fiber-reinforced cement composites (FRC) in *High Performance Concrete Technology and Applications* (2018). The paper synthesizes the **physical and mechanical properties of 5 primary commercial fiber families (steel, glass, carbon, synthetic polymers such as PP/PVA/polyolefin, and industrial recycled fibers), geometric configurations (straight, hooked-end, crimped, twisted), fiber-matrix interfacial bonding mechanics, and composite mechanical behavior**. The review establishes multi-scale hybridization principles (combining macro steel fibers and micro polymer fibers) that amplify flexural fracture toughness by **$3\sim 10$-fold** across multi-scale cracking regimes.

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive synthesis of 5 primary fiber families (steel, glass, carbon, synthetic, recycled)**:
   - Evaluated the complete property spectrum: tensile strength ($300\sim 4000\ \text{MPa}$), elastic modulus ($5\sim 400\ \text{GPa}$), elongation at break ($1.5\sim 25\%$), and alkali resistance.
2. **Single fiber pullout and interfacial friction bridging mechanics**:
   - Formulated debonding fracture energy ($G_d$), frictional bond strength ($\tau_0$), snubbing coefficient ($f$), and geometric mechanical anchorage (hooked-end, crimped, twisted) governing pullout energy dissipation.
3. **Synergistic multi-scale hybrid fiber systems**:
   - Demonstrated that combining micro synthetic fibers (delaying microcrack initiation) with macro steel fibers (bridging macro flexural cracks) maximizes both modulus of rupture (MOR) and post-cracking toughness.
4. **Structural engineering of recycled industrial fibers (tire steel wires, plastic waste)**:
   - Established design baselines for sustainable, circular-economy FRC incorporating industrial waste fibers to lower embodied carbon.

## Methods

- **Literature scope and technical standards (Sections 1–6, Tables 1–4)**:
  - Synthesized over 80 seminal FRC studies and ACI 544 guidelines.
  - Fiber systems analyzed:
    1. Steel fibers (hooked-end, straight, crimped).
    2. Glass fibers (alkali-resistant AR-glass).
    3. Carbon fibers (pitch-based, PAN-based).
    4. Synthetic polymeric fibers (PP, PE, PVA, polyolefin).
    5. Natural and recycled fibers (recycled tire steel, bagasse, sisal).
- **Performance evaluation parameters**:
  - Direct tensile strength, modulus of rupture (MOR), flexural toughness indices (ASTM C1018, JSCE-SF4), and impact fracture energy.

### 2. Single fiber pullout and multi-scale bridging mechanics
- **Mechanical anchorage in hooked-end steel fibers**: Plastic straightening of terminal hooks amplifies pullout peak loads by over 3-fold compared to straight fibers, dramatically boosting flexural energy absorption.
- **Hybrid multi-scale synergy**: Established a 2-stage defense mechanism where micro synthetic fibers arrest microcracks while macro steel fibers bridge macrocracks.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review systematized fiber bridging mechanics and hybridization design rules across all major fiber classes, establishing an authoritative textbook reference for fiber-reinforced concrete.
