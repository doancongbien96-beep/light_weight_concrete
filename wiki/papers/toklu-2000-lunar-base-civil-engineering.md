## Summary
An ASCE Space 2000 conference classic that comprehensively proposes the design framework and construction methodology of Space Civil Engineering — traditional civil-engineering structural design theory (structural optimization, soil-structure interaction SSI, modular assembly engineering) adapted to the space environment — for building a permanent base economically and safely under the harsh lunar-surface environment (1/6 g low gravity, ultra-high vacuum, diurnal $270 °C$ thermal cycles, solar-wind radiation, micrometeorites). The paper derives bearing-capacity formulas for lunar regolith and optimum cross-section design formulas for inflatable bolted-dome hybrid structures.

## Key Contributions
- **Systematization of the four core disciplines of space civil engineering**: (1) lunar regolith geotechnics, (2) structural optimization under vacuum, (3) ISRU in-situ construction materials science, (4) robotics-based autonomous construction engineering.
- **Modified Terzaghi ultimate bearing capacity formula under lunar low gravity (1/6 g)**: establishment of the estimation model $q_{ult} = c N_c + \frac{1}{6} \gamma B N_\gamma + q N_q$.
- **Lunar landing loads and base dome structure optimization**: derivation of the optimal surface that offsets the bending moment induced by internal pressurization under a 2.5 m regolith cover.
- **Presentation of a phased lunar infrastructure master plan**: establishment of the construction sequence from unmanned rover site-preparation work through completion of a permanent airtight habitat.

## Methods
- Structural mechanics: tension membrane theory and beams on elastic foundation.
- Lunar regolith ground properties: cohesion $c = 0.5 \sim 3.0 kPa$, internal friction angle $\phi = 35° \sim 48°$, unit weight $\gamma = 15 \sim 19 kN/m^3$.

## Results
- Reference table of design parameters and load combinations for lunar base structures:

| Structural element | Primary load combination | Design performance requirement | Optimum structural form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Habitat module | 1 atm internal pressurization + overburden pressure | Tension-compression balance, radiation shielding | Inflatable Kevlar dome + 2.5 m regolith cover |
| Landing pad | $1500 °C$ rocket plume + landing impact | Suppression of high-temperature ejecta, assured bearing capacity | Sintered regolith tiles + compacted subbase layer |
| Connecting roadways | Unmanned transport rover wheel loads ($10 kN$) | Dust-ejecta suppression, flatness retention | Microwave-melted solidified regolith pavement |
