## Summary
A doctoral dissertation presented by Dr. Zhe Zhang of Professor Victor Li's research laboratory at the University of Michigan, this work is a landmark treatise consolidating the mathematical theory of the intrinsic reinforcement provided by discontinuous micro-fibers (PVA, PE, steel, and natural fibers) in ECC: the fiber-matrix interfacial chemical bonding ($G_d$) and frictional slip ($\tau_0$), the snubbing effect ($f$), the matrix spalling factor ($f'$), and the micromechanics-based mathematical framework for multiple fine crack propagation behavior. Through three-dimensional spatial orientation of discontinuous fibers and interface tailoring, it completed a design theory for next-generation highly ductile composites that achieve an ultimate tensile strain capacity of **12.5 %** with only 1.5 vol % fiber content.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of unified constitutive equations for discontinuous micro-fiber bridging micromechanics**: complete integral formulation of the four-stage sequence from single-fiber pull-out to bridging stress-crack opening displacement ($\sigma-\delta$) to multiple cracking propagation.
- **Establishment of interface parameter tailoring criteria**: identification of the optimal frictional stress ($\tau_0 = 1.2 \sim 2.0 MPa$) and chemical bonding ($G_d \le 2.0 J/m^2$) ranges for fully slip-dominated pull-out without fiber rupture.
- **Construction of a comparative database across diverse fiber systems (PVA, UHMWPE, cellulose, metallic)**: provision of a standard micromechanics reference guide for highly ductile composite researchers worldwide.
- **Design innovation for next-generation eco-friendly low-cost ductile infrastructure materials**: laying the groundwork for standardization of seismic strengthening of global civil structures.

## Methods
- Micromechanics analysis: energy release rate equilibrium equations, and a fiber strength distribution model coupled with Weibull statistics.
- Experimental verification: more than 500 single-fiber pull-out tests, dogbone direct tensile tests, and fluorescence microscopy analysis of fiber orientation/dispersion on polished sections.
