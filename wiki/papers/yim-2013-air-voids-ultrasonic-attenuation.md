# Quantification of pore size distribution based on ultrasonic attenuation

## Summary

A user-group paper (co-authored by Jae-Hong Kim of KAIST and Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University). It proposes a nondestructive quantification technique that interprets ultrasonic attenuation measurements caused by pores in hardened cementitious materials with a modified Roney model and derives the three-dimensional pore volume fraction and size distribution through nonlinear optimization (inversion). Using a log-normal distribution as the basis function, cross-validation was performed against image analysis and permeable pore tests, and the method remains effective even at low frequencies of 500 kHz and below, enabling application to thick real structures.

## Key Contributions

1. **Ultrasonic attenuation spectrum to 3D pore distribution inversion framework**: volume fraction and size distribution derived simultaneously via the modified Roney model plus nonlinear optimization. Good agreement with permeable pore volume fraction.
2. **Log-normal distribution is the optimal basis**: goodness-of-fit testing against image-processing distributions showed log-normal superiority; using it as the basis function for inversion, first and second moments show no significant difference from image analysis results.
3. **Overcoming the high-frequency limit**: effective at low frequencies of 500 kHz and below — field applicability to thick members secured.
4. **High-frequency attenuation condition identified**: high-frequency attenuation surges at a permeable pore volume fraction of 34 % — corresponding to an inverted pore volume fraction of 8.2 %.

## Methods

- Materials: cement paste (specimens with modulated permeable pore volume fractions).
- Equipment: attenuation spectra measured with ultrasonic transmitting and receiving probes.
- Analysis: modified Roney model + nonlinear optimization with a log-normal basis → volume fraction and size distribution derived.
- Verification: cross-compared against ASTM C642 permeable pore volume fraction and sectional image analysis (size distribution).

## Results

- The increase in high-frequency attenuation is governed by the pore volume fraction.
- Inverted volume fraction versus permeable pore volume fraction: agreement improves when the log-normal basis is used.
- Effective even at low frequencies (500 kHz and below) — NDT of real structures possible.
