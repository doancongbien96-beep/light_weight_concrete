# Crack healing in ECC with mineral healing agent and SAP — evaluation by a modified permeability test

## Summary

A paper (corresponding author Hyeong-Ki Kim of Chosun University with co-corresponding
Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University) that adapts a plain-mortar permeability setup
for ECC (cast-in injection hole plus a truss jig for active strain control — a novel active
strain-control method for multiple-cracking ECC), then evaluates crack healing over 28 days
by permeability, resonant frequency, stiffness, and crack imaging as functions of pre-load
strain, fiber content, mineral healing agent (SHA), and superabsorbent polymer (SAP). Core
conclusion: healing performance is governed by fiber content rather than SHA/SAP addition;
SAP contributes early-age sealing while SHA contributes stiffness recovery in unrestrained
conditions.

## Key Contributions

1. Novel active strain-control method for multiple-cracking ECC: measuring and adjusting
   strain with bottom pins and digital calipers holds target pre-load strain within ±10 % —
   alongside the plain-mortar active crack-width control (Van Mullem truss jig), enabling
   direct comparison between ECC and mortar.
2. Pre-load strain effect (R1-F1.25): maximum crack widths MCW of 0.05–0.1/0.18/0.58/0.80 mm
   at 0.3/1/2/3 % strain. Even at 2–3 % strain the healed fraction reached 0.9 within 24–48 h
   — beyond the limit of width control, fiber bridging preserves most of the healing capacity.
3. Dominance of fiber content: reducing PE from 1.25 % to 0.5 % raises initial flow 8–30x,
   and the 28-day healed fraction falls −64 %/−67 %/−73 %/−84 % in R1/R2/SHA15/SAP1.0.
   Below 0.2 mm crack width the binder type governs healing; above it, fiber content does.
4. SHA (OPC clinker : CSA clinker : anhydrite = 4:1:1) at 7.5/15 % binder substitution:
   ductility penalty (higher MCW, lower TCL) → fewer but wider cracks worsen permeability-
   based healing (28-day healed fraction 0.66/0.77 versus control R3 1.0). Unrestrained RF
   and stiffness recovery however beat the control — dense healing products plus crack closure
   on load removal.
5. SAP (acrylamide/sodium acrylate copolymer) at 0.5/1.0 %: no ductility loss; early sealing
   adding +0.2–0.4 to the healed fraction within 12–24 h (swollen particles directly sealing
   cracks; the gap narrows to ~0.1 after 7 days). Considering compressive/tensile strength
   losses, 0.5 % is optimal. Final 28-day healed fractions: SAP0.5-F1.25-S2 0.95,
   SAP1.0-F1.25-S2 and R3-F1.25-S2 1.0, both remaining controls 0.91 — achieved even at MCW
   0.6–0.74 mm.

## Methods

Binder = slag cement (OPC:GGBS = 1:1 by weight; OPC CEM II/B-S per ASTM C595 Type I, GGBS
KS F2563 Type 3 / ASTM C989 grade 100); silica sand (median 190 μm) + crumb rubber (400 μm,
omitted in R2 and SAP series to avoid matrix porosity increase); PE:PVA = 25:2 by volume
(PE 1.25 % + PVA 0.10 %). With SAP, 9.5 g water per gram of SAP is added extra. Ø5 mm
injection holes were formed in prism specimens with a straw during casting; cracks were
introduced by three-point flexure then fixed in the jig. Permeability test: constant head
0.5 m, load cell (±0.1 g) logged every 2 s, 11 measurement points from 5 min to 28 days;
healed fraction = 1 - Q/Qmax. Between measurements specimens were water-soaked at the crack
and stored in a 20 °C / RH 60 % chamber. Auxiliary tests: dog-bone (33×6×1.3 cm) tension to
peak, ASTM C215 RF, stiffness recovery, crack imaging (healed fraction and relative width).

## Results

- Against plain mortar: an unreinforced specimen at 2 % strain formed a single 1.42 mm crack,
  beyond self-healing. SHA15-F0 mortar (0.85 mm crack) had initial flow ~700 g/min, over 25x
  that of ECC (F1.25), with a 28-day healed fraction below 0.1 versus ECC's 0.77.
- Calcite crystal deposition on fiber surfaces (XRD confirmed, prior work) is the key sealing
  mechanism — fibers provide both permeability reduction and deposition sites for healing
  products.
- Cross-validation of methods: healed-fraction trends agree with Liu 2017 (mean tensile crack
  47 µm, falling head, PVA 2 %) and Ma 2014 (splitting crack 25 µm, pressurized, PVA 2 %),
  confirming reproducibility of ECC healing trends across setups and crack-generation methods.
  Absolute comparisons require caution since strain and crack width definitions differ by
  method.
