# Effects of Infilled Concrete and Longitudinal Rebar on Flexural Performance of Composite PHC Piles

## Summary

This study (user group; co-authored by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University,
corresponding author Prof. Yun Yong Kim, Chungnam National University) evaluated the flexural
performance of ICP (infilled composite PHC) piles, in which a hollow PHC (prestressed,
centrifugally cast high-strength concrete) pile is reinforced internally with infilled
concrete and longitudinal rebar, through four-point loading tests and nonlinear layered
sectional analysis. The key finding splits in two: infilled concrete alone improves
pure-flexural performance by a negligible margin (1.04×), whereas combined with longitudinal
rebar the maximum flexural moment surges by 1.95–2.31×. The reason infill alone is ineffective
is that the tensile strength of the infilled concrete is only about 1/2 that of the parent PHC
body while sitting at the section core near the neutral axis, where it cannot contribute to
flexural resistance.

## Key Contributions

- Quantified proof that infilled-concrete-only reinforcement is ineffective: ICP-24-D0 (infill
  only) reached a maximum load of 212 kN vs 203 kN for the reference PHC-500 (1.04×), and
  first-cracking loads were practically identical at 138 vs 132 kN — rebutting the common
  assumption that "infill = reinforcement" under pure flexure.
- Strength gains when longitudinal rebar accompanies the infill: with eight D19 bars, maximum
  load 408 kN (2.01×) / flexural moment 390 kN·m (1.95×); with eight D25 bars, 497 kN (2.41×) /
  463 kN·m (2.31×). First-cracking loads rose to 167 (+20.1 %) and 174 kN respectively.
- Role of the interface-integration device clarified: D10 lateral shear rebars inserted during
  PHC fabrication (inverted-triangular star-shaped arrangement, spaced at 200 mm) mechanically
  tie the body–infill boundary; no delamination whatsoever occurred throughout the tests.
- Reinforcement effect extended through P-M interaction analysis: although meaningless in pure
  flexure, the infilled concrete does contribute to axial compressive capacity, so the
  strengthening effect grows the more compression-dominated the region. A design guideline is
  offered: where axial force falls below the axial force corresponding to maximum moment,
  longitudinal rebar is essential.

## Methods

- Parent PHC: φ500-80t, diameter 500 mm / thickness 80 mm / length 8,000 mm, compressive
  strength 87.9 MPa. Skeleton: twelve high-strength rebars (HSR; nominal diameter 8.3 mm,
  tensile strength 1,450 MPa / yield 1,300 MPa) prestressed to 70 % of their tensile strength.
  Centrifugal casting + steam curing for 12 h, then demolding (Bang et al. 2013 process).
- Infill: normal-strength concrete of compressive strength 28.4 MPa (slump 150 mm, maximum
  coarse aggregate size 20 mm) plus eight longitudinal D19 or D25 bars (grade fy = 400 MPa;
  measured yield 483–492 MPa). After infilling, 28 d air-dried curing.
- Testing: four-point loading per KS F 4306, pure-flexure span 1,000 mm, load-controlled via a
  1,500 kN actuator, midspan deflection measured by LVDT (capacity 100 mm). Four specimen types
  (PHC-500 control, ICP-24-D0/D19/D25).
- Analysis: nonlinear sectional analysis applying the theoretical stress–strain relations of
  concrete, HSR, and rebar to layered sections (initial prestress included; terminated at a
  concrete compressive strain of 0.003), plus P-M interaction analysis by the same procedure.

## Results

- Maximum load: 203 → 212 → 408 → 497 kN (D0/D19/D25 at 1.04/2.01/2.41× respectively).
  Deflection at maximum load fell 69.5 → 65.5 → 60.0 → 49.5 mm (0.94/0.86/0.71×): the heavier
  the reinforcement, the lower the ductility — added longitudinal rebar over-reinforces the
  section so compressive-side concrete crushing governs.
- Initial stiffness (before first cracking): 29.1 → 31.5 → 44.8 → 45.3 kN/mm.
- Moment prediction accuracy: analysis gave 200/200/380/476 kN·m against experimental
  199/204/390/463 kN·m, errors -2.54 % to +2.80 % (D19 -2.54 %, D25 +2.80 %) — the
  layered-section approach predicts the ICP maximum flexural moment precisely.
- Crack patterns: cracks stayed localized at midspan of the pure-flexure region in the control,
  while the reinforced specimens saw cracking spread along the bottom face over the entire span
  (stiffness increase → deflection-confinement effect).
