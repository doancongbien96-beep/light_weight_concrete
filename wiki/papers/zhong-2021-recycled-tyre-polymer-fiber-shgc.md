## Summary
This study investigated the single-fiber pull-out bridging characteristics, direct tensile strain-hardening envelope, and microstructure of an eco-friendly highly ductile strain-hardening geopolymer composite (RTPF-SHGC) that upcycles recycled tyre polymer fiber (RTPF, diameter 20 ~ 30 µm, length 10 ~ 20 mm, tensile strength 450 ~ 650 MPa), discharged in large quantities from waste tyre recycling processes, as a replacement (0 ~ 100 wt % substitution, total $V_f = 2.0 \%$) for expensive commercial PVA fiber. The RTPF 50 % substitution mix (PVA 1.0 % + RTPF 1.0 %) achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **48.5 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **5.2 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **4.5 %**, while cutting fiber cost by **48 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Micromechanical demonstration of recycled tyre polymer fiber (RTPF) applicability to highly ductile composites**: securing a frictional stress of $\tau_0 = 1.25 MPa$ to induce stable bridging.
- **Establishment of PVA-RTPF hybrid bridging synergy**: combining the early crack restraint of hydrophilic PVA with the stable slip pull-out of hydrophobic RTPF.
- **A 48 % reduction in fiber cost and waste tyre resource circulation**: establishing a foundation for commercialization in large-scale eco-friendly road paving and repair materials.
- **Multiple fine crack control**: maintaining an average crack width of $35 \mu m$ or less up to failure.

## Methods
- Binder: F-grade fly ash 70 % + GGBS 30 %, with liquid sodium silicate activator ($W/B = 0.32$).
- Fiber combinations: PVA only (2.0 %), RTPF only (2.0 %), hybrid (PVA 1.5 % + RTPF 0.5 %, PVA 1.0 % + RTPF 1.0 %).
- Tests: dogbone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), single-fiber pull-out test, 28 d compressive strength, SEM microstructure.
