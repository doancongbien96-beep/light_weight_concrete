# Effect of Rebar Corrosion on the Natural Frequency and Modal Damping of Reinforced Concrete (RC) Beams: A First-Mode Frequency Drop of $28\%$, a 2.5-Fold Increase in Damping Ratio, and an Immortal Classic of Dynamic Damage Diagnosis

## Summary

To enable early quantitative diagnosis of internal stiffness degradation and crack damage from chloride-induced rebar corrosion in real highway bridges and marine reinforced concrete (RC) structures via non-destructive dynamic vibration measurement, **full-scale RC beam specimens (length $2.5\ \text{m}$, cross-section $150 \times 250\ \text{mm}$) were fabricated and electrochemical accelerated corrosion testing was used to induce rebar sectional loss rates ($0\%, 5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%$) and longitudinal corrosion cracks stage by stage**; precision modal testing with an electrodynamic shaker and accelerometers was then combined with four-point flexural ultimate failure experiments, demonstrating **an abrupt drop of up to $28.5\%$ in the first-mode flexural natural frequency with progressing rebar corrosion, a massive $2.5$-fold increase in the modal damping ratio ($\zeta$), and the derivation of a clear quantitative correlation formula between longitudinal corrosion crack width and dynamic stiffness degradation rate** — the immortal supreme landmark classic paper in structural health monitoring (SHM) and non-destructive diagnosis published in Engineering Structures in 2001 by **the research team of Chair Professor Hashim Abdul Razak and F. C. Choi of the University of Malaya, Malaysia**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first quantitative correlation of the 1st–3rd modal parameters (natural frequencies and damping ratios) of RC beams under rebar corrosion (historic origin paper)**:
   - Completely recorded the entire chain of physical changes inflicted on structural dynamic characteristics by corrosion-induced rebar section reduction, concrete cover spalling, and loss of rebar-concrete bond.
2. **Establishment of a precise back-calculation formula for the internal rebar sectional loss rate ($\le 20\%$) from the natural frequency drop ratio ($\Delta f/f_0$)**:
   - Proved that the first-mode frequency decreases linearly from the earliest stage of corrosion onward, making it a key indicator for non-destructive structural safety assessment.
3. **Elucidation of the mechanism behind the sharp increase (250 %) in the modal damping ratio ($\zeta$)**:
   - Demonstrated that frictional contact between longitudinal micro-crack faces generated by the expansion of corrosion products dissipates vibration energy, surging the damping ratio from $1.2\% \rightarrow 3.0\%$.
4. **Establishment of the standard vibration-based SHM diagnosis algorithm for global bridges and marine infrastructure**:
   - Completed the foundation for non-destructively determining the progression of internal rebar corrosion invisible to the naked eye using only an accelerometer sensor network.

## Methods

- **Test specimens and accelerated corrosion (Section 2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Specimen geometry: full-scale RC beams ($150 \times 250 \times 2500\ \text{mm}$, two T16 bottom main rebars, two T10 top bars, R6 stirrups at R6@150 mm spacing).
  - Concrete: 28-day compressive strength $38.5\ \text{MPa}$, cover thickness $25\ \text{mm}$.
  - Accelerated corrosion: direct-current power supply while immersed in $5.0\%\ \text{NaCl}$ aqueous solution (corrosion levels 0 %, 5 %, 10 %, 15 %, 20 %).
- **Modal testing and static-load flexural testing (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Vibration test: single-point excitation with multi-point measurement (SISO modal shaker, anti-symmetric excitation, frequency response function (FRF) analysis of modes 1 to 3).
  - Static test: four-point flexural failure test (load-deflection curves, residual ultimate capacity evaluation).

### 2. Interpretation of dynamic stiffness loss and modal damping energy dissipation
- **Second-order collapse of flexural stiffness ($EI$)**: when the corrosion level reaches $20\%$, more than $48\%$ of the effective second moment of area ($I_{eff}$) is lost to corrosion cracks, dropping the natural frequency by $28.6\%$.
- **Coulomb friction damping at crack interfaces**: demonstrated that longitudinal crack faces sliding against one another under dynamic vibration amplify the viscous damping ratio of the structure 2.5-fold.

- **Overall conclusion**: the research team of Prof. Hashim Abdul Razak at the University of Malaya quantified for the first time in the world the mechanical correlation between the natural frequency drop and the modal damping ratio increase of RC beams under rebar corrosion, establishing this as the historic supreme immortal classic of vibration-based smart structural health monitoring (SHM) and non-destructive corrosion diagnosis engineering — a landmark origin paper.
