## Summary
This study evaluated the physicochemical characteristics, tensile strength, alkali/heat resistance durability, and RC structural strengthening performance of eco-friendly inorganic basalt fibers (Basalt Fiber) manufactured by melt-spinning volcanic basalt rock, together with basalt FRP (BFRP) sheets. Comparative analysis against carbon fiber (CFRP) and glass fiber (GFRP) confirmed that basalt fiber possesses outstanding heat resistance, retaining 90 % of its tensile strength even at $600 °C$ high temperature, and demonstrated excellent structural strengthening efficiency, increasing the flexural capacity of surface-bonded flexural and shear strengthening of RC beam members by more than 40 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the heat and alkali environmental resistance of basalt fiber**: higher alkali resistance than glass fiber and better economy/elongation ($\approx 2.5 \%$) than carbon fiber were demonstrated.
- **Analysis of the bond failure behavior of externally bonded BFRP sheet flexural strengthening of RC beams**: interfacial shear bond strength ($\tau_{max} \approx 2.8 MPa$) and anchorage details preventing premature debonding were proposed.
- **Evaluation of residual strength after high-temperature exposure**: while CFRP drops off rapidly owing to epoxy carbonization upon $400 °C$ exposure, inorganic-matrix-bonded BFRP maintains structural performance at high temperature.

## Methods
- **Fiber specifications**: basalt continuous fiber (filament diameter 13 µm, density $2.75 g/cm3$, tensile strength 3100 MPa, elastic modulus 90 GPa).
- **Test items**: single-fiber and strand tensile tests, accelerated durability testing by immersion in $60 °C$ alkaline solution ($pH 12.5$), and full-scale RC beam flexural strengthening tests.

## Results
- Flexurally strengthened RC beams: yield load increased by 22 % and ultimate flexural capacity by 44 % compared with the unstrengthened control.
- Tensile strength retention after 28 d alkaline exposure: 82 % for basalt fiber (superior to 55 % for E-glass fiber).
