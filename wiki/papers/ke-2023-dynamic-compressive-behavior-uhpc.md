# Dynamic Compressive Behavior and Constitutive Model of Ultra-High-Performance Concrete

## Summary

This study experimentally and analytically investigates the dynamic compressive mechanical behavior and fracture mechanisms of ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC, static compressive strength 135 MPa) under high strain rates ($\dot{\epsilon} = 30 \sim 250\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $30 \sim 250 s^{-1}$) using a Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB) system. The authors quantified the dynamic increase factor ($DIF$), dynamic elastic modulus, and energy absorption density across steel fiber volume fractions (0 vol %, 1.5 vol %, 2.5 vol %) and varying strain rates, proposing a dynamic nonlinear damage constitutive model that integrates crack inertial resistance and viscous damping mechanisms.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of UHPC dynamic compression across high-strain-rate regimes**: Demonstrated that dynamic compressive strength increases by up to 1.62-fold ($DIF = 1.62$) as the strain rate rises from $35\ \text{s}^{-1}$ ($35 s^{-1}$) to $210\ \text{s}^{-1}$ ($210 s^{-1}$).
- **Mitigation of impact shattering via steel fiber content**: Adding $V_f = 2.5\%$ steel fibers prevented specimen pulverization under high-speed impact, preserving cylindrical geometry and increasing energy absorption density by 3.8-fold.
- **Formulation of strain-rate-dependent nonlinear damage constitutive model**: Developed a continuum damage model combining Weibull statistical damage distributions with rate sensitivity exponents to capture wide strain rate regimes.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions**: $W/B = 0.18$, silica fume 20 %, silica sand (0.1 ~ 0.5 mm), straight high-strength steel fibers (diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm, tensile strength 2800 MPa).
- **SHPB impact testing**: Diameter 75 mm aluminum/high-strength steel pressure bar system with pulse shapers to ensure dynamic stress equilibrium and constant strain rates.
- **Dynamic damage characterization**: High-speed camera (100000 fps) recording crack propagation and 3D fragment size statistical analysis.

## Results

- Static compressive strength: $V_f = 0\%: 122.5\ \text{MPa}$ ($122.5 MPa$), $V_f = 1.5\%: 138.4\ \text{MPa}$ ($138.4 MPa$), $V_f = 2.5\%: 152.0\ \text{MPa}$ ($152.0 MPa$).
- Dynamic impact tests ($\dot{\epsilon} \approx 180\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $180 s^{-1}$):
  - $V_f = 0\%$: Dynamic strength $165.2\ \text{MPa}$ ($165.2 MPa$, $DIF = 1.35$), complete shattering into fine debris.
  - $V_f = 2.5\%$: Dynamic strength $246.2\ \text{MPa}$ ($246.2 MPa$, $DIF = 1.62$), fracture energy absorption density $18.4\ \text{MJ/m}^3$ ($18.4 MJ/m3$).
