## Summary
A landmark poster paper presented by Prof. Victor Li and Dr. Ravi Ranade of the University of Michigan with support from the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF). Through nano-scale C-S-H gel and fiber-matrix nano-interface modification (Nanostructure Engineering), it established the micromechanical design principles of a construction material for resilient, sustainable infrastructure (Resilient ECC) that resists cryogenic/ultra-high temperatures and seismic/impact extreme loads, together with an autogenous self-healing (Self-healing) mechanism. By doping nanoparticles (nano-silica, nano-clay), the fiber-matrix chemical bond energy ($G_d$) was controlled to $1.8 J/m^2$ or below, demonstrating tensile strain capacity of 5.0 % or higher and a self crack-closure rate of 95 % or higher.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishing the synergy between nanostructure control, strain-hardening, and self-healing**: densifying the matrix with nano C-S-H gel refines matrix flaws while securing the PSH strain-hardening condition ($J_b'/J_{tip} > 5.0$).
- **Proposing a nano-confinement mechanism limiting crack width to $30 \mu m$ or below**: accelerated reprecipitation of $CaCO_3$ crystals from unhydrated nanoparticles upon moisture contact inside cracks.
- **Realizing eco-friendly highly ductile infrastructure with a 50 % reduction in embodied energy**: mix design fusing fly ash and nano-pozzolans.

## Methods
- Matrix: cement + fly ash ($FA/C = 1.5$) + nano-silica ($1.0 \sim 3.0 wt \%$), $W/B = 0.26$.
- Fiber: Kuraray PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm).
- Testing: uniaxial dogbone direct tensile test; air/water permeability measured after self-healing via underwater/wet-dry cycling.
