# Autonomous Robotic Lunar Base Construction via Contour Crafting 3D Printing and ATHLETE Hex-Legged Rover: ISRU Regolith Shielding Domes and Extraterrestrial Civil Infrastructure

## Summary

To overcome the exorbitant cost of transporting construction materials from Earth (tens of thousands of dollars per kilogram) and the extreme environmental challenges on the Moon (high vacuum, $-150\ ^\circ\text{C} \leftrightarrow +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$ temperature swings, cosmic radiation, and micrometeorite bombardment), researchers at USC (Prof. Behrokh Khoshnevis, Prof. Neil Leach) and NASA (Dr. Madhu Thangavelu) proposed an automated construction framework. The system mounts a **Contour Crafting 3D printing toolhead (combining layered extrusion and automated troweling)** onto **NASA's 6-legged All-Terrain Hex-Legged Extra-Terrestrial Explorer (ATHLETE) rover**. Utilizing **in-situ lunar regolith-based cementless binders (ISRU binders: sulfur concrete, microwave sintering, geopolymers)**, the system enables autonomous, unformed construction of **self-supporting parabolic vault domes, landing pads, and radiation shielding walls exceeding $1.5\ \text{m}$ in thickness**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First formulation of a 3D Contour Crafting robotic framework for extraterrestrial construction**:
   - Integrated computer-controlled trowels flanking extrusion nozzles to produce smooth surface finishes and dense monolithic walls without temporary falsework.
2. **Mechanisms for $100\%$ In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)**:
   - Reduced Earth payload requirements by over $95\%$ by coupling solar sintering, sulfur concrete, and regolith-based geopolymer binders with Contour Crafting.
3. **Integration with NASA ATHLETE hex-legged mobile rover**:
   - Utilized 6 independently actuated robotic limbs for self-leveling across uneven crater terrain, enabling continuous printing of large domes up to 20 m in diameter.
4. **Parametric design of radiation and micrometeorite shielding vaults**:
   - Developed double-curved catenary shells designed to minimize tensile stresses and channel dead loads along compression thrust lines.

## Methods

- **Contour Crafting (CC) principles and robotics (Sections 2–3, Figs. 1–6)**:
  - 3D printing process: Continuous extrusion along X-Y-Z axes paired with top and side troweling blades.
  - ATHLETE rover integration: NASA JPL 6-legged wheel-and-limb rover (payload capacity 450 kg, operational radius 10 m).
- **ISRU material systems and structural types (Sections 4–6, Figs. 7–12)**:
  - Binder options: Regolith geopolymer, sulfur concrete, and microwave-sintered ceramics.
  - Structural typologies: Pressurized habitat shielding vaults, rocket plume blast deflectors, roads, and landing pads.

### 2. In-situ regolith shielding and plume protection mechanics
- **$1.5\ \text{m}$ regolith radiation shielding**: Blocks $99.9\%$ of Galactic Cosmic Radiation (GCR) and Solar Particle Events (SPE), keeping annual astronaut radiation exposure below the terrestrial safety limit ($<20\ \text{mSv/year}$).
- **Landing pad plume mitigation**: Dense, sintered CC apron surfaces eliminate $100\%$ of high-velocity regolith dust scouring during rocket descents.

- **Overall conclusion**: This foundational paper by Contour Crafting inventor Behrokh Khoshnevis and Neil Leach demonstrated the feasibility of fully autonomous, in-situ regolith 3D printing for 1.5 m thick shielding domes on the Moon, serving as the benchmark reference for extraterrestrial construction robotics.
