# Evaluating the Time to Shrinkage-Crack Initiation in OPC and Slag Cement Incorporating CFBC Bottom Ash

## Summary

Paper by the user's group (first author B.Y. Lee of Chonnam National University; corresponding author H.-K. Kim of Chosun University). A study that incorporates CFBC bottom ash as an expansion agent into OPC and slag cement, and predicts the time to shrinkage-crack initiation by comparing direct tensile strength against shrinkage stress computed as shrinkage strain × elastic modulus. It showed that ash incorporation delays the time to crack initiation, and that this time is governed more strongly by drying conditions than by the ash dosage.

## Key Contributions

1. **Crack-initiation-time prediction framework**: quantifies the moment of cracking as the crossover point between direct tensile strength and shrinkage stress (ε_sh × E) — offering a practical design index.
2. **Shrinkage-compensating effect of CFBC ash**: ash incorporation delays the time to crack initiation — confirming its role as an expansion agent (a follow-up to lee-2019).
3. **Dominance of drying conditions**: the time to cracking depends more on the drying environment (humidity) than on the ash dosage — underlining the importance of curing management in field construction.

## Methods

- Materials: CFBC bottom ash incorporated as an expansion agent into OPC and slag cement respectively.
- Testing: direct tension (strength), autogenous and drying shrinkage (strain), elastic modulus → shrinkage stress computed.
- Analysis: time to crack initiation derived from the intersection of the strength curve and the shrinkage stress curve.

## Results

- Incorporating CFBC ash delays the time to crack initiation (shrinkage compensation).
- The time to cracking is dominated by drying conditions; the effect of ash dosage is comparatively small.
