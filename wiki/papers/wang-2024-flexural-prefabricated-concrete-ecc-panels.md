## Summary
A study clarifying the flexural behavior of composite panels with a high-ductility ECC layer applied on the tension side and high-strength concrete cast integrally on the compression side, in order to maximize the flexural capacity and deflection ductility of lightweight precast slab panels for modular construction and prefabricated bridge decks, through experiments and nonlinear section analysis. Flexural crack progression, post-yield deflection capability, and interfacial shear integrity were analyzed according to the ECC tension-layer thickness ratio ($h_{ecc}/h = 0.2 \sim 0.4$) and reinforcement arrangement. It was demonstrated that the optimally designed composite panel increased flexural toughness by a factor of 4.2 over conventional reinforced concrete panels and controlled service-load crack widths to at most $0.04 mm$.

## Key Contributions
- **Optimal section configuration of lightweight precast composite slab panels derived**: maximum flexural ductility ($\mu > 6.0$) with minimum self-weight at $h_{ecc}/h = 0.3$.
- **Shear delamination blocked at the source by wet integral casting at the interface**: interface slip remained at most $0.02 mm$ until flexural failure, maintaining fully composite behavior.
- **Crack control and long-term durability improved**: fine multiple cracking distributed across the entire tension layer prevents damage to waterproofing layers.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $f_c = 52 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $4.2 \%$) + high-strength concrete ($f_c = 60 MPa$).
- **Specimens**: $600 \times 100 \times 2000 mm$ precast panels (four-point bending, span 1800 mm).
- **Instrumentation**: LVDT midspan deflection, strain-gauge sectional strain distributions, optical microscope crack measurement.

## Results
- Maximum flexural load $78.5 kN$ (a 35 % improvement over the reference RC panel); midspan deflection at failure $45 mm$ (ductile deflection up to about $1/40$ of the span).
- After rebar yield, a gentle plastic plateau formed without load drop.
