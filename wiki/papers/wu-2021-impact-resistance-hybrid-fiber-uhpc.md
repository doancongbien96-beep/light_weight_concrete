## Summary
A study evaluating the impact failure behavior of hybrid fiber-reinforced UHPC (compressive strength $f_c > 140 MPa$) combining macro hooked-end steel fibers with micro straight steel fibers or high-strength polyethylene (PE) fibers through drop-weight impact tests, in order to maximize the drop-weight impact resistance and dynamic toughness of ultra-high performance concrete. Owing to multi-scale bridging synergy, in which micro fibers restrain early crack propagation under impact and macro steel fibers dissipate the bulk impact energy after crack opening, the total impact energy absorption was demonstrated to increase by more than a factor of 3.5 over single-fiber systems.

## Key Contributions
- **Multi-scale hybrid fiber impact bridging synergy mechanism clarified**: combining micro steel fiber (1.0 vol %) with hooked steel fiber (1.5 vol %) increased initial-crack impact count ($N_1$) by a factor of 4.2 and final failure blow count ($N_2$) by 6.8.
- **Damage progression index under repeated impact blows established**: dynamic damage accumulation curves quantified via rebound acceleration and impact force waveform measurement.
- **Fragment spalling suppression rate of 95 % achieved**: post-impact residual integrity maximized when high-strength PE fiber is mixed in.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: $W/B = 0.18$, silica fume 20 %, total fiber volume fraction $V_f = 2.5 \%$.
- **Fiber combinations**: (1) micro steel fiber only; (2) hooked steel fiber only; (3) micro (1.0 %) + hooked (1.5 %); (4) micro steel fiber (1.5 %) + PE fiber (1.0 %).
- **Impact testing**: repeated drop-weight impact per ACI 544.2R with a 20 kg hammer (drop height 500 mm).

## Results
- Optimal hybrid mix (micro 1.0 % + hooked 1.5 %): failure impact energy $18.4 kJ$, a 3.5-fold increase over single steel fiber ($5.2 kJ$).
- Post-impact residual compressive strength of $115 MPa$ maintained, demonstrating excellent protective performance.
