# Functional Foam Concrete Combining Exfoliated Vermiculite-Capric Acid PCM Carrier with Nanosilica and Natural Fibers: Thermal Insulation, Heat Storage, and Mechanical Behavior

## Summary

To simultaneously achieve ultra-lightweight thermal insulation and passive latent heat storage in building exterior walls and roof slabs, Professor Frédéric Kuznik and co-workers at INSA Lyon and Amrita University fabricated shape-stabilized phase change material (CA-V) carriers by vacuum-impregnating organic capric acid ($\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{20}\text{O}_2$, phase change temperature $31.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $152.8\ \text{J/g}$) into high-temperature ($1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$) exfoliated vermiculite ($<300\ \mu\text{m}$), incorporating them into micro-foam concrete (dry density $750\ \text{kg/m}^3$) reinforced with nano-silica (5 wt.%) and natural coconut fibers (2 wt.%). Through 28-day compressive strength testing, thermal conductivity measurements (Hot Disk), DSC latent heat analysis, drying shrinkage, and EnergyPlus building simulations, the optimized mix (PNC-5 %) achieved a 28-day compressive strength of **7.96 MPa** (exceeding the 6.5 MPa structural lightweight insulation requirement) and reduced thermal conductivity to **$0.15\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$**. Synergistic pozzolanic C-S-H densification by nano-silica and shrinkage crack bridging by coconut fibers eliminated PCM leakage (0 % leakage), resulting in a **21.8 % reduction in annual building energy consumption**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Vacuum impregnation and shape stabilization of capric acid PCM in exfoliated vermiculite**:
   - Adsorbed 75 wt.% capric acid into nano/micropores of layered vermiculite, synthesizing composite phase change material (CA-V) without liquid leakage even under heating at $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Pore filling and pozzolanic strength compensation via nano-silica**:
   - Incorporating nano-silica (50~100 nm, 5 wt.%) stimulated early reactions with $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, densifying the C-S-H gel and increasing compressive strength by **22 % to 7.96 MPa**.
3. **Synergistic shrinkage crack suppression via natural coconut fibers**:
   - Fiber bridging restrained drying shrinkage deformations, preventing matrix cracking and capsule damage.
4. **Ultra-low thermal conductivity ($0.15\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) and 21.8 % building energy savings**:
   - Coupling foam concrete porosity with capric acid latent heat storage ($152.8\ \text{J/g}$) reduced indoor temperature fluctuation amplitude by 4.2 °C and cut annual building cooling energy.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and PNC foam concrete mix proportions (Tables 1–4)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (OPC 53 grade) + M-sand (particle size $<300\ \mu\text{m}$, fineness modulus 4.65).
  - Lightweight carrier: Exfoliated vermiculite (density $0.12\ \text{g/cm}^3$, specific surface area $4.5\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, replacing fine aggregate at 5 %, 10 %, 15 %, and 20 %).
  - Organic PCM: High-purity (>98 %) capric acid (melting point $31.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $152.8\ \text{J/g}$, molecular weight $171.3\ \text{g/mol}$).
  - High-performance additives: Nano-silica (particle size 50~100 nm, 5.0 wt.% of cement mass) + coconut fibers (length 15 mm, diameter $0.25\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 185 MPa, 2.0 wt.% of cement mass).
  - Foaming agent: Protein-based foaming agent (target slurry density of $750\ \text{kg/m}^3$).
  - Mix groups: Control, V-5~20 (vermiculite only), PCM-5/10 (CA-V composite), PNC-5/10 (PCM + nano-silica + coconut fibers).
- **Testing apparatus and experimental methods**:
  - Compressive strength: $70.6\ \text{mm}$ cubes (IS 2185 Part 4, tested after 28 days of water curing).
  - Thermal conductivity: TPS 2500 S Hot Disk thermal constants analyzer (ISO 22007-2).
  - Thermal stability and phase-transition properties: DSC (TA Instruments Q20, $-10\ ^\circ\text{C} \sim 60\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and thermal leakage filter paper tests.
  - Building energy simulation: EnergyPlus / DesignBuilder (standard 2-story tropical building model).

### 2. Thermal comfort and building energy reduction analysis
- **Indoor thermal buffering**: Applying PNC-5 % panels to external walls maintained interior surface temperatures below $28.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ during peak outdoor ambient temperatures ($41.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$), reducing air conditioning hours.
- **Structural-insulation integration**: A compressive strength of 7.96 MPa provided sufficient structural safety for partition walls, roof insulation, and prefabricated panels.

- **Overall conclusion**: Incorporating capric acid PCM in exfoliated vermiculite with nano-silica and coconut fibers achieved 0.15 W/m·K thermal insulation and 21.8 % energy savings while maintaining structural strength.
