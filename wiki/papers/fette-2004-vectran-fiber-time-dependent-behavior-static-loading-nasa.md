# Time-Dependent Behavior and Static Creep Rupture Mechanics of High-Strength Liquid Crystal Polymer (Vectran HS) Fibers: 3.2 GPa Tensile Strength, 10,000-Hour Creep Life, and NASA Aerospace/Tendon Design Standards

## Summary

To evaluate the long-term load-bearing reliability of aromatic liquid crystal polyester (LCP) Vectran HS (High Strength) fibers used in Mars rover landing airbags (Mars Pathfinder/MER), inflatable space habitat modules, high-tension space tethers, and prestressing tendons, Russell B. Fette and Marjorie F. Sovinski at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC) characterized uniaxial tensile properties ($\sigma_f = 3.20\ \text{GPa}$, elastic modulus $E_f = 75.0\ \text{GPa}$) and conducted long-term creep rupture tests up to 10,000 hours under sustained tensile stress levels (30 %, 50 %, 70 %, and 80 % UTS). The authors developed time-stress superposition creep kinetic models demonstrating superior abrasion and creep rupture resistance over Kevlar and aramids, a guaranteed creep life exceeding 10 years under 50 % UTS sustained stress, high energy absorption with an elongation at break of 3.8 %, and property retention under vacuum and cryogenic environments.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of the first comprehensive long-term creep rupture database for Vectran LCP fibers**:
   - Recorded creep rupture lifetimes across sustained tensile stress levels up to 10,000 hours at ambient and vacuum conditions, developing Arrhenius-based lifetime prediction charts.
2. **Demonstration of ultra-high strength (3.2 GPa) and low creep strain (<0.8 %)**:
   - Under 50 % UTS sustained load, the secondary steady-state creep rate converged below $10^{-8}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ after 1 year, preserving dimensional stability.
3. **Superior flexural fatigue and abrasion endurance compared to Kevlar-29/49**:
   - Inter-fiber frictional wear damage during folding and deployment of inflatable structures was reduced to less than 50 % of that observed in Kevlar.
4. **Standard guidelines for space inflatable habitats and composite prestressing tendons**:
   - Established safe allowable stress limits ($\le 45\%\ \text{UTS}$) for structural cables subjected to sustained long-term tension.

## Methods

- **Materials and fiber properties (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Fiber specifications: Celanese/Kuraray Vectran HS LCP fibers (filament diameter $23\ \mu\text{m}$, density $1.47\ \text{g/cm}^3$, denier 1500d).
  - Static tension: Universal testing machine (gage length 250 mm, ASTM D2256, displacement rate 50 mm/min).
- **Long-term creep apparatus (Sections 2.3–3.5 & Figs. 5–10)**:
  - Dead-weight lever arm sustained loading frames (30 %, 50 %, 65 %, 75 %, and 85 % UTS).
  - Instrumentation: Continuous automated recording of creep strain and rupture time via LVDTs and optical extensometers.

### 2. Molecular chain alignment and creep slip mechanisms
- **Rigid-rod nematic liquid crystal structure**: Benzene ring polymer backbones aligned along the tensile axis under load, suppressing inter-domain sliding and maintaining low steady-state creep rates.
- **Prestressing tendon reliability**: Under sustained stresses below 50 % UTS, prestress tension loss due to creep remained below 3 % over a 10-year service life.

- **Overall conclusion**: This NASA technical report established the long-term creep rupture mechanics and time-dependent deformation kinetics of ultra-high-strength Vectran liquid crystal polymer fibers, providing standards for aerospace inflatable structures and civil prestressing cables.
