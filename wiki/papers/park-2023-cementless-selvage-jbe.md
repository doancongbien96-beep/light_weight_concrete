# Cementless Ultra-Ductile Composites Reinforced with Selvage Fibers

## Summary

User-group paper (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University). Develops cementless ultra-ductile composites in which both cement and fibers are completely replaced by industrial byproducts. AAS-S (slag-based AAS + selvage fiber) achieves 33.2 MPa compressive strength · 7.8 MPa tensile strength · 8.9 % strain capacity · 471 kJ/m³ toughness, while AAF-S (fly-ash-based) achieves 21.9 MPa compressive strength · 7.0 MPa tensile strength · **13.8 %** strain capacity · 688 kJ/m³ toughness — toughness per unit MSI is 1.3~12 times better than representative existing high-ductility composites.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of dual sustainability through cementless mix + recycled fiber**: alkali-activated binders (AAS · AAF) cut CO₂ emissions while selvage waste fiber reduces embodied energy and cost — applying both axes simultaneously maximizes toughness per unit MSI.
2. **AAF-S strain capacity of 13.8 %**: fly-ash-based AAF is far more ductile than slag-based AAS (compared with 8.9 %). It secures ultra-high ductility while satisfying the minimum compressive strength for structural concrete — proposed as a material for resilient infrastructure.
3. **Strength-ductility trade-off of selvage fibers**: compared with ordinary PE fibers (AAS-P · AAF-P), selvage fibers (AAS-S · AAF-S) give lower tensile strength but higher strain capacity. Mean crack widths are wider — AAS-S 101 µm · AAF-S 275 µm — than those of existing high-ductility composites, exceeding the self-healing threshold (100 µm) — a caution flagged for durability design.

## Methods

- **Four mixes**: AAS-S · AAS-P (slag precursor), AAF-S · AAF-P (fly ash precursor). -S denotes short selvage fiber reinforcement; -P ordinary PE fiber reinforcement.
- **Testing**: compressive strength; direct tension (dumbbell specimens) to derive tensile strength, strain capacity, and toughness. Crack pattern analysis (crack number, spacing, width). MSI model for quantitative comparison of embodied energy, CO₂, and cost.

## Results
