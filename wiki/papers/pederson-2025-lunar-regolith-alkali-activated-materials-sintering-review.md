# Comprehensive Review of Lunar Regolith-Based Alkali-Activated Materials (AAM) and High-Temperature Sintered Regolith as Space Civil Construction Technologies: N-A-S-H Gelation, $1200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Sintering Strength ($120\ \text{MPa}$), and the 2025 Landmark of Off-Earth 3D Printing

## Summary

To erect the permanent landing pads, habitat domes, and protective walls required for the Artemis program and the construction of the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS), this is the latest 2025 landmark review paper in Advances in Space Research from **Professor Hee-Jeong Kim's research group in the Department of Civil and Architectural Engineering at the University of Arizona (USA)**, which comprehensively consolidates the physicochemical crystalline-phase transformation kinetics of the two pillars of lunar in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) — 1) lunar alkali-activated materials (AAM: alkaline dissolution of basalt/anorthosite minerals and N-A-S-H gelation, compressive strength $35\sim 55\ \text{MPa}$) and 2) high-temperature microwave/solar/laser sintering and melt-bonded bodies (Sintered Regolith: $1050\sim 1250\ ^\circ\text{C}$, compressive strength $60\sim 120\ \text{MPa}$) — together with their suitability for additive manufacturing (AM) processes and their durability under hard vacuum ($10^{-10}\ \text{Torr}$) and extreme thermal cycling between $-150\ ^\circ\text{C}\leftrightarrow +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first establishment of comparative engineering guidelines for the two major off-earth construction technologies (AAM geopolymer vs high-temperature sintered body)**:
   - Systematizes that AAMs are advantageous for low-energy ($<80\ ^\circ\text{C}$) 3D-printed habitable structures, while sintered bodies are optimal for waterless rocket landing pads and high-strength shielding tiles.
2. **Elucidation of the alkaline dissolution reaction kinetics of plagioclase and pyroxene in lunar regolith**:
   - Demonstrates that N-A-S-H gels form successfully under increased specific surface area via nano-milling and optimized alkali-activation conditions, developing room-/elevated-temperature curing strength.
3. **Interpretation of the $120\ \text{MPa}$ ultra-high-strength mechanism of high-temperature microwave/direct-solar molten sintered bodies**:
   - Confirms that at $1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$) and pyroxene form partial liquid phases that fusion-fill the pores, converting the material into a dense ceramic-rock structure.
4. **Establishment of a standard specification for autonomous off-earth 3D-printing construction of future lunar bases**:
   - Completes vacuum-defect prevention guidelines for slipform extrusion deposition and powder-bed laser sintering processes.

## Methods

- **Analysis of the AAM and sintering processes (Sections 2~5 & Figs. 1~8)**:
  - AAM chemistry: lunar regolith simulants (JSC-1A, FJS-1, CAS-1) + $\text{NaOH}/\text{KOH} + \text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 \implies$ oligomer-polymerized N-A-S-H gel.
  - Sintering chemistry: electromagnetic microwave heating ($2.45\ \text{GHz}$), concentrated Fresnel-lens solar heating, and $\text{CO}_2$ laser sintering ($T = 1000\sim 1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Evaluation of mechanics and environmental durability (Sections 6~8 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 9~15)**:
  - Compressive strength, flexural strength, weight loss under prolonged vacuum exposure, thermal shock resistance, and radiation shielding fraction.

### 2. Microstructural stability analysis under thermal cycling and high vacuum
- **Thermal shock resistance of sintered bodies across cryogenic-to-ultra-high temperatures**: even after 100 cycles of abrupt temperature change between $-150\ ^\circ\text{C} \leftrightarrow +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the strength degradation of the sintered body remained below $5\%$.
- **Vacuum dehydration control of AAM**: surface nano-sealing coatings prevented rapid moisture evaporation in vacuum and crack formation at a rate of $100\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review paper is the latest top landmark source review of 2025 in the field of space civil engineering materials, in which Professor Hee-Jeong Kim's research group comprehensively consolidated the reaction mechanisms, mechanical performance, and off-earth 3D-printing technologies of lunar-regolith-based alkali-activated geopolymers and high-temperature sintered bodies, presenting the core materials-engineering standard for 21st-century lunar base construction.
