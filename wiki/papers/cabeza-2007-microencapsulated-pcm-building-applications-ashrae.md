## Summary
To maximize the thermal energy storage (TES) capacity of buildings and reduce peak heating and cooling loads, this ASHRAE paper documents full-scale experimental validation by Professor L.F. Cabeza's research team at the University of Lleida, Spain, of indoor temperature control performance and heat storage behavior of cast-in-place concrete exterior walls containing 5 wt % microencapsulated paraffin-based phase change material (Microencapsulated PCM: BASF Micronal, phase-change temperature **26 °C**, latent heat capacity **110 kJ/kg**) in a $2.0 \times 2.0 \times 3.0 m$ cubicle house. Under summer outdoor temperatures reaching $38 °C$, the PCM concrete room was shown to lower peak indoor temperature by **4.0 °C** versus the standard concrete room and to save **15 ~ 20 %** of daytime cooling energy consumption.

## Key Contributions
- **Full-scale ($2.0 \times 2.0 \times 3.0 m$) PCM concrete cubicle demonstration test system**: one-to-one real-time environmental comparison between a standard concrete room and a PCM 5 wt % concrete room.
- **Demonstrated 4.0 °C reduction in peak indoor temperature**: rapid cutoff of indoor overheating via latent heat absorption within the 26 °C phase-change interval.
- **Elucidation of the night-time natural ventilation heat release mechanism**: latent heat accumulated during the day is discharged to the night air ($20 \sim 22 °C$), completing a continuous heat storage cycle.
- **Standard guideline for building energy savings presented**: HVAC plant capacity downsized by 25 % along with reduced energy cost.

## Methods
- PCM properties: BASF Micronal DS 5001 X (paraffin wax core + polymethyl methacrylate shell, particle diameter 5 µm, melting point 26 °C, latent heat 110 kJ/kg).
- Concrete mix: OPC 350 kg/m³, sand 950 kg/m³, gravel 850 kg/m³, water 175 L, PCM 5 wt % (17.5 kg/m³ by cement weight).
- Test facility: two outdoor cubicles at Puigverd de Lleida, Spain (wall thickness 120 mm, south-facing glazing, uninsulated concrete walls), continuous logging on 40 K-type thermocouple channels.

## Results
- Summer and winter cubicle indoor/outdoor temperature measurements and thermal performance comparison data:

| Measurement | Standard concrete room | PCM concrete room (with 5 % PCM) | Performance improvement |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Summer peak indoor temperature ($°C$)** | 36.5 °C (outdoor 38.2 °C) | **32.5 °C** | **4.0 °C peak reduction** |
| **Summer diurnal temperature swing ($°C$)** | 12.5 °C | **7.5 °C** | **swing reduced by 40 %** |
| **Peak temperature delay** | baseline | **2.5 h delay** | load peak shift |
| **Cooling energy consumption ($kWh/day$)**| 14.8 | **11.9** | **19.6 % energy saving** |
| **28 d concrete compressive strength (MPa)** | 35.0 | **28.5** | structural strength secured |
