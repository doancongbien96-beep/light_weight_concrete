## Summary
This study evaluated the residual uniaxial direct tensile behavior, microstructural phase changes, and thermal degradation mechanisms of fly ash- and slag-based strain-hardening geopolymer composites (EGC) after exposure to high temperatures ($100 \sim 800 °C$). Unlike conventional OPC-based ECC, whose matrix collapses at high temperatures through dehydration reactions of $Ca(OH)_2$, the geopolymer matrix was confirmed to retain a stable zeolitic ceramic skeleton even at $800 °C$. It was also demonstrated that even after melting of the PVA fibers ($230 °C$), channels for venting high-temperature steam formed and completely prevented spalling, and that 60 % ($3.2 MPa$) of the room-temperature tensile strength was retained even after exposure to $400 °C$.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of the ultra-high-temperature refractory mechanism of the geopolymer matrix**: upon high-temperature heating, amorphous N-A-S-H gel sinters and transforms into a crystalline leucite (Leucite) phase, securing structural stability.
- **Derivation of residual tensile stress-strain responses by temperature range**: strain-hardening ($\epsilon_u > 3.0 \%$) was preserved up to $200 °C$, while above $400 °C$ the response transitioned to quasi-brittle softening yet zero spalling was achieved.
- **Demonstration of steam spalling resistance**: formation of porous channels that relieve vapor pressure preserved the original form perfectly even at ultra-high strength.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: Class F fly ash 70 % + GGBS 30 %, sodium hydroxide and sodium silicate alkaline solutions, PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15).
- **Heating test**: heating in an electric furnace to $100, 200, 400, 600, 800 °C$ at $5 °C/min$, followed by holding for 2 h.
- **Mechanical testing**: after cooling to room temperature, uniaxial dog-bone direct tensile tests and measurement of 28 d residual compressive strength.

## Results
- $200 °C$: residual tensile strength $4.85 MPa$ ($95 \%$ retention), tensile strain capacity $3.25 \%$.
- $400 °C$: residual tensile strength $3.20 MPa$, compressive strength $46.5 MPa$ ($82 \%$ retention), no spalling observed.
