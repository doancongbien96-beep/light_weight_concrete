# Effects of Sand and Silica Fume on the Vibration Damping Behavior of Cement

## Summary

A communication paper (4 pp) showing that adding sand to cement paste degrades the vibration damping capacity (loss tangent tanδ and storage modulus), yet adding surface-treated silica fume restores the damping capacity beyond the original paste level. Silica fume raises the loss modulus of mortar by up to three orders of magnitude (2 GPa at 0.5 Hz); the experiment extends D.D.L. Chung's (Composite Materials Research Laboratory, SUNY Buffalo) cement-based damping series (Fu & Chung 1996 CCR 26:69-75; the paste studies of Li & Chung 1998 CCR 28:493-498) to mortar. Note that despite the filename's "sand and silica fume", this is a paper about vibration damping behavior, not strength.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic report that sand addition degrades damping capacity: the mortar's tanδ falls below 10^-4 at all frequencies and its storage modulus drops to the 13.7 → 9.43 GPa (0.2 Hz) level. The cause lies in the intrinsically low damping capacity of sand particles themselves and their incomplete bonding with the cement paste.
- Recovery effect of surface-treated silica fume: the tanδ of sand+silica fume mortar rises to 0.14 (at 0.5 Hz), overtaking the paste (0.016), and the loss modulus increases by up to three orders of magnitude. The mechanism combines the energy-dissipation contribution of the vast interface created by silica fume's small particles with the strengthening capability afforded by strong bonding.
- Frequency dependence: the superiority of silica fume mortar grows with increasing frequency within the ≤1 Hz range, with the loss modulus peaking (2 GPa) at 0.5 Hz.
- Design implication: aggregate proportions chosen primarily around compressive strength may be unsuitable for vibration-reduction design — the paper provides a starting point for damping-oriented mix design.

## Methods

- Mix: Type I Portland cement (Lafarge), w/c 0.35, natural sand (#4 sieve pass, 99.9 % SiO2) at s/c 1.0, naphthalene-sulfonate water reducer TAMOL SN at 2 % of cement weight.
- Silica fume: EMS 965 (Elkem) at 15 % of cement weight, fully surface-treated — immersion in 96 % sulfuric acid for 2 h, then washing, filtration, and drying at 150 °C for 1~2 days.
- Curing: mixing in a Hobart mixer, compaction on an external vibrator, demolding at 24 h, then 28 d in a moist room (RH 100 %).
- Testing: ASTM D4065-94 dynamic mechanical analysis (Perkin-Elmer DMA 7E), three-point bending, specimens 68×11×3.5 mm, span 62 mm, frequencies 0.2/0.5/1/2 Hz, 20 °C, heating rate 2 °C/min, six specimens per mix.

## Results

- tanδ (±0.01): paste 0.016 (at 0.2 Hz; below 10^-4 at other frequencies) / mortar below 10^-4 at all frequencies / sand+silica fume 0.021 (0.2 Hz), 0.14 (0.5 Hz), 0.01 (1 Hz), below 10^-4 (2 Hz).
- Storage modulus (GPa, ±0.2): paste 13.7~14.48 / mortar degraded to 9.43~11.67 / sand+silica fume recovered to 13.11~14.34.
- Loss modulus (GPa, ±0.2): paste 0.22 (0.2 Hz), below 0.001 elsewhere / mortar below 0.001 throughout / sand+silica fume 0.28 (0.2 Hz), 2.00 (0.5 Hz), 0.13 (1 Hz).
- Conclusion: sand addition sharply degrades damping capacity, and adding silica fume restores it beyond the original state. Silica fume is effective with or without aggregate, but the improvement is dramatic on the aggregate-bearing side (without aggregate, tanδ is already high).
