## Summary
A top-tier classic review paper comprehensively surveying alkali-activated cements and concretes (Alkali-Activated Materials, AAM / Geopolymer) as low-carbon sustainable binders of the 21st century to replace Portland cement (OPC): their chemical reaction kinetics, reaction product crystalline phases (C-A-S-H and N-A-S-H gels), microstructure, and mechanical/durability properties. It classified hydration product formation pathways according to alkali activator type (liquid silicates, hydroxides, carbonates) and precursor chemical composition ($CaO-Al_2O_3-SiO_2$ ternary system phase diagram), and systematized both the unrivaled advantages of high-temperature fire resistance, acid resistance, heavy metal immobilization, and chloride attack resistance and the challenges of drying shrinkage and workability control.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of the two-family classification framework for AAM binder reaction products**:
  1. High-calcium system (slag): highly cross-linked $C-(A)-S-H$ gel structure.
  2. Low-calcium system (fly ash/metakaolin): aluminosilicate network polymer with a three-dimensional zeolite-like structure ($N-A-S-H$ gel).
- **Formulation of alkali activator dissolution/polymerization kinetics**: established the catalytic mechanism by which alkali metal ions ($Na^+, K^+$) and $OH^-$ concentration govern the cleavage of $Si-O-Si$ and $Al-O-Si$ covalent bonds and gel reprecipitation.
- **Identification of early-age shrinkage and rheological technical challenges**: proposed admixtures and optimal aggregate packing strategies for mitigating high autogenous shrinkage.

## Methods
- Comprehensive meta-analysis of some 300 global alkali-activated materials literature reports.
- Thermodynamic gel equilibrium modeling and mapping of the ternary phase equilibrium diagram ($CaO-SiO_2-Al_2O_3$).

## Results
- AAM binders: greenhouse gas emissions from the manufacturing process reduced by 70 ~ 80 % versus OPC.
- Demonstrated more than 3 times the residual strength of OPC and excellent structural preservation under ultra-high temperature ($1000 °C$) and strongly acidic ($5 \% H_2SO_4$) environments.
