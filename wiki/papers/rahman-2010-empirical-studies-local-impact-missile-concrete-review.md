# Holistic Comparative Review of Local Impact Damage (Penetration, Rear-Face Scabbing, Perforation) of Protective Concrete Structures under Hard Missile Impact and the Ten Global Empirical Formulae (Modified NDRC, ACE, BRL, UKAEA)

## Summary

To enable quantitative protective design of nuclear power plant containment barriers and underground military bunkers exposed to military missile warheads, aircraft engine impacts, tornado-generated debris, and explosive fragment impacts, this paper elucidates **the failure mechanisms of the five major local damage modes that concrete structures undergo under hard missile impact (1: frontal spalling, 2: penetration, 3: cone cracking and plugging, 4: rear-face scabbing, 5: complete perforation)**, and carries out a full comparative analysis of the input parameters (missile diameter $d$, mass $M$, impact velocity $V_0 = 25\sim 1000\ \text{m/s}$, nose shape factor $N$, concrete uniaxial compressive strength $f_c'$, reinforcement ratio $\rho$) and validity ranges of the **ten global engineering empirical formulae developed over the past 70 years in the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and elsewhere (Modified NDRC, ACE, BRL, UKAEA, Haldar-Miller, Chang, Hughes, CEA-EDF, Bechtel, Kar)**, codifying **a protective design standard that estimates the concrete scabbing limit thickness ($h_s$) and perforation limit thickness ($e$) to within $\pm 8\%$ error** — the definitive-authority, 25-page landmark classic review (2010) by the team of **Prof. Ismail Abdul Rahman of Universiti Tun Hussein Onn Malaysia (UTHM) and Prof. Ahmad Mujahid Zaidi of the National Defence University of Malaysia (UPNM)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Complete elucidation of the stage-by-stage mechanics of the five major local impact damage modes of concrete under hard missile impact (historical origin document)**:
   - Systematizes the physical process from frontal crater formation through shear-plug formation to rear-face concrete spall ejection (scabbing) caused by reflection of tensile stress waves.
2. **Precise comparative analysis of the ten global protective empirical formulae (NDRC, ACE, BRL, etc.) and derivation of an error matrix**:
   - The Modified NDRC formula shows the highest reliability in evaluating nose shape ($N$) and penetration depth ($x$), and proves the standard relation $h_s/x = 2.12 + 0.26(x/d)$ for estimating the scabbing-prevention thickness ($h_s$).
3. **Quantification of the effects of the missile nose shape factor ($N$) and impact velocity ($V_0$)**:
   - Demonstrates that sharp ogive noses ($N = 1.08$) increase penetration depth by up to a factor of $1.5$ compared with flat noses ($N = 0.72$).
4. **Establishment of design-standard guidance for nuclear plant domes and military protective fortifications worldwide**:
   - Completes the technical foundation for safety factors in extreme protective-thickness design and for adopting high-toughness composites (UHPC/ECC).

## Methods

- **Local impact mechanics and classification of the five damage modes (Sections 1~2 & Figs. 1~6)**:
  - Frontal spalling, penetration depth ($x$), shear-cone plugging, rear-face scabbing, perforation.
- **Comparative analysis framework of the ten empirical formulae (Sections 3~5 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 7~15)**:
  - 1) Modified NDRC (National Defence Research Committee)
  - 2) ACE (Army Corps of Engineers, USA)
  - 3) BRL (Ballistic Research Laboratory, USA)
  - 4) UKAEA (United Kingdom Atomic Energy Authority)
  - 5) CEA-EDF (Commissariat a l'Energie Atomique, France)
  - 6) Haldar & Miller (introduces the impact coefficient $I$)
  - 7) Chang formula, 8) Hughes formula, 9) Bechtel formula, 10) Kar formula.

### 2. Interpretation of concrete strength ($f_c'$) and fiber reinforcement effects

- **Reduction of penetration depth with increasing compressive strength**: as $f_c'$ rises from $30\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 150\ \text{MPa}$, penetration depth plunges by more than $45\%$, yet the brittle cone-fracture area (spalling/scabbing) may conversely expand, making fiber reinforcement (UHPC/ECC) essential.
- **Rear-face scabbing suppression mechanism**: tensile bridging by steel fibers and highly ductile fibers suppresses debris scattering driven by the rear-face tensile stress wave by more than $90\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review comprehensively compares and analyzes the five major local damage modes of concrete structures under hard missile impact together with the ten global empirical formulae, codifying the highest-reliability basis for scabbing- and perforation-thickness design — a 25-page, top-tier immortal landmark review in the field of military protection and nuclear-safety structural engineering by Prof. Ismail Abdul Rahman's UTHM research team.
