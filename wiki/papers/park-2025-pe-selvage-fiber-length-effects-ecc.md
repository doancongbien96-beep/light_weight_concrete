# Length Effects of Recycled PE Selvage Fibers ($6\sim 24\ \text{mm}$) on ECC Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 7.50\%$) and the Mechanism of a $75\%$ Material-Cost Reduction

## Summary

To drastically lower the high virgin synthetic-fiber price ($55\ \text{\$/kg}$) and embodied carbon emissions of high-performance strain-hardening cementitious composites (ECC), **recycled PE-based selvage fibers — waste from the fabric-weaving industry — were introduced for the first time**, and fresh slump flow, cross-sectional fluorescence-microscopy-based 2D fiber dispersion coefficient ($\alpha$), 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile stress-strain curves, and crack patterns were precisely evaluated across **four fiber lengths ($L_f = 6, 12, 18, 24\ \text{mm}$ at a volume fraction $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, with **the $18\ \text{mm}$ selvage-fiber mix (`P18`) achieving a 28-day compressive strength of $52.5\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate uniaxial tensile strength of $6.85\ \text{MPa}$, and ultra-high ductility with an ultimate tensile strain of $7.50\%$, while cutting fiber material cost by $75\%$ relative to virgin PE fibers** — the latest landmark origin paper (2025, Journal of Sustainable Cement-Based Materials) by the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author, Chonnam National University), Dr. Se-eon Park, and Prof. Yun Yong Kim (Chungnam National University).

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first upcycling of industrial-waste PE selvage fibers as ECC reinforcement**:
   - Established an eco-friendly, low-cost fiber technology in which discarded ultra-high-molecular-weight PE woven-selvage edge fibers are cut to uniform length and deliver mechanical performance on par with costly commercial virgin PE fibers.
2. **Optimization of interfacial bridging and tensile ductility across fiber lengths ($6\sim 24\ \text{mm}$)**:
   - Derived the optimal fiber length of $18\ \text{mm}$ that overcomes both the premature pullout loss of short fibers ($6\ \text{mm}$) and the mixing-entanglement problem of very long fibers ($24\ \text{mm}$), achieving a tensile strain of $7.50\%$.
3. **Direct measurement of the fiber dispersion coefficient ($\alpha$) via fluorescence cross-sectional image analysis**:
   - Micromechanically elucidated the trade-off between dispersion degradation as fiber length increases ($\alpha = 0.85 \rightarrow 0.65$) and the increase in bridging length.
4. **$75\%$ reduction in ECC material cost and a drastic cut in carbon footprint**:
   - Cut the fiber cost, which accounts for $87\%$ of standard ECC mix cost, by $75\%$, fully securing the economics for large-scale commercialization of high-ductility composites.

## Methods

- **Materials and selvage ECC mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binders: Type I OPC + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, 40 wt.%) + expansive agent (5 wt.%).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40, W/B = 0.28).
  - Reinforcing fiber: recycled PE selvage fiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 32\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 2600\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 85\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Four fiber-length levels:
    1. `P6`: $L_f = 6\ \text{mm}$.
    2. `P12`: $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$.
    3. `P18`: $L_f = 18\ \text{mm}$.
    4. `P24`: $L_f = 24\ \text{mm}$.
- **Testing and analysis procedures (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: JSCE dumbbell-type specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, displacement control at 0.2 mm/min).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (28-day age).
  - Fiber dispersion: fluorescent staining of cut cross-sections $\rightarrow$ grid analysis under optical microscopy $\rightarrow \alpha$ derivation.

### 2. Bridging-length vs dispersion trade-off and micromechanics interpretation
- **Effect of the critical $18\ \text{mm}$ bridging length**: the frictional resistance distance during fiber pullout ($L_f/2 = 9\ \text{mm}$) is secured amply, maximizing the complementary energy $J_b'$ absorbed by individual fibers across the crack plane.
- **Saturation of multiple-cracking density**: compared with $24\ \text{mm}$ fibers, the $18\ \text{mm}$ fibers clump less and induce uniform stress transfer, forming 38 saturated multiple fine cracks within the gauge length.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the latest landmark origin paper in the field of sustainable high-ductility construction composites, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University optimized the length of waste-derived PE selvage fibers to achieve a compressive strength of 52.5 MPa and ultra-high ductility of 7.50% while cutting material cost by 75%.
