# Ultra-Low Permeability ($k \approx 10^{-10}\ \text{m/s}$) of Crack-Controlled PVA-ECC Under $1.5\%$ Tensile Strain and Its Self-Healing Watertightness Recovery Mechanism

## Summary

To prevent premature deterioration from moisture and aggressive ion ingress through cracks in concrete structures and to extend service life dramatically, this paper investigates **PVA-ECC (2.0 vol.% PVA fiber) forming dense multiple fine cracks with average crack width at or below $60\ \mu\text{m}$ under a high uniaxial tensile strain of $1.5\%$** against **ordinary concrete and steel fiber-reinforced concrete (FRC) developing single large cracks ($w = 150\sim 500\ \mu\text{m}$)** through **constant head permeability tests and long-term spontaneous self-healing permeability tracking tests**, elucidating the mechanism by which **even at the ultimate tensile strain of $1.5\%$ ECC retains an ultra-low hydraulic conductivity of $k \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-10}\ \text{m/s}$ — more than 10000 times lower than cracked ordinary concrete ($k \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m/s}$) — and recovers watertightness 100 % to the uncracked level ($10^{-11}\ \text{m/s}$) after 30 days of immersion-driven self-healing**. This is the landmark origin paper by Prof. Victor C. Li (University of Michigan) and Prof. Michael D. Lepech (Stanford University), Cement and Concrete Composites, 2009.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of a four-order-of-magnitude permeability difference between ECC and ordinary concrete under tensile loading**:
   - At identical $1.5\%$ tensile strain, ordinary concrete sees its conductivity explode to $10^{-6}\ \text{m/s}$ through a through-crack ($w > 300\ \mu\text{m}$), while ECC holds it strictly below $10^{-10}\ \text{m/s}$ via microcracks of $60\ \mu\text{m}$ or less.
2. **Verification of the cubic law ($Q \propto w^3$) and the hydraulic superiority of ECC microcrack dispersion**:
   - Distributing flow over 50 microcracks of $w = 10\ \mu\text{m}$ instead of one large crack of $w = 500\ \mu\text{m}$ cuts total flux to 1/2500 — experimentally confirmed without exception.
3. **Demonstration of 100 % self-healing watertightness recovery of PVA-ECC microcracks**:
   - Continued hydration of unreacted fly ash/cement plus calcium carbonate crystallization on the walls of cracks at or below $60\ \mu\text{m}$ restores conductivity fully to the sound-specimen level within 30 days.
4. **Presentation of an infrastructure life-extension numerical model (linked with Life-365)**:
   - Based on chloride diffusion and moisture ingress coefficients, maintenance cycles for marine bridge decks and road pavements can be extended more than 2-fold.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - `ECC mix (M45)`: Type I OPC + low-calcium fly ash (FA/C = 1.2) + silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36, W/B = 0.26) + Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, 1.2 % oil coating).
  - `Comparison mixes`: ordinary concrete (OPC) and steel fiber-reinforced concrete (FRC, $V_f = 1.0\%$).
- **Tensile pre-cracking and permeability testing (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Tensile pre-cracking: uniaxial strains $\varepsilon = 0.5\%, 1.0\%, 1.5\%$ applied to $100 \times 100 \times 10\ \text{mm}$ plate specimens.
  - Permeameter: constant-head cell at $0.2\ \text{bar}$ ($2.0\ \text{m}$ water head).
  - Instrumentation: continuous automatic logging of flow history → hydraulic conductivity $k$ computed by Darcy's Law.
  - Self-healing condition: retested after 30 days submerged at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

### 2. Interpretation of crack-gap flow mechanics and self-healing crystal precipitation
- **Surface roughness and capillary flow damping**: inside microcrack walls of $60\ \mu\text{m}$ or less, wall friction resistance (roughness effect) and capillary tension dominate, decelerating flow sharply within the laminar regime.
- **Spontaneous healing by abundant unreacted binder**: residual pozzolanic particles in the high-volume fly ash matrix (FA/C = 1.2) react with permeating water, filling the crack gap with dense C-S-H and calcite crystals to restore watertightness 100 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is the highest-authority landmark origin paper establishing the paradigm of permanent-durability structural design, proving that even under 1.5 % extreme tensile strain ECC lowers conductivity by 10000 times through mean fine crack control at 60 µm and achieves complete self-healing watertightness within 30 days.
