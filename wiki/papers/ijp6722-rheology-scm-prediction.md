# Prediction of Rheological Properties of Fresh Cementitious Suspensions Based on Microstructural Parameters

## Summary

This paper investigates the rheology of non-blended fresh suspensions of Portland cement (PC), fly ash (FA), blast-furnace slag (BS), and silica fume (SF) across solid volume fractions $\phi = 0.28\sim 0.44$ (Chonnam National University, Bang Yeon Lee co-author; Keimyung University, Su-Tae Kang corresponding author). Yield stress was predicted using the Yield Stress Model (YODEL) and plastic viscosity using the Krieger-Dougherty (K-D) equation without fitting parameters. Model predictions matched experimental results with correlation coefficients $r \ge 0.96$ for YODEL and $r \ge 0.94$ for K-D.

## Key Contributions

1. **Microstructural interpretation of SCM rheology**: FA improved fluidity via spherical particle morphology, BS enhanced flow via smooth surface textures and low reactivity, while ultrafine SF particles reduced interparticle spacing and increased direct contact points, increasing yield stress and viscosity.
2. **First-principles yield stress prediction with YODEL**: Derived yield stress directly from particle size distribution (PSD), interparticle forces, and percolation threshold parameters ($r \approx 0.98$ for PC, 0.99 for FA, 0.96 for BS, 0.96 for SF).
3. **Plastic viscosity prediction via K-D equation**: Utilized maximum packing fraction $\phi_m$ and intrinsic viscosity as key inputs ($r \approx 0.99$ for PC, 0.98 for FA, 0.98 for BS).
4. **Mix design implications**: Measuring the $\phi$-dependent rheology of individual SCMs enables predictive modeling of multi-component blended suspensions, providing a scientific basis for superplasticizer dosage optimization.

## Methods

- **Materials**: General purpose cement, FA, BS, densified/undensified SF; non-blended single SCM suspensions prepared.
- **Variables**: Solid volume fraction $\phi = 0.28\sim 0.44$.
- **Testing**: Rotational rheometer measuring shear stress vs shear rate, fitted to the Bingham model for yield stress and plastic viscosity.
- **Prediction models**: YODEL for yield stress and Krieger-Dougherty for plastic viscosity using PSD and $\phi_m$ without fitting parameters.

## Results

| Suspension | YODEL $r$ (Yield Stress) | K-D $r$ (Plastic Viscosity) |
|---|---|---|
| PC | 0.98 | 0.99 |
| FA | 0.99 | 0.98 |
| BS | 0.96 | 0.98 |
| SF | 0.96 | 0.94+ |

- Increasing $\phi$ elevated both yield stress and plastic viscosity, with superior fluidity at lower volume fractions.
