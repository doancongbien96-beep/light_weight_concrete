# Sulfuric Acid Resistance of EGC

## Summary

A paper quantitatively demonstrating the sulfuric acid resistance of fly ash based geopolymer EGC (Engineered Geopolymer Composite, PVA fibers 1.5 %) against cement concrete (RC) and ECC M45 (PVA 2 %) controls (Ohno at U. Tokyo + Li at Michigan, NSF CMMI 1068005). In the Kaempfer-Berndt accelerated test (one cycle = 6 days immersion in pH 2 sulfuric acid + 1 day water immersion; 15 cycles = 105 days ≈ 20 years of sewer pipe corrosion), the weight loss rate of EGC was about 1/3 that of concrete·ECC, and compressive strength, MOR, and deflection capacity all showed no significant deterioration. A representative paper of the chemical-resistance axis showing the combined benefit of an acid-resistant geopolymer matrix + self-controlled fine cracking for wastewater infrastructure, where biogenic sulfuric acid attack from domestic sewage is the dominant deterioration mechanism.

## Key Contributions

- Weight loss rate 3× slower: initially (up to about 35 days) weight rises somewhat due to continued hydration · gypsum formation · absorbed-water gain, but thereafter the loss rate of concrete·ECC is about 3× that of EGC. After 15 cycles (105 days), the weight loss of concrete·ECC exceeds 2× that of EGC.
- Proof of interfacial durability difference: PVA fibers on the ECC surface easily detached with only brush washing after exposure, while EGC lost only a small amount — the geopolymer–PVA interface is more durable in sulfuric acid than the cement matrix–PVA interface.
- Compressive strength: after water-immersion control correction, EGC·ECC showed no significant deterioration (weight loss did not translate into strength deterioration); only concrete deteriorated continuously. A substantial share of ECC's weight loss was surface fiber detachment, so cement matrix loss was limited, and the high fly ash content (Torii-Kawamura effect) also contributed to acid resistance.
- Flexural performance (Table 4): RC MOR 9.2±1.4 → 8.3 MPa (f1/f0=0.90); ECC 15.0±0.4 → 13.8 MPa (0.92 — the 8 % decrease is statistically significant against specimen scatter, since surface fiber detachment directly reduces the number of bridging fibers); EGC 10.8±1.0 → 11.6 MPa (1.08, not significant). Deflection capacity: ECC 1.23±0.49 → 2.30 mm (+87 %, a significant increase); EGC 5.39±1.09 → 5.26 mm (0.98, not significant). The 28-day deflection capacity itself is 5.39 mm for EGC, more than 4× the 1.25 mm of RC and 1.23 mm of ECC.
- Flexural stiffness retention ratio (linear regression over the 0.5~4 MPa range, r²>0.9): RC 5 %·ECC 43 %·EGC 33 % — all three materials decreased significantly. However, the cracks of RC·ECC healed completely during exposure even in the acidic environment (cementitious self-healing operating), and ECC benefited from stiffness recovery thanks to it, whereas RC showed no stiffness recovery despite healing. EGC retained a relative stiffness retention ratio higher than RC despite leaving only surface fine cracks without healing, suggesting limited fiber–matrix interface damage.
- Interpretation of the paradox of ECC's deflection capacity +87 %: acid erosion lowers the matrix tensile strength (first-cracking strength), so multiple cracking occurs more extensively under load — matrix deterioration can actually favor deflection-hardening and ductility (an observation connecting to the PSH strength criterion).

## Methods

- Mix proportions (kg/m³): EGC — fly ash A 463+B 694 (from different power plants, CaO 15.66 %/5.08 % and Fe2O3 9.20 %/19.48 % respectively), fine silica sand 463, NaOH pellets 60.1, sodium silicate solution (Na2O 8.9 wt %+SiO2 28.7 wt %) 260, premix water 26.6+additional water 116 for workability adjustment, PVA 19.5 (1.5 vol %). ECC M45 — cement 581+fly ash 697, fine silica sand 464, PVA 26 (2 vol %), water 337, superplasticizer 4.4. RC — cement 292+fly ash 103, coarse aggregate (max 25 mm) 873, fine aggregate 956, water 174, reinforced equivalent to ASTM C76 Class IV Wall C pipe (W1.4 welded wire mesh).
- PVA fiber: strength 1620 MPa, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm, elastic modulus 42.8 GPa, elongation 6.0 %, oil-agent coating 1.2 %.
- Curing: EGC demolded then 60 °C dry curing 24 h → air-dried to 28 days; ECC moist-cured 6 days → air-dried; RC moist-cured 28 days.
- Sulfuric acid exposure: Kaempfer-Berndt method. At 28 days of age, after 2 days of water immersion, weight measured (precision 0.01 g) → 6 days immersion in pH 2 sulfuric acid (daily pH check · replenishment) → brush washing under running water → 24 h water immersion = 1 cycle; 15 cycles (105 days). The acid bath was replaced every cycle.
- Testing: compression — nine Ø76×152 mm cylinders, 3 crushed at every 5 cycles (loading 0.25±0.05 MPa/s); for concrete·ECC, simultaneous-duration water-immersion controls isolated the effect of continuing hydration (EGC controls omitted since preliminary tests showed no strength gain after 28 days). Flexure — 102×102×356 mm beams, 4-point bending over a 305 mm span (0.076 mm/min), deflection by two jig-mounted LVDTs. Three preload beams were stopped at a net deflection of 0.51 mm (1/600 of span) before exposure and loading.

## Results

- Weight: the loss rate of concrete·ECC was about 3× that of EGC; after 105 days the total loss exceeded 2×. ECC surface PVA detachment is included in ECC's weight loss.
- Compression: no significant deterioration for EGC·ECC; continuous deterioration for concrete.
- Flexure (Table 4): the Key Contributions figures above. For RC·EGC both MOR and deflection-capacity changes were non-significant by t-test; for ECC both the MOR decrease and deflection-capacity increase were significant (one RC beam lost data to a power outage during loading, so averages are over two beams).
- Stiffness retention ratio: RC 5 %·ECC 43 %·EGC 33 %.
- Self-healing: RC·ECC cracks fully sealed (operating even in the acidic environment); only ECC showed measured stiffness recovery. No sealing observed for EGC.
- Limitation: the accelerated test cannot reproduce field microorganisms (biogenic sulfuric acid generation), so final assessment of sewer-environment durability requires further study.
- Strain capacity context: the tensile ductility of this EGC mix is 4 % or more (introduction section), standard-grade for EGC. Contrasted with the strain-record lineage ([[nguyen-2021-ud-egc-13-7-percent]] 13.7 % ↔ [[nguyen-2023-hsud-egc-pe-pva-healing]] 10.51 % ↔ [[luong-2023-ed-aasc-22-percent]] 22 % ↔ [[lao-2022-seawater-sea-sand-egc]] about 8 %), this paper's position is a practical mix for durability demonstration rather than ultra-high ductility.
