## Summary

A paper that achieved a uniaxial direct tensile strain capacity of 22.3 % (R5-S8-M1 mix) in an alkali-activated slag composite (AASC) using no cement at all (Chonnam National University, Prof. Bang Yeon Lee corresponding author; Ceramics International, vol. 49). The strategy combines ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) with powdered calcium hydroxide as the binder, and incorporates silica sand (80 % of binder mass) and waste-tire crumb rubber (CR) particles with an average particle size of 400 µm to lower the matrix first-crack strength and promote multiple cracking. A new mixing procedure (M1: increased mixing time and speed; M2: dry pre-mixing of aggregates and fiber) improved fiber dispersion and homogeneity, securing more than twice the strain capacity of standard mixing (M0, 9.81 %) in the identical mix. The tensile strain capacity exceeds the minimum elongation requirements for reinforcing bars in ASTM and ISO, while being accompanied by a compressive strength of 53.6 MPa.

## Key Contributions

- **Achievement of a strain capacity of 22.34 ± 0.48 %**: the highest level among cement-based and cement-free strain-hardening composites reported in the literature. In the identical mix, M0 gave 9.81 %, M1 22.34 %, and M2 20.89 %, proving that the mixing procedure dominates performance.
- **Crumb rubber defect-introduction design**: CR particles (400 µm, density 1.13 g/cm³) act as intentional flaws that lower the first-crack strength (f_cr 4.12~5.08 MPa) and induce saturated multiple cracking. The f_ts/f_c ratio of the M2 mix was highest at 15.68 %.
- **80 % high-volume silica sand filling**: realizes an inexpensive mix together with volume stability and stiffness. Saturated multiple cracking with crack spacing of 0.95~1.27 mm.
- **Proposal of a performance-index comparison frame**: fair comparison with existing highly ductile composites using the two indices f_c·ε_ts and f_ts·ε_ts, and direct contrast of strain capacity against minimum bar elongation requirements (ASTM A615/A706, ISO 6934-2).

## Methods

- Binder: GGBS (Blaine fineness 4030 cm²/g, specific gravity 2.91) 0.90 + Ca(OH)₂ 0.10 (mass ratio).
- Fillers: CR 0.05, silica sand 0.80. Water 0.18 (0.26 when counting SP solids), SP 0.106, defoamer 0.001.
- Fiber: PE fiber (V_f = 1.75 vol.%, diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, density 0.97 g/cm³, elastic modulus 88 GPa).
- Mix variables are three types of mixing procedure (R5-S8-M0/M1/M2): M0 follows the preceding study (Lương et al. 2021) procedure, M1 increases mixing time and speed, and M2 dry-premixes the solids and fiber before adding the activating solution.
- Tests: density (50 mm cubes), compressive strength, and JSCE-recommended uniaxial direct tension (crack count by magnifier over the gauge length; crack width computed by dividing ultimate elongation by the crack count).

## Results

| Mix | First-crack strength f_cr (MPa) | Tensile strength f_ts (MPa) | Strain capacity ε_ts (%) | Toughness E_t (MPa m/m) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| R5-S8-M0 | 5.08 ± 0.74 | 7.92 ± 1.09 | 9.81 ± 2.92 | 0.64 |
| R5-S8-M1 | 4.70 ± 0.38 | 7.63 ± 0.23 | **22.34 ± 0.48** | 1.38 |
| R5-S8-M2 | 4.12 ± 0.21 | 8.19 ± 0.39 | 20.89 ± 2.13 | 1.29 |

- Compressive strength: 53.6 MPa for the M1 mix (stated in the abstract); the f_ts/f_c ratio of 14.24~15.68 % exceeds ordinary concrete (about 10 %).
- Crack characteristics (Table 6): crack counts M0 66.5 ± 17.3, M1 86.4 ± 13.5, M2 81.8 ± 5.3. Crack spacing 0.95~1.27 mm, average crack width 117.2~212.0 µm (M1 212.0 ± 33.2 µm).
- Interpretation: crack counts of M1 and M2 increased by 29.9 % and 23.0 % versus M0, respectively. Even with larger crack widths (on the order of 200 µm), the strain capacity reached the record high, showing that in ductility-oriented design a crack-width trade-off arises, separate from the self-healing critical crack-width discussion ([[crack-width-control]], [[self-healing-ecc]]).
