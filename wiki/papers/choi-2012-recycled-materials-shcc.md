## Summary

A Journal of the Korea Concrete Institute paper by the research team of Prof. Yun Yong Kim at Chungnam National University that established the mix design, compressive strength, uniaxial direct-tension strain-hardening envelope, and fine-cracking characteristics of an eco-friendly circular-economy high-ductility composite (Recycled SHCC) in which commercial PVA fiber and natural silica sand were replaced with recycled PET fiber recovered from waste beverage bottles ($L_f = 12 mm, d_f = 45 \\mu m, f_t = 650 MPa$) and recycled fine aggregate (RFA). Combining PET fiber (1.5 vol %), 100 % recycled fine aggregate replacement, and fly ash ($FA/C = 0.2 \\sim 0.4$) in an optimal proportion achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **23.5 ~ 26.0 MPa**, a direct-tensile strength of **3.45 MPa**, a uniaxial tensile strain capacity of **1.85 %**, and a **45 %** reduction in material cost.

## Key Contributions

- **Development of Recycled SHCC with waste-PET fiber and 100 % recycled fine aggregate replacement**: replacing high-cost PVA fiber and maximizing resource circularity.
- **Interfacial frictional stress control through fly ash addition**: fly ash substitution mitigated matrix fracture toughness to induce multiple-crack bridging capacity in the PET fiber.
- **Demonstrated preservation of compressive strength (23.5 ~ 26.0 MPa)**: stable structural performance maintained even with recycled fine aggregate, without degradation of the parent-matrix compressive strength.
- **Formation of multiple fine cracks (15 or more)**: verification of the surface-embossing frictional interlocking effect of the recycled fiber.

## Methods

- Binders: OPC + Class F fly ash (replacement ratios 0, 20, 40 wt %), $W/B = 0.45$.
- Aggregate: recycled fine aggregate (specific gravity 2.45, absorption 4.8 %, $d_{max} = 0.6 mm$) at 100 % replacement.
- Fiber combinations: PVA-only (2.0 %), PET-only (1.5, 2.0 %), PVA+PET hybrid.
- Tests: dogbone direct-tension test ($0.2 mm/min$, LVDT displacement measurement), 28 d compressive strength (50 mm cubes).
