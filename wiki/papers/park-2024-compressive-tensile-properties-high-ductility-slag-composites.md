# Interaction Mechanism of Compressive Strength ($0.25\sim 0.35$) and Tensile Ductility ($56.5\ \text{MPa}$) in Slag-Based High-Ductility Composites as Governed by Water-to-Binder Ratio (W/B $\varepsilon_u = 6.25\%$) and Fiber Type (PVA vs. PE)

## Summary

A landmark 2024 origin paper published in the Journal of the Korea Institute of Resources Recycling by the team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), Dr. S.-E. Park, and Prof. J.-I. Choi at Chonnam National University. To substantially reduce cement usage while optimizing the mechanical balance and constructability of slag-based composites, the study systematically combined **an eco-friendly matrix using ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) as the main binder with combined replacement by waste tire rubber powder (CR 5 wt.%)** across three water-to-binder ratios (W/B = 0.25, 0.30, 0.35) and two synthetic fibers (hydrophilic PVA vs. hydrophobic PE, $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$), and precisely evaluated 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile stress-strain curves, the number of multiple cracks within the gauge section, and average crack width, demonstrating the optimal high-ductility strain-hardening performance of the **W/B = 0.30 PE-fiber mix: 28-day compressive strength $56.5\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial ultimate tensile strength $7.15\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strain $6.25\%$, and average crack width $46\ \mu\text{m}$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Derivation of the W/B ratio (0.30) optimized for the slag-rubber matrix and of the fiber interactions**:
   - Establishes the optimum W/B = 0.30 water-ratio condition that overcomes the fiber agglomeration arising under the highly viscous environment at W/B = 0.25 and the excessive matrix strength loss at W/B = 0.35.
2. **Contrast of the uniaxial tensile behavior of hydrophilic PVA vs. hydrophobic PE fibers**:
   - PVA-fiber composites exhibit high first-cracking strength ($\sigma_{fc} = 5.10\ \text{MPa}$) yet their elongation stalls at $3.25\%$ owing to premature fiber rupture, whereas PE-fiber composites achieve the ultra-high ductility of $6.25\%$ through stable frictional slip.
3. **Demonstration of fiber interfacial friction optimization enabled by GGBS replacement**:
   - The dense C-A-S-H gel formed by slag hydration imparts an appropriate frictional shear stress ($\tau_0 \approx 2.0\ \text{MPa}$) to PE fiber surfaces, maximizing pseudo strain-hardening (PSH).
4. **Realization of ultra-fine crack restraint with an average crack width of $46\ \mu\text{m}$**:
   - Even under ultimate tensile strains exceeding $6\%$, 32 densely distributed microcracks form, holding crack width strictly below $50\ \mu\text{m}$.

## Methods

- **Materials and composite mix proportions (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binders: ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS 50 wt.%) + Type I OPC (50 wt.%) + expansive agent (EA 5 wt.%).
  - Admixture: crumb rubber powder (CR, particle size $0.3\sim 0.6\ \text{mm}$, 5 wt.% of binder).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.50).
  - Three W/B levels: 0.25, 0.30, 0.35.
  - Two fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$):
    1. `PVA fiber`: Kuraray REC15 ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$).
    2. `PE fiber`: Spectra PE ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 2700\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Test program (Section 2.2 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: JSCE dumbbell specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, displacement control at 0.2 mm/min).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (28-day age).
  - Crack analysis: optical-microscopy-based measurement of crack counts and average crack width.

### 2. Interpretation of the hydrophilic/hydrophobic interfacial chemistry and micromechanical failure mechanisms
- **Limit of chemical over-bonding of the PVA fiber**: the $-\text{OH}$ groups of PVA fibers bond excessively with slag hydration products, so fibers rupture during pull-out and elongation is limited.
- **Frictional pull-out and multiple-crack amplification of the PE fiber**: hydrophobic PE fibers pull out continuously without interfacial damage at the proper W/B (0.30), redistributing the crack-plane load fully to the adjacent matrix and delivering the enormous strain capacity of $6.25\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark origin work on eco-friendly high-ductility construction materials in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's team at Chonnam National University systematically established the interaction between W/B ratio and fiber type in slag-based high-ductility composites and demonstrated, through the PE fiber and W/B = 0.30 combination, 56.5 MPa compressive strength, 6.25 % tensile strain capacity, and 46 µm crack-control performance.
