# Flexural Behavior and Analytical Modeling of Lightweight Self-Consolidating Concrete (LWSCC) Beams Strengthened with Ultra-Lightweight ECC (ULW-ECC) and FRP Mesh

## Summary

To enhance the flexural strength and energy dissipation of lightweight self-consolidating concrete (LWSCC: bulk density $1804\ \text{kg/m}^3$, 28 d compressive strength 58.0 MPa, slump flow 730 mm) beams without adding dead weight, Dr. Zhiheng Hu and co-workers at the University of Western Australia and RMIT developed a hybrid strengthening layer (20~40 mm thick) combining ultra-lightweight engineered cementitious composites (ULW-ECC: density $1420\ \text{kg/m}^3$, 28 d compressive strength 48.5 MPa, tensile strain capacity 4.2 %, incorporating fly ash cenospheres FAC and calcined petroleum coke CPC) with fiber-reinforced polymer (FRP) meshes (GFRP, BFRP, CFRP, and externally bonded CFRP sheets). Testing 9 full-scale RC beams under 4-point bending ($0.5\ \text{mm/min}$ displacement control) and formulating a non-linear sectional strain compatibility model, the authors demonstrated that the **ULW-ECC-FRP mesh hybrid layer increased ultimate flexural capacity by 19.5 ~ 39.0 % (reaching 112.5 kNm)**. In particular, GFRP and BFRP meshes preserved the characteristic multiple microcracking ($w < 0.05\ \text{mm}$) of ULW-ECC, **100 % preventing premature interface debonding common to externally bonded sheets and increasing the structural ductility index by 85 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Ultra-lightweight ECC (ULW-ECC, $1420\ \text{kg/m}^3$) and FRP mesh hybrid strengthening**:
   - Upgraded the tensile zone of brittle LWSCC beams without adding dead weight, enhancing flexural capacity by 19.5~39.0 %.
2. **Elimination of premature debonding inherent to externally bonded (EB) FRP sheets**:
   - Enveloping the FRP mesh inside the ductile ULW-ECC matrix prevented epoxy peeling and utilized over 90 % of the ultimate tensile capacity of FRP materials.
3. **Clarification of ductility and crack dispersion across mesh types (GFRP vs BFRP vs CFRP)**:
   - Lower-modulus GFRP and BFRP meshes promoted extensive matrix microcracking and stress redistribution, yielding the highest ductility index ($\mu = 4.8$), whereas stiff CFRP meshes localized deformations.
4. **Non-linear analytical flexural prediction model**:
   - Formulated sectional moment equilibrium equations coupling ULW-ECC tensile strain-hardening constitutive laws with FRP tension stiffening, predicting beam capacities within ±4.5 % error against experimental data.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3)**:
  - LWSCC substrate: OPC, fly ash, pre-wetted lightweight aggregate (LWA), W/B = 0.32, density $1804\ \text{kg/m}^3$, slump flow 730 mm, 28 d compressive strength 58.0 MPa.
  - ULW-ECC overlay: OPC 1.0, silica fume 0.15, fly ash cenospheres (FAC, $d_{50} = 45\ \mu\text{m}$) 0.35, calcined petroleum coke (CPC) 0.15, W/B = 0.28, PVA fibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, density $1420\ \text{kg/m}^3$, 28 d compressive strength 48.5 MPa, tensile strength 4.5 MPa, tensile strain capacity 4.2 %.
  - FRP mesh specifications:
    1. GFRP mesh (grid $25\ \text{mm} \times 25\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 1200 MPa, modulus 50 GPa).
    2. BFRP mesh (grid $25\ \text{mm} \times 25\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 1450 MPa, modulus 65 GPa).
    3. CFRP mesh (grid $20\ \text{mm} \times 20\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 2400 MPa, modulus 230 GPa).
    4. EB CFRP sheet (thickness 0.167 mm, tensile strength 3800 MPa, modulus 240 GPa).
- **Beam specimen design and testing (Figures 1–2 & 8)**:
  - Dimensions: $150\ \text{mm} \times 250\ \text{mm} \times 2200\ \text{mm}$ (span 1900 mm, pure bending zone 600 mm).
  - 4-point bending: Displacement control ($0.5\ \text{mm/min}$), 3 mid-span LVDTs, and strain gauges on rebar and concrete.

### 2. Structural mechanisms and seismic energy dissipation
- **Ductility and energy absorption**: GFRP/BFRP mesh strengthened beams absorbed 65 % more energy up to failure than brittle EB CFRP sheets, accommodating extensive plastic hinge rotations under seismic demands.
- **Interfacial bond integrity**: Monolithic cementitious compatibility between LWSCC and ULW-ECC ensured full composite action without shear slip.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that hybrid strengthening combining ultra-lightweight ECC (ULW-ECC) and ductile FRP meshes eliminates premature peeling, substantially enhancing the flexural capacity and ductility of lightweight concrete beams.
