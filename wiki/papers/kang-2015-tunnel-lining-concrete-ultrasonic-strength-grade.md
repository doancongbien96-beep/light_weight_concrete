# Suggestion for Concrete Strength Grade using Ultrasonic Velocity of Tunnel Lining Concrete

## Summary

To rapidly and accurately evaluate the in-situ durability and structural integrity of tunnel lining concrete in operational infrastructure, Professor Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University, Professor Su-Tae Kang at Daegu University, and Professor Seung-Jun Kwon at Hannam University conducted non-destructive testing on operational railway (Site A) and road (Site B) tunnels. Using two ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) devices (PUNDIT 54 kHz indirect transmission and TR-300), Schmidt rebound hammer testing (NR Type), and compressive strength tests on 100 mm drilled cores across a 267-point field database, the authors analyzed regression correlations between UPV ($V_p$, km/s), rebound hardness (MPa), and measured core compressive strengths (10.5~36.5 MPa), alongside measurement angle orientation effects (0° horizontal vs 90° vertical). The study established a **five-grade non-destructive ultrasonic strength grading standard (Grade I ~ Grade V)** for tunnel lining concrete. Results identified a critical threshold velocity of **$3.74\ \text{km/s}$ to verify the standard $20.0\ \text{MPa}$ design strength** (87.5 % classification reliability) and a lower-bound threshold of **$3.20\ \text{km/s}$ for identifying severe internal defects and porous degradation ($f_c < 15\ \text{MPa}$)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a 267-point field database correlating UPV and core compressive strength**:
   - Compiled direct matching data from operational tunnel linings subjected to real-world aging and leakage rather than idealized laboratory specimens.
2. **Identification of the critical threshold velocity ($3.74\ \text{km/s}$) for the 20 MPa design strength**:
   - Statistically demonstrated that $V_p \ge 3.74\ \text{km/s}$ yields an 87.5 % probability of achieving at least 20 MPa compressive strength.
3. **Establishment of a 5-grade non-destructive strength classification system (Grade I~V)**:
   - Proposed quantitative rating thresholds: Grade I ($\ge 4.0\ \text{km/s}$, >25 MPa), Grade II ($3.7\sim 4.0\ \text{km/s}$, $20\sim 25\ \text{MPa}$), Grade III ($3.2\sim 3.7\ \text{km/s}$, $15\sim 20\ \text{MPa}$), Grade IV ($2.8\sim 3.2\ \text{km/s}$, $10\sim 15\ \text{MPa}$), and Grade V ($< 2.8\ \text{km/s}$, <10 MPa).
4. **Quantification of sensor orientation (0° vs 90°) and couplant thickness ($\le 1\ \text{mm}$) effects**:
   - Quantified a $\pm 0.25\ \mu\text{s}$ variability margin associated with manual contact pressure, providing standardized field execution protocols.

## Methods

- **Field survey program and inspection overview (Section 3.1 & Tables 1–2)**:
  - Site A (Railway Tunnel): Severe aging with crown and sidewall cracking/deterioration, tested at portal (STA 0+0m) and interior sections (STA 0+20m, 0+45m) across 161 test points.
  - Site B (Road Tunnel): Minor water leakage but generally sound condition, tested at STA 0+10m, 0+25m, 0+40m across 106 test points.
  - Testing scope: TR-300 UPV (47 tests), PUNDIT UPV (69 tests), Schmidt hammer (63 tests), $\phi 100\ \text{mm}$ core extraction (88 cores).
- **Non-destructive equipment and test procedures (Section 2 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - PUNDIT (C.N.S Electronics): 54 kHz frequency, $\pm 0.1\ \mu\text{s}$ resolution, surface indirect transmission with 200 mm transducer spacing.
  - TR-300 (Techno Research): Ultrasonic surface wave velocity meter.
  - Schmidt Hammer: NR Type per KS F 2730.
  - Core testing: Compressive strength of 100 mm cores per KS F 2422.

### 2. Proposed non-destructive ultrasonic strength grading standard for tunnel linings (Table 7)

| Strength Grade | UPV Range ($V_p$, km/s) | Estimated Strength ($f_c$, MPa) | Structural Condition & Maintenance Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grade I (Excellent)** | **$V_p \ge 4.0\ \text{km/s}$** | **$f_c \ge 25.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **Completely sound (routine maintenance)** |
| **Grade II (Good)** | **$3.7\le V_p < 4.0\ \text{km/s}$** | **$20.0\le f_c < 25.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **Meets design criteria (good condition)** |
| **Grade III (Fair)** | **$3.2\le V_p < 3.7\ \text{km/s}$** | **$15.0\le f_c < 20.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **Minor degradation (regular detailed inspection)** |
| **Grade IV (Poor)** | **$2.8\le V_p < 3.2\ \text{km/s}$** | **$10.0\le f_c < 15.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **Strength deficit (repair/retrofitting evaluation)** |
| **Grade V (Severe)** | **$V_p < 2.8\ \text{km/s}$** | **$f_c < 10.0\ \text{MPa}$** | **Severe internal defects (urgent retrofitting mandatory)** |

- **Overall conclusion**: This study established the empirical relationship between UPV and core compressive strength in operational tunnel linings, proposing the critical $3.74\ \text{km/s}$ threshold for 20 MPa compliance and a five-tier safety grading framework.
