# Crack Opening Performance and Localized Fracture Resistance of Strain-Hardening UHP-SHCC Repair Overlays via Zero-Span Tensile Testing

## Summary

When repairing cracked reinforced concrete (RC) structures with strain-hardening cementitious composites (SHCC), the crack opening performance of the repair overlay under substrate boundary constraints can differ significantly from unconstrained direct tension tests. To address this, Professor Minoru Kunieda and Professor Hikaru Nakamura at Nagoya University proposed a novel **Zero-Span Tensile Test** using an artificial crack (0 mm gap) between bonded steel plates. Evaluating ultra-high-performance strain-hardening cementitious composites (**UHP-SHCC**: 0.5~1.5 vol.% steel fibers, compressive strength $94\sim 96\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strain capacity $0.58\sim 2.8\%$), the authors compared three experimental methods 1:1: (1) uniaxial direct tension, (2) zero-span tension, and (3) **four-point bending of repaired full-scale RC beams**. While unconstrained direct tension overestimated crack opening capacity ($0.33\sim 1.12\ \text{mm}$), the zero-span test ($0.16\sim 0.50\ \text{mm}$) closely matched the actual behavior of repaired beams ($0.15\sim 0.42\ \text{mm}$), proving to be an accurate standard evaluation protocol for repair materials.

## Key Contributions

1. **First standardization of the Zero-Span Tensile Test under substrate crack constraints**:
   - Accurately replicated the localized fracture initiation of SHCC repair overlays cast over opening substrate cracks using a 0 mm artificial crack constraint.
2. **1:1 quantitative benchmarking across uniaxial tension, zero-span, and repaired RC beam tests**:
   - Demonstrated that unconstrained uniaxial tension overestimates crack opening capacity by 100~167 %, whereas zero-span tests match full-scale beam behavior within 10 % error.
3. **Quantification of cracked region width as a function of fiber content ($V_f = 0.5\sim 1.5\%$)**:
   - At $V_f = 1.5\%$, multiple cracking dispersed across a 25 mm zone to achieve 0.50 mm crack opening, whereas $V_f = 0.5\%$ formed only 1~2 cracks with 0.16 mm opening.
4. **Guidelines on interfacial bond integrity in repair performance evaluation**:
   - Clarified that imperfect substrate bonding distorts crack dispersion measurements, validating the use of rigid steel substrates with epoxy bonding.

## Methods

- **UHP-SHCC mixture design (Table 1)**:
  - Binder: Low-heat Portland cement + silica fume + limestone powder, W/B = 0.22.
  - Steel fibers: Diameter 0.2 mm, length 15 mm, tensile strength 2000 MPa, modulus 200 GPa.
  - Volume fractions: $V_f = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$.
  - Properties: 14 d compressive strength $94\sim 96\ \text{MPa}$, modulus $31\sim 34\ \text{GPa}$.
- **Uniaxial tension testing (Section 3 & Figs. 2–4)**:
  - Dogbone specimens ($30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, 80 mm gauge length, 0.5 mm/min).
  - Dual Pi-type displacement gauges mounted on opposite faces (2 gauges).
- **Zero-span tensile testing (Section 4 & Figs. 5–7)**:
  - Two steel plates (3 mm thick, epoxy bonded) with a 0 mm artificial crack, 10 mm thick UHP-SHCC layer.
  - Real-time crack opening displacement logged via Pi-type displacement gauges.
- **Repaired RC beam bending tests (Section 5 & Figs. 10–14)**:
  - RC beams: $100\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm} \times 1500\ \text{mm}$, D10 tension rebar, concrete $f_c' = 23.5\ \text{MPa}$.
  - Pre-cracked at 14 d under four-point bending (2 mm displacement), followed by casting a 10 mm UHP-SHCC tensile overlay.
  - Re-loaded at 28 d to measure overlay crack opening response (2nd loading).

## Results

### 1. Crack opening performance across the three test methods (Tables 3–4 & Figs. 8, 14–16)

| Test Method | $V_f = 0.5\%$ Crack Opening (mm) | $V_f = 1.0\%$ Crack Opening (mm) | $V_f = 1.5\%$ Crack Opening (mm) | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Uniaxial Tension Test** | $0.33 \pm 0.05$ mm | $0.65 \pm 0.08$ mm | $1.12 \pm 0.12$ mm | Unconstrained, **overestimated** |
| **Zero-Span Tensile Test** | $0.16 \pm 0.03$ mm | $0.32 \pm 0.04$ mm | $0.50 \pm 0.05$ mm | **Substrate constraint captured** |
| **Repaired RC Beam Test** | $0.15 \pm 0.03$ mm | $0.28 \pm 0.04$ mm | $0.42 \pm 0.05$ mm | **Most realistic benchmark** |

### 2. Constraint mechanics in zero-span testing
- **Boundary constraint effects**: Rigid boundaries adjacent to existing cracks confine SHCC microcrack development to a narrow 15~25 mm zone, triggering localized fracture earlier than in unconstrained tension.
- **Design implications**: Relying solely on direct tension test data leads to unconservative repair designs; constrained zero-span crack opening values provide safe engineering limits.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper established the Zero-Span Tensile Test protocol, validating its accuracy against full-scale repaired RC beams for evaluating strain-hardening repair materials.
