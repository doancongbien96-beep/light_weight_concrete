## Summary
A landmark paper in which Professor M. Shinozuka's research team at Columbia University clarified the energy dissipation area, residual strain, and damage damping ratio of rubberized structural concrete (RuC) incorporating recycled waste-tire crumb rubber (volume replacement 0 ~ 20 vol %) under repeated compressive loading, together with the seismic lateral hysteretic behavior of full-scale reinforced concrete columns. At a rubber content of 15 vol %, the cyclic compression hysteretic energy dissipation density of the concrete increased by a factor of **2.4** over ordinary concrete, and the seismic displacement ductility index of full-scale columns improved by **45 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the plastic hysteretic energy dissipation enhancement mechanism of RuC**: viscoelastic rubber particles trap shear microcracks, suppressing crushing-type brittle failure.
- **Cyclic compression constitutive hysteresis loop model established**: damage evolution equation formulated between the energy dissipation area $E_D$ and plastic strain $\epsilon_p$.
- **Seismic plastic hinge performance of full-scale RuC columns demonstrated**: prevention of early cover-concrete spalling and stability of axial load support secured.
- **Simultaneous achievement of sustainable waste-tire resource circulation and structural seismic safety**: eco-friendly seismic design standard presented.

## Methods
- Mixes: OPC ($W/C = 0.42$), rubber particle replacement ratios 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 vol % (particle size $1 \sim 4 mm$).
- Tests: material cyclic compression tests ($150 \times 300 mm$ cylinders, displacement control), quasi-static cyclic lateral loading tests of full-scale RC columns ($250 \times 250 \times 1500 mm$, axial load ratio $n = 0.20$).
