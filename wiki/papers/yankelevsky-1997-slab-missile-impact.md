## Summary

This is a globally recognized landmark theoretical paper by Professor D.Z. Yankelevsky of the Technion, Israel, that fully formalizes the local response (penetration depth Penetration, front-face ejecta crater, rear-face fragment spalling Scabbing, complete perforation Perforation) of nuclear power plant containment structures and protective bunker slabs struck by high-speed rigid projectiles (Missile Impact, $v = 100 \sim 500 m/s$) using physics-based one-dimensional spherical/conical cavity expansion theory (Cavity Expansion Theory) and a fragment cone failure mechanism. Moving beyond empirical formulas, an analytical model was established that physically predicts the ultimate penetration depth and the limit perforation thickness ($t_p$) from the dynamic compressive/shear constitutive relations of concrete.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of an integrated physical analysis model for the local behavior of concrete under projectile impact**: (1) front crushed-crater formation, (2) tunneling penetration resistance, and (3) rear shear-tensile fragment cone formation unified under a single governing equation.
- **Physical derivation of the scabbing initiation threshold velocity ($v_{sc}$)**: formalization of the correlation between the reflected tensile shock wave stress and the dynamic tensile strength ($f_{td}$) of concrete.
- **Demonstration of prediction accuracy 30 % higher than empirical formulas (NDRC, Hughes, ACE equations)**: analytical accommodation of various nose shapes (flat nose, hemispherical nose, conical nose).
- **Provision of optimal design thickness criteria for protective slabs**: establishment of minimum thickness criteria for nuclear power plant containment walls and military bunker slabs.

## Methods

- Theoretical model: integration of the spherical cavity expansion pressure $P_c(v)$ over the nonlinear compressive crushing zone and the elastoplastic zone.
- Failure criteria: stress wave reflection at the rear free surface (Spall criteria) and shear plugging (Shear Plugging) limit states.
