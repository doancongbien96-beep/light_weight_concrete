## Summary

This representative study from Universiti Sains Malaysia (USM) precisely characterized the flexural strain-hardening behavior, initial cracking strength, modulus of rupture (MOR), deflection ductility, and multiple fine-cracking characteristics of green ECC in which palm oil fuel ash (POFA), a tropical agricultural byproduct, was finely ground (median particle size $d_{50} = 4.3 \mu m$, specific surface $1240 m^2/kg$) and used to replace cement in bulk at up to 70 wt % (POFA/Binder = 0.0 ~ 0.7), using four-point flexural loading tests (span 300 mm) and microstructural analysis. The ultra-fine pozzolanic reaction of POFA moderated the excessive chemical bonding between PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$) and the cement matrix, and the 50 % POFA mix achieved excellent strain-hardening performance with a flexural strength of 11.8 MPa, an ultimate deflection of 10.5 mm, a flexural toughness index $I_{30} = 42.5$, and a mean crack width of 45 µm or less.

## Key Contributions
- **Demonstrated flexural deflection-hardening behavior of high-content POFA (50 ~ 70 wt %) Green ECC**: greatly reduced cement use while securing flexural ductility equal to or better than the reference ECC (POFA 0 %).
- **Mechanism of interfacial frictional-slip optimization by ultra-fine ground POFA identified**: fine siliceous particles control the hydrophilic chemical bonding at the fiber–matrix interface, suppressing fiber rupture and inducing stable pullout.
- **Multiple fine-cracking control verified**: more than 35 fine cracks dispersed across the entire flexural tensile region, confining crack widths below $50 \mu m$ even under ultimate load.
- **Sharp reduction in embodied carbon and environmental burden**: agricultural waste resource circulation together with a cut of more than 55 % in binder embodied energy.

## Methods
- Binder composition: ordinary Portland cement (OPC) + heat-treated unground POFA ($d_{50} = 4.3 \mu m$, LOI 1.8 %, $SiO_2$ 64.2 %).
- Mix design: $W/B = 0.36 \sim 0.38$, Sand/Binder = 0.36 (silica sand maximum particle size 0.3 mm), superplasticizer (SP) $0.8 \sim 1.5 wt \%$.
- Fiber reinforcement: oil-coated PVA fiber from Kuraray (Japan) ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elastic modulus 41 GPa).
- Specimens and test methods:
  - Flexural specimens: $100 \times 100 \times 500 mm$ prismatic beams, four-point loading (constant-moment span 100 mm, lower support span 300 mm), displacement control ($0.5 mm/min$), midspan deflection measured with LVDTs.
  - Compressive strength: $100 \times 200 mm$ cylinders per ASTM C39 (7 d, 28 d, 90 d).

## Results
- Comparison of 28-day mechanical properties by mix:

| Mix | POFA replacement (%) | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | Initial cracking flexural strength (MPa) | Modulus of rupture MOR (MPa) | Ultimate deflection (mm) | Flexural toughness index $I_{30}$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Control (POFA 0) | 0 | 48.5 | 4.85 | 10.2 | 8.2 | 34.2 |
| POFA 30 | 30 | 45.2 | 4.62 | 11.0 | 9.8 | 38.6 |
| POFA 50 | 50 | 41.8 | 4.25 | 11.8 | 10.5 | 42.5 |
| POFA 70 | 70 | 32.4 | 3.50 | 8.5 | 11.2 | 44.0 |

- At longer ages (90 d) the POFA 50 % mix reached a compressive strength of 49.5 MPa through the sustained pozzolanic reaction, with its flexural strain-hardening envelope extended by more than 30 % versus the reference mix.
