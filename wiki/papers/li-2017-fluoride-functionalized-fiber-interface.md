# Mechanism of Fiber–Polymer Interface Compatibility and Mechanical·Dielectric Performance Enhancement via Two-Step Surface Fluoride-Functionalization Chemical Grafting

## Summary

A landmark primary-source paper published in RSC Advances in 2017 by the research team of **Zhenhua Li and Jingsong Liu at Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST)**. To solve the severe interfacial incompatibility and micro-void formation between hydrophobic fluoropolymer matrices and hydrophilic inorganic fibers, it developed a **two-step surface fluoride-functionalization chemical grafting (Fluoride-Functionalization) technique using a silane coupling agent ($\gamma$-MPS) and trifluoroethyl methacrylate (TFEMA)**, successfully bonding fluorinated organic chains onto glass fiber (F-GF) surfaces, and elucidated the effects of fluoride modification on the composite's tensile strength, elongation at break, water absorption, coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE), and 10 GHz high-frequency dielectric loss. The results proved that fluorinated surface modification eliminates fiber-matrix interfacial voids and maximizes mechanical adhesion, enhancing **tensile strength from $30.52\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 37.12\ \text{MPa}$ (+21.6 %) while slashing water absorption from $0.032\% \rightarrow 0.012\%$, a $62.5\%$ plunge**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of a low-cost two-step fluoride-functionalization chemical grafting (Surface Fluoride-Grafting) process**:
   - Substituting for expensive fluoroalkylsilanes by inducing TFEMA radical polymerization after commercial $\gamma$-MPS silane treatment, economically introducing dense fluorinated functional groups onto the fiber surface.
2. **Maximization of chemical compatibility between the hydrophobic matrix and the inorganic fiber**:
   - Fluorine atoms on the fiber surface form strong van der Waals bonds and molecular entanglement with the fluorinated matrix, suppressing micro-delamination within the interphase at its origin.
3. **Simultaneous attainment of composite tensile strength ($+21.6\%$) and dimensional stability (CTE reduction)**:
   - Tensile strength raised to $37.12\ \text{MPa}$ relative to untreated fibers, with the thermal expansion coefficient stabilized to the level of copper foil ($17\times 10^{-6}/\text{K}$).
4. **Achievement of ultra-low water absorption ($0.012\%$) and ultra-low high-frequency dielectric loss ($\tan\delta = 0.00085$)**:
   - Blocking interfacial moisture ingress to maximize the reliability of microwave communication substrates and composites for harsh environments.

## Methods

- **Surface modification chemistry (Section 2.2 & Schemes 1~2)**:
  - Step 1: grafting of the $\gamma$-MPS silane coupling agent onto E-glass fiber ($d_f = 13\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 6\ \text{mm}$) surfaces (fabrication of M-GF).
  - Step 2: radical polymerization of M-GF with 2,2,2-trifluoroethyl methacrylate (TFEMA) under BPO initiator at $75\ ^\circ\text{C}$ $\rightarrow$ synthesis of fluorinated fiber (F-GF).
- **Composite molding and property measurement (Section 2.3~2.4)**:
  - PTFE powder 95 wt.% + fiber 5 wt.% mixed with ultrasonic ethanol dispersion $\rightarrow$ static press molding at 45 MPa $\rightarrow$ sintering at $370\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
  - Mechanical properties: tensile strength and elongation at break (ASTM D638, universal testing machine).
  - Interfacial analysis: XPS photoelectron spectroscopy (verification of C-F bonds), FE-SEM fracture surface analysis.

### 2. Interpretation of interfacial chemical bonding and failure mode
- **Strengthened interfacial shear adhesion**: untreated fibers (GF), lacking affinity with the matrix, pull out cleanly from the fiber surface under tension, whereas F-GF exhibits cohesive failure in which the matrix resin remains firmly attached to the fiber surface thanks to chemical interactions among the fluorinated chains.
- **Blocking moisture ingress pathways at their source**: the extreme water repellency of fluorine atoms ($\text{Contact Angle} > 110^\circ$) completely suppresses capillary moisture uptake along the fiber-matrix interface, reducing water absorption to the 1/3 level.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark primary source in the polymer–inorganic composite field that established a precision interface-control technology maximizing interfacial compatibility between dissimilar materials through surface fluoride-functionalization chemical grafting, dramatically improving the mechanical strength and moisture-absorption durability of composites.
