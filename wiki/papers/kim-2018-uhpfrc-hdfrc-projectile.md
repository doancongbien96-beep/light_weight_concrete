# Response of UHPFRC and HDFRC Subjected to Projectile Impact

## Summary

Under ultra-high-velocity projectile impact from a 7.62 mm caliber armor-piercing round ($v = 850 \sim 1000\ \text{m/s}$, $v = 850 \sim 1000 m/s$), Prof. Sung Wook Kim, Prof. Chul Woo Park, Prof. Dong Joo Kim, and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee comparatively investigated the localized impact damage (penetration depth, front crater volume, rear scabbing, and fragment ejection) and energy absorption mechanisms of ultra-high-performance fiber-reinforced concrete (UHPFRC, $f_c = 150\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 150 MPa$, straight steel fibers $V_f = 2.0\%$, $V_f = 2.0 \%$) and high-ductility fiber-reinforced concrete (HDFRC / PE-SHCC, $f_c = 45\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 45 MPa$, ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers $V_f = 2.0\%$, $V_f = 2.0 \%$, $\epsilon_u = 7.5\%$, $\epsilon_u = 7.5 \%$) using ballistic tests and high-speed photography. Experimental results demonstrated that UHPFRC minimizes frontal penetration depth via ultra-high matrix strength, whereas HDFRC completely suppresses (100 %) rear scabbing by dissipating shock waves through widespread ductile multiple microcracking.

## Key Contributions

- **Comparative evaluation of strength-based (UHPFRC) vs ductility-based (HDFRC) impact defense mechanisms**: High-strength composites provide superior penetration resistance, whereas high-ductility composites excel at eliminating rear fragment scabbing.
- **Verification of zero scabbing in HDFRC**: The bridging network of PE fibers with 7.5 % tensile strain capacity completely arrests tensile spalling caused by reflected shock waves.
- **Dual-layer composite protective panel design (UHPFRC front + HDFRC rear)**: Exploited material synergies to reduce overall protective barrier thickness by over 40 %.
- **Development of modified empirical models for high-velocity projectile penetration depth**: Formulated predictive formulas coupling projectile kinetic energy with fiber toughness parameters.

## Methods

- Target panel dimensions: $400 \times 400 \times 100\ \text{mm}$ ($400 \times 400 \times 100 mm$, or $150\ \text{mm}$, $150 mm$).
- Materials tested: Normal concrete (NC 35 MPa), steel-fiber UHPFRC ($f_c = 152\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 152 MPa$, diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm steel fibers), PE-HDFRC ($f_c = 46\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 46 MPa$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm PE fibers).
- Projectile: 7.62 mm military rifle bullet (mass 9.6 g, impact velocity $850 \pm 20\ \text{m/s}$, $850 \pm 20 m/s$, powder gun firing).
- Diagnostic tools: High-speed video camera ($50000\ \text{fps}$, $50000 fps$) and 3D optical scanning to quantify front crater and rear scabbing volumes.
