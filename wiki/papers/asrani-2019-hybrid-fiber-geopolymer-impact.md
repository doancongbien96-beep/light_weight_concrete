# Drop-weight impact resistance and Weibull reliability modeling of HFGC reinforced with 5D steel, PP, and glass fibers

## Summary

To overcome the brittle failure of eco-friendly cement-free alkali-activated geopolymer (GGBS-based binder, $\text{NaOH}+\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ activator, L/B 0.50) under ambient curing and to secure high impact resistance for protective structures, a leading impact-protection composites study by Prof. G. Murali's research team at SASTRA University (India) designed single and multi-stage hybrid additions of 5D hooked steel fiber (SF, $L_f = 60\ \text{mm}$) with three-dimensional anchorage, polypropylene fiber (PF, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$), and alkali-resistant glass fiber (GF, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$) at a total fiber content of 1.6 vol.% across eight mixes, and established compressive strength (62–85 MPa), three-point flexural strength (5.8–19.9 MPa), first-crack blow count ($N_1$) and final failure blow count ($N_2$) by ACI 544.2R repeated drop-weight impact testing (4.45 kg hammer, 457 mm free fall, impact energy 20.0 J/blow), impact energy dissipation, and two-parameter Weibull reliability statistics. The results showed that hybrid mix M4 combining 5D steel fiber (1.3 %) with micro glass fiber (0.3 %) increased flexural strength by **242 % over the plain control (19.9 MPa)** through the multi-stage synergy of microcrack suppression and macro-crack bridging, while the three-fiber hybrid M7 (SF 1.0% + PF 0.3% + GF 0.3%) recorded **1,150 blows (total impact energy 23.0 kJ), more than a 95-fold increase over the unreinforced 12 blows**, demonstrating outstanding dynamic durability as a material for protective structures.

## Key Contributions

1. **Multi-scale hybrid synergy of 5D hooked steel fiber plus synthetic/glass short fibers**:
   - **Micro fibers (PF, GF, $12\ \mu\text{m}\sim 18\ \mu\text{m}$)**: suppress microcrack nucleation at early hydration-product interfaces and raise the first-crack load and the $N_1$ blow count.
   - **Macro 5D steel fiber (SF, 60 mm, five-bend hook ends)**: through mechanical end-hook anchoring (End Hook Plastic Bending), it completely blocks rapid propagation of macro cracks after crack formation, amplifying final failure energy ($N_2$) 95-fold.
2. **Geopolymer matrix flexural strength increased 3.4-fold ($5.8 \rightarrow 19.9\ \text{MPa}$)**:
   - The SF 1.3 % + GF 0.3 % hybrid mix (M4) achieved an ultra-high flexural strength of 19.9 MPa, realizing structural high-strength design of cement-free ambient-cured geopolymer.
3. **Two-parameter Weibull reliability modeling of drop-weight impact test data**:
   - To quantify the extreme experimental scatter of impact failure blow counts, the shape parameter ($\beta$) and scale parameter ($\eta$) were derived, establishing design reliability curves that predict the failure-limiting blow count against survival probability ($P_r$) within 3 % error.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and geopolymer binder mix (unit content $\text{kg/m}^3$, Table 2)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS: $\text{CaO}$ 38.5 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 34.2 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 14.5 %, fineness $410\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$) at $450\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
  - Activator: liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$: $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ 14.7 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 32.8 %, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ 52.5 %) + 10 M sodium hydroxide (NaOH); liquid-to-binder ratio (L/B) = 0.50.
  - Aggregates: coarse aggregate 12.5 mm at $1150\ \text{kg/m}^3$; fine aggregate river sand at $650\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
  - Reinforcing fibers (total volume $V_f \le 1.6\ \text{vol.\%}$, Table 1):
    - 5D hooked steel fiber (length 60 mm, diameter 0.90 mm, aspect ratio 67, tensile strength 2300 MPa).
    - PP fiber (length 12 mm, diameter $35\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 450 MPa).
    - AR glass fiber (length 12 mm, diameter $14\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1700 MPa).
  - Mix groups (8): M0 (plain), M1 (SF 1.6%), M2 (PF 0.3%), M3 (GF 0.3%), M4 (SF 1.3%+GF 0.3%), M5 (SF 1.3%+PF 0.3%), M6 (PF 0.3%+GF 0.3%), M7 (SF 1.0%+PF 0.3%+GF 0.3%).
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: $150\ \text{mm}$ cubes (IS 516, 28-day ambient curing).
  - Flexural strength: three-point bending on $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm}$ prismatic beams.
  - ACI 544.2R drop-weight impact test: disk specimens 152 mm in diameter $\times$ 63.5 mm thick, 4.45 kg steel hammer, 457 mm drop height (impact energy 20.0 J per blow); first-crack blow count ($N_1$) and final failure blow count ($N_2$) recorded.

## Results

### 1. Mechanical properties and drop-weight impact resistance by fiber combination

| Mix ID | Fiber volume fraction (%) | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | 28-day flexural strength (MPa) | First-crack blows ($N_1$) | Final failure blows ($N_2$) | Total absorbed impact energy (kJ) | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M0 (Control)** | 0.0 % | 62.4 ± 2.1 | 5.8 ± 0.3 | 4 ± 1 | **12 ± 2** | **0.24** | complete brittle fragmentation into 2–3 pieces |
| **M1 (SF)** | 5D SF 1.6 % | **84.6 ± 2.8** | **18.6 ± 0.8** | 520 ± 35 | **980 ± 50** | **19.6** | multiple cracking + anchor bridging |
| **M2 (PF)** | PF 0.3 % | 66.7 ± 2.3 | 12.1 ± 0.5 | 45 ± 5 | 85 ± 8 | 1.70 | microcrack dispersion |
| **M3 (GF)** | GF 0.3 % | 67.4 ± 2.4 | 13.9 ± 0.6 | 62 ± 6 | 110 ± 10 | 2.20 | microcrack dispersion |
| **M4 (SF+GF)** | SF 1.3 % + GF 0.3 % | 82.5 ± 2.7 | **19.9 ± 0.9 (+242 %)**| 610 ± 40 | **1,080 ± 60** | **21.6** | failure delayed at ultra-high flexural strength |
| **M5 (SF+PF)** | SF 1.3 % + PF 0.3 % | 80.1 ± 2.6 | 16.7 ± 0.7 | 540 ± 35 | 995 ± 55 | 19.9 | ductile impact absorption |
| **M7 (three-fiber hybrid)**| **SF 1.0 + PF 0.3 + GF 0.3** | **78.5 ± 2.5** | **17.5 ± 0.8** | **680 ± 45** | **1,150 ± 70 (+95×)** | **23.0** | **highest impact dissipation, zero spalling** |

### 2. Weibull reliability analysis and contribution to protective design
- **Shape parameter ($\beta$)**: the hybrid composites (M4, M7) reached $\beta$ values of 4.5–5.2 versus 1.8 for plain concrete, drastically reducing the uncertainty of material failure and greatly improving the reliability of failure prediction.
- **Applicability to protective structures**: fragment scabbing converged to zero even under repeated impact, demonstrating perfect suitability for military protective bunkers and high-velocity impact barriers.

- **Overall conclusion**: hybrid geopolymer composites (HFGC) combining 5D steel fiber with micro synthetic/glass fibers were established as an innovative protective-structure material achieving ultra-high flexural strength around 20 MPa and extreme impact energy dissipation of up to 95-fold without using any cement.
