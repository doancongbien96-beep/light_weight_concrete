# Microbially Induced Calcite Precipitation (MICP) and Biomimetics for Concrete Crack Self-Healing and Strength Enhancement: A Review

## Summary

To reduce the environmental carbon footprint of concrete structures and lower lifecycle maintenance costs, researchers at the Structural Engineering Research Centre (CSIR-SERC, India) led by Dr. V. Ramesh Kumar and Director Nagesh R. Iyer synthesized the principles of **biomimetic self-healing concrete** using biomineralization and biodeposition. The review details the **MICP (Microbially Induced Calcite Precipitation) biochemical reaction pathways**, in which ureolytic bacteria (*Bacillus pasteurii* / *Sporosarcina pasteurii*, *Bacillus sphaericus*, *Bacillus subtilis*, and anaerobic extremophiles *Shewanella* sp.) secrete urease enzyme to hydrolyze urea ($\text{CO(NH}_2)_2$) into carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$), adsorbing $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions onto negatively charged bacterial cell walls to precipitate insoluble calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals. Introducing bacterial endospores at optimal concentrations ($10^5\sim 10^6\ \text{cells/mL}$) densifies cement mortar pore networks, increasing 28 d compressive strength by **25 ~ 30 %**, completely sealing crack widths of $0.2\sim 0.5\ \text{mm}$ (100 %) without external grout injection, and reducing water absorption by 65 %.

## Key Contributions

1. **Formulation of MICP biochemical reaction pathways and calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) precipitation mechanics**:
   - Systematized the 3-step chemical reaction: electrostatic binding of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ to negatively charged peptidoglycan cell walls, enzymatic urea decomposition, and heterogeneous calcite nucleation.
2. **Demonstration of microcrack ($0.2\sim 0.5\ \text{mm}$) self-healing and 65 % watertightness recovery**:
   - Moisture and oxygen ingress reactivated dormant bacterial endospores along crack surfaces, sealing crack throats with calcite crystals to block chloride penetration.
3. **Identification of micro-filler mechanisms yielding 25~30 % compressive strength gains**:
   - Demonstrated that nanoscale $\text{CaCO}_3$ crystal deposition fills capillary matrix pores, increasing mortar compressive strength by 25~30 %.
4. **Bacterial survival strategies and carrier immobilization in high-alkaline (pH 12~13) environments**:
   - Proposed microencapsulation and immobilization techniques using polyurethane hydrogels, porous ceramics, and lightweight aggregate (LWA) carriers.

## Methods

- **Governing MICP biochemical reactions**:
  1. Urea hydrolysis:
     $$\text{CO(NH}_2)_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Urease}} \text{NH}_2\text{COOH} + \text{NH}_3$$
     $$\text{NH}_2\text{COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$$
  2. Ammonia equilibrium and pH elevation:
     $$2\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \leftrightarrow 2\text{NH}_4^+ + 2\text{OH}^- \quad (\text{pH } 9.0\sim 9.5)$$
  3. Carbonate precipitation and calcium binding:
     $$\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{OH}^- \leftrightarrow \text{CO}_3^{2-} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
     $$\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{Cell} \rightarrow \text{Cell-Ca}^{2+}$$
     $$\text{Cell-Ca}^{2+} + \text{CO}_3^{2-} \rightarrow \text{Cell-CaCO}_3 \downarrow\ (\text{Calcite})$$
- **Microbial strains and nutrient sources**:
  - Bacterial strains: *Sporosarcina pasteurii* (ATCC 11859), *Bacillus sphaericus*, *Shewanella* sp., *Bacillus subtilis*.
  - Calcium/nutrient media: Calcium lactate, calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$), yeast extract, corn steep liquor.
- **Evaluation parameters (Table 1 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Compressive strength variation, crack sealing efficiency, ultrasonic P-wave velocity recovery, water/gas absorption.

### 2. Biomimetic self-healing mechanics and durability implications
- **Dosage optimization**: At $10^5\sim 10^6\ \text{cells/mL}$, crystal growth densified capillary voids, increasing compressive strength by up to 30 %; exceeding $10^8\ \text{cells/mL}$ created void defects from decaying dead biomass.
- **Autonomous zero-maintenance capability**: Encapsulated bacterial endospores survive for decades, repeatedly re-activating upon crack formation to provide permanent self-healing.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review synthesized microbially induced calcite precipitation mechanisms for autonomous crack healing and matrix densification, establishing the foundation for biomimetic sustainable concrete.
