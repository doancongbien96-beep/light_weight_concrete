# Micromechanical Design of Green and Durable ECC (GD-ECC) Combining Recycled Fishing-Net PE Fibers, Desert Sand, and Carbonation Curing: 4.50 % Tensile Ductility and 72 % Carbon Reduction

## Summary

To resolve marine plastic pollution and natural sand depletion while drastically curtailing construction carbon emissions, Professor Victor C. Li and co-workers at the University of Michigan designed a green and durable engineered cementitious composite (GD-ECC) based on pseudo-strain-hardening (PSH) micromechanics. The composite integrates recycled polyethylene fibers extruded from waste marine fishing nets ($d_f = 26\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 2100\ \text{MPa}$), unrefined fine desert sand ($d_{50} = 135\ \mu\text{m}$), industrial by-products (GGBS and fly ash), and accelerated 20 % $\text{CO}_2$ carbonation curing. Evaluating direct tension, compressive strength, sulfate attack, rapid chloride penetrability, freeze-thaw durability, and life cycle assessment (LCA), the authors produced a high-performance material achieving **4.50 % uniaxial tensile ductility, 6.20 MPa ultimate tensile strength, 48.5 MPa 28 d compressive strength, a 72 % reduction in carbon footprint, and a 58 % cost reduction** relative to standard ECC.

## Key Contributions

1. **First application of recycled fishing-net PE fibers in micromechanically designed ECC**:
   - Demonstrated that while recycled fibers have lower tensile strength than virgin PE ($2100\ \text{MPa}$ vs $3000\ \text{MPa}$), an adequate complementary bridging energy ratio ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 2.85$) is maintained, delivering 4.50 % tensile strain capacity.
2. **100 % replacement of processed silica sand with natural desert sand**:
   - Exploited the spherical geometry and micro-packing of desert sand to replace 100 % of manufactured silica sand while tailoring matrix fracture toughness.
3. **Permanent $\text{CO}_2$ sequestration and microstructure refinement via carbonation curing**:
   - Permanently mineralized $\text{CO}_2$ (12.5 % uptake by cement weight) into calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals during early hydration, sealing surface pores.
4. **LCA-verified 72 % carbon reduction and 58 % cost improvement**:
   - Confirmed circular sustainability and carbon-negative potential across raw material extraction, processing, and long-term service life.

## Methods

- **Materials and GD-ECC mixture design (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Binders: Low-carbon composite binder (OPC + GGBS + FA, W/B = 0.22).
  - Aggregate: 100 % natural desert sand ($d_{50} = 135\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Recycled fishing-net PE micro-fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 26\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 2100\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 75\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Curing regime: 24 h mold curing $\rightarrow$ 24 h in 20 % $\text{CO}_2$ carbonation chamber (0.1 MPa pressure, 70 % RH) $\rightarrow$ water curing.
- **Testing protocols (Sections 2.3–2.5 & Figs. 5–8)**:
  - Direct tension: Dogbone specimens (0.5 mm/min).
  - Durability: ASTM C1202 rapid chloride permeability, ASTM C666 freeze-thaw (300 cycles), 5 % sodium sulfate immersion for 120 d.
  - LCA analysis: SimaPro v9.0 and Ecoinvent database.

### 2. Recycled PE fiber interface and carbonation self-sealing mechanics
- **Interfacial friction control**: Surface micro-roughness from waste net extrusion provided an ideal frictional bond ($\tau_0 = 1.25\ \text{MPa}$), avoiding premature fiber rupture and sustaining 4.50 % elongation.
- **$\text{CO}_2$ mineralization**: Calcite crystals precipitated in desert sand interfacial zones, lowering rapid chloride permeability charge below 150 C.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that pairing recycled fishing net fibers, desert sand, and early carbonation curing achieves 4.50 % tensile ductility and superior durability while reducing carbon emissions by 72 %.
