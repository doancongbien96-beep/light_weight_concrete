## Summary
A representative study of the Japan Society of Civil Engineers (JSCE) that elucidated the multiple microcrack propagation behavior and fiber bridging degradation mechanisms of high-ductility composites (PVA-ECC and PE-ECC) under flexural fatigue loading. Four-point flexural fatigue tests were performed to evaluate whether the excellent multiple-cracking strain-hardening performance exhibited under static flexural loading remains effectively sustained under repeated fatigue loading. It was comparatively demonstrated that PE-ECC, owing to the high abrasion resistance of its fibers, maintained multiple cracking and recorded fatigue runout over $2 \times 10^6$ cycles even at a fatigue load ratio of $S = 0.80$, whereas PVA-ECC exhibited a fatigue limit ratio at the $S = 0.65$ level due to interfacial frictional wear.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of differences in flexural fatigue failure mechanisms by fiber type (PE vs PVA)**: characterization of the superior fatigue pull-out retention of PE fibers versus the fatigue softening behavior of PVA fibers caused by interfacial abrasion.
- **Measured saturation behavior of multiple microcracking under repeated flexural loading**: more than 30 microcracks proven to remain stable over more than 80 % of the fatigue life.
- **Establishment of fatigue design curves for bridge decks and road pavements**: provision of an $S-N$ fatigue design envelope for application under the JSCE road bridge specifications.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15) and PE-ECC ($V_f = 1.5 \%$, diameter 12 µm, strength 2700 MPa).
- **Fatigue test**: four-point flexural fatigue test on $100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$ beam specimens ($R = 0.1$, frequency 5 Hz, up to $2 \times 10^6$ cycles).
- **Instrumentation**: dynamic deflection, acoustic emission (AE) crack detection, and optical microscopic crack-width measurement.

## Results
- PE-ECC: no failure up to $2 \times 10^6$ cycles at fatigue stress ratio $S = 0.80$ (fatigue limit $S_e = 0.80$).
- PVA-ECC: secured $S_e = 0.65$, proving far superior fatigue durability compared with conventional fiber-reinforced concrete ($S_e \approx 0.45$).
