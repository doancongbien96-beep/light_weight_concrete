# Challenges for Strain Hardening Cementitious Composites with Ultra High Performance

## Summary

This landmark FraMCoS-5 conference study by Prof. M. Kunieda (Nagoya University), Prof. K. Rokugo (Gifu University), and Prof. Victor Li (University of Michigan) systematizes the micromechanical challenges—mitigating elevated matrix fracture toughness $K_m$, controlling single fiber pullout frictional stress, and optimizing particle packing density—for achieving ultra-high ductility with direct tensile strain capacity exceeding **4.0 %** in ultra-high strength matrices (compressive strength 150 MPa or higher). The authors proposed and experimentally validated a prototype Ultra-High Performance Strain-Hardening Cementitious Composite (UHP-SHCC / Super-ECC) combining high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 1.5 \sim 2.0\%$) with a dense silica fume-slag binder. The composite achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **155 MPa**, direct tensile strength of **13.5 MPa**, and tensile strain capacity of **4.2 %**.

## Key Contributions

- **Identification of 3 core challenges for PSH in ultra-high strength matrices**: (1) suppressing the surge in $K_m$ accompanying high compressive strength, (2) tailoring slip-hardening interfaces to prevent fiber rupture, and (3) controlling fiber dispersion rheology.
- **Formulation of simultaneous ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility mix design**: Optimized mineral particle packing (silica fume, micro-slag) to eliminate brittle compressive crushing and promote stable multiple microcracking.
- **Characterization of single PE fiber pullout mechanics**: Confirmed frictional shear stress $\tau_0 =$ 2.1 MPa ($\tau_0 = 2.1\ \text{MPa}$) with pronounced slip-hardening behavior.
- **Maximization of tensile energy absorption density**: Achieved tensile fracture energy $G_F > 450\ \text{kJ/m}^3$ ($450\ \text{kJ/m}^3$), establishing a materials foundation for blast- and seismic-resistant structural applications.

## Methods

- Matrix: Low-heat Portland cement + silica fume (15 %) + micro-slag (25 %), $W/B = 0.16$, micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 100\ \mu\text{m}$).
- Fibers: High-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 1.5, 2.0\%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa).
- Testing: Dog-bone direct tension tests (0.2 mm/min, 0.2 mm displacement rate), three-point bending fracture toughness tests, and 28 d compressive strength on 100 mm cubes.
