# Dynamic Strain Sensing with CNT Cement-Based Transducers

## Summary

A first-of-its-kind study that used MWCNT cement paste as an embedded dynamic strain transducer within structures. The electrical resistance response to sinusoidal and frequency-sweep loading was quantitatively evaluated in the vibration frequency range of large civil structures (0.1–5 Hz), securing a gauge factor GF ≈ 220.

## Key Contributions

1. Extension of self-sensing into the dynamic regime: unlike the existing literature, which centered on static and quasi-static loading, the response was validated through sinusoidal tests at 0.1–5 Hz plus bidirectional sweeps. PSD analysis confirmed the frequency match between input (strain) and output (electrical resistance).
2. Quantification of the gauge factor: average GF = (ΔR/R₀)/Δε of 220 — a value higher than in prior literature, sufficient for vibration monitoring. GF increases as frequency rises (weak nonlinearity).
3. Polarization treatment protocol: pre-conditioning for 1000 s at 30 V under zero load before measurement mitigates polarization effects. In dynamic applications, removing the mean and using only the relative change makes absolute values unnecessary.
4. Rationale for selecting MWCNTs: their many junctions give superior sensitivity compared with single-wall nanotubes. Dosage 2 % of cement by weight (32.4 kg/m3); two-step dispersion via ultrasonication at 225 W plus mechanical stirring at 1500 rpm for 15 min (confirmed by SEM).
5. Summary of the structural advantages as a sensor: embeddable (made of the same material as the structure), durable, and able to acquire displacement history directly where accelerometers cannot.

## Methods

40×40×160 mm paste prisms (w/c 0.4), two 1 mm copper-wire electrodes embedded at 40 mm spacing, 2-probe method (Keithley 6517B, 30 V), MTS hydraulic press under force control, two strain gauges.

## Results

- In the elastic uncracked state (1.25–2.5 MPa), resistance decreases under compression.
- The FRF is nearly flat from 0.1 to 3.5 Hz but shows a slight increasing trend (electrochemical origin).
- Estimated natural frequency f₀ ≈ 6.4 kHz (assuming E of 7 GPa) — ample margin against the working frequency range.
