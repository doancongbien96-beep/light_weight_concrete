# Accelerated Carbonation Behavior, C-S-H Decalcification Suppression, and Strength Retention Mechanisms of Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Mortar under Mechanochemical Basicity Control ($K_b = 1.0\sim 2.5$)

## Summary

A pioneering landmark AAS durability source paper by the research team of Professors Bang Yeon Lee and Jin-Kyu Song at Chonnam National University, which set out to fundamentally overcome the rapid carbonation penetration rate and the post-carbonation strength loss problem — the greatest durability vulnerability of cement-free alkali-activated blast-furnace slag (AAS) mortar, which can reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 80 % compared with ordinary Portland cement (OPC) — by incorporating calcium oxide ($\text{CaO}$) through a mechanochemical ball-milling process to **systematically control the basicity (Basicity coefficient: $K_b = \frac{\text{CaO}+\text{MgO}}{\text{SiO}_2+\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3} = 1.0\sim 2.5$)** of the slag base material, and then performing **carbonation depth (phenolphthalein spraying), 28-day compressive strength before and after carbonation, XRD, FT-IR, and TGA thermal analysis** under an accelerated carbonation chamber environment (temperature $20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, relative humidity $60\pm 5\%$, $\text{CO}_2$ concentration 5.0 %, 14-day exposure) to precisely identify the physicochemical transition behavior of microstructural hydration products (C-S-H gel, $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, calcite $\text{CaCO}_3$). The results showed that as the basicity of the raw slag increased within $1.0\sim 2.5$, the 14-day accelerated carbonation depth dropped drastically by 77.6 %, from **$12.5\ \text{mm} \rightarrow 2.8\ \text{mm}$**, and in particular the high-basicity mix (B2.5, $K_b = 2.05$) exhibited carbonation resistance superior to ordinary Portland cement (OPC control, 3.5 mm). Furthermore, whereas the unmodified plain slag ($K_b = 1.28$) suffered a sharp 23.0 % drop in compressive strength after carbonation due to decalcification of the C-S-H gel, **the B2.0 and B2.5 mixes with a basicity of $K_b \ge 1.7$ or higher instead increased their post-carbonation compressive strength by 5.6 % ($41.2 \rightarrow 43.5\ \text{MPa}$)**, establishing for the first time in the world a critical basicity criterion ($K_b \ge 1.7$) that completely overcomes structural strength loss caused by carbonation.

## Key Contributions

1. **77.6 % reduction in AAS carbonation depth through mechanochemical basicity control ($12.5 \rightarrow 2.8\ \text{mm}$)**:
   - Ball milling binds $\text{CaO}$ perfectly into the slag's inorganic lattice, dramatically increasing the supply of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ within the hydrating matrix and thereby achieving carbonation-blocking performance that surpasses OPC.
2. **First identification of the critical basicity ($K_b \ge 1.7$) that achieves 0.0 % post-carbonation strength loss**:
   - Blocks at the source the 23 % post-carbonation strength collapse of ordinary AAS and establishes the optimal basicity design region in which strength actually increases through the carbonation densification reaction.
3. **Demonstration of the C-S-H gel decalcification defense mechanism via $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ buffering**:
   - XRD/FT-IR reveal that the calcium hydroxide generated in high-basicity slag reacts preferentially with $\text{CO}_2$ to form a sacrificial carbonation buffer layer, completely blocking the destruction of the load-bearing C-S-H gel.
4. **Quantification of three-way microstructural transitions through XRD, FT-IR, and TGA thermogravimetric analysis**:
   - Precisely tracks the spectral changes at $3648\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ ($\text{O-H}$ stretching), $1420\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ ($\text{C-O}$ carbonate stretching), and $960\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ ($\text{Si-O-Si}$ depolymerization) to complete a quantitative carbonation reaction pathway.

## Methods

- **Material specifications and basicity control process (Tables 1~4)**:
  - Raw slag: normal water-quenched ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (Plain, specific surface area $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{CaO}$ 41.5 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 34.2 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 14.8 %, $\text{MgO}$ 6.5 %, reference basicity $K_b = 1.28$).
  - Basicity adjustment: powdered calcium oxide ($\text{CaO}$) was proportioned to target basicities ($K_b = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0$), blended with the slag, and subjected to 9 hours of mechanochemical mechanical–chemical synthesis in a planetary ball mill (specific surface area increased by 500 $\text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Alkali activator: aqueous sodium silicate solution ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}\cdot 3.1\text{SiO}_2$ mixed with $10\ \text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$; $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ equivalent 6.0 wt.% by mass of slag).
  - Aggregate: standard silica sand (S/B = 2.0, W/B = 0.50).
- **Experimental equipment and test methods (Figures 1~5)**:
  - Accelerated carbonation chamber: temperature $20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, relative humidity $60\pm 5\%$, $\text{CO}_2$ concentration 5.0 %, continuous 14-day exposure (top and bottom faces epoxy-coated to induce one-dimensional diffusion).
  - Carbonation depth: 1.0 % phenolphthalein indicator sprayed on the split fracture surface of $40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 160\ \text{mm}$ mortar prisms; the discoloration depth was measured with digital vernier calipers and averaged over 10 locations.
  - Compressive strength: KS L ISO 679 (non-carbonated vs. carbonated specimens compared at the identical 28-day age).
  - Microstructural analysis: Rigaku D/Max-2500 XRD ($2\theta = 5^\circ\sim 65^\circ$), PerkinElmer FT-IR ($4000\sim 400\ \text{cm}^{-1}$), TA Instruments TGA 5500 ($25\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $10\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$).

### 2. Interpretation of the chemical reactions and the anti-decalcification mechanism
- **C-S-H decalcification and silica gel collapse**: The Plain mix lacks a $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ buffer, so when $\text{CO}_2$ penetrates, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ rapidly leaches out of the C-S-H gel, which collapses into amorphous silica gel ($\text{SiO}_2\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O}$); matrix porosity increases and strength drops by 23 %.
- **Self-healing densification of high-basicity slag**: In the B2.5 mix, abundant $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ is liberated and precipitates high-density calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals inside the pores while preserving the C-S-H skeleton, so porosity decreases and the compressive strength instead increases by 5.6 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper by the Chonnam National University research team of Professors Bang Yeon Lee and Jin-Kyu Song is a landmark materials-chemistry source paper of lasting importance that completely overcame the carbonation-induced strength degradation — the greatest challenge facing cement-free alkali-activated slag — through mechanochemical basicity control, raising carbonation resistance to a level beyond that of OPC.
