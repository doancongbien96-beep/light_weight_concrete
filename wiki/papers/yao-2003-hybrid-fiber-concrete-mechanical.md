## Summary

This is the representative CCR source paper in which Professor Wu Yao's research team at Tongji University systematically elucidated the synergy effects (Synergy Effect) on compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, flexural modulus of rupture (MOR), and flexural toughness of concrete hybrid-blended in binary and ternary systems of steel fiber, polypropylene (PP) fiber, and carbon fiber at a low total fiber volume fraction (total $V_f \le 0.5 \%$). Through a multi-scale bridging mechanism in which micro carbon/PP fibers suppress micro-pore cracks while macro steel fibers take charge of bridging large cracks, it was demonstrated that flexural toughness increased **4.5-fold** and splitting tensile strength improved by **42 %** compared with single-fiber reinforcement.

## Key Contributions

- **Demonstration of the hybrid fiber synergy coefficient ($\alpha_{syn} > 1.0$) at the low volume fraction ($V_f = 0.5 \%$)**: splitting tensile strength improved by 42 % for the combination of steel fiber (0.35 %) + PP (0.15 %).
- **Expression of the highest mechanical performance in the ternary (Steel + Carbon + PP) hybrid composite**: demonstration of three-stage continuous bridging of fine, intermediate, and large cracks.
- **Presentation of an economical high-toughness structural concrete mix**: excellent flexural toughness secured with a small fiber amount without degradation of flowability.
- **Excellent workability and dispersibility**: feasibility of site ready-mixed concrete placement demonstrated by securing a slump of 150 mm or more.

## Methods

- Materials: steel fiber (length 30 mm, diameter 0.5 mm), carbon fiber (length 6 mm, diameter 7 µm), PP fiber (length 12 mm, diameter 20 µm).
- Concrete mix: OPC ($W/B = 0.42$), maximum coarse aggregate size 20 mm, sand ratio 40 %.
- Tests: ASTM C39 compressive strength, ASTM C496 splitting tensile strength, ASTM C1018 four-point bending test (span 450 mm).
