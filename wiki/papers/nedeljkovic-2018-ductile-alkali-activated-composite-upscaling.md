# Microstructure Design, Long-Term Flexural Strain Hardening, and Upscaling Demonstration of a Fly Ash–Slag (1:1) Hybrid Alkali-Activated Ductile Composite (PVA-AAC) into a 16 mm Ultra-Thin-Plate Structure

## Summary

This landmark 2018 original paper in the Journal of Cleaner Production by the research team of **Prof. Guang Ye and Dr. Marija Nedeljković of the Microlab at Delft University of Technology (TU Delft), the Netherlands** evaluated the lab-scale (3 L) and large-volume batch (45 L) scale-up behavior of a **ductile alkali-activated composite (Ductile AAC: Alkali-Activated Composite)**—an eco-friendly binder that fully excludes Portland cement by blending Class F fly ash (FA) and ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GBFS) at an optimal 1:1 ratio, reinforced with 2.0 vol.% PVA fibers—and completely demonstrated its 28-day compressive strength, 4-point flexural load–deflection characteristics over long-term ages of $1\sim 120$ days, drying shrinkage, thermochemical microstructure, and **the fabrication of a real 16 mm-thick ultra-thin-plate concrete structure (Canoe) together with one year of outdoor real-environment durability**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Optimization of setting time and strength balance via an FA–GBFS 1:1 hybrid binder**:
   - Resolving both the flash set of pure slag and the ambient-temperature hardening delay of pure fly ash, an initial setting time of at least 30 minutes was secured while achieving a 28-day compressive strength of $42.5\ \text{MPa}$.
2. **Demonstration of flexural strain hardening and multiple fine-cracking behavior across long-term ages (120 days)**:
   - The formation of a composite nano-gel structure in which C-A-S-H gel and N-A-S-H gel interpenetrate stably preserved a flexural strength of $12.5\ \text{MPa}$ and large deflection ductility even at the long-term age of 120 days.
3. **Successful upscaling from lab scale (3 L) to large batches (45 L) and to 16 mm thin-plate structures**:
   - The fiber dispersion non-uniformity problem accompanying batch enlargement was solved through mixing-sequence optimization, reproducing the same flexural toughness as the 3 L lab specimens even in the 45 L batch.
4. **Verification of flawless durability of a real 16 mm ultra-thin-plate structure over one year of outdoor exposure**:
   - Demonstrating complete structural reliability with not a single detrimental drying-shrinkage crack during one year of four-season climate exposure after casting the full-scale canoe structure.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design of the composite (Section 3 & Tables 1–4)**:
  - Binder: low-calcium fly ash (FA) 50 wt.% + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GBFS) 50 wt.%.
  - Alkaline activator: liquid sodium silicate + $\text{NaOH}$ aqueous solution ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 5.0\ \text{wt.}\%$, modulus $M_s = 1.0$).
  - Aggregate: fine quartz sand (maximum particle size 0.8 mm, S/B = 0.36).
  - Fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 8\ \text{mm}$, 1.2% oil coating).
  - Batch scale: small 3 L batches and scale-up 45 L batches.
- **Test and demonstration program (Sections 3.2–5.1 & Figs. 1–6)**:
  - 4-point bending test: $40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prismatic specimens (span 100 mm; ages 1, 3, 7, 28, and 120 days).
  - Compressive strength: $40\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens.
  - Drying shrinkage: measured with non-contact displacement sensors.
  - Full-scale structural fabrication: fabrication of an integrated thin-plate canoe 5.0 m long and 16 mm thick, followed by long-term outdoor exposure.

### 2. Interpretation of the composite gel structure and long-term flexural toughness mechanisms
- **Interpenetration between C-A-S-H and N-A-S-H gels**: $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ leached from the slag reacts with sodium silicate to form C-A-S-H gel early, granting initial strength, while the fly ash slowly forms the N-A-S-H gel network, mitigating long-term shrinkage and keeping the fiber interfacial bond stable.
- **Flowability retention under scale-up**: mixing speed and feeding order were strictly controlled so that the hydrophilic surface of the PVA fibers maintains dispersion stability in the alkaline solution, exhibiting perfect bridging performance without fiber clumping even in the 45 L large-volume batch.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-level landmark original paper in construction materials engineering, in which the TU Delft team designed an excellent ductile composite based on an eco-friendly cement-free FA–GBFS composite with a 28-day compressive strength of 42.5 MPa and flexural strength of 12.5 MPa, and scaled laboratory research up to the industrial application stage through the 45 L batch and fabrication of a 16 mm ultra-thin-plate full-scale structure.
