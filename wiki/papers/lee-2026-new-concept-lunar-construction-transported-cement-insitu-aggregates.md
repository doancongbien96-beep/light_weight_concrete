# Hybrid Lunar Base Construction Paradigm Fusing Earth-Transported Special Cement with In-Situ Crushed Lunar Aggregates (Crushed Lunar Aggregates): $85\%$ Reduction in Transported Mass, $65.0\ \text{MPa}$ Compressive Strength, and 1.5m Shielding Module Structure Design

## Summary

To overcome the technical limitations of the fully local resource utilization approach (100% ISRU lunar regolith sintering/melting), which demands enormous local energy consumption and complex chemical smelting facilities and therefore takes a long time to reach practicality, and to realize early lunar base construction, this study **proposes a hybrid lunar construction concept that transports only a minimal quantity of high-performance cement clinker and nano superplasticizer from Earth ($15\ \text{wt.}\%$), combines them with lunar basalt/anorthosite aggregates crushed and graded locally on the lunar surface ($85\ \text{wt.}\%$) and water extracted from permanently shadowed region (PSR) ice, and produces and assembles high-strength precast concrete members (compressive strength $65.0\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $34.0\ \text{GPa}$) inside pressurized chambers on the lunar surface**, and precisely evaluates hydration reaction kinetics under the lunar environment (vacuum, $1/6\text{g}$ gravity, cryogenic/extreme-heat cycles), structural analysis of a 1.5 m thick radiation and micrometeorite shielding dome, and launch vehicle payload economics of the transported mass, thereby demonstrating **an $85\%$ drastic reduction in launch transport mass compared to the full Earth-transport approach, a 3× acceleration of base completion time compared to 100% ISRU, interception of annual space radiation exposure by $99.8\%$ ($<5\ \text{mSv/year}$), and the feasibility of permanent crewed lunar base infrastructure with a 200-year design life** — the 2026 latest, most authoritative landmark original paper in the space civil engineering and architecture field by the research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author) and Prof. Jeong-Il Choi of Chonnam National University, Prof. Su-Tae Kang of Daegu University, and Prof. Jin-Keun Kim of KAIST**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first establishment of the Hybrid Lunar Construction framework**:
   - Establishes an optimal trade-off model of Earth-transported binder ($15\%$) + local aggregates ($85\%$) that simultaneously overcomes both the astronomical cost of 100% Earth transport and the technical difficulties of 100% local inorganic smelting.
2. **High-strength ($65\ \text{MPa}$) mix design based on locally crushed lunar aggregates (Crushed Lunar Aggregates)**:
   - Based on Apollo/Artemis lunar regolith chemical composition data, optimizes the particle size distribution of crushed basalt and anorthosite aggregates to complete a densely packed matrix.
3. **Establishment of a precast curing process (Precast Curing) inside pressurized modules on the lunar surface**:
   - Presents a safe construction method in which steam curing is performed inside the pressurized environment of the lander before assembly into an outer-shell dome, preventing the instantaneous evaporation of free water in the Moon's high-vacuum environment.
4. **Demonstration of the space radiation/micrometeoroid shielding completeness of a 1.5 m self-standing interlocking bolted dome**:
   - Designs a bolted-joint structure that exploits the self-weight compression force of the arch ribs under $1/6\text{g}$ gravity conditions, securing crack-free safety even under strong seismic events and thermal expansion.

## Methods

- **Materials and lunar concrete mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Binder: Earth-transported ultra rapid-hardening aluminosilicate cement + silica fume ($15.0\ \text{wt.}\%$ of total batch weight, W/C = 0.32).
  - Aggregates: locally excavated crushed lunar basalt (Basalt) and anorthosite (Anorthosite) coarse and fine aggregates ($85.0\ \text{wt.}\%$).
  - Mixing water: purified water extracted from polar permanently shadowed crater (PSR) ice.
- **Lunar-environment computational analysis and structural design (Sections 3~5 & Figs. 5~10)**:
  - Gravity condition: lunar gravity $g_{moon} = 1.62\ \text{m/s}^2$ ($1/6\text{g}$).
  - Radiation analysis: GEANT4 Monte Carlo simulation (calculation of GCR and SPE particle shielding depths).
  - Structural analysis: 3D nonlinear FEM (double-curvature precast interlocking dome, 25 m diameter and 1.5 m thickness).

### 2. Precast interlocking dome structure and radiation shielding physics analysis
- **Arch compression line under $1/6\text{g}$ gravity**: In the Moon's low-gravity environment, the self-weight stress of the structure falls to $1/6$ of Earth's, so the interlocking blocks maintain perfect self-support within their allowable compressive stress even while carrying the massive 1.5 m thick shielding mass.
- **$100\%$ protection against radiation and micrometeoroids**: The 1.5 m thickness of the basalt-aggregate concrete confines micrometeoroid penetration at speeds of $20\ \text{km/s}$ to within 0.3 m and completely annihilates solar proton events (SPE).

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the highest-level landmark original paper in future space civil engineering and extreme planetary infrastructure engineering, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University presented the world's first hybrid lunar construction concept combining Earth-transported special cement with locally crushed lunar aggregates, reducing launch mass by 85% and demonstrating the construction of a shielded dome base with 65.0 MPa compressive strength and a 1.5m shielding dome.
