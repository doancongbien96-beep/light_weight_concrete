## Summary
A bio-inspired cementitious composite study that mimicked the hierarchical brick-and-mortar microstructure of natural nacre (mother-of-pearl) to deflect the crack propagation path of brittle cement composites and maximize toughness and impact energy absorption capacity. A hierarchical structure was fabricated by alternately stacking high-strength brittle cement mortar plate tiles (bricks) and ductile PVA-ECC interlayer strips (mortar layer), and impact and flexural fracture tests were performed. It was demonstrated that cracks do not propagate straight but branch and deflect into the layers through interlayer sliding and shear debonding, increasing the energy absorption capacity by more than 15 times compared with a single brittle material.

## Key Contributions
- **Design of a nacre-architecture-based cementitious multilayer hierarchical composite section**: implementation of a crack-deflection mechanism through alternating stacking of high-strength segments and highly ductile bridging interfaces.
- **Dramatic enhancement of energy dissipation mechanisms**: a 15-fold increase in flexural fracture energy through interlayer shear sliding and tensile plastic yielding of the ECC layer.
- **Demonstration of drop-weight impact and penetration resistance**: under high-speed impact, rear-face cone penetration fracture was suppressed and impact energy was dissipated through interlayer delamination friction.

## Methods
- **Material composition**: high-strength mortar layers (thickness 5 mm, $f_c = 85 MPa$) and PVA-ECC layers (thickness 2 mm, tensile strain capacity $4.5 \%$).
- **Test items**: three-point bending test, drop-weight impact test, and high-speed camera filming of crack propagation.

## Results
- Single high-strength mortar: brittle fracture immediately upon crack formation, fracture energy $0.8 kJ/m2$.
- Bio-inspired 4-layer laminated composite: multi-stage crack deflection yielded fracture energy of $12.5 kJ/m2$ (a 15.6-fold improvement) and secured high residual load-bearing capacity.
