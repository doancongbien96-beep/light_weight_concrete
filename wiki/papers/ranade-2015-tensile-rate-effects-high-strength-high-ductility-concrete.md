# Tensile Strain-Rate Dependence of High-Strength High-Ductility Concrete (HSHDC) ($\dot{\varepsilon} = 10^{-5}\sim 10^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-1}$): Amplification of Tensile Strength to $21.2\ \text{MPa}$ (DIF = 1.46) and the Mechanism Preserving Dynamic Strain-Hardening

## Summary

To elucidate micromechanically the dynamic tensile behavior of the 100 MPa-class high-strength high-ductility concrete (HSHDC / HS-ECC: 28-day compressive strength $165\ \text{MPa}$, PE fiber $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$) applied to military protective, impact-resistant, and seismic structures, the joint research team of **Prof. Victor C. Li (University of Michigan), Prof. Ravi Ranade (University at Buffalo), and Dr. William F. Heard (US Army Engineer Research and Development Center, ERDC)** systematically carried out uniaxial direct tensile tests, high-rate single-PE-fiber pullout tests ($v = 9\ \mu\text{m/s}\sim 0.9\ \text{m/s}$), and matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) rate-dependence tests across **a strain-rate range spanning four orders of magnitude from quasi-static rates ($10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1}$) up to high-speed impact strain rates ($10^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-1}$)**, thereby identifying a dynamic micromechanics mechanism in which **the tensile strength is amplified to $14.5 \rightarrow 21.2\ \text{MPa}$ (DIF = 1.46) under dynamic loading while saturated multiple fine-cracking strain-hardening performance at or beyond an ultimate tensile strain of $3.2\%$ remains stably preserved**, published as the 2015 landmark original paper in Cement and Concrete Research (CCR).

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration that HSHDC built on an ultra-high-strength matrix (165 MPa) secures dynamic tensile strength and ductility simultaneously**:
   - Unlike ordinary concrete and UHPC, which show severe brittle fracture under high-rate loading, HSHDC preserves a vast energy absorption capacity combining dynamic tensile strength $21.2\ \text{MPa}$ and tensile strain $3.2\%$.
2. **First quantification of the rate dependence ($\tau_0$) of the interfacial friction between single PE fiber and the ultra-high-strength matrix**:
   - Demonstrated that as the pullout velocity rises by five orders of magnitude from $9\ \mu\text{m/s} \rightarrow 0.9\ \text{m/s}$, the interfacial frictional shear strength increases to $\tau_0 = 1.85 \rightarrow 2.75\ \text{MPa}$ ($+48.6\%$).
3. **Verification that the micromechanics PSH strain-hardening criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1.0$) is satisfied under dynamic conditions**:
   - Proved mathematically that the rate-driven increase of the fiber bridging complementary energy $J_b'$ ($+45\%$) stays larger than that of the matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ ($+28\%$), so the multiple fine-cracking mechanism is retained even under dynamic impact conditions.
4. **Establishment of dynamic increase factor (Dynamic Increase Factor, DIF) modeling**:
   - Derived strain-rate-dependent constitutive relations for the first-cracking strength ($\text{DIF}_{fc} = 1.58$) and the ultimate tensile strength ($\text{DIF}_{tu} = 1.46$).

## Methods

- **Materials and HSHDC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Matrix: OPC Type I + silica fume (SF/C = 0.30) + micro silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/C = 0.80, W/B = 0.22, high-range water reducer).
  - Fiber: Spectra 1000 ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2600\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 73\ \text{GPa}$).
  - 28-day compressive strength: $165.0 \pm 4.5\ \text{MPa}$ (steam curing).
- **High-rate testing system and instrumentation (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Servo-hydraulic high-speed tensile testing machine (Instron 10 kN, fitted with a lost-motion assembly).
  - Six strain-rate levels: $\dot{\varepsilon} = 10^{-5}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 5\times 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-1}$.
  - Instrumentation: piezoelectric load cell and laser displacement gauge ($10^5\ \text{Hz}$ ultra-high-speed sampling).
  - Six single-fiber pullout velocities: $v = 9\times 10^{-6}\sim 0.9\ \text{m/s}$.

### 2. Interpretation of interfacial thermo-viscoelasticity and inertial bridging mechanisms under high-rate loading
- **Amplification of viscous shear stress at the PE fiber pullout interface**: during high-rate pullout, localized viscous resistance and asperity-interlocking resistance rise at the frictional interface between the PE fiber surface and the ultra-high-density C-S-H matrix, increasing $\tau_0$ by $48.6\%$.
- **Supremacy of the dynamic bridging energy $J_b'$**: although the matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ also rises with rate, the accompanying increases of fiber strength and interfacial friction maintain an ample energy margin of $J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 2.2$, completely preventing brittle single-crack fracture even under high-rate tensile loading.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the most authoritative landmark original publication on extreme structural design against blast and impact, in which Prof. Victor C. Li and Prof. Ravi Ranade precisely established the tensile rate dependence of 165 MPa-class ultra-high-strength HSHDC up to $10^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ and simultaneously demonstrated a dynamic tensile strength of 21.2 MPa along with high ductility of 3.2 %.
