# Autogenous Healing of Cracks in Cementitious Materials with Mineral Admixtures

## Summary

This study quantitatively evaluates autogenous healing efficiency and watertightness recovery as a function of crack width ($w = 50 \sim 300\ \mu\text{m}$, $w = 50 \sim 300 \mu m$) in cement mortar and composite systems incorporating mineral admixtures, including ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS), fly ash (FA), and silica fume (SF). Pre-cracked specimens were cured for 28 d under water immersion and wet-dry cyclic regimes, evaluated via constant-head water permeability testing, optical microscopy crack closure measurements, and SEM-EDS/XRD crystallographic characterization. Delayed hydration of unreacted mineral admixture grains and calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$, $CaCO_3$) precipitation drove complete sealing of microcracks narrower than $100\ \mu\text{m}$ ($100 \mu m$).

## Key Contributions

- **Differentiation of autogenous healing mechanisms across mineral admixtures**:
  - GGBS mixtures: Latent hydraulic reactivity produced dense C-S-H gels that filled crack interiors.
  - FA mixtures: Pozzolanic reaction coupled with dissolved $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ($Ca^{2+}$) and carbonates precipitated calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$, $CaCO_3$) crystals.
- **Quantification of critical autogenous healing crack width**: Under continuous water curing, cracks with initial width $w \le 100\ \mu\text{m}$ ($w \le 100 \mu m$) achieved over 95 % hydraulic conductivity recovery within 14 d.
- **Healing evaluation under wet-dry cycles**: Confirmed that wet-dry cycling (12 h immersion + 12 h air exposure, $12 h$ immersion + $12 h$ air) accelerated surface crystal growth relative to full submersion by increasing atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ ($CO_2$) supply.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions**:
  - Cementitious mortar: OPC base, GGBS replacement at 30 % and 50 %, FA replacement at 20 % and 30 %, SF replacement at 5 %.
  - Water-to-binder ratio $W/B = 0.45$, sand-to-binder ratio $S/B = 2.0$.
- **Crack induction**: Through-cracks ($50 \sim 250\ \mu\text{m}$, $50 \sim 250 \mu m$) induced via splitting tensile loading.
- **Evaluation protocols**:
  - Constant-head water permeability tests tracking flow rate and hydraulic conductivity ($k$) decay over time.
  - Image analysis of optical micrographs to quantify surface crack closure area fractions.

## Results

- **Water flow reduction and closure rates**:
  - Crack width $80\ \mu\text{m}$ ($80 \mu m$) in 50 % GGBS mixture: Water flow decreased by $99.2\%$ after 28 d water curing, restoring uncracked watertightness.
  - Crack widths $\ge 150\ \mu\text{m}$ ($150 \mu m$): Exhibited surface crystallization without complete internal sealing, demonstrating the necessity of intrinsic crack width control via materials like ECC.
