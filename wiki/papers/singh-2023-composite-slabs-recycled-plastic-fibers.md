## Summary

This study investigates recycled plastic fibers (RPF, length 30 mm, volume fraction 0.5–1.5 vol %) processed from industrial plastic waste (recycled polypropylene/PET), incorporated into the concrete topping and integrated with profiled steel decking to form composite steel-deck slabs ($1000 \times 2500 \times 120 mm$). Using four-point bending tests and the m-k method, it establishes their longitudinal shear bond behavior, load-deflection response, end slip, and ultimate flexural capacity. It demonstrates that recycled plastic fibers delay fine shear-crack propagation at the deck-concrete interface, raising the longitudinal shear bond strength by **28 %** and inducing ductile flexural failure.

## Key Contributions

- **Elucidation of the shear bond behavior of composite deck slabs incorporating recycled plastic fibers**: fiber bridging suppresses brittle shear slip at the deck embossing interlocks.
- **Derivation of Eurocode 4 standard m-k design parameters**: securing $m = 185 N/mm^2, k = 0.082 N/mm^2$ raises the shear bond capacity by 30 %.
- **Complete prevention of drying-shrinkage cracking**: controlling drying-shrinkage crack widths on the slab top surface to $0.05 mm$ or less demonstrates the possibility of omitting secondary welded mesh reinforcement.
- **Plastic-waste resource circulation and slab self-weight reduction**: realizing an environmentally friendly building floor structural system.

## Methods

- Six full-scale composite slabs ($1000 \times 2500 \times 120 mm$; deck plate thickness 0.9 mm, galvanized steel sheet).
- Concrete: $f_c = 32.5 MPa$; RPF ($V_f = 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 \%$, diameter 0.5 mm, tensile strength 450 MPa).
- Tests: symmetric four-point bending (span 2200 mm), with central deflection LVDT and end-interface slip transducer instrumentation.
