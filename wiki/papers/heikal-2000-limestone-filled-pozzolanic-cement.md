# Effects of Limestone Powder Replacement on Carboaluminate Formation, Hydration Kinetics, and Compressive Strength of Calcined Clay (Homra) Pozzolanic Cement

## Summary

To reduce $\text{CO}_2$ emissions in cement manufacturing and optimize the reactivity of calcined clay waste from crushed brick manufacturing (Homra, a pozzolanic material), Professor M. Heikal and co-workers at Zagazig University investigated **limestone-filled pozzolanic cement pastes** consisting of ordinary Portland cement (OPC 80 wt.%) and Homra (20 wt.%) where Homra was systematically replaced with fine limestone powder ($\text{CaCO}_3$, fineness $3100\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$) at 0 %, 5 %, 10 %, 15 %, and 20 wt.%. The authors evaluated combined water content, free lime, early electrical conductivity, DSC thermal analysis, bulk density, total porosity, and compressive strength from 3 d to 90 d of hydration. Limestone particles acted as physical micro-fillers and provided heterogeneous nucleation sites accelerating early $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ crystallization. In particular, the 1:1 blend of 10 wt.% limestone and 10 wt.% Homra (**Mix I.10**) formed abundant calcium monocarboaluminate ($\text{C}_3\text{A}\cdot\text{CaCO}_3\cdot 11\text{H}_2\text{O}$) via reaction between aluminate phases and carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$), preventing ettringite conversion and achieving a 90 d compressive strength of **58.5 MPa (+18.5 % over unblended pozzolanic cement)** with a minimum total porosity of **18.2 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of limestone-calcined clay synergy and carboaluminate ($\text{C}_3\text{A}\cdot\text{CaCO}_3\cdot 11\text{H}_2\text{O}$) stabilization**:
   - Carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) substituted sulfate in monosulfate ($\text{AFm}$) to form stable monocarboaluminate, preserving ettringite ($\text{AFt}$) and enhancing solid volume expansion to densify the capillary pore structure.
2. **Dual physical (nucleation/filler) and chemical (carbonate reaction) action of limestone**:
   - Accelerated portlandite ($\text{CH}$) and C-S-H nucleation on fine calcite surfaces, advancing the peaks of hydration heat and electrical conductivity.
3. **Identification of optimal 10 wt.% limestone + 10 wt.% Homra replacement ratio**:
   - Synergistic interaction between carbonate reaction from limestone and pozzolanic silica reaction from Homra produced maximum 90 d compressive strength (**58.5 MPa**) and minimum total porosity (**18.2 %**).
4. **Circular economy utilizing 100 % industrial by-products**:
   - Formulated sustainable binder systems reducing OPC clinker by 20 % using brick waste Homra and low-grade limestone without sacrificing long-term mechanical strength.

## Methods

- **Raw material properties (Tables 1–2)**:
  - OPC: $\text{CaO}$ 63.8 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 20.8 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 5.8 %, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ 3.2 %, fineness $3250\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$.
  - Homra (calcined clay brick waste): $\text{SiO}_2$ 58.5 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 15.2 %, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ 9.5 %, $\text{CaO}$ 4.5 %, specific surface area $3100\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$.
  - Limestone: $\text{CaCO}_3$ 97.2 % ($\text{CaO}$ 54.5 %, $\text{CO}_2$ 42.7 %), specific surface area $3100\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$.
- **Mixture proportions (5 mixes)**:
  - I.0: OPC 80 % + Homra 20 % + Limestone 0 % (Control).
  - I.5: OPC 80 % + Homra 15 % + Limestone 5 %.
  - I.10: OPC 80 % + Homra 10 % + Limestone 10 % (Optimal).
  - I.15: OPC 80 % + Homra 5 % + Limestone 15 %.
  - I.20: OPC 80 % + Homra 0 % + Limestone 20 %.
- **Testing procedures**:
  - Mixed at standard consistency (W/B = 0.25~0.27).
  - Setting times: Vicat needle apparatus (ASTM C191).
  - Compressive strength: 1 in (25.4 mm) cubes cured in water (3, 7, 28, 90 d), loading rate 0.5 kN/s.
  - Hydration kinetics: Free lime ($\text{CH}$) via Franke extraction, combined water via loss on ignition at 950 °C, electrical conductivity cell (1 kHz), and DSC at 10 °C/min.

### 2. Hydration reaction mechanisms and microstructure
- **Pozzolan-carbonate synergy**: Homra provides reactive $\text{SiO}_2$ and $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ forming additional C-S-H and C-A-H, while limestone supplies $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ stabilizing aluminates into voluminous monocarboaluminate, mutually refining capillary porosity.
- **Limits of excess limestone**: When limestone exceeds 15 wt.%, insufficient reactive pozzolanic silica is available, degrading long-term strength and validating the 1:1 (10:10) ratio as optimal.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that combining calcined clay (Homra) with fine limestone powder promotes monocarboaluminate formation and matrix densification, establishing the chemical foundation for modern $\text{LC}^3$ (Limestone Calcined Clay Cement) systems.
