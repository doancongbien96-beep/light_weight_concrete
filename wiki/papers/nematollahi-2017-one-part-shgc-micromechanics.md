# Micromechanics-Based Investigation of an Ambient Temperature Cured One-Part Strain Hardening Geopolymer Composite

## Summary

Study by the Nematollahi group at Swinburne University that overcomes the two major barriers to commercializing geopolymer SHCC (SHGC): user-unfriendly liquid activators and heat curing. Using a 'dry mix' one-part formulation with a solid activator (anhydrous sodium metasilicate powder), it achieved a tensile strength of 4.6 MPa and a strain capacity of 4.2 % by ambient temperature curing alone, without heat curing, and verified through a micromechanics model that the PSH performance index satisfies the strength and energy criteria. Compared with the representative SHCC M45: 76 % lower CO₂ and 36 % lower energy.

## Key Contributions

- Development of an ambient temperature cured one-part SHGC: fly ash 50 % + slag 50 % (precursor) + anhydrous sodium metasilicate powder 8 % (solid activator) + W/P 0.35. Removing the liquid alkali handling problem and the 60 °C heat curing secures field applicability.
- Verification of applying the PSH performance index framework to geopolymers: computed the Kanda-Li indices (strength σ₀/σ_fc > 1.3, energy J′b/Jtip > 2.7) and confirmed that every mix satisfies the saturated multiple cracking condition. Ambient curing yielded PSH energy indices 14~20 % higher than heat curing, an advantage in strain capacity.
- Slag type effect: ordinary slag containing 2 % gypsum (T) showed PSH indices 15~30 % higher than gypsum-free slag (GF) and 14~38 % superior strain capacity — establishing a positive correlation between matrix fracture toughness (Km) and frictional bond (τ0).
- Matrix fracture toughness–interfacial frictional bond correlation law: higher Km → rougher fracture surface → larger fiber-matrix contact area → increased τ0 (lowest 1.14 MPa to highest 2.14 MPa). Proposes a new empirical law for predicting interfacial properties.
- Quantified environmental performance (MSI): CO₂ 0.15 kg/kg (SHCC M45 0.63), embodied energy 3.85 MJ/kg (M45 5.99) — the cumulative effect of complete cement elimination + 78 % activator savings + removal of heat curing.

## Methods

- Materials: Class F fly ash from the Gladstone power station (mean particle size 8.66 µm), Australian ordinary slag (2 % gypsum)/gypsum-free slag, anhydrous sodium metasilicate (Na₂O 51 % + SiO₂ 46 %), PVA fibers (RECS15, length 8 mm, diameter 40 µm, oil coating 1.2 %) at 2 vol %. No fine silica sand used (the amorphous fraction serves as the aggregate).
- Two curing regimes: heat curing (60 °C for 24 h, then room temperature, tested at 3 days) vs ambient curing (demolded after 23±3 °C for 24 h, then 27 days in water, tested at 28 days).
- Tests: compression per ASTM C109 (50 mm cubes), direct tension (400×75×10 mm, LVDT gauge 80 mm, 0.25 mm/min), three-point bending of notched beams (Em and Km obtained via ECM), single-fiber pullout (Gd, τ0, slip-hardening β).

## Results

- Density 1800~1874 kg/m³ (10~11 % lower than SHCC M45's 2077, meeting lightweight concrete criteria), compressive strength 43.3~52.5 MPa.
- Tension: ultimate strength 4.3~4.6 MPa, strain capacity 2.6~4.2 %. Ambient curing gave +15~24 % first-crack strength and +17~42 % strain capacity. Multiple crack spacing uniform at 2~3 mm, with tightly controlled crack widths.
- Matrix properties: Km 0.237~0.316 MPa√m, Jtip 13.1~18.8 J/m². Heat curing and gypsum-free slag were brittleness-increasing factors.
- Interfacial properties: Gd 0.46~1.03 J/m², τ0 1.14~2.14 MPa, slip-hardening β 0.021~0.049.
