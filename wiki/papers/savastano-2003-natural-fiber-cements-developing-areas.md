# Natural Plant Fiber (Sisal/Banana) and Clinker-Free Slag Binder-Based Thin Fiber Cement Panels: Flexural Strength of $18.5\ \text{MPa}$ and the Long-Term Fiber Mineralization Suppression Mechanism

## Summary

To overcome the hazards of asbestos and supply sustainable building materials for low-cost housing construction in developing countries, this Cement and Concrete Composites 2003 landmark origin paper by the research team of **Prof. Holmer Savastano Jr. of the University of São Paulo, Brazil and Dr. R. S. P. Coutts of CSIRO, Australia** combined **natural plant fibers (sisal, banana, and eucalyptus pulp at $8\sim 12\ \text{wt.}\%$) with a clinker-free alkali-activated slag binder (GGBS + gypsum + hydrated lime)**, manufactured $6\ \text{mm}$ ultra-thin fiber cement panels through the industrial Hatschek wet dewatering forming process, and precisely evaluated the three-point flexural modulus of rupture (MOR), fracture toughness ($G_F$), elastic modulus, and the **aging behavior under one year of accelerated immersion-drying cycles and natural outdoor exposure**, achieving an initial flexural strength of $18.5\ \text{MPa}$ and fracture toughness of $1.65\ \text{kJ/m}^2$ in the sisal fiber-slag panels and, thanks to the slag matrix characteristics lacking free $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, **completely blocking the mineralization embrittlement of natural fibers**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First successful application of agricultural by-product natural fibers (sisal/banana) to the Hatschek process**:
   - Replacing costly wood pulp and asbestos, sisal and banana long fibers, abundantly produced in developing countries, were perfectly dispersed and layered in the slurry dewatering process.
2. **Source-level prevention of the long-term mineralization (petrification) of natural fibers via a clinker-free slag binder**:
   - The penetration of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ crystals into natural fiber interiors and the alkaline hydrolysis embrittlement arising in ordinary OPC matrices were excluded through the pozzolanic slag matrix, preserving more than $85\%$ of toughness even after one year of aging.
3. **Achieved a flexural modulus of rupture of $18.5\ \text{MPa}$ and fracture toughness of $1.65\ \text{kJ/m}^2$**:
   - At a fiber content of $10\ \text{wt.}\%$, comfortably exceeded the required strength of the international standard (ISO 8336) for roofing and exterior siding materials.
4. **Established a sustainable composite paradigm for low-cost eco-friendly housing construction**:
   - Realized a fully circular-economy construction material by combining near-zero-carbon industrial by-product slag with renewable agricultural fibers.

## Methods

- **Materials and fiber cement mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, Blaine $500\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$, 70 wt.%) + flue-gas desulfurization gypsum (Gypsum 20 wt.%) + hydrated lime (Hydrated Lime 10 wt.%).
  - Three natural fiber types ($8\sim 12\ \text{wt.}\%$):
    1. `Sisal fiber`: average length $L_f = 10\sim 25\ \text{mm}$, diameter $d_f = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength $\sigma_f = 450\ \text{MPa}$.
    2. `Banana fiber`: pseudostem-derived fiber ($L_f = 15\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 350\ \text{MPa}$).
    3. `Eucalyptus kraft pulp`: refined wood pulp ($L_f = 1.0\ \text{mm}$, auxiliary dispersing reinforcement for the matrix).
- **Forming and accelerated aging tests (Sections 3~4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Forming: laboratory-scale Hatschek simulator (slurry suction filtration $\rightarrow 3.2\ \text{MPa}$ compression molding $\rightarrow 6\ \text{mm}$ flat-panel production).
  - Curing: after 28-day standard moist curing, (1) 50 wet-dry accelerated aging cycles, (2) one year of natural outdoor atmospheric exposure.
  - Three-point bending test: $160 \times 40 \times 6\ \text{mm}$ specimens (span 100 mm, displacement control at 0.5 mm/min).

### 2. Interpretation of the natural fiber degradation mechanism and the protective effect of the slag matrix
- **Calcium hydroxide crystallization attack inside the OPC matrix**: the highly alkaline saturated $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ solution of OPC penetrated the cellulose lumen of the natural fibers and crystallized as calcite, entirely robbing the fibers of their flexibility and degrading fracture toughness by more than $70\%$.
- **Absence of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ in the slag matrix**: the activated slag system generates almost no free $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (it is fully immobilized into C-A-S-H gel), suppressing in-fiber mineralization to $0.0\%$ even after one year and permanently preserving high toughness.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark origin paper in the field of eco-friendly construction fiber cement panels, fusing natural plant fibers with a slag-based clinker-free binder to achieve a flexural strength of 18.5 MPa and overcoming at the source the chronic mineralization embrittlement of natural fibers.
