# Chemical Composition, Drawing Process, Interfacial Silane Sizing, Alkali Resistance, and Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) Mechanisms of Glass Fibers (E, AR, and S-Glass): A Review

## Summary

To establish the materials science foundation and engineering applications of silicate glass fibers in high-performance composites (FRC, GFRP, cementitious matrices), Professor Frank R. Jones at the University of Sheffield and Dr. Norman T. Huff at Owens Corning authored this comprehensive landmark treatise. The chapter covers atomic structures (disordered 3D silica network), melt-drawing spinning processes, mechanical properties (tensile strengths 2.0~4.8 GPa, elastic moduli 70~90 GPa), interfacial sizing and organosilane coupling chemistry, moisture-induced siloxane bond ($\text{Si-O-Si}$) hydrolysis and stress corrosion cracking (SCC), and $\text{ZrO}_2$ passivation layer protection mechanisms in alkaline cementitious environments across commercial glass families (E-glass, high-strength S-glass, and alkali-resistant AR-glass). The authors elucidate the nano-mechanics of aminosilane ($\gamma\text{-APS}$) sizing agents applied to fiber surfaces within microseconds of drawing, providing lifetime prediction models for glass-fiber-reinforced composites under corrosive service conditions.

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive composition-property mapping across global glass fiber families (E, E-CR, S-2, AR, R, C, D)**:
   - Established structural databases correlating network formers ($\text{SiO}_2$) and modifiers ($\text{CaO}, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{MgO}, \text{B}_2\text{O}_3, \text{ZrO}_2$) with tensile strength, modulus, and chemical resistance.
2. **Alkali-resistance mechanism of high-$\text{ZrO}_2$ (>16 %) AR-glass in cementitious matrices**:
   - Demonstrated that a protective zirconium hydroxide/zirconia passive film ($\text{Zr-O-Zr}$) prevents hydroxyl ion ($\text{OH}^-$) attack on the silica network in high-alkali ($\text{pH} > 13$) environments.
3. **Three-step interfacial coupling mechanism of organosilanes ($\gamma\text{-APS}$)**:
   - Formulated the sequential steps: alkoxysilane hydrolysis ($\text{Si-OH}$) $\rightarrow$ hydrogen bonding with glass surfaces $\rightarrow$ thermal condensation into covalent $\text{Si-O-Si}$ linkages with amino groups crosslinking into the polymer/cement matrix.
4. **Moisture-assisted stress corrosion cracking (SCC) and Charles lifetime models**:
   - Modeled subcritical crack growth rates where water molecules hydrolyze stressed siloxane bonds at crack tips ($\text{Si-O-Si} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{Si-OH}$).

## Methods

- **Glass compositions and fiber spinning (Sections 19.1–19.3 & Table 19.1)**:
  - Melt spinning: Platinum-rhodium (Pt-Rh) bushing nozzles at $1500\sim 1600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (drawing speed $10\sim 50\ \text{m/s}$, fiber diameter $10\sim 25\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Formulations:
    - E-glass: $\text{SiO}_2$ 54 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 14 %, $\text{CaO}$ 22 %, $\text{B}_2\text{O}_3$ 8 % (general reinforcement).
    - AR-glass (Alkali-Resistant): $\text{SiO}_2$ 61 %, $\text{ZrO}_2$ 16.5 %, $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ 15 %, $\text{CaO}$ 5 % (cement composites).
    - S-2 glass: $\text{SiO}_2$ 65 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 25 %, $\text{MgO}$ 10 % (high-strength aerospace and armor).
- **Interfacial sizing chemistry (Section 19.5)**:
  - Sizing formulations: Organosilane coupling agent ($\gamma\text{-aminopropyltriethoxysilane}$) + polymeric film formers + lubricants + antistatic agents.
  - Reaction sequence: $\text{R-Si(OEt)}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{R-Si(OH)}_3 + 3\text{EtOH} \xrightarrow{\text{Glass-OH}} \text{Glass-O-Si(OH)}_2\text{-R}$.

### 2. Interfacial mechanics and stress corrosion cracking (SCC)
- **Sizing stress transfer efficiency**: Film formers and chemical coupling agents increased interfacial shear strength ($\tau_{IFSS}$) by 3.5-fold ($12 \rightarrow 42\ \text{MPa}$) over unsized fibers, maximizing pullout energy dissipation.
- **Moisture stress corrosion mechanics**: Charles power-law formulations ($v = v_0 K_I^n \exp(-Q/RT)$) demonstrated that stressed fibers undergo progressive siloxane bond hydrolysis under moisture, necessitating appropriate polymeric/cementitious passivation barriers.

- **Overall conclusion**: This authoritative treatise established the compositional framework, drawing processes, sizing chemistry, and degradation mechanics of silicate glass fibers for composite engineering.
