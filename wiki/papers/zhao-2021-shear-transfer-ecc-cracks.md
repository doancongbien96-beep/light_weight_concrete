## Summary
A study that systematically evaluated the shear transfer behavior across crack planes and the associated shear-friction mechanism of high-ductility composites (PVA-ECC and PE-ECC) through push-off direct shear tests. Pure shear loading was applied to specimens pre-cracked in direct tension (initial crack width $w_0 = 0.1 \sim 0.5 mm$), and the shear stress-slip ($\tau-s$) relationship, the crack opening displacement ($w$), and the residual fiber bridging force were measured. The study demonstrated that even in a fine-aggregate matrix with negligible aggregate interlock, the shear resistance of fibers (dowel action and inclined bridging) combined with crack-plane friction develops high shear transfer strength ($\tau_{max} = 6.5 \sim 8.5 MPa$), and established a constitutive model for shear transfer.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a decay function of ECC shear transfer strength versus pre-crack width**: formulation of the $\tau_{max}(w_0) = \tau_{max0} e^{-\alpha w_0}$ model.
- **Elucidation of the combined shear dowel and tensile bridging action of fibers**: under shear slip, the tensile force generated by inclined elongation of the fibers clamps the crack faces strongly (clamping effect), increasing shear friction.
- **Provision of a basis for revising the reinforced concrete shear friction design provisions (ACI 318)**: demonstration that a shear friction coefficient of $\mu = 1.4 \sim 1.8$ is secured.

## Methods
- Material proportions: PVA-ECC and PE-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$).
- Test technique: push-off shear specimens conforming to the JSCE standard were tested, with shear loading applied after introduction of pre-tensile cracks.
- Instrumentation: shear slip ($s$) and normal crack opening displacement ($w$) were measured simultaneously through LVDTs.

## Results
- PE-ECC ($w_0 = 0.2 mm$): peak shear transfer strength of $\tau_{max} = 8.45 MPa$, with high shear ductility maintained up to a shear slip of $2.5 mm$.
- While the shear strength of ordinary concrete drops by 60 % at a crack width of $0.2 mm$, ECC retains 85 % or more of its shear transfer capacity.
