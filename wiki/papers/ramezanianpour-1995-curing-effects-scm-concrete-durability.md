# Compressive Strength, Chloride-Ion Penetration Resistance (RCPT $<500\ \text{C}$), and Pore-Structure Evolution Mechanisms of Admixture (Slag/Fly Ash/Silica Fume) Concretes as a Function of Curing Conditions

## Summary

Targeting concretes in which mineral admixtures were substituted — ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS 25%, 50%), fly ash (FA 25%, 58% HVFA), and silica fume (SF 10%) — to reduce portland cement consumption and secure the long-term durability of marine and bridge concretes, this study systematically elucidates how **standard continuous moist curing versus early-dry curing simulating poor field practice (2 days moist followed by ambient air drying)** affect compressive strength development from 7 to 180 days, the ASTM C1202 rapid chloride-ion penetration test (RCPT), mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), and water permeability — revealing the curing-sensitivity mechanism whereby **with sufficient moist curing the 180-day chloride passing charge of the 58% HVFA and 50% slag concretes reaches $<500\ \text{Coulombs}$ (very low permeability, Very Low), whereas early drying halts the pozzolanic reaction and surface porosity surges 2.5-fold** — the immortal landmark classic of Cement and Concrete Composites (1995) by **Dr. V. M. Malhotra and Prof. A. A. Ramezanianpour of the Canada Centre for Mineral and Energy Technology (CANMET)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first quantification of the curing sensitivity of SCM concretes**:
   - Proves that slag and high-volume fly ash (HVFA) concretes are far more vulnerable to early drying than plain OPC concrete, mandating an essential minimum moist curing period of 7 days.
2. **Demonstration of a dramatic improvement in long-term chloride-ion penetration resistance ($<500\ \text{Coulombs}$)**:
   - With 180 days of continuous curing, the 10% silica fume (320 C), 58% fly ash (450 C), and 50% slag (480 C) concretes block $85\sim 90\%$ of the chloride permeability of normal concrete (3500 C).
3. **Elucidation of the MIP pore-refinement mechanism**:
   - Quantifies the process whereby secondary pozzolanic reaction products (C-S-H gel) subdivide and close coarse capillary pores above $50\ \text{nm}$ into fine gel pores below $10\ \text{nm}$.
4. **Official validation of the long-term strength and durability of high-volume fly ash concrete (HVFA 58%)**:
   - Achieves a 28-day compressive strength of $48\ \text{MPa}$ and a 180-day compressive strength of $68\ \text{MPa}$ through the combination of low W/B (0.35) and superplasticizer, completing the case for eco-friendly structural concrete.

## Methods

- **Materials and concrete mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Binder: ASTM Type I ordinary portland cement.
  - Five admixture mixes:
    1. `Control (OPC)`: W/B = 0.50.
    2. `Slag-25 & Slag-50`: GGBS 25%, 50% replacement (W/B = 0.50).
    3. `FA-25 & FA-58 (HVFA)`: Class F fly ash 25% (W/B = 0.50), 58% (W/B = 0.35, superplasticizer).
    4. `SF-10`: silica fume 10% replacement (W/B = 0.50).
- **Curing and durability testing (Section 2.2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Curing conditions: 1) $23\ ^\circ\text{C}$ continuous underwater moist curing (28/180 days), 2) 2 days immersion followed by ambient air curing at $23\ ^\circ\text{C}, 50\%\ \text{RH}$.
  - Compressive strength: $\phi 100 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylinder specimens (7, 28, 91, 180 days).
  - Chloride permeability: ASTM C1202 RCPT (passed charge measured under $60\ \text{V}$ DC for 6 hours).
  - Pore structure: mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) and absorption tests.

### 2. Interpretation of moisture diffusion and secondary pozzolanic reaction arrest

- **Hydration arrest upon early drying**: slag and fly ash particles hydrate more slowly than OPC; if moisture evaporates within just 2 days, the capillary water film is destroyed, the pozzolanic reaction stops permanently, and surface porosity rises sharply.
- **Dense C-S-H gel formation under continuous moist curing**: under a sustained moisture supply, free $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ is progressively consumed and nanopores close completely, driving the charge at 180 days down to 500 C or below for maximal blocking.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper quantifies strength development and chloride penetration resistance of admixture concretes across curing regimes through 180-day long-term tracking, setting the curing criteria adopted worldwide in concrete specifications and the standard for durable marine mix design — the historic top-authority landmark classic by Dr. V. M. Malhotra.
