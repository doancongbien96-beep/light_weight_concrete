# UM/ERDC Annual Report on HSHDC Development

## Summary

First-year annual report of the micromechanics-based HSHDC (High Strength High Ductility Concrete) development project. ERDC (the U.S. Army Engineer Research and Development Center) introduced Cor-tuf — its ultra-high-strength steel-fiber composite (compressive strength 190~220 MPa, steel fiber ZP305 at 3.6 vol %) — to the University of Michigan to characterize its macro- and micro-scale properties, explained on energy criteria why this material cannot achieve strain-hardening, and then laid out an HSHDC design roadmap consisting of PE fiber substitution, matrix toughness reduction, and artificial flaws. It is the source document for ranade-2012-hshdc-poster and ranade-2013-hshdc-micromechanics.

## Key Contributions

- Micromechanical diagnosis of Cor-tuf's lack of ductility: from the interface parameters Gd ≈ 0 J/m², τ0 = 3.0 MPa, and snubbing f = 0.5, the σ-δ relationship (σ0 = 14.93 MPa, δ0 = 26 µm) was derived, and complementary energy Jb' ≈ 22 J/m² < crack-tip toughness Jtip ≈ 24.2 J/m² (Km = 1.1 MPa√m, E = 50 GPa) quantitatively proved that steady-state multiple cracking cannot be established — the contrast with ECC (PVA-ECC's δ0 ≈ 100 µm, Jb' ≈ 30 J/m² vs Jtip ≈ 18 J/m²) is the crux.
- Experimental demonstration of fiber-diameter effects: the high transverse stiffness of 0.55 mm-diameter steel fibers induced matrix spalling around inclined fibers and disrupted multiple cracking — direct-tension coupons averaged 9.8 MPa while dogbones reached only about 2/3 of that (7.4 MPa).
- PE fiber substitution experiment: switching to Spectra 1000 PE fibers (diameter 40 µm, length 12 mm) at 2 vol % achieved strain capacity up to 4.3 % while retaining a tensile strength of 10.8 MPa (compressive strength 142 MPa) — the skeleton of HSHDC v1.
- Matrix tailoring: sand reduction plus addition of 10 % rice husk ash (RHA) lowered Km from 1.12 to 0.91 MPa√m (≈ 20 % reduction) while trading compressive strength down from 190 → 140 MPa — demonstrating the feasibility of decoupling compressive strength from matrix toughness (Appendix C includes the Stellenbosch ACM conference manuscript).
- Artificial flaw testing: introducing plastic beads of about 4.8 mm diameter at 2 vol % confirmed crack-dispersion effects in 50 % of the specimens, but flaw agglomeration eroded strength and ductility, flagging uniform-dispersion rheology as follow-up work — same lineage as the polystyrene-bead artificial-flaw axis of li-2011-hes-ecc.
