# Sulfur Concrete for Lunar Construction Applications: Feasibility and Performance

## Summary

This study investigated the feasibility and engineering performance of sulfur concrete as a waterless composite material for lunar base construction. Under the extreme water scarcity of lunar environments, the thermoplastic manufacturing route utilized basaltic lunar regolith simulant (MLS-1) combined with elemental sulfur binder via melt-mixing at 135 ~ 150 °C followed by solidifying through cooling. Under ultra-high vacuum ($10^{-6} \sim 10^{-9}\ \text{Torr}$) and extreme thermal cycling (-150 ~ +120 °C), strategies including protective coatings and chemical modifiers were proposed to prevent sulfur sublimation and maintain structural stability.

## Key Contributions

- **Formulation of lunar in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) waterless concrete concepts**: Developed rapid-curing (< 1 h) structural materials utilizing phase-change binding of molten sulfur without consuming scarce water resources.
- **Optimization of lunar regolith simulant (MLS-1) proportions**: Achieved high early compressive strengths of 35 ~ 45 MPa immediately upon cooling without requiring 28 d curing at 30 ~ 35 wt % sulfur content.
- **Mitigation strategies for vacuum sublimation**: Outlined surface sealants and olefinic chemical modifiers to prevent progressive sublimation of sulfur binders in lunar vacuum.

## Methods

- **Materials**:
  - Binder: Commercial elemental sulfur (purity 99.5 %, melting point 115 °C).
  - Aggregate: Lunar mare and highland simulants MLS-1 and JSC-1.
  - Binder ratios: Sulfur contents of 25 wt %, 30 wt %, 35 wt %, and 40 wt %.
- **Processing**: Regolith simulant and sulfur were heated and stirred at 140 °C, cast into preheated molds, compacted, and cooled to ambient temperature.
- **Testing**: Compressive strength, flexural strength, coefficient of thermal expansion, and vacuum exposure mass loss.

## Results

- **Mechanical properties**:
  - At optimal sulfur contents of 30 ~ 35 wt %, room-temperature compressive strengths reached 38.5 ~ 44.2 MPa and flexural strengths reached 6.8 ~ 8.2 MPa.
  - Over 90 % of ultimate strength developed rapidly within 1 ~ 2 h of cooling.
- **Lunar environmental vulnerabilities**:
  - Thermal cycling from -150 °C ~ +120 °C induced microcracking from solid-state phase transitions between monoclinic ($\beta$-sulfur) and orthorhombic ($\alpha$-sulfur).
  - Incorporating polymeric modifiers (such as DCPD) improved thermal fatigue resistance and tensile ductility.
