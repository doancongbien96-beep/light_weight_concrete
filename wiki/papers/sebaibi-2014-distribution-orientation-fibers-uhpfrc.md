## Summary

This study precisely establishes, using high-resolution digital image analysis and four-point flexural loading tests, how the casting method (central casting vs end casting), mold shape, and flow shear gradients in ultra-high performance fiber-reinforced concrete (UHPFRC, steel fibers $V_f = 2.0 \sim 3.0 \%$) affect the three-dimensional spatial distribution and orientation angle ($\theta$) of steel fibers. It demonstrates that when the shear flow path of the matrix during casting and the wall effect align steel fibers with the principal tensile stress direction ($0°$), the post-cracking peak flexural strength (MOR) and energy absorption capacity increase by up to 2.2× relative to random orientation, and it establishes a fiber orientation coefficient ($\eta_\theta$) model.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of the correlation between casting-flow mechanism and fiber orientation angle**: with end casting inducing linear flow, the fiber orientation coefficient $\eta_\theta = \cos^2 \theta$ rises markedly to 0.82 versus central radial casting ($\eta_\theta = 0.54$).
- **Statistical analysis of cross-sectional fiber count and position based on image segmentation**: automatic extraction of steel fiber diameter, major/minor axis ratio, and two-dimensional coordinates from cut cross-sections to compute dispersion coefficients and the orientation tensor.
- **Refined prediction of flexural strength and tensile strain-softening behavior**: a modified bridging model incorporating the orientation coefficient $\eta_\theta$ reduces the error in predicted flexural load-deflection curves of UHPFRC members to within 8 %.

## Methods

- **Mix design**: $W/B = 0.18$, cement + silica fume, straight high-strength steel fibers (diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm, tensile strength 2500 MPa, $V_f = 2.5 \%$).
- **Casting conditions**: prismatic column molds of $100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$, with (1) unidirectional end casting and (2) central pour casting.
- **Measurement**: four-point flexural test (span 300 mm); cross-sections ground and then analyzed by high-resolution optical scanning image analysis.

## Results

- End unidirectional-cast beam: flexural strength $38.4 MPa$, fracture energy $24.5 kJ/m2$, effective orientation coefficient $\eta_\theta = 0.82$.
- Central-pour-cast beam: flexural strength $21.2 MPa$, fracture energy $11.8 kJ/m2$, effective orientation coefficient $\eta_\theta = 0.54$ (flexural strength 45 % lower).
