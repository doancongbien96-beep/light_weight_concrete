# Tensile Strain Hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 4.65\%$) of PE-PP Hybrid Fiber Reinforced Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC) and Establishment of a Bilinear Crack-Bridging Constitutive Model

## Summary

To overcome the economic limitations of engineered geopolymer composites (EGC) arising from the use of expensive ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (PE) fibers and to secure the practicality of cement-free low-carbon eco-friendly binders, this landmark 2023 original paper by the research team of Professor Yong-chang Guo at Guangdong University of Technology (GDUT), published in Composite Structures, precisely evaluates the flowability, uniaxial direct tensile stress–strain response, and multiple fine-cracking characteristics (crack number, average crack width) of fly ash/slag-based EGC as a function of the **hybrid substitution ratio of PE fibers and low-cost polypropylene (PP) fibers ($\text{PP} = 0\%, 25\%, 50\%, 75\%, 100\%$, total $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, establishes a **nonlinear tensile constitutive equation** based on a fiber pullout slip spring model of the crack plane, and **reduces the fiber unit cost by more than $40\%$ while securing a tensile strength of $4.82\ \text{MPa}$ and a tensile strain of $4.65\%$ with the 50% PP hybrid (PE 1.0% + PP 1.0%) mix**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Ultra-high ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 4.65\%$) realized through PE-PP hybrid bridging synergy**:
   - The high-stiffness PE fibers guarantee tensile bridging competence while the flexible, long PP fibers accommodate wide crack opening displacements, further improving ductility by $22\%$ compared with pure PE-EGC ($\varepsilon_u = 3.82\%$).
2. **Composite material cost reduction of $40\%$ and 2.2× improvement in fresh flowability**:
   - Replacing $50\%$ of the expensive PE fibers with low-cost PP fibers substantially lowers the material cost and improves the slump flow from $54\% \rightarrow 121\%$, maximizing constructability.
3. **Quantification of multiple fine-cracking evolution and crack width control characteristics**:
   - At PP substitution ratios of $50\%$ or below, 15~20 dense multiple cracks form, stably suppressing the average crack width to $0.15\ \text{mm}$ or less up to ultimate load.
4. **Establishment of a nonlinear tensile crack-bridging constitutive model based on equivalent spring stiffness**:
   - Combining the equivalent elastic pullout stiffnesses ($K_{pe}, K_{pp}$) of PE and PP fibers with the crack density predicts the tensile stress–strain curve within an error range of $5\%$.

## Methods

- **Geopolymer matrix and fiber mix design (Section 2.1~2.2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Precursor: Class F fly ash (FA) 80 wt.% + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) 20 wt.%.
  - Alkaline activator: mixed solution of liquid sodium silicate + sodium hydroxide ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ 6 wt.%, modulus $M_s = 1.2$, activator/precursor ratio = 0.40).
  - Fiber hybrid combinations (total $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$):
    1. `R-0`: PE 100% (PE 2.0%, PP 0%).
    2. `R-25`: PE 75% + PP 25% (PE 1.5%, PP 0.5%).
    3. `R-50`: PE 50% + PP 50% (PE 1.0%, PP 1.0%).
    4. `R-75`: PE 25% + PP 75% (PE 0.5%, PP 1.5%).
    5. `R-100`: PP 100% (PE 0%, PP 2.0%).
- **Test methods (Section 2.3 & Figs. 2~4)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone-shaped specimen ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, displacement rate 0.5 mm/min).
  - Flowability: ASTM C1437 flow table test.
  - Real-time tracking of crack patterns/widths using a high-resolution camera and optical microscopy.

### 2. Interpretation of hybrid fiber bridging and strain-hardening mechanisms
- **Ductility synergy**: while the PE fibers strictly constrain fine crack opening through high interfacial shear strength, the PP fibers, with their lower elastic modulus and excellent extensibility, stretch without rupturing at crack planes, extending the tensile strain of the composite overall up to $4.65\%$.
- **Accuracy of the crack-bridging spring analysis**: the bilinear spring constitutive model precisely predicts both the first-cracking strength reduction with PP substitution and the slope of the plastic strain-hardening segment, demonstrating the analytical reliability of hybrid EGC member design.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original study in the field of low-carbon highly ductile construction materials, achieving a tensile strain capacity of $4.65\%$ and completing a practical constitutive equation by hybridizing expensive PE fibers and low-cost PP fibers at a 50:50 ratio in a cement-free geopolymer matrix while reducing the material unit cost by $44\%$.
