# Form-Stabilized Composite PCMs Based on Coconut-Shell Activated Carbon (CAC) and Graphene Nanoplatelet (GnP) Hybrids: 195 % Thermal Conductivity Enhancement and Latent Heat Storage

## Summary

To resolve low thermal conductivity in building energy storage and green thermal regulation envelopes, Professor Gökhan Hekimoğlu at Karadeniz Technical University developed a 3D thermally conductive hybrid framework (CAC@GnP) combining coconut-shell-derived activated carbon (CAC) with graphene nanoplatelets (GnP: 5 wt.%, 10 wt.%). Vacuum impregnating 50 wt.% of the organic bio-ester phase change material **methyl stearate (MtS)** into the hybrid carrier, the author evaluated microstructure, phase change enthalpies ($\Delta H_m = 104.2\ \text{J/g}$), thermal conductivity, charging/discharging kinetics, and thermal reliability across 1,000 thermal cycles. The resulting **CAC90@GnP10/MtS composite PCM exhibited 0.0 % liquid leakage at $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$, increased thermal conductivity by 195 % ($0.22 \rightarrow 0.65\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) relative to pure PCM, and reduced thermal charging/discharging durations by 45 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Biomass activated carbon-GnP hybrid thermal conductive networks**:
   - Combined high surface area mesoporous activated carbon (CAC, $950\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$) for capillary liquid retention with graphene nanoplatelets (GnP) establishing high-speed phonon transport channels.
2. **~3-fold increase in thermal conductivity ($0.22 \rightarrow 0.65\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$)**:
   - Resolved slow thermal response in organic PCMs, reducing thermal charging and discharging times by 42.5 % and 46.8 %, respectively.
3. **High latent heat capacity ($104.2\ \text{J/g}$) with zero leakage**:
   - Stored $104.2\ \text{J/g}$ at a phase transition temperature of $37.2\ ^\circ\text{C}$ without shape deformation or liquid exudation up to $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
4. **Thermal and chemical reliability across 1,000 heating-cooling cycles**:
   - Maintained latent heat retention within 1.5 % degradation, with FT-IR and XRD verifying chemical inertness and long-term stability.

## Methods

- **Composite PCM synthesis (Section 2 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Carrier: Coconut-shell activated carbon (CAC, $d_{50} = 25\ \mu\text{m}$) blended with GnP (5 wt.%, 10 wt.%) under ultrasonication.
  - Phase change material: Pure methyl stearate (MtS: pure latent heat $215.5\ \text{J/g}$, melting point $38.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Impregnation: Vacuum oven at $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (0.08 MPa) for 3 h, followed by surface wiping and drying.
- **Thermal characterization (Section 2.3 & Figs. 2–3)**:
  - DSC analysis: Hitachi 7020 differential scanning calorimeter ($15\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 3 °C/min).
  - Thermal conductivity: Linseis THB (Transient Hot Bridge, error within 5 %).
  - Dynamic thermal response: Constant-temperature water bath thermal cycling mapped in real time via FLIR infrared thermal camera.

### 2. Capillary confinement and phonon transport mechanics
- **Mesoporous capillary confinement**: The $2\sim 50\ \text{nm}$ mesopores in CAC exert strong capillary suction and surface tension on liquid MtS molecules, completely preventing liquid leakage (100 % retention) upon melting.
- **GnP phonon highways**: 2D graphene sheets bridge point contacts between activated carbon particles into continuous planar interfaces, accelerating carbon lattice vibration (phonon) transport to amplify thermal conductivity 3-fold.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the synthesis of leak-free form-stabilized composite PCMs utilizing biomass-derived activated carbon and graphene nanoplatelets, achieving 104.2 J/g latent heat storage and 3-fold thermal conductivity enhancement for building energy efficiency.
