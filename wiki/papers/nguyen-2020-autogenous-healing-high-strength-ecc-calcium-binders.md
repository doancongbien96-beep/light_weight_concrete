# Autogenous healing and re-loading tensile-strength $98.5\%$ recovery mechanism of a 100 MPa-class ultra-high-strength ECC (HS-ECC) using calcium-containing binders (GGBS/CSA/lime)

## Summary

To permanently seal early-age microcracks that form in ultra-high-strength concrete under an extremely low water-binder ratio (W/B = 0.20) and to make structural durability lasting, three calcium-supplying binders (`M-C`: ground granulated blast-furnace slag GGBS 30%, `M-E`: CSA-based expansive agent 10%, `M-S`: slaked lime $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ 10%) were incorporated into a **100 MPa-class ultra-high-strength strain-hardening composite (HS-ECC, PE fiber $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, 28-day compressive strength of 100 MPa)**, and after uniaxial direct-tensile pre-damage induction, **water-immersed self-healing curing, image-processing-based 2D crack-area reduction ratio ($A_{red}$), secondary re-loading mechanical recovery behavior, and SEM-EDS microstructural phase analysis** were precisely evaluated, demonstrating **a compressive strength of $104.2\ \text{MPa}$, a tensile strength of $12.5\ \text{MPa}$, and a tensile strain of $5.20\%$, together with $100\%$ complete closure of cracks up to $50\ \mu\text{m}$ and a reloading tensile-strength recovery rate of $98.5\%$**, in this Construction and Building Materials (2020) landmark origin paper by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's team at Chonnam National University (corresponding author), Dr. Choi J.-I., and Dr. Cha S.-L. of KAIST.

## Key Contributions

1. **Simultaneous realization of ultra-high ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 5.20\%$) and high tensile strength ($12.5\ \text{MPa}$) in a 100 MPa-class matrix**:
   - Optimizing nanosilica fume and slag particle packing while harmonizing the bridging capacity of 1.5 vol.% PE fiber achieved saturated multiple fine cracking with strain hardening free of brittle failure even at 100 MPa ultra-high strength.
2. **Quantification of autogenous-healing crack-area reduction ($>90\%$) via 2D image processing by calcium binder type**:
   - The slag-substituted mix (`M-C`) sealed $92.5\%$ of the total crack area after 28 days of water immersion and fully restored microcracks up to $50\ \mu\text{m}$ at $100\%$.
3. **Demonstration of the structural bond strength of healing products through re-loading tensile testing**:
   - Under secondary tensile loading after healing completion, the previously healed cracks did not reopen; secondary microcracks formed at new locations within the matrix, perfectly restoring $98.5\%$ of the initial tensile strength.
4. **Elucidation of the combined healing mechanism of secondary pozzolanic reaction of unreacted particles and carbonation**:
   - Proven that the highly concentrated unhydrated slag/cement particles retained by the extremely low water-binder ratio continuously precipitate secondary C-S-H gel and calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals via penetrating water, forming permanent bonding capacity.

## Methods

- **HS-ECC mix design (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Reference matrix: OPC Type I + silica fume (SF/B = 0.05) + micro silica sand (S/B = 0.36) (W/B = 0.20, high-range water-reducing agent applied).
  - Three calcium binder substitutions:
    1. `M-C`: 30 wt.% substitution of ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS, Blaine fineness $435\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
    2. `M-E`: 10 wt.% substitution of a calcium sulfoaluminate (CSA)-based expansive agent.
    3. `M-S`: 10 wt.% substitution of high-purity slaked lime powder ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$).
  - Fiber: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2700\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 88\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Damage application, self-healing, and reloading tests (Section 2.2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Primary tensile damage: dogbone specimens at 28 days loaded to $1.5\%$ tensile strain then unloaded $\rightarrow$ imaged by high-resolution optical microscopy.
  - Healing condition: 28-day water immersion at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
  - Image processing: ImageJ binarization and area integration to compute the 2D crack-area reduction ratio ($A_{red}$).
  - Secondary retension test: healed specimens reloaded in uniaxial tension up to fracture to measure strength and ductility recovery behavior.

### 2. Interpretation of autogenous-healing robustness and the reloading mechanism of the ultra-high-strength matrix

- **Healed-zone strength surpassing the mother matrix**: Dense $\text{C-S-H}$ and $\text{CaCO}_3$ crystals generated during immersion rigidly bonded the crack gaps so that, upon secondary reloading, the bond shear strength of the healed crack faces exceeded the matrix strength, realizing perfect structural healing in which new microcracks form inside the matrix instead of reopening the existing cracks.
- **Sustained hydration potential of slag**: Owing to the extremely low W/B = 0.20, more than $40\%$ of unhydrated slag particles remain preserved even at 28 days, supplying abundant hydration products immediately upon crack formation.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the world-leading landmark origin study on ultra-high-strength self-healing construction composites, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's team at Chonnam National University achieved a tensile strength of 12.5 MPa and a ductility of 5.2% through slag substitution in a 100 MPa-class ultra-high-strength HS-ECC, demonstrating 92.5% crack-area healing within 28 days and a complete 98.5% tensile-strength recovery upon reloading.
