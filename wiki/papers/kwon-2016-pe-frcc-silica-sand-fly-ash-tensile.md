# Effects of Silica Sand and Fly Ash Replacement (FA 0~60%) on Compressive Strength, Direct Tensile Behavior, and Crack Width ($54\ \mu\text{m}$) of Polyethylene (PE) Fiber-Reinforced Cementitious Composites

## Summary

In high-ductility cementitious composites reinforced with high-strength polyethylene (**PE fibers: diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, tensile strength $2900\ \text{MPa}$, volume fraction $1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, this study systematically evaluates the effects of **silica sand addition (oven-dried sand, S/B = 0.4) and fly ash replacement (FA: 30 %, 60 % replacement of cement)** on effective water-to-binder ratio, compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile response (first crack strength, ultimate tensile strength, tensile strain capacity), and crack width control. Conducted by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), Seong-Joon Kwon, Su-Tae Kang, and Jeong-Il Choi at Chonnam National University (2016), the experimental results demonstrated that the **60 % fly ash replacement mixture (C-PE-F6) achieved an ultra-high tensile strain capacity of $5.58\%$ (a 237-fold increase over plain matrix), a tensile-to-compressive strength ratio of $21.1\%$ (2.7-fold higher than the 7.7 % of plain paste), and an average crack width tightly controlled to $54\ \mu\text{m}$**, simultaneously securing structural durability and extreme ductility.

## Key Contributions

1. **Achievement of $5.58\%$ ultra-high tensile strain capacity via high-volume fly ash (60 %)**:
   - Fly ash lowered matrix fracture toughness and optimized interfacial frictional shear stress with PE fibers, maximizing multiple microcracking efficiency.
2. **Derivation of $21.1\%$ tensile-to-compressive strength ratio for limit state design**:
   - Secured a tensile strength fraction more than double that of conventional concrete ($7\sim 10\%$), providing a mechanical basis for incorporating concrete tensile contributions into flexural and shear limit state designs.
3. **Identification of effective W/B reduction mechanism from oven-dried silica sand**:
   - Sand water absorption (2.04 %) lowered effective W/B by 0.008 during mixing, increasing compressive ($58.9\ \text{MPa}$) and tensile strengths ($6.75\ \text{MPa}$) while reducing tensile strain capacity to $1.58\%$ due to elevated matrix toughness.
4. **Verification of self-controlled microcrack widths ($54\sim 78\ \mu\text{m}$)**:
   - Maintained crack widths near $60\ \mu\text{m}$ even under large tensile deformations exceeding $5\%$, effectively preventing ingress of deleterious ions and rebar corrosion.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–2)**:
  - Binders: Ordinary Portland cement (OPC), fly ash (Class F, replacement ratios 0 %, 30 %, 60 %).
  - Fine aggregate: No. 7 silica sand (mean grain size $0.1\sim 0.3\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 2.65, water absorption 2.04 %, S/B = 0.4).
  - PE fibers: Diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length $15\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength $2900\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $88\ \text{GPa}$, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, volume fraction $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$.
  - Chemical admixtures: Polycarboxylate ether superplasticizer (SP), cellulose-based viscosity modifying agent (VMA).
  - 5 mixtures: C (Control paste), C-PE (PE fiber reinforced paste), C-PE-S (PE + silica sand), C-PE-F3 (FA 30 %), C-PE-F6 (FA 60 %).
- **Testing procedures (Section 2.2 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Compressive strength: $\phi 50\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm}$ cylinders cured for 28 d, loaded at $0.2\ \text{MPa/s}$.
  - Direct uniaxial tension: Coupon dogbone specimens ($30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, $100\ \text{mm}$ gauge length) loaded at $0.1\ \text{mm/min}$ with dual LVDT displacement monitoring.
  - Crack width: Post-failure optical microscopy measurement of average crack width.

### 2. Micromechanical interpretations and tension-compression relationships
- **Interfacial mechanics of hydrophobic PE fibers**: Unlike hydrophilic PVA fibers, hydrophobic PE fibers exhibit no chemical bonding. First crack strength ($\sigma_{fc}$) drops slightly due to fiber inclusion, but frictional slip during pullout sustains large tensile strain capacity ($> 3.7\%$).
- **Mechanism of elevated tensile/compressive strength ratio with fly ash**: Replacing 60 % cement with FA reduces compressive strength to $27.5\ \text{MPa}$ due to delayed hydration, but fiber bridging capacity is preserved at $5.80\ \text{MPa}$, causing the tensile-to-compressive ratio to surge to $21.1\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's group demonstrated that optimizing effective W/B via silica sand and tailoring interfacial friction with 60 % fly ash enables PE-FRCC to achieve $5.58\%$ tensile strain capacity, a $21.1\%$ tensile/compressive ratio, and tightly controlled $54\ \mu\text{m}$ crack widths.
