## Summary

A study that establishes the free-vibration decay response, dynamic modulus of elasticity ($E_d$), and material damping ratio ($\zeta$) of rubber modified concrete (RMC) — in which recycled waste-tire rubber powder (particle size $0.5 \sim 2.0 mm$) volume-replaces sand as fine aggregate at 0, 5, 10, 15, and 20 vol % — through cantilever-beam free-vibration ring-down tests and impact hammer modal testing. Quantitatively demonstrating that, owing to the high viscoelastic energy dissipation of rubber powder and the matrix-rubber interfacial friction action, the damping ratio at a rubber replacement of 20 vol % reaches $\zeta = 2.88 \%$, a **2.3-fold** increase over ordinary concrete ($\zeta = 1.25 \%$), and that the vibration decay time is shortened by 60 %.

## Key Contributions

- **Formulation of an index for the increase of concrete damping ratio with rubber particle content**: establishes the linear correlation model $\zeta = \zeta_0 (1 + 0.065 r_v)$ ($R^2 = 0.98$).
- **Decay analysis of free-vibration acceleration time histories**: extraction of highly reliable modal damping ratios via measurement of the logarithmic decrement $\delta$.
- **Analysis of the mechanical trade-off between dynamic modulus reduction and damping enhancement**: elastic modulus decreases gently from 35 GPa to 24 GPa while impact absorption capacity improves by 130 %.
- **Establishment of applicability as a cushioning structural material for railway track slabs and road pavements**: realizes a 5 dB reduction of passing train and vehicle vibration.

## Methods

- Mix proportions: ordinary Portland cement ($W/C = 0.42$); rubber powder replacement rates of $0, 5, 10, 15, 20 vol \%$.
- Specimens: $100 \times 100 \times 500 mm$ beam specimens (free vibration measured after releasing an initial displacement under cantilever support).
- Instrumentation: high-sensitivity accelerometer (PCB 352C33), dynamic signal analyzer (FFT analyzer); uniaxial compressive strength on 100 mm cubes.
