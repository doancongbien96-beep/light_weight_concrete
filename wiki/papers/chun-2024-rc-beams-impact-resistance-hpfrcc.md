# Enhanced impact resistance of RC beams using various types of high-performance fiber-reinforced cementitious composites

## Summary

Ten large RC beams (300×450 mm section, 2700 mm span) were taken through a three-stage program of static flexure → drop-weight impact (3 t hammer × 1020 mm drop height = 30 kJ kinetic energy) → residual-capacity retest. The set comprised an NSC control plus four HPFRCC types: ECC (PVA fiber 2 %), UHPFRC (steel fiber 2 %), URSHCC (ultra-rapid-hardening SHCC, PE fiber 2 %), and HP-SHCC (high-performance SHCC, PE fiber 2 %). At the material level, UHPFRC posted the highest compressive strength of 140.34 MPa and tensile strength of 17.28 MPa, while HP-SHCC recorded the highest strain capacity of 9.08 % and strain energy density of 824.76 kJ/m³. At the structural level, the R-UHPFRC and R-HP-SHCC beams carried the highest static peak loads at about 490 kN (+43 % over NSC), yet under the identical 30 kJ impact energy only the R-UHPFRC beam collapsed completely (a large reaction force combined with low structural ductility fractured the rebar prematurely). The R-HP-SHCC beam limited maximum deflection to 47.0 mm, a 29.1 % reduction versus NSC, and even raised its residual capacity by 5.4 % after impact damage (RRI 1.054), and is proposed as the optimal material.

## Key Contributions

1. **Material ductility ≠ structural ductility, demonstrated**: although every HPFRCC exhibited tensile strain hardening, post-yield behavior split into two camps. UHPFRC, with the high pullout stiffness of its E_s ≈ 200 GPa steel fibers, drove crack localization, and its strong bond to the rebar concentrated rebar strain on a single localized crack face, producing premature fracture even in the static test — deflection capacity 8.15 mm, ductility index μ_p = Δ_p/Δ_y = 1.13 (68.5 % of R-NSC's 1.65). By contrast, URSHCC and HP-SHCC, whose strain capacities were sufficiently large (around 9 % each), kept carrying tension through the composite itself after rebar yielding, so the load continued to rise, giving μ_p = 7.52 and 8.02 (R-HP-SHCC about 5× NSC). ECC (ε 4.28 %) sat in between, with μ_p 1.52, or 92.1 % of NSC.
2. **Explaining the paradox that only UHPFRC collapsed completely under the same 30 kJ**: higher compressive strength raises the peak impact force (1514 → 2562 kN, +69.2 %) and the support reaction (UHPFRC 1271 kN, +39.4 % over NSC), and the higher the FRC grade the lower its strain-rate sensitivity, so the increment of impact resistance shrinks. Whereas NSC dissipated part of the energy through crushing at the contact zone, UHPFRC transferred an even larger reaction without any contact-zone damage, and deflection beyond its static ultimate deflection of 43.8 mm funneled rebar fracture into a single macrocrack at midspan — the beam kept deflecting without rebound (complete failure).
3. **Mechanism behind the reduced number of impact cracks**: the HPFRCC beams developed fewer flexural cracks than the NSC beam, and fewer still under impact than under static loading. Because the cracking strength of the cement matrix is more sensitive to strain rate than the fiber pullout (bridging) strength (after Yoo et al.), under impact the stress transferred from bridging fibers back into the matrix suppresses the creation of new cracks. R-ECC was the sole exception, with widely distributed cracks plus top-surface cracking (rebound against the restraint of the anchoring steel rods).
4. **Residual capacity framework (RRI, damage index)**: D = 1 − RRI, where RRI = (peak load of the impact-damaged beam)/(peak load of the intact beam). Results: R-NSC 0.970, R-ECC 1.045, R-URSHCC 1.005, R-HP-SHCC 1.054 (+5.4 % even after damage, credited to rebar strain hardening). All sit in the low-damage band (D = 0~0.2). Post-damage ultimate deflection nevertheless decreased significantly across the board because of residual deformation (permanent compressive strain and residual rebar strain), and the damaged R-ECC and R-URSHCC beams showed abrupt load drops from premature rebar fracture.
5. **Curing-dependent tensile behavior of URSHCC**: URSHCC, which replaces OPC with ultra-rapid-hardening cement, secured multiple-fine-crack strain hardening as early as 4 h after casting (37.9 MPa at 4 h ≈ about 50 % of the 74.7 MPa at 28 days, per reference data). As curing proceeds from 4 h to 28 days, the rising matrix strength reduces the number of flaws and raises the initial cracking strength, but tensile strength and strain capacity decrease slightly once loaded — a curing-axis instance of the PSH logic (Yang and Li; Kim et al.) that sufficient flaw density is the prerequisite for strain capacity.
6. **Material selection guide**: HP-SHCC is the optimum from the impact and residual performance standpoint. Showing that compressive-strength-first design (UHPFRC) can actually undermine structural safety under impact is the practical design implication.

## Methods

- **Mix proportions (Table 1, ratios by cement mass)**: NSC — cement 1 : FA 0.15 : BFS 0.15, water 212(B%), fine aggregate 250(B%), coarse aggregate up to 25 mm, ready-mix targeting 30 MPa. ECC — OPC + FA 2.2, W/B 0.284, SP 1.90 %. UHPFRC — OPC + silica fume 0.25 (W/B 0.20), silica flour filler 24 %(B%), silica sand, SP 1.60 %. URSHCC — ultra-rapid-hardening cement with BFS 0.5 : LSP 0.25 : CKD 0.1 : silica fume 0.2, SP 3.84 %. HP-SHCC — GGBFS 0.5 + silica fume 0.25, water 30 %(B%), SP 2.60 %. Common to the HPFRCCs: silica sand of 0.2~0.3 mm diameter, no coarse aggregate, fiber content 2.0 vol %.
- **Fibers (Table 2)**: PVA d_f 38 µm × l_f 8 mm (aspect ratio 210, 1560 MPa, E_f 24 GPa); steel fiber 200 µm × 19 mm (95, 2580 MPa, E_f 200 GPa); UHMW PE 30~32 µm × 18 mm (562.5~600, 3000 MPa, E_f 100 GPa).
- **Reinforcement**: flexural D19 (f_y 480 MPa, f_u 604 MPa), 2 bars — under-reinforced, ρ = A_s/bd = 0.0057; shear D10 stirrups (f_y 461 MPa) @100 mm — deliberately over-reinforced in shear to force flexural failure. E_s 200 GPa.
- **Specimens**: 10 beams (2 per material: 1 static + 1 impact). Section 300×450 mm, span 2700 mm.
- **Material tests**: compression on Φ100×200 mm cylinders, 3000 kN UTM (compressometer with 3 LVDTs); direct tension per JSCE dog-bone (crack-induced section 13×30 mm², gauge length 80 mm, 2 LVDTs) on a 300 kN UTM; at least 6 specimens averaged for tension.
- **Impact test**: drop weight 3 t × drop height 1020 mm (E_k = 30 kJ). Free-to-rotate roller supports (uplift and overturning prevented by top plates bolted down with steel rods). Instrumentation: 4 impact load cells of 5000 kN (box above the hammer), 2 reaction load cells of 3000 kN, 9 laser displacement sensors of 100 mm range (midspan plus offsets of 200 and 300 mm), 5 accelerometers of 10 000 g, 9 rebar strain gauges. Sampling at 1 MHz (DEWETRON), 2 high-speed cameras at 5000 fps.
- **Static test**: same support system, 1500 kN actuator, two-point loading at 300 mm spacing (anti-sway), 3 LVPs.
- **Damage indices**: μ_p = Δ_p/Δ_y (peak deflection / rebar-yield deflection, with the yield point determined from the rebar strain curve), D = 1 − RRI.

## Results

- **Materials**: NSC 35.92 MPa compression, E 29.05 GPa; UHPFRC 140.34 MPa, 33.45 GPa, f_t 17.28 MPa, ε 0.80 % (crack-localizing hardening — the large pullout stiffness of the steel fibers keeps stress rising steadily, after which some fine cracks widen); HP-SHCC 110.28 MPa, ~30.6 GPa, f_t 11.13 MPa, ε 9.08 %, strain energy density 824.76 kJ/m³ (about 7.94× UHPFRC and about 5.64× ECC as stated in the paper); URSHCC 107.77 MPa, ~30.6 GPa; ECC 59.36 MPa, 21.75 GPa (lowest elastic modulus, no coarse aggregate), f_t 3.85 MPa, ε 4.28 % (stable, flat crack propagation).
- **Static structural behavior**:

| Beam | Yield load (kN) | Peak load (kN) | Δ_p (mm) | Ultimate deflection (mm) | μ_p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-NSC | 329.5 | 343.1 | 17.9 | 72.5 | 1.65 |
| R-ECC | – | 396.1 | – | similar to NSC | 1.52 |
| R-UHPFRC | 469.8 | 487.4 | 8.15 | 43.8 | 1.13 |
| R-URSHCC | – | 478.0 | – | more than 4× NSC | 7.52 |
| R-HP-SHCC | – | 494.0 | – | ≈ 90 (no fracture) | 8.02 |

- R-UHPFRC: highest post-cracking flexural stiffness (high E_f steel fibers); after the peak the load fell away through steel-fiber slip as a single midspan fine crack localized and widened, fracturing the rebar early. R-HP-SHCC: PE-fiber bridging sustained multiple fine cracks, and the beam deflected to ≈ 90 mm without compressive-zone failure (large compressive ductility and ultimate strain; its compressive strength is 21.4 % lower than UHPFRC's, which also lowers rebar bond strength and disperses rebar strain).
- **Impact (30 kJ)**: peak impact force ordered by compressive strength, NSC 1514 kN < … < UHPFRC 2562 kN. Maximum support reaction UHPFRC 1271 kN (+39.4 %). Maximum midspan deflection: NSC 66.3 mm, ECC 56.0 (−15.5 %), URSHCC 53.1 (−19.9 %), HP-SHCC 47.0 mm (−29.1 %, smallest). Only R-UHPFRC collapsed completely (the central crack widened past the midspan LDS range; compared via the LDS #2 offset by 200 mm; deflection continued without rebound). All the others survived because their peak impact deflections stayed below their static ultimate deflections. Cracking: numerous flexural cracks plus crushing at the impact point in NSC; fewer, midspan-concentrated cracks in the HPFRCCs (only 6 cracks in UHPFRC); distributed cracks plus top-surface cracks in ECC.
- **Residual capacity**:

| Beam | Post-damage peak load (kN) | RRI | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-NSC | 332.9 | 0.970 | 0.030 |
| R-ECC | 413.7 | 1.045 | −0.045 |
| R-UHPFRC | – (collapsed completely, excluded) | – | – |
| R-URSHCC | 480.5 | 1.005 | −0.005 |
| R-HP-SHCC | 520.7 | 1.054 | −0.054 |

- Post-damage stiffness: slight degradation in NSC and ECC, essentially unchanged in URSHCC and HP-SHCC (minor compression-zone damage + narrow cracks). The slight post-damage load increases are a rebar strain-hardening effect. The reduced post-damage ultimate deflection and (in places) premature rebar fracture are traces of residual deformation.
