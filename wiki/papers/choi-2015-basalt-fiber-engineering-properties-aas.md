# Evaluation of the Chemical Bond Characteristics of Basalt Fiber in an Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Matrix and the Strength Reduction Coefficient ($f'=1.54$) versus Orientation Angle ($0^\circ\sim 67.5^\circ$)

## Summary

A leading landmark origin paper on single-fiber composite engineering by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University, which investigated the engineering feasibility of using eco-friendly inorganic basalt fiber (manufactured by melt-spinning the natural mineral basalt; diameter $17\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1773 MPa, density $2.75\ \text{g/cm}^3$) as a reinforcing fiber in an eco-friendly cement-free alkali-activated slag (AAS, compressive strength 42.0 MPa) matrix. With the representative organic synthetic fibers polyvinyl alcohol (PVA, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$) and polyethylene (PE, $d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$) as control groups, the team performed **single-fiber pullout tests (chemical bonding $G_d$, frictional bonding $\tau_0$, slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$)** and, applying a precision aluminum inclined jig, quantitatively measured **the tensile-strength reduction rate and strength reduction coefficient ($f'$) versus fiber orientation angle ($0^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 67.5^\circ$)**. The results showed that basalt fiber forms **high chemical bonding of $G_d = 2.59\ \text{J/m}^2$** against the AAS matrix, exhibiting an **87.6 % higher interfacial bond strength** than hydrophilic PVA fiber ($1.38\ \text{J/m}^2$). However, owing to the intrinsic brittleness of inorganic rock-based fibers, tensile strength plunged **83.0 % ($1773 \rightarrow 302$ MPa)** as the fiber inclination angle increased from $0^\circ \rightarrow 67.5^\circ$, and the strength reduction coefficient obtained by exponential regression analysis, **$f' = 1.54$**, came out 9.0× higher than PVA fiber (0.171) and 3.2× higher than PE fiber (0.475). The paper thereby quantitatively demonstrated that while basalt fiber dispersed as short fibers in cement- and geopolymer-based composites is highly favorable for raising the initial tensile strength of the composite, surface coating treatment that relaxes interfacial chemical bond strength and buffers bending stress concentration on the fiber is essential to secure post-cracking ductility (strain-hardening).

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantitative demonstration of the high interfacial chemical bond strength of basalt fiber in an AAS matrix ($G_d = 2.59\ \text{J/m}^2$)**:
   - Proved that the silica–alumina constituents of basalt fiber form strong chemical bonds with alkali-activated slag hydrates (C-A-S-H gel), exhibiting 87.6 % higher bond energy than PVA fiber.
2. **Precise formulation of the orientation-angle-dependent tensile-strength reduction coefficient of basalt fiber ($f' = 1.54$)**:
   - Derived the relation $\sigma_{fu}(\theta) = \sigma_{fu}(0) e^{-f'\theta}$ through inclined tensile tests from $0^\circ \rightarrow 67.5^\circ$, establishing that the strength reduction coefficient of basalt fiber is 3~9× larger than that of organic synthetic fibers.
3. **Completion of a micromechanical bond-property database for PVA, PE, and basalt fibers**:
   - Assembled complete sets of measured $G_d, \tau_0, \beta$ values and orientation-angle-dependent tensile strengths, establishing the essential base parameters for multiscale fiber bridging theory ($\sigma$-$\delta$).
4. **Presentation of surface-modification guidelines for high ductility of inorganic short-fiber-reinforced composites**:
   - Presented design directions for chemical sizing and organic coating to prevent premature fiber rupture caused by excessive chemical bonding and to induce slip-pullout behavior.

## Methods

- **Material specifications and fiber properties (Table 2~3)**:
  - Matrix: alkali-activated slag (AAS, based on ordinary ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder, 28-day compressive strength 42.0 MPa), fine silica sand ($d \le 100\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40), water-to-binder ratio W/B = 0.34.
  - Comparison fibers (3 types):
    1. Basalt fiber: diameter $d_f = 17\ \mu\text{m}$, density $2.75\ \text{g/cm}^3$, reference tensile strength 1773 MPa, elastic modulus 89 GPa.
    2. Polyvinyl alcohol fiber (PVA): diameter $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, density $1.30\ \text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength 1202 MPa, elastic modulus 41 GPa (oil-coated).
    3. Polyethylene fiber (PE): diameter $d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength 2757 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa.
- **Test equipment and methods (Figure 2~3)**:
  - Single-fiber pullout test: fibers embedded perpendicular to the matrix at thicknesses of 1~2 mm, 28-day moist curing, precision load cell up to 2 N and $4\ \mu\text{m}$ laser displacement gauge, displacement-controlled rate $10\ \mu\text{m/s}$ ($0.6\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Tensile test per orientation angle: special precision aluminum jig ($\theta = 0^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 67.5^\circ$), both fiber ends fixed with high-strength adhesive, tensile rate $10\ \mu\text{m/s}$, fracture load measurement and nonlinear exponential regression analysis.

### 2. Fracture-mechanics mechanism interpretation
- **Chemical bonding and initial strength**: the $G_d = 2.59\ \text{J/m}^2$ of basalt fiber is 87.6 % higher than PVA, delaying early crack formation and contributing to the increase of the first-cracking strength of the composite.
- **Shear kinking fracture at inclined angles**: because of the weak transverse shear strength of basalt fiber, larger inclination angles lead to premature fracture at $f' = 1.54$, unable to sustain the bending stress concentration at the crack edge.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University is a representative landmark origin paper on eco-friendly recycled construction resource composites, having completely established experimentally the high chemical bond strength exhibited by inorganic basalt fiber in an alkali-activated slag matrix and its steep strength-degradation behavior under inclined orientation.
