# Tensile Strain-Hardening and Crack-Control Behavior of a 150 MPa-Class Ultra-High-Performance Mortar (UHPM) Reinforced with High-Strength PE Fibers

## Summary

To overcome the limited tensile elongation (<1.0 %), high self-weight, and corrosion vulnerability that arise when micro steel fibers (Microsteel Fiber 1.5 vol.%) reinforce a 150 MPa-class ultra-high-performance cement matrix (UHPM), composites were fabricated by incorporating high-strength polyethylene (PE) monofilament fibers at volume fractions (1.25, 1.50, 1.75 vol.%) into an ultra-dense matrix designed on a modified particle size distribution (modified Andreasen & Andersen model), with W/B 0.20 and 25 % zirconia silica fume replacement, and their density, compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, tensile fracture toughness, and surface crack characteristics were quantitatively compared against a steel-fiber UHPC control group (corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University; co-authors Seung Yeop Jang of the Korea Railroad Research Institute and Seung-Jun Kwon of Hannam University). The experiments demonstrated that the P1.75 mix incorporating PE fibers at 1.75 vol.% maintained a compressive strength of 124 MPa while exhibiting a uniaxial direct tensile strength of **14.60 MPa**, a tensile strain capacity of **4.05 %**, and a tensile toughness of **$0.454\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m/m}$**; against the steel-fiber UHPC control group (tensile strength 19.46 MPa, elongation 0.85 %, toughness 0.139), the elongation increased **4.8×** and the tensile fracture toughness **3.3×**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Achieving 4.05 % tensile elongation in a 120–150 MPa-class ultra-high-strength matrix**:
   - Overcoming the limitation of steel-fiber UHPC, where the high elastic modulus of steel fibers ($E_s = 200\ \text{GPa}$) causes early localization into a single dominant crack, saturated fine multiple cracking was induced through frictional-slip pullout of high-strength PE fibers ($E_f = 110\ \text{GPa}$, tensile strength 2950 MPa), realizing ultra-high ductility of 4.05 %.
2. **Demonstration of the correlation between the stress performance index ($f_t/f_{1st}$) and tensile strain capacity**:
   - As the PE fiber volume fraction increased from 1.25 % $\rightarrow$ 1.50 % $\rightarrow$ 1.75 %, the stress performance index ($f_t/f_{1st}$) rose from 1.49 $\rightarrow$ 1.54 $\rightarrow$ **1.86**.
   - Identified that, as the margin of satisfaction of the PSH condition widened, tensile elongation surged from 1.24 % to **4.05 %** and tensile toughness from 0.117 to **0.454 MPa·m/m**.
3. **Securing lightweight and non-corroding advantages**:
   - Applying PE fibers (density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$) instead of steel fibers (density $7.8\ \text{g/cm}^3$) lightened the unit volumetric mass of the composite from 2.41 to $2.33\ \text{g/cm}^3$ and fundamentally blocked the risk of surface spalling caused by fiber corrosion upon exposure to chloride environments.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and particle size optimization (Tables 1 & 3)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (OPC, density $3.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$, fineness $345\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
  - Zirconia silica fume ($\text{SiO}_2$ 96.0 %, $\text{ZrO}_2$ 3.0 %, mean particle diameter 0.15 µm).
  - Silica flour filler ($d_{50} = 4.2\ \mu\text{m}$), micro quartz sand ($d_{50} = 210\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - The modified A&A (Andreasen & Andersen) packing model ($q = 0.23$) was applied for tightest-packing design.
  - Mix proportions (mass ratios to cement): SF 0.25, filler 0.30, quartz sand 1.10, expansive agent (EA) 0.075, defoamer 0.0007, SP 0.014–0.018, W/B 0.20.
- **Reinforcing fiber specifications (Table 2)**:
  - **Micro steel fiber (S1.50)**: diameter 200 µm, length 19.5 mm, aspect ratio 97.5, tensile strength 2500 MPa, elastic modulus 200 GPa, density $7.8\ \text{g/cm}^3$, volume fraction 1.50 vol.%.
  - **High-strength PE fiber (P series)**: diameter 32 µm, length 12 mm, aspect ratio 375, tensile strength 2950 MPa, elastic modulus 110 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, volume fraction 1.25, 1.50, 1.75 vol.%.
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 (six $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens, 28-day water curing).
  - Uniaxial direct tension test: five JSCE-standard dumbbell specimens (gauge section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm), displacement control (0.2 mm/min), two LVDTs installed.
  - Crack-pattern analysis: precise evaluation of the number of cracks within the gauge section, mean crack spacing, and crack width after reaching peak load.

## Results

### 1. Density and 28-day compressive strength characteristics (Table 4)

| Mix ID | Fiber type and volume fraction | Measured density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | Relative compressive strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1.50** | Steel fiber 1.50 vol.% | 2.41 ± 0.07 | **151 ± 6.3** | 1.00 (UHPC criterion met) |
| **P1.25** | PE fiber 1.25 vol.% | 2.34 ± 0.01 | 125 ± 4.9 | 0.83 |
| **P1.50** | PE fiber 1.50 vol.% | 2.34 ± 0.01 | 124 ± 2.9 | 0.82 |
| **P1.75** | PE fiber 1.75 vol.% | 2.33 ± 0.01 | **124 ± 3.2** | 0.82 |

### 2. Uniaxial direct tensile behavior and tensile fracture toughness (Table 5)

| Mix ID | First-cracking strength (MPa) | Maximum tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | Tensile toughness ($\text{MPa}\cdot\text{m/m}$) | PSH stress ratio ($f_t/f_{1st}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1.50** | **13.24 ± 1.62** | **19.46 ± 0.83** | 0.85 ± 0.21 | 0.139 | 1.47 |
| **P1.25** | 7.65 ± 2.30 | 11.42 ± 0.77 | 1.24 ± 0.44 | 0.117 | 1.49 |
| **P1.50** | 8.35 ± 1.49 | 12.90 ± 0.58 | **3.70 ± 1.35 (+335 %)** | **0.393 (+182 %)** | 1.54 |
| **P1.75** | 7.83 ± 1.09 | **14.60 ± 0.62** | **4.05 ± 0.79 (+376 %)** | **0.454 (+227 %)** | **1.86** |

### 3. Crack control and multiple-cracking behavior
- S1.50 (steel fiber) showed extremely fine crack widths of about 20 µm but few cracks, with abrupt softening occurring at 0.85 % strain.
- P1.75 (PE fiber) developed saturated fine multiple cracking with crack widths of about 110 µm across the entire gauge section, continuously transmitting strain-hardening stress up to 4.05 %.
