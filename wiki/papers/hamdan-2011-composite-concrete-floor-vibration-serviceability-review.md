# Review of Dynamic Property Analysis, Vibration Serviceability Control, and Damping Mitigation in Composite Concrete Floors (CCF)

## Summary

To resolve human annoyance and precision equipment malfunctions caused by walking, jumping, and rhythmic activities on modern long-span lightweight steel-concrete composite floors (CCF), Professor Sinin Hamdan and Mohammad Nurul Hoque at Universiti Malaysia Sarawak (UNIMAS) synthesized structural dynamic modal parameters (fundamental natural frequency $f_n$, modal mass $M$, damping ratio $\zeta$), dynamic Fourier load models (dynamic load factors DLF), ISO 2631 / AISC Design Guide 11 tolerance criteria, and material damping composite technologies. The authors established structural dynamic guidelines showing that **maintaining $f_n \ge 8.0\ \text{Hz}$ combined with increasing modal damping from $\zeta = 1.0\% \rightarrow 4.5\%$ via polymer- or rubber-modified concrete suppresses peak floor accelerations by 65 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Synthesis of 200-year evolution in human-induced floor vibration mechanics**:
   - Comprehensive historical review from Tredgold's 1828 observations to modern layered composite slab dynamics.
2. **Formulation of dynamic load factors (DLF) and harmonic resonance conditions**:
   - Quantified resonance acceleration amplification when 1st to 4th harmonics of human walking steps ($1.6\sim 2.2\ \text{Hz}$) align with structural floor frequencies.
3. **Comparison of global vibration serviceability standards (ISO 2631, AISC DG11, BS 6472)**:
   - Tabulated peak acceleration limits ($0.5\%\sim 5.0\%\ \text{g}$) and Vibration Dose Value (VDV) thresholds across residential, office, and hospital/laboratory floors.
4. **Integration of high-damping concrete for vibration mitigation**:
   - Proposed composite floor solutions enhancing matrix damping 3-fold via crumb rubber and viscoelastic polymers without enlarging structural cross-sections.

## Methods

- **Literature scope and systems evaluated (Sections 1–8 & Figs. 1–15)**:
  - Over 140 foundational studies on composite floor dynamics, modal testing, and damping.
  - Floor systems evaluated:
    1. Steel-deck composite slabs.
    2. Precast hollow-core slabs.
    3. Post-tensioned flat plate slabs.
- **Dynamic evaluation parameters**:
  - Fundamental natural frequency ($f_n$), dynamic stiffness, modal damping ratio ($\zeta$), and RMS acceleration spectra.

### 2. Resonance attenuation and damping dissipation mechanics
- **Dynamic amplification reduction**: Increasing floor damping from $\zeta = 1.0\% \rightarrow 4.0\%$ drops the resonance Dynamic Magnification Factor ($DMF = 1/(2\zeta)$) by 75 % from 50 to 12.5.
- **Impact noise and micro-vibration mitigation**: Viscoelastic polymer/rubber particles dissipate impact energy through internal shear friction.

- **Overall conclusion**: This review synthesized the structural dynamics, human excitation models, and material damping strategies for controlling vibration serviceability in composite concrete floors.
