# Tensile Behavior of UHP-HFRC: Macro Fiber and Micro Fiber Hybrid

## Summary

A paper evaluating under direct tension the tensile stress-strain behavior of 16 UHP-HFRC mixes in which a 200 MPa-compressive-strength UHPC matrix was fixed with four types of steel macro fibers each at 1.0 % while micro fiber content was varied over 0~1.5 % (corresponding author Prof. D.J. Kim of Sejong University, joint work with KICT; outside the user's group). Mindful that existing UHP-HFRC is expensive because it requires 4~11 % fiber to achieve strain hardening, the study targeted achieving hardening at a low fiber content (up to 2.5 %). Conclusion: the shape of the tensile curve is governed by the macro fiber type; once 0.5 % or more micro fiber is added, strain hardening and multiple fine cracking are secured with any macro fiber combination; and the twisted (T) macro fiber series shows the best performance (post-cracking strength 18.6 MPa, strain capacity 0.64 %, average crack spacing 3.8 mm).

## Key Contributions

1. Quantitatively demonstrated through a systematic 16-mix experimental program that the macro fiber type (length and geometric shape) is the primary factor governing the tensile curve shape while the micro fiber plays an auxiliary reinforcing role in hardening and multiple cracking — a role division; reconfirms Rossi's multi-mode observation of fiber composites at the UHPC scale.
2. Provided a criterion for judging whether micro fiber addition is beneficial: when micro fiber content is raised 0→1.5 %, the post-cracking strength gain (Δσpc 3.96~6.30 MPa) always exceeds the first-cracking strength change (Δσcc −0.95~3.55 MPa), which favors strain hardening.
3. Explained the mechanism behind the superiority of T fibers: during pullout the twist unwinds (untwisting) so that most of the embedded length engages mechanical resistance, maintaining bond resistance up to a slip of 70~80 % of the embedded length → maximum pullout energy and equivalent bond strength.
4. Connected crack spacing to fiber volume fraction and equivalent bond strength σeq through an average crack spacing estimation equation based on Naaman's tensile prism model.
5. In comparison with UHP-MFRC (Wille et al. 2011 single-fiber systems), confirmed that for the HA macro fiber series hybridization is more favorable for strain-capacity enhancement than the single-fiber system.

## Methods

- Matrix (mass ratio): cement (Type I) 1.00 / silica fume 0.25 / silica sand (≤0.5 mm) 1.10 / glass powder (mean 10 µm, SiO2 98 %) 0.30 / high-range water reducer 0.067 / water 0.20 → compressive strength 200 MPa. Mixed in a 60 L forced-action mixer; for the fibers, the micro fiber was charged together with water first and the macro fiber added afterward, and casting was performed without vibration.
- Fibers: four macro types — LS (smooth, 0.3 × 30 mm, tensile strength 2580 MPa), HA (hook A, 0.375 × 30 mm, 2311 MPa), HB (hook B, 0.775 × 62 mm, 1891 MPa), T (twisted, triangular cross-section with equivalent diameter 0.3 × 30 mm, 2428 MPa after twisting), with a common elastic modulus of 200 GPa. Each macro fiber at 1.0 vol % plus micro fiber SS (smooth, 0.2 × 13 mm, 2788 MPa) at 0/0.5/1.0/1.5 vol % → 16 mixes (e.g., T10SS15), with 3~5 specimens per mix.
- Tensile test: cross-section 50 × 100 mm, gauge length 175 mm, Shimadzu AG-300KNX displacement control at 0.4 mm/min, strain determined from the average of LVDTs mounted on both sides. Curing: 48 h at room temperature before demolding → water curing at 90 ± 2 °C for 3 days → tested after 21 days of drying. Polyurethane coating applied for crack identification.
- First-cracking strength determination: defined as the first peak when the load drop after the first peak is pronounced, otherwise as the intersection of the elastic slope line and the hardening slope line (minimizing subjectivity). Crack counting was performed visually by two people and averaged over the two faces.

## Results

| Mix | σcc (MPa) | εpc (%) | σpc (MPa) | Crack count | Average crack spacing (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LS10SS00 | 10.09 | 0.088 | 8.84 (softening) | 2 | 87.50 |
| LS10SS15 | 9.88 | 0.365 | 13.22 | 27 | 6.66 |
| HB10SS15 | 10.65 | 0.611 | 12.01 | 39 | 4.50 |
| HA10SS15 | 10.52 | 0.562 | 13.84 | 34 | 5.15 |
| T10SS00 | 7.98 | 0.303 | 12.26 | 32 | 5.57 |
| T10SS15 | 10.70 | 0.636 | 18.56 | 46 | 3.80 |

- Strain-hardening ratio σpc/σcc: T10SS– series 1.391~1.735 (highest) vs HB10SS– series 0.908~1.240 (lowest).
- Performance ranking (micro fiber 1.5 %): by σpc, T > HA > LS > HB; by εpc and multiple cracking, T > HB > HA > LS.
- Deformation-mediated fibers (HA·HB·T) start from lower σcc than LS because activation of mechanical bonding during tension inflicts greater matrix damage, but the damage is mitigated when the micro fiber is added, so Δσcc reaches 2.72~3.55 MPa for the deformation-mediated fiber series (LS: −0.21 MPa).
- Δεpc is not proportional to micro fiber content — as micro fiber increases, crack widths narrow while crack counts grow, and the two effects offset each other.
- Stated limitation: HB fibers (62 mm) are longer than the specimen width (50 mm), so axial alignment bias may have acted slightly in favor of the results.
