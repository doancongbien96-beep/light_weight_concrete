## Summary

Experimental study that fired a deformable lead-core rifle projectile (7.62 x 39 mm, impact velocity $v \approx 700 m/s$) at slim UHPFRC targets 30 ~ 50 mm thick to determine high-velocity impact resistance, penetration depth, and rear-face spall crater diameter experimentally. The ballistic limit and the rear-face scabbing suppression mechanism were analyzed as functions of the volume fraction of micro straight steel fibers ($V_f = 1.0 \%, 2.0 \%, 3.0 \%$) and target thickness. With $V_f = 2.0 \%$ or more incorporated, a slim panel only $50 mm$ thick demonstrated outstanding protective performance, stopping complete perforation by the $700 m/s$ rifle projectile.

## Key Contributions

- **Derivation of the protective critical thickness of UHPFRC under hypervelocity projectile impact**: for a $v \approx 700 m/s$ military projectile, $50 mm$ thick UHPFRC with $V_f = 2.0 \%$ forms the complete-perforation stoppage limit (ballistic limit).
- **Suppression of rear-face scabbing by steel fiber volume fraction**: rear-face crater volume decreases by 82 % as fiber content increases from 1 % to 3 %.
- **Identification of the projectile energy dissipation mechanism**: plastic deformation/mushrooming of the projectile and high-density micro-bridging friction of the steel fibers effectively absorb the impact kinetic energy.

## Methods

- **Materials**: UHPFRC matrix ($W/B = 0.18$, silica fume 25 %, 28 d compressive strength $145 MPa$), micro steel fibers (diameter 0.175 mm, length 13 mm, tensile strength 2200 MPa, $V_f = 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 \%$).
- **Ballistic tests**: standard firearms test bench, 7.62 mm FMJ projectiles (mass 8.0 g, velocity $710 \pm 15 m/s$). Target dimensions $300 \times 300 mm$, thicknesses 30, 40, 50 mm.
- **Instrumentation**: optical velocity gauge, precise crater volume measurement via laser 3D scanning.

## Results

- $30 mm$ thick targets: perforation occurred in all mixes.
- $50 mm$ thick targets ($V_f = 2.0 \%, 3.0 \%$): no perforation occurred (partial penetration), residual penetration depth $28 mm$, rear-face perforation successfully prevented.
