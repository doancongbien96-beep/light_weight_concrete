# UHPC comprehensive review: mechanical performance, durability, sustainability, implementation barriers

## Summary

A 25-page literature review by Abbas (UCT Lahore), Nehdi (Western University), and Saleem.
A survey organizing the global research and construction database into topic-wise tables,
spanning material composition — mix design — fresh state — mechanical performance —
durability — cost/sustainability — implementation barriers. No new experiments; the core
value is re-editing scattered quantitative data into topical tables. For this ECC/EGC wiki
it serves as a reference axis contrasting how strength-axis performance (compression
≥150 MPa) trades off against multiple fine-crack ductility.

## Structure and key quantitative content

### 1. Material composition (Table 2 typical ranges, wt.%)
- Cement 27–40 %, silica fume 6–12 %, quartz flour 7–14 %, fine aggregate 35–45 %,
  SP 0.5–3 %, water 4–10 %, steel fiber 0–8 %
- Cement optimum: compressive strength rises up to about 1700 kg/m³ then falls
  (aggregate participation limit). C₃A < 6 % and Blaine ~4000 cm²/g preferred.
- Unhydrated cement replacement: crushed quartz up to 30 vol%, slag 36 vol%, fly ash
  40 vol% without strength loss.
- Silica fume: 20–30 % (of binder); 25 % of cement mass is Wille et al.'s recommended optimum.
- w/b: minimum reported 0.08, optimal 0.13–0.20; yet over 150 MPa possible even at 0.25 →
  w/b alone does not govern strength (curing, mixer, and mixing procedure co-govern).
- Steel fiber: typically 13 mm × 0.20 mm; Richard & Cheyrezy recommend 2 vol%. Nanoparticles
  (SiO₂, CaCO₃ etc.) 1–5 %; nS optimum 3.74 % of binder mass.

### 2. Mix design model lineage
LPDM → SSM → CPM (de Larrard & Sedran) → modified Andreasen & Andersen (Yu et al., cement
<675 kg/m³) → statistical models (ANFIS, RSM, ANN — ANN optimal at cement 24 vol%, silica
fume 9 vol%), D-optimal design.

### 3. Fresh concrete
- Air content 0.3–5.4 % (critical 2 %). Delayed SP addition cuts it from 2.5 to 1 %.
- Setting 6–12 h; with very high SP dosage, delays to 30–40 h are possible.
- Workability: small aspect-ratio fibers (e.g., 6×0.15 mm) tolerated to 10 vol%; 12×0.15 mm
  to 3 vol%.

### 4. Mechanical performance
- Compression: clear specimen-size effect (70×140 mm cylinders +21 % versus 100×200 mm).
  Heat treatment at 90 °C adds on average +40 %. Extreme: 800 MPa via 400 °C plus 50 MPa
  compaction (Richard & Cheyrezy).
- Stress-strain linear up to 80–90 % of peak. Elastic modulus gains only +7 % from 2 vol%
  fiber; 57→70 GPa with 250 °C treatment. E–f'c models listed (ACI 363R, Graybeal
  E=3840√f'c, etc.).
- Flexure: up to 48 MPa. **Casting direction dominates** — horizontal casting 49.6 MPa vs
  vertical 11.7 MPa (~5x, fiber orientation difference). End casting beats mid casting by
  +56 % (flow-aligned fibers). Layered casting with a high-speed chute (0.50 m/s) outperforms
  central-point casting.
- Flexural size effect: 700×150×150 mm beams −33 % versus 160×40×40 mm (small specimens
  benefit from 2-D fiber orientation).
- Flexural capacity under load-bearing friction support conditions: +30–60 %.
- Bond: twisted-fiber pullout 46.9 MPa and hooked 42.2 MPa versus straight 10.4 MPa.
  Rebar bond up to 68–70 MPa (28–56 d water curing).
- Fatigue: no failure signs after 10⁶ cycles under some conditions; three-stage strain
  growth (0–5 %, 5–95 %, 95–100 % of fatigue life). Strain modulus drops 39→10 GPa as strain
  grows toward 1.6 %.
- Impact & blast: UHPC column deflection −30 % versus NSC; impact resistance energy 4x.
  Shear governs under blast loading.

### 5. Durability
- Pores: mean size <5 nm, porosity 1–2 %. Heat treatment cuts 8.4→1.5 %. Pressurizing at
  initial setting reduces porosity −50 % (Roux).
- Absorption ~1/10 of HPC and ~1/60 of NSC. Sorptivity <0.044 kg/m²/h⁰·⁵ (~1/15 of HPC).
- Chloride diffusion coefficient 2×10⁻¹⁴ m²/s (one to two orders below HPC's 6×10⁻¹³ and
  NSC's 1×10⁻¹²). Passed charge 22 C (heat-treated) vs HPC 216 C, NSC 1736 C. Steel fibers do
  not cause short circuits in RCPT.
- Rebar corrosion rate 0.01 µm/year (limit 1 µm/year).
- Freeze-thaw: 800 cycles without deterioration; surface scaling 8–60 g/m² (limits
  1000–1500 g/m²).
- ASR expansion ≤0.02 % @600 d (threshold 0.04 %), markedly reduced by heat treatment.
- Fire: low porosity blocks vapor-pressure release → spalling vulnerability; mitigated by
  0.6 vol% PP fiber. Compressive strength rises to 300 °C, then declines.

### 6. Cost, sustainability, implementation challenges
- Cost: Europe $750–1400/m³, North America $2620/m³. Racky: material cost can fall −56 %
  against NSC including member-section reduction effects. Schmidt & Teichmann: energy −50 %.
- Six challenges: (1) mix optimization beyond trial-batch dependence, (2) high-energy
  mixers, (3) fiber orientation control, (4) drying shrinkage larger than ordinary concrete,
  (5) field heat curing, (6) absence of universal design codes.

## Position in this wiki

- A reference note drawing the antipode of the strain-hardening lineage (ECC/EGC:
  tensile ductility first, compression 30–100 MPa) versus UHPC (compressive
  strength/ductility first, 150+ MPa). Both share the grammar "dense matrix + fiber" but
  differ in the governing design variables (UHPC: particle packing, heat curing, steel fiber
  orientation; ECC: PSH design, PE/PVA interfaces).
- The data that fiber orientation swings flexural performance fivefold connect directly to
  orientation quantification studies of the [[kang-2008-fiber-orientation-impacts-on-the]] line.
- The fire spalling vulnerability and its PP-fiber mitigation logic compare with the
  elevated-temperature notes.
