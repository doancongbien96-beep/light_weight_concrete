# Mechanical Behaviour of Corroded Reinforced Concrete Beams - Part 1: Experimental Study of Corroded Beams

## Summary

Part 1 of an LMDC (Toulouse) long-term study comparing the serviceability- and ultimate-limit behaviour of four full-scale RC beams that underwent natural corrosion (13 years of salt-fog exposure) rather than electrochemically accelerated corrosion. A paired comparison of B1CL vs B2CL isolates the result that compression-side corrosion cracking has no significant effect on serviceability — deterioration originates from the tension side. Tensile rebar cross-section loss alone (up to about 20 %) cannot explain the observed 35 % stiffness loss; rebar-to-concrete bond degradation is shown to contribute nearly half of the serviceability deterioration, whereas the ultimate-capacity reduction matches the section loss exactly, confirming that bond loss does not affect load capacity. Ductility decreased by about 70 %.

## Key Contributions

- Natural-corrosion long-term experimental platform (started 1984): to avoid the poor representativeness of impressed-current accelerated corrosion, beams were exposed to a salt-fog environment for 13 years and stored while sustaining a service-load state (three-point bending moment 13.5 kN·m, safety factor 2.5 on the 28-day strength) reflecting the operating conditions of real structures. The effects of load and first cracking on corrosion progress were included among the experimental variables.
- Isolation of compression-side vs tension-side deterioration effects (B1CL vs B2CL): B2CL, whose corrosion cracks developed on the compression side, showed no serviceability change whatsoever; only B1CL, cracked on the tension side, exhibited increased mid-span deflection (from 1.8 mm to 2.35 mm at maximum load, roughly a 35 % stiffness loss) and asymmetric displacement-field behaviour. The asymmetry corresponds exactly to the corrosion distribution of the tensile reinforcement (zone F 16 %, zone G 11 % vs C 9 %, D 5 %).
- Decomposition of the causes of stiffness loss: the maximum tensile rebar section reduction measured by the residual-weight method was about 20 % near mid-span, yet standard RC theory explains at most 15 % of the stiffness loss from section loss alone. The remaining share (of the 35 %) is attributed to bond-strength degradation — measurements of secondary (corrosion) crack widths opening under increasing load (crack f1) provide evidence of local bond failure.
- Governing factor of ultimate behaviour confirmed: B1CL yield and maximum loads of 38 and 42 kN are about 20 % below control beam B1T (42 and 54 kN), matching exactly the maximum mid-span section-loss rate. Both beams failed by fracture of the mid-span (most heavily corroded) tensile reinforcement after yielding. Bond loss has no effect on ultimate capacity — provided the compression side remains undamaged, residual capacity can be assessed from tensile rebar section loss alone (a conclusion consistent with subsequent studies).
- 70 % ductility loss and the notch effect: local section defects (notches) from corrosion pitting are identified as the principal mechanism degrading rebar tensile ductility — emphasized as the most unfavourable outcome from a safety standpoint, beyond the capacity reduction.

## Methods

- Specimens: four 3000 × 150 × 280 mm RC beams (B1CL, B2CL corroded / B1T, B2T controls) with 10 mm cover (to accelerate corrosion). Mix: aggregate 5/15 mm 1220 kg/m³ + sand 820 kg/m³ + high-performance OPC cement 400 kg/m³ + water 200 kg/m³ (w/c 0.5). 28-day compressive strength 45 MPa, elastic modulus 32000 MPa. At 14 years, core strength was B1T 65.3 MPa / 36300 MPa vs B1CL 63.4 MPa / 35000 MPa — no deterioration of the concrete body itself.
- Corrosion environment: NaCl 35 g/L salt fog. Years 0–6 continuous indoor spraying; years 6–9 one-week spraying + one-week drying cycles (indoors); years 9–13 the same cycle after relocation outdoors.
- Instrumentation: eccentric loading at seven positions (B–H) to probe asymmetric behaviour (section moments 8.5–14 kN·m); displacement transducer arrays over the full span; opening-displacement transducers on secondary cracks (tension side f1/f4, compression side f2/f3/f5); rupture tests (B1CL-B1T only) recording mid-span deflection, compressive extreme-fibre strain, and tension-side strain; corrosion level determined from residual rebar weight after fracture.

## Results

- Serviceability: B1CL lost about 35 % stiffness and behaved asymmetrically, right-side displacements exceeding left (absent in the control beams and in B2CL). Secondary cracks widened with load only on the tension side (f1); the compression-side crack (f2) actually narrowed slightly. Crack widening was governed by the moment value at the given section rather than by the loading-point position.
- Ultimate: yield load 38 kN, maximum load 42 kN (B1T 42/54 kN) — about a 20 % capacity loss and about a 70 % ductility loss. Strain measurement confirmed contraction of the plastic-hinge-forming region (zone E).
- Corrosion level: mean tensile rebar corrosion F 16 %, G 11 %, C 9 %, D 5 %; maximum about 20 % near mid-span. Concrete stiffness and strength were virtually identical between corroded and control beams — the behavioural differences originate entirely from rebar-side factors (section loss + bond degradation).
