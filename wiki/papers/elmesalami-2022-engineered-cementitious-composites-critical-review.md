# 30 Years of Engineered Cementitious Composites (ECC) Research: A Comprehensive Critical Review of Raw Materials, Micromechanics, Strain-Hardening, Extreme Durability, and Global Structural Applications

## Summary

Synthesizing global research across three decades (1992~2022) since the invention of Engineered Cementitious Composites (ECC) by Professor Victor C. Li in the early 1990s, this comprehensive critical review by Professor Mustafa Sahmaran and co-workers at Hacettepe University analyzed over 1200 core publications using VOSviewer bibliometric mapping and in-depth engineering critique. The review covers the entire spectrum of ECC: 1) raw material optimization (fly ash, slag, silica fume, PVA, PE, PP, natural fibers), 2) micromechanical pseudo strain-hardening (PSH) design criteria ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1, \sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} \ge 1$), 3) uniaxial tensile strain capacities of $3\sim 12\%$ and self-controlled crack widths ($w \le 60\ \mu\text{m}$), 4) extreme durability (autogenous self-healing, 300 freeze-thaw cycle integrity, sulfate/chloride resistance, fire resistance), and 5) field infrastructure applications (bridge link slabs, seismic dampers, tunnel linings).

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive bibliometric mapping of 30-year global ECC research trends**:
   - Mapped the evolution of research topics from early mix proportions and micromechanics to sustainable low-carbon binders, extreme durability/self-healing, and 3D printing and smart infrastructure.
2. **Quantitative correlation of micromechanical PSH parameters with mixture proportions**:
   - Synthesized the interactions among fiber-matrix chemical bonding ($G_d$), frictional shear bonding ($\tau_0$), fiber tensile strength ($\sigma_{fu}$), and matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$).
3. **Comparative analysis of PVA-ECC vs PE-ECC performance spectrums**:
   - Quantified differences in mechanical behavior and durability between hydrophilic PVA-ECC (M45 standard, elongation $3\sim 5\%$) and hydrophobic high-strength PE-UHDCC (elongation $8\sim 12\%$).
4. **Design guidelines for high-volume supplementary cementitious material (HVFA/HVBS) sustainable ECC**:
   - Elucidated the carbon reduction and long-term self-healing enhancement achieved by replacing $70\sim 85\%$ of Portland cement with fly ash and slag.

## Methods

- **Literature database and analytical methodology (Sections 1–6 & Figs. 1–12)**:
  - Analysis of over 1200 papers compiled from Scopus and Web of Science.
  - VOSviewer keyword co-occurrence and co-authorship network mapping.
  - Covered technical domains:
    1. Raw materials and matrix rheology.
    2. Uniaxial tensile and compressive behavior.
    3. Dynamic impact, fatigue, and seismic damping.
    4. Long-term durability and self-healing.
    5. Field structural applications.

### 2. Micromechanical PSH criteria and self-healing mechanism synthesis
- **Significance of the energy criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1$)**: To maintain steady-state flat crack propagation, fiber complementary energy must exceed matrix tip fracture toughness, rationalizing the exclusion of coarse aggregates and the use of fine silica sand.
- **Autogenous self-healing and durability**: Restricting crack widths below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ enables unhydrated cement to continue hydration and precipitate $\text{CaCO}_3$ crystals under moisture exposure, restoring $100\%$ watertightness.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark review by Mustafa Sahmaran compiled 30 years of global ECC advancements, establishing a comprehensive guide to raw materials, micromechanics, ultra-high tensile ductility, durability, and field structural standards.
