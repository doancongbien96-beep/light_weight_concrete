## Summary
This paper, from the research team of Professor M.H. Zhang at the National University of Singapore (NUS), precisely characterized the local projectile impact damage behavior (penetration depth, front-face cratering volume, rear-face scabbing) of high-ductility PVA-HPFRCC ($f_c = 55 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $3.5 \%$), high-strength steel fiber reinforced concrete (Steel-FRC, $f_c = 110 MPa$), and normal concrete (NC 45 MPa) under the high-velocity impact loads of a 12.7 mm military armor-piercing projectile ($v = 600 \sim 750 m/s$), using live-fire gas gun impact tests and a fracture energy dissipation model. It demonstrated that the ductile multiple fine crack network of PVA-HPFRCC disperses the shock wave, reducing the front-face crater volume by **70 %** compared with normal concrete and blocking 100 % of rear-face penetration and fragment ejection.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the ductile energy absorption mechanism of PVA-HPFRCC under hypervelocity projectile impact**: converted the shear-comminution failure mode of high-strength brittle materials into a finely distributed crack dissipation mode.
- **Derivation of the critical panel thickness for suppressing rear-face scabbing**: under identical impact energy, the limiting thickness that prevents rear-face fragment ejection was reduced by 50 %, from 200 mm to 100 mm.
- **Identification of the hybrid impact protection synergy between steel and PVA fibers**: steel fibers support front-face penetration resistance while PVA fibers completely suppress rear-face tensile fragment ejection.
- **Proposal of an engineering prediction formula for defense bunker and military protective wall design**: established a penetration depth estimation equation combining projectile velocity and material ductility index.

## Methods
- Test panels: $300 \times 300 \times 100 mm$ (and 150 mm) unreinforced/fiber-reinforced slabs were used.
- Materials: normal concrete (NC 45 MPa), steel fiber reinforced concrete (Steel 1.5 vol %, 110 MPa), PVA-ECC (Kuraray 2.0 vol %, 55 MPa), Hybrid (Steel 1.0 % + PVA 1.0 %).
- Impact test: a powder-propelled gas gun was used (12.7 mm armor-piercing projectile, mass 43 g, impact velocity $680 \pm 20 m/s$).
- Instrumentation: front-face crater diameter/volume and penetration depth were measured precisely by three-dimensional optical scanning.
