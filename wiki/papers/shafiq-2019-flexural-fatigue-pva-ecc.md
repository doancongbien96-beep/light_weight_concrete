## Summary
This study evaluated the repeated deflection growth behavior, microcrack damage accumulation, and fatigue life of high-ductility PVA-ECC under flexural fatigue loading. Repeated loading was applied at a frequency of 5 Hz up to a maximum of $2 \times 10^6$ cycles under fatigue stress ratio ($S$) conditions corresponding to 60 %, 70 %, 80 %, and 90 % of the static flexural strength. Unlike conventional fiber-reinforced concrete (FRC), PVA-ECC was proven to exhibit excellent fatigue toughness by maintaining more than 20 fine multiple cracks instead of early fracture of a single crack even under fatigue loading.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the three-stage flexural fatigue deflection behavior of PVA-ECC**: characterization of deflection curves into an initial rapid deflection stage ($< 5 \% N_f$), a steady-state stable propagation stage of fine cracks ($5 \sim 90 \% N_f$), and a rapid softening stage just before fatigue failure ($> 90 \% N_f$).
- **Derivation of the fatigue strength coefficient**: a fatigue limit stress ratio of $S = 0.65$ was secured at $2 \times 10^6$ cycles (a level of 65 % of the static flexural strength).
- **Verification of crack width suppression during fatigue damage**: the crack width remained at or below $50 \mu m$ even under the maximum fatigue load, securing durability under dynamic loading.

## Methods
- **Material mix**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, FA/C = 2.2). Static flexural strength of $14.2 MPa$, 28 d compressive strength of $48.5 MPa$.
- **Test method**: $100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$ beams, four-point flexural fatigue testing machine (load ratio $R = 0.1$, amplitude control at 5 Hz).
- **Instrumentation**: dynamic displacement sensors, high-frequency data logger, crack microscope.

## Results
- Fatigue stress ratio $S = 0.60$: no failure at $2 \times 10^6$ cycles (fatigue run-out achieved).
- Fatigue stress ratio $S = 0.80$: mean fatigue life of $N_f = 85400$ cycles; deflection at failure increased ductilely up to 1.3 times the static fracture deflection.
