# Cost-efficient high performance lightweight concrete by incorporating air-entraining and lightweight aggregate

## Abstract from PDF

This study presents a novel and cost-effective mix design for high-performance lightweight concrete (HPLC) aimed at addressing the challenges of excessive self-weight and insufficient flexural strength in precast concrete structures. Air-entraining agents, lightweight aggregates, and economical alternative materials were sequentially incorporated into a baseline ultra-high- performance concrete (UHPC) matrix. The dry apparent density, mechanical properties, and microstructural characteristics of the resulting mixtures were comprehensively evaluated, and a quantitative cost – performance analysis was conducted to identify the optimal HPLC formulation. The results indicate that weight-reduction strategy of introducing 0.1 wt% α -olefin sulfonate (AOS) air-entraining agent significantly increased matrix porosity from 5.61% to 31.71%, leading to an initial lightweighting effect with a 30.7% reduction in dry apparent density. Further incorporation of 25 wt% ceramic sand lightweight aggregate enabled a favorable balance be - tween low density (1.44 g/cm 3 ) and high flexural strength (11.0MPa). Subsequent economic optimization, achieved by partially replacing steel fibers and quartz sand with 50 vol% PVA fibers and 75 wt% natural sand, reduced material cost by 30.8% while simultaneously enhancing flexural strength to 12.4 MPa at a density of 1.59 g/cm 3 . The quantitative analysis showed that this optimized mix proportion in this study exhibited the highest cost-effectiveness index of 1.360, demonstrating a synergistic balance among lightweight characteristics, mechanical per - formance, and economic efficiency. These findings highlight the proposed HPLC as a promising and practical solution for high-performance precast concrete applications.
