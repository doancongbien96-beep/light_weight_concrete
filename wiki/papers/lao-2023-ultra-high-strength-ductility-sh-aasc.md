# Micromechanical Design of Strain-Hardening Alkali-Activated Fly Ash/Slag Composites (SH-AAFSC) Simultaneously Achieving Ultra-High Compressive Strength ($120.4\ \text{MPa}$) and Ultra-High Tensile Ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 10.8\%$)

## Summary

To overcome the persistent trade-off dilemma in strain-hardening alkali-activated composites (SH-AAC)—where gaining strength diminishes ductility and achieving high ductility reduces compressive strength ($< 30\ \text{MPa}$)—researchers at Hong Kong PolyU (Prof. Jian-Guo Dai), Zhejiang University (Prof. Bo-Tao Huang), and Northwestern University (Prof. Surendra P. Shah) developed an ultra-high performance system. The authors micromechanically tailored a **ternary cementless precursor of fly ash (FA), ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS), and silica fume (SF) with fine silica sand (FSS), borax retarder, high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, $L_f = 18\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_{fu} = 3000\ \text{MPa}$), and low water-to-binder ratios ($W/P = 0.20\sim 0.24$)**. The resulting composite simultaneously achieved a compressive strength of **$120.4\ \text{MPa}$**, an ultimate tensile strength of **$17.8\ \text{MPa}$**, and a tensile strain capacity of **$10.8\%$**. Using BET nitrogen adsorption, nanoindentation, single-fiber pullout testing, and a meta-analysis of 40 global SH-AAC studies, the authors elucidated the underlying nanostructural and micromechanical mechanisms.

## Key Contributions

1. **Simultaneous achievement of $120.4\ \text{MPa}$ compressive strength and $10.8\%$ tensile ductility**:
   - Surpassed previous benchmark envelopes (102 MPa at 1.0 % strain; 13.7 % strain at 19 MPa), pioneering a design domain where compressive strength $> 120\ \text{MPa}$ coincides with tensile strain capacity $> 10\%$.
2. **Matrix pore structure refinement via 5:5 FA/GGBS ratio and silica fume**:
   - Reduced harmful capillary pores ($> 10\ \text{nm}$) to below $3.2\%$ via BET analysis, converting voids into harmless gel micropores ($< 10\ \text{nm}$).
3. **Identification of ultra-high-density (UHD) C-(N)-A-S-H phase via nanoindentation**:
   - Proved that $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heat curing accelerates GGBS hydration, maximizing the volume fraction of the ultra-high-density phase ($E > 40\ \text{GPa}$).
4. **Satisfaction of saturated strain-hardening criteria ($J_b'/J_{tip} > 3.0$ and $\sigma_0/\sigma_{cr} > 1.5$)**:
   - Maintained steady frictional fiber pullout without premature rupture due to hydrophobic PE fiber characteristics and high fiber tensile strength ($3000\ \text{MPa}$) in high-strength matrices.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–2)**:
  - Precursors: Class F fly ash (FA) + GGBS (Blaine fineness $435\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$) + silica fume (SF, 10 wt% fixed).
  - FA/GGBS ratios: F8S2 (8:2), F5S5 (5:5), F2S8 (2:8).
  - Aggregate: Fine silica sand (FSS, $d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Water-to-binder ratios: W/P = 0.20, 0.22, 0.24.
  - Activator: Solid NaOH + liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 7.0\%$, Ms = 1.2), borax retarder 1.5 wt%.
  - Fibers: UHMWPE fibers (diameter $24\ \mu\text{m}$, length 18 mm, strength $3000\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $100\ \text{GPa}$, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
- **Curing and testing procedures (Section 2.3, Figs. 1–5)**:
  - Curing: Demolded at 24 h $\rightarrow$ sealed heat curing at $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 72 h.
  - Compression: ASTM C109 $50\ \text{mm}$ cubes loaded at $1.0\ \text{MPa/s}$.
  - Direct tension: JSCE dumbbell specimens loaded at $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$.
  - Microstructure: BET nitrogen adsorption-desorption, Agilent G200 nanoindentation ($2000\ \mu\text{N}$ peak load, $10 \times 10$ grid), SEM-EDS.

### 2. Mechanisms of simultaneous ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility
- **Synergy of 5:5 FA-GGBS ratio**: GGBS supplies early high-density C-(N)-A-S-H gel for compressive strength, while spherical FA particles maintain fresh workability and serve as unreacted micro-fillers.
- **Interfacial shear stress balance**: Despite a matrix compressive strength of $120\ \text{MPa}$, the hydrophobic PE fiber interface limits bonding to pure frictional shear ($\tau_0 \approx 2.5\sim 3.0\ \text{MPa}$), avoiding fiber breakage and delivering $10.8\%$ ductility.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper broke the strength-ductility trade-off in alkali-activated composites by achieving $120.4\ \text{MPa}$ compressive strength and $10.8\%$ tensile strain capacity through micromechanical tailoring.
