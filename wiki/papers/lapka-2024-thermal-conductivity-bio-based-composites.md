# Experimental and Numerical Estimation of Thermal Conductivity of Bio-Based Building Composite Materials with Enhanced Thermal Capacity

## Summary

This study investigated the effective thermal conductivity ($\lambda_{eff}$) and volumetric heat capacity of bio-based insulating concrete composed of hemp shives aggregate, hydraulic lime/magnesia binders, and microencapsulated phase change materials (PCM) using steady-state guarded hot plate testing and 3D microstructural finite volume simulations. The authors precisely simulated thermal transport paths through the complex pore networks of porous bio-aggregates and adsorbed PCM, establishing design criteria for carbon-negative, highly insulating envelope materials with thermal conductivities of $0.065 \sim 0.115\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ ($0.065 \sim 0.115 W/(m\cdot K)$) and high latent heat storage.

## Key Contributions

- **Development of a 3D thermal transport numerical model for porous bio-concrete**: Coupled heat conduction across the solid matrix, internal pore voids, and adsorbed PCM.
- **Simultaneous achievement of ultra-low thermal conductivity and high thermal storage**: Achieved a thermal conductivity of $0.075\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ ($0.075 W/(m\cdot K)$) and specific heat capacity of $1.85\ \text{kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ ($1.85 kJ/(kg\cdot K)$).
- **Carbon-sequestration-based sustainable building envelopes**: Demonstrated net-negative cradle-to-gate embodied carbon emissions.

## Methods

- **Materials**: Hemp shives aggregate + hydraulic lime binder + microencapsulated PCM (paraffin core, $\Delta H = 135\ \text{J/g}$, $\Delta H = 135 J/g$).
- **Characterization**: Thermal conductivity via ISO 8302 Guarded Hot Plate method, specific heat via DSC, and 3D microstructural thermal modeling using $\mu\text{CT}$ ($\mu CT$) reconstructions.

## Results

- Optimized composite insulation: Thermal conductivity $0.078\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ ($0.078 W/(m\cdot K)$), bulk density $380\ \text{kg/m}^3$ ($380 kg/m3$), and 28 d compressive strength $1.85\ \text{MPa}$ ($1.85 MPa$).
- Validated high-performance envelope systems achieving 32 % reductions in annual building heating/cooling loads.
