# Physical, Mechanical, Thermal, and Solar Thermoregulation Performance of Cement-Based Thermal Energy Storage Mortar Containing Blast Furnace Slag/Capric Acid Shape-Stabilized PCM (BFS/CA)

## Summary

To reduce building heating and cooling loads and enhance indoor thermal comfort, Professors Osman Gencel at Bartin University and Ahmet Sarı at KFUPM synthesized a shape-stabilized phase change material composite (BFS/CA SSPCM, 29.5 wt.% impregnation ratio, latent heat $55.5\ \text{J/g}$) by vacuum adsorbing capric acid (CA: melting point $28.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $188\ \text{J/g}$) into porous industrial blast furnace slag (BFS, mean particle diameter $155\ \mu\text{m}$). Replacing standard silica sand by 15 wt.%, 30 wt.%, and 45 wt.% with BFS/CA in cement-based thermal energy storage mortar (CBTESM), the authors evaluated 28 d compressive and flexural strengths, thermal conductivity, DSC/TGA thermal stability, and indoor solar thermoregulation under simulated halogen radiation. Slag micropores retained capric acid without leakage across 100 thermal cycles. Mortar incorporating 45 wt.% BFS/CA (CBTESM45) achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **18.5 MPa**, latent heat capacity of **$18.5\ \text{J/g}$**, and thermal conductivity of **$0.55\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$** (a 51 % reduction compared to control mortar), damping peak indoor temperature by **$6.2\ ^\circ\text{C}$**, delaying peak arrival by **85 min**, and reducing building energy consumption by 24.5 %.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of leakage-free SSPCM utilizing industrial blast furnace slag (BFS)**:
   - Utilized waste slag as a carrier to absorb 29.5 wt.% capric acid, completely eliminating liquid leakage (100 %) via capillary tension and hydrogen bonding without expensive synthetic ceramic carriers.
2. **51 % reduction in thermal conductivity and combined insulation-latent heat storage**:
   - The low-density porous structure of BFS/CA reduced mortar thermal conductivity from **1.12 to $0.55\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$**, combining thermal insulation with latent heat storage.
3. **Verification of solar thermoregulation (6.2 °C peak damping, 85 min delay)**:
   - Solar simulator chamber experiments proved that latent heat absorption and release prevented daytime overheating and stabilized nighttime indoor temperatures.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and BFS/CA SSPCM synthesis (Tables 1–3)**:
  - Binders: CEM I 42.5 R cement (specific gravity 3.11, Blaine fineness $3650\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, 28 d compressive strength 53.5 MPa).
  - Porous carrier: Blast furnace slag (BFS: $\text{CaO}$ 34.2 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 36.5 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 12.8 %, specific surface area $4200\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, $d_{50} = 155\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Phase change material: Capric acid (CA, purity 99 %, melting point $29.4\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $188\ \text{J/g}$).
  - Vacuum impregnation: Liquid CA absorbed into BFS in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 2 h $\rightarrow$ BFS/CA SSPCM (latent heat $55.5\ \text{J/g}$).
- **Mortar mix matrix (4 mixes)**:
  - CBM (Control): Cement 1 : standard sand 3 : water 0.50 (mass ratio).
  - CBTESM15: 15 wt.% sand replaced by BFS/CA.
  - CBTESM30: 30 wt.% sand replaced by BFS/CA.
  - CBTESM45: 45 wt.% sand replaced by BFS/CA.
- **Testing program**:
  - Thermal analysis: DSC (Hitachi 7020 at 3 °C/min in $\text{N}_2$), TGA (Perkin Elmer, 25~500 °C).
  - Thermal conductivity: ISOMET 2114 thermal analyzer.
  - Mechanical testing: $40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prisms (TS EN 196-1, 28 d).
  - Solar simulation: $150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm}$ insulated chamber, 300 W halogen lamp, 6-channel K-type thermocouples.

### 2. Energy savings and environmental sustainability
- **Building envelope compliance**: CBTESM45 maintained a structural compressive strength of 18.5 MPa while meeting thermal insulation standards ($0.55\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$).
- **Carbon reduction**: Recycling metallurgical slag reduced natural sand mining by 45 % and cut operational heating/cooling carbon emissions by $18.2\ \text{kg }\text{CO}_2/\text{m}^2$ annually.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work converted industrial blast furnace slag into a shape-stabilized capric acid thermal storage carrier, maximizing insulation and latent heat buffering in cement mortar for energy-efficient building envelopes.
