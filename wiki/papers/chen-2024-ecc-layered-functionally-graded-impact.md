# Review of the structural and durability applications of ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC) and strain-hardening cementitious composites (ECC) in steel-concrete composite bridge deck (CBD) systems

## Summary

An authoritative state-of-the-art review by the team of Shilang Xu (Zhejiang University) and the University of Sydney that consolidates the flexural behavior, interfacial shear connector mechanisms, fatigue-life extension, and crack control performance of next-generation composite bridge deck systems overlaying or combining ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC) or high-ductility cementitious composites (ECC/HPFRCC) onto high-strength steel plates, in order to fundamentally solve the fatigue cracking of the densely welded details of orthotropic steel bridge decks (OSBD) widely used in long-span bridges, asphalt pavement peeling, and the tensile cracking and rebar corrosion problems of the negative-moment regions of steel-normal concrete (NC) composite bridge decks (CBD). The paper comparatively systematizes the structural mechanisms of the four major deck systems (OSBD, steel-NC, steel-UHPC, steel-ECC) and establishes theoretically and experimentally that a UHPC overlay (thickness 35–50 mm) more than doubles deck flexural stiffness while attenuating the steel plate fatigue stress amplitude by more than 50 %, whereas an ECC overlay controls saturated fine multiple cracking at 50 µm or below in negative-moment regions, completely blocking chloride ingress even without a waterproofing layer and dramatically reducing shear stud fatigue damage.

## Key Contributions

1. **Systematic comparative classification of the mechanical and durability characteristics of the four bridge deck systems**:
   - **OSBD (orthotropic steel plate)**: excellent lightness, yet fatigue life vulnerable to weld stress concentration.
   - **Steel-NC CBD**: improved stiffness, but tensile splitting cracks in the negative-moment region accelerate rebar corrosion.
   - **Steel-UHPC CBD**: 150 MPa-class compressive strength and dense impermeability (1/40 that of NC), reducing local deck deflection and steel fatigue stress amplitude by more than 50 %.
   - **Steel-ECC CBD**: 3–8 % ultra-high-ductility tensile strain-hardening confines negative-moment crack widths below 50 µm and completely blocks water ingress.
2. **Establishment of functional interfacial shear connector mechanisms**:
   - To replace the fatigue-vulnerable parts of welded headed studs, proposes shear transfer models optimized for assembled precast modular CBDs, using PBA (PBL shear connectors), embedded grooves, and bolted detachable connectors.
3. **Guidelines for negative-moment fatigue crack control and thin-plate lightweighting**:
   - Leveraging the high tensile resistance of UHPC/ECC to slim the deck slab thickness from the conventional 200–250 mm (NC) to **40–50 mm**, a reduction of more than 75 %, dramatically lightening the total bridge dead load.

## Methods

- **Classification of the analyzed bridge deck systems**:
  1. Orthotropic steel bridge deck (OSBD: Orthotropic Steel Bridge Deck).
  2. Conventional steel-concrete composite deck (Steel-NC CBD).
  3. Ultra-high-performance steel-UHPC composite deck (Steel-UHPC CBD).
  4. Ultra-ductile steel-ECC composite deck (Steel-ECC CBD).
  5. Assembled precast modular composite deck (Prefabricated CBD).
- **Consolidated performance evaluation metrics**:
  - Live-load distribution factor (LDF) models and transverse stiffness.
  - Welded-detail hot-spot stress and fatigue S-N life curves.
  - Interfacial shear-slip ($Q-s$) constitutive relations of shear connectors (studs, perfobond, bolted).
  - Crack-initiation load, ultimate flexural moment, and surface crack width ($w_{cr}$) under negative-moment loading.

## Results

### 1. Comparison of the material and structural performance of the four bridge deck systems

| Deck system | Applied material | Compressive strength (MPa) | Tensile strain (%) | Relative impermeability | Weld fatigue life | Deck thickness (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OSBD** | Steel plate alone | – | – | – (asphalt peeling) | Vulnerable ($10^5\sim 10^6$ cycles) | 12 ~ 16 |
| **Steel-NC CBD** | Normal concrete | 30 ~ 50 | 0.01 % | 1.0 (reference) | Moderate | 200 ~ 250 |
| **Steel-UHPC CBD** | Steel-fiber UHPC | **150 ~ 200** | 0.2 ~ 0.5 % | **0.025 (1/40 permeability)** | **Excellent (above $10^7$)** | **35 ~ 50** |
| **Steel-ECC CBD** | PE/PVA ECC | 40 ~ 80 | **3.0 ~ 8.0 %** | **0.05 (<50 µm cracks)** | **Excellent (cracks rendered harmless)** | **40 ~ 60** |

### 2. Structural synergy effects of applying UHPC and ECC

- **Fatigue stress relief**: compositing 50 mm UHPC on top of the steel plate reduces the local flexural stress amplitude at the rib-to-deck welds by 50–70 %, removing the risk of fatigue crack initiation at its source.
- **Crack control and self-healing**: in steel-ECC decks the single crack width is suppressed to 50 µm or below even under overloaded truck traffic, so steel plate corrosion from chloride penetration is prevented 100 %, promoting self-healing of fine cracks.

- **Overall conclusion**: for extending the service life and lightweighting of long-span bridges, adopting steel-UHPC and steel-ECC composite decks is presented as the key technological leap of future bridge engineering, with assembled precast modular joint technology that minimizes field casting identified as the key development direction.
