# Thermal and Mechanical Performance, Building Life-Cycle Economics, and Latent Heat Storage of Shape-Stabilized Phase Change Material (SSPCM) Cementitious Composites Incorporating Expanded Glass Aggregates (EGA)

## Summary

To mitigate building envelope thermal losses and HVAC energy consumption while resolving liquid leakage and strength degradation during phase transitions, Professor Yong-Rak Kim and co-workers at Texas A&M University developed smart shape-stabilized phase-change material (SSPCM) mortar composites. The material utilizes high-porosity expanded glass aggregates (EGA, Poraver, particle size $1\sim 2\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity $0.24\ \text{g/cm}^3$) vacuum-impregnated with organic paraffin PCM (phase transition temperature $23.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $180.0\ \text{J/g}$) as fine aggregate replacements ($0\sim 100\ \text{vol.}\%$). Evaluating properties via DSC, transient plane source (TPS) thermal conductivity, universal testing machine (UTM), and EnergyPlus whole-building simulations, the authors demonstrated **zero liquid leakage (0.0 %), an effective latent heat of $42.5\ \text{J/g}$, a 28 d compressive strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$, an indoor peak temperature reduction of $3.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, annual cooling energy savings of 24.8 %, and an economic payback period of 6.8 yr**.

## Key Contributions

1. **100 % shape-stabilized encapsulation using expanded glass aggregates (EGA)**:
   - Utilized capillary forces in porous recycled glass bodies to lock paraffin within micro/nano-pores, preventing liquid exudation during phase transitions above melting points.
2. **Optimal balance between thermal storage and structural strength**:
   - The 50 vol.% EGA replacement mix satisfied structural mortar compressive strength standards ($28.5\ \text{MPa}$) while providing $42.5\ \text{J/g}$ of latent heat capacity.
3. **55 % reduction in thermal conductivity and enhanced insulation**:
   - Lowered composite thermal conductivity from $1.45 \rightarrow 0.65\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$, delivering concurrent thermal insulation and latent heat buffering.
4. **EnergyPlus building energy modeling and economic validation (ROI)**:
   - Reduced cooling loads by 24.8 % and shifted peak electricity demand by 4.5 h in hot-climate zones (Houston, Texas).

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binder: ASTM C150 Type I Ordinary Portland Cement (W/C = 0.45).
  - Porous carrier: Poraver expanded glass aggregates (particle size $1\sim 2\ \text{mm}$, water absorption >75 %).
  - PCM: Organic paraffin wax ($T_m = 23.0\ ^\circ\text{C}, \Delta H = 180.0\ \text{J/g}$, vacuum loading ratio $72\ \text{wt.}\%$).
  - Aggregate replacement variables: EGA-PCM replacing natural river sand at 0 %, 25 %, 50 %, 75 %, 100 % by volume.
- **Testing program and building simulations (Sections 2.3–4.5 & Figs. 4–10)**:
  - Thermodynamics: DSC thermal analysis ($-10\ ^\circ\text{C} \leftrightarrow 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$), TPS thermal conductivity.
  - Mechanical: 50 mm cube compressive strength, flexural prism testing.
  - EnergyPlus: 3D residential building model simulated using Houston/College Station, Texas climate data.

### 2. Porous glass carrier mechanics and latent thermal buffering
- **Structural strength retention**: The rigid ceramic skeleton of sintered glass particles supported compressive loads even when the internal PCM was liquefied, preventing catastrophic strength drops.
- **Indoor thermal comfort**: Shifting the peak cooling load by 4.5 h transferred demand to off-peak tariff periods, stabilizing electrical grid operations.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that shape-stabilized PCM composites based on expanded glass aggregates achieve structural compressive strength (28.5 MPa), 24.8 % cooling energy reduction, and a 6.8 yr economic payback.
