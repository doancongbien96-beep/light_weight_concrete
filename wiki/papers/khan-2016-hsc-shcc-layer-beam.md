# Flexural Behavior of High-Strength Concrete Beams Strengthened with SHCC Layer

## Summary

To control tensile brittle failure, rapid crack width widening after rebar yielding, and compressive crushing in 75 MPa-grade high-strength concrete (HSC) beams, this study investigated the flexural capacity, load-deflection envelopes, crack dispersion, and energy dissipation of layered composite R/HSC-SHCC beams featuring a cast-in-place bottom layer of PVA-SHCC via four-point bending tests and nonlinear sectional analysis. Increasing the bottom SHCC layer thickness ($t = 25, 50, 75\ \text{mm}$, $t = 25, 50, 75 mm$, corresponding to 10 ~ 30 % of total beam height) transformed single localized macrocracks into over 80 dispersed microcracks, restricting crack widths under service loads below 0.04 mm ($0.04 mm$) and increasing the displacement ductility index by **185 %**.

## Key Contributions

- **Ductile flexural failure transition in HSC beams**: The bottom SHCC layer relieved tensile stress concentrations after rebar yielding, completely preventing premature brittle collapse.
- **Maximum crack width restriction**: Reduced crack widths by 88 % compared to plain HSC beams ($0.35\ \text{mm}$, $0.35 mm$), ensuring long-term durability against water and chemical ingress.
- **2.4-fold expansion of flexural energy dissipation**: Substantially enhanced seismic resistance against severe dynamic cyclic loads.
- **Formulation of 2-layer composite beam flexural design models**: Established moment-curvature limit state models compliant with ACI 318 provisions.

## Methods

- Eight full-scale beams ($150 \times 250 \times 2000\ \text{mm}$, $150 \times 250 \times 2000 mm$, 1700 mm span, tensile rebar ratio $\rho = 1.25\%$, $f_y = 460\ \text{MPa}$, $f_y = 460 MPa$).
- Materials: Upper HSC ($f_c = 75.4\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 75.4 MPa$) + bottom PVA-SHCC layer ($t = 0, 25, 50, 75\ \text{mm}$, $t = 0, 25, 50, 75 mm$, $f_c = 48.0\ \text{MPa}$, $f_c = 48.0 MPa$, tensile strain capacity 3.5 %).
- Testing: Four-point bending (500 mm constant moment zone), central LVDT deflection tracking, DIC crack mapping.
