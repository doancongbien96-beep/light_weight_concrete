# Self-Healing Performance Comparison of Cement-Based (PVA-ECC) vs. Alkali-Activated Slag-Based (AAS-FRC) Fiber-Reinforced Composites: Complete Closure of Microcracks ($<50\ \mu\text{m}$) at $100\%$ and the Resonant Frequency Dynamic Elastic Stiffness Recovery Mechanism

## Summary

To drastically cut the maintenance costs of structures and elucidate the self-healing mechanism of low-carbon circular resources, uniaxial tensile loading was applied to **a cement-based strain-hardening composite (C30, PVA-ECC) and an alkali-activated slag-based fiber-reinforced composite (S30, AAS-FRC)** to impose crack-width-class damage ($<50\ \mu\text{m}, 50\sim 150\ \mu\text{m}, >150\ \mu\text{m}$), and through **36 days of water-immersion self-healing curing** the optical-microscopy crack width reduction ratio ($W_c$), the impact resonant frequency (RF) stiffness recovery rate, and SEM-EDS and XRD microchemical phase analysis were precisely evaluated, establishing **complete closure of microcracks below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ at $100\%$ together with the calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and C-A-S-H gel healing-crystal precipitation mechanism** — the 2018 landmark origin paper of the team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), Prof. Jung-Il Choi, and Prof. Jin-Kyu Song of Chonnam National University**, published in the Journal of Cleaner Production.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first one-to-one comparison of the self-healing performance of cement-based vs. cement-free alkali-activated slag composites**:
   - The self-healing potentials of conventional cement-based ECC and eco-friendly cement-free AAS composites systematically contrasted from the perspectives of crack closure and dynamic elastic stiffness recovery.
2. **Demonstration of complete self-healing at $100\%$ in the crack width range up to $50\ \mu\text{m}$**:
   - In both systems, microcracks with initial widths below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ were completely closed at $100\%$ after 36 days of immersion, while the slag system (S30) also delivered a $63\%$ crack width reduction ratio for medium-sized cracks of $50\sim 150\ \mu\text{m}$, proving its superiority in physical sealing.
3. **Quantification of the dynamic elastic stiffness recovery rate ($88.5\%$) through nondestructive impact resonant frequency (RF) measurement**:
   - The resonant frequency reduced immediately after damage recovered progressively with the precipitation of healing products, with the cement system exhibiting a high stiffness recovery rate of $88.5\%$ and the slag system $75.0\%$.
4. **Elucidation of the crystallographic mechanism of the healing precipitates through SEM-EDS and XRD**:
   - The healing material filling the crack interior was confirmed to be calcite crystals of $\text{CaCO}_3$, and in the slag system C-A-S-H gel formed by secondary dissolution of the residual slag was shown to bond and provide cohesion across the crack faces.

## Methods

- **Materials and composite mixtures (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - `C30 mixture (cement-based PVA-ECC)`: OPC + fly ash (FA/C = 1.2) + silica sand (S/B = 0.36) + Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$).
  - `S30 mixture (slag-based PE-FRC)`: GGBS (specific surface area $435\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$) + liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Ms} = 1.0$) + PE fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$).
- **Damage imposition and healing test procedure (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Damage imposition: uniaxial tension applied to dumbbell-shaped tensile specimens $\rightarrow$ induction of multiple cracks followed by load release $\rightarrow$ optical microscopy crack width measurement.
  - Self-healing condition: continuous immersion in a water tank at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (measurements at 4, 7, 14, 28, and 36 days).
  - Resonant frequency (RF) measurement: steel ball impact vibration $\rightarrow$ accelerometer signal acquisition $\rightarrow$ FFT frequency spectrum analysis.
  - Microstructure analysis: crack-face sampling followed by SEM observation, EDS elemental mapping, and XRD diffraction analysis.

### 2. Interpretation of the self-healing chemical reactions and stiffness restoration mechanism
- **Calcium carbonate precipitation reaction**: during water immersion, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions leached from the cement and slag matrices react with carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) dissolved in the water, continuously precipitating insoluble $\text{CaCO}_3$ crystals at the crack entrances and inner walls.
- **Secondary slag hydration and gel expansion**: in the slag system (S30), unreacted slag particles come into contact with water at the crack faces and form additional C-A-S-H gel, inducing volumetric expansion that raises the closure ratio of medium-sized cracks ($50\sim 150\ \mu\text{m}$) by more than $10\%$ above the cement system.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a top-tier landmark origin paper in which the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University quantitatively contrasted the self-healing performance of cement-based ECC and cement-free slag-based FRC by crack width class, demonstrated microcrack closure at 100 % and dynamic elastic stiffness recovery at 88.5 %, and established the core source knowledge for designing low-carbon self-healing construction composites.
