## Summary
This is a practical study that established the design theory, full-scale flexural-fatigue testing, and field applicability of bridge deck link slabs made with high-ductility PVA-ECC, in order to eliminate expansion joints, a primary cause of rising bridge maintenance costs and structural deterioration, and to realize integrated jointless bridges. Repeated tensile-flexural deformation ($\epsilon \approx 1.5 \sim 2.0 \%$) induced by thermal expansion of the superstructure girders and flexural rotation due to live-load deflection was safely absorbed by multiple fine cracks ($w < 50 \mu m$) in the PVA-ECC layer, establishing a jointless bridge deck system that fundamentally blocks joint leakage and reinforcement corrosion.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of an integrated flexural-expansion design criterion for ECC link slabs**: formulation of the tensile strain accommodation capacity corresponding to the girder end rotation ($\theta = 0.01 rad$).
- **Verification of watertightness and fatigue durability under repeated fatigue loading ($2 \times 10^6$ cycles)**: crack width remained confined below $50 \mu m$ even under repeated flexural deformation, completely preventing penetration of deicing salt solution.
- **Analysis of field constructability and economy**: demonstrated a 60 % reduction in life-cycle maintenance cost compared with conventional steel expansion joints.

## Methods
- **Material mix**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash replacement ratio of 60 %) was used. The 28 d tensile strength was $4.8 MPa$, the tensile strain capacity $4.5 \%$, and the compressive strength $52 MPa$.
- **Specimen**: a half-scale two-span continuous precast concrete girder-deck link slab specimen (span 8.0 m) was tested.
- **Loading**: static flexural loading and $2 \times 10^6$ cycles of repeated fatigue loading were applied.

## Results
- Conventional concrete link slab: a single large crack ($w = 1.2 mm$) formed at a load of $35 kN$, followed by early shear failure.
- PVA-ECC link slab: stable flexural hinge behavior was exhibited up to a load of $85 kN$, with more than 60 fine cracks ($w < 0.04 mm$) distributed across the entire length of the slab.
