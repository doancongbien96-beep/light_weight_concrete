## Summary

To evaluate the durability performance of strain-hardening cementitious composites (SHCC) in extreme compound-disaster environments coupling fire and explosion impact, this study assessed the dynamic compression and impact fracture behavior at high strain rates ($\dot{\epsilon} = 50 \sim 200 s^{-1}$) after elevated-temperature exposure ($200 \sim 800 °C$) using an SHPB apparatus. It established that steam-pressure spalling was completely prevented through the formation of fine venting channels created by high-temperature melting ($150 \sim 230 °C$) of PVA and PE fibers, and elucidated the mechanism by which the residual dynamic strength increase factor (DIF) and energy absorption capacity are maintained even after high-temperature degradation.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of the dynamic fracture mechanism of SHCC under combined fire-impact loading**: construction of dynamic strength and strain response data under the coupled condition of high-temperature degradation and high-velocity impact loading.
- **Demonstration of spalling prevention via polymer fiber melting**: as PVA/PE fibers melt away at $200 °C$ and above, the porous capillary channels thereby formed release internal vapor pressure, achieving zero spalling even at UHPC-grade high strength.
- **Proposal of a temperature-strain-rate coupled dynamic damage constitutive relation**: establishment of a damage index incorporating an interaction term between exposure temperature ($T$) and strain rate ($\dot{\epsilon}$).

## Methods

- **Material mix**: high-strength SHCC ($W/B = 0.22$, PVA and PE fibers $2.0 vol \%$).
- **Heating test**: heating in an electric furnace to $200 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C, 800 °C$ at $5 °C/min$, holding for 2 h, then ambient cooling.
- **Impact test**: high-strain-rate compressive impact testing with a 75 mm diameter SHPB apparatus.

## Results

- Spalling behavior: ordinary UHPC suffered severe explosive spalling at $400 °C$, whereas SHCC retained its integrity without spalling up to $800 °C$.
- Residual dynamic compressive strength: after exposure at $400 °C$, room-temperature impact produced a dynamic compressive strength of $95 MPa$ ($DIF = 1.45$), while $52 MPa$ was retained after exposure at $600 °C$.
