## Summary
This study quantitatively evaluated, through accelerated corrosion testing and four-point bending tests, the reinforcement section-loss ratio ($0 \sim 25 \%$), the corrosion-induced crack width, the degradation of steel-concrete bond strength, and the rate of reduction in residual flexural capacity of reinforced concrete (RC) beams whose rebars had corroded due to chloride ingress. It demonstrated that localized pitting corrosion, relative to uniform corrosion, abruptly reduces the fracture elongation of reinforcing bars and eliminates flexural ductility, and that once the corrosion level exceeds 10 %, loss of steel-concrete bond causes the flexural capacity to plunge by more than 40 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a nonlinear correlation model between reinforcement section-loss ratio and flexural strength reduction**: quantification of the flexural moment reduction ratio as a function of reinforcement mass-loss ratio ($x_{pit}$) ($M_{res}/M_0 = 1 - \alpha x_{pit}$).
- **Elucidation of the interfacial bond-loss mechanism caused by corrosion cracking**: proof that when longitudinal cover cracks develop under the expansion pressure of corrosion products, bond strength degrades by more than 70 %.
- **Identification of the failure-mode transition in corrosion-damaged members**: demonstration of the shift from ductile flexural yielding failure to premature brittle rupture of the reinforcement or bond-slip failure.

## Methods
- **Specimens**: $100 \times 150 \times 1000 mm$ RC beams (two D10 main bars).
- **Accelerated corrosion**: immersion in $3.5 \% NaCl$ electrolyte with impressed direct current (constant-current accelerated corrosion method, $i_{app} = 100 \mu A/cm2$).
- **Measurements**: post-corrosion cover crack width, four-point bending load tests, and determination of the measured reinforcement mass-loss ratio via acid cleaning (gravimetric analysis).

## Results
- Reinforcement corrosion below $5 \%$: flexural strength reduction under $8 \%$, negligible.
- Corrosion level $15 \%$: a longitudinal cover crack width of $1.2 mm$ develops, and bond loss cuts the ultimate flexural capacity by $48 \%$ while the flexural ductility index plummets by $65 \%$.
