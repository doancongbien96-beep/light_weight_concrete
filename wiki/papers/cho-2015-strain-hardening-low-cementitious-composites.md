## Summary

To cut carbon emissions by drastically reducing Portland cement use while still achieving
excellent uniaxial direct-tensile strain-hardening, Prof. Chang-Geun Cho (Chosun University)
and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chungnam National University) characterized a low-cement strain-
hardening composite (SHFLC) — GGBS with powdered alkaline activators (calcium hydroxide
$Ca(OH)_2$ and sodium sulfate $Na_2SO_4$) as binders, reinforced with PVA fiber
($V_f = 2.0 \%$) — across activator type/dosage and water-binder ratios ($W/B = 0.25 \sim
0.35$), measuring compressive strength, direct tensile stress-strain envelopes, and multiple
fine-cracking behavior in a SAGE Advances in Mechanical Engineering paper. The optimal
cement-free mix with $Ca(OH)_2\ 10\ \%$ activator achieved 28-day compressive strength of
**34.8 MPa**, direct tensile strength **3.85 MPa**, and uniaxial tensile strain capacity
**2.85 %**.

## Key Contributions

- **Zero-cement slag-based SHFLC mix system developed**: powdered $Ca(OH)_2 / Na_2SO_4$
  replaces expensive liquid waterglass, improving field workability.
- **Governors of tensile hardening identified across W/B and activator concentration**:
  $W/B = 0.30$ forms optimal interface friction, inducing stable pullout without fiber rupture.
- **Multiple fine-crack dispersion demonstrated**: over 25 fine cracks formed by tensile
  failure (mean width ≤ $45 \mu m$).
- **75 % embodied-carbon reduction criteria established for eco-friendly infrastructure
  repair**: applicability shown for building overlays and seismic strengthening.

## Methods

- Binder: GGBS (specific surface $4200 cm^2/g$), powdered activators $Ca(OH)_2$
  (5 ~ 10 wt %), $Na_2SO_4$ (2.5 ~ 5 wt %), silica sand ($S/B = 0.40, d_{50} = 120 \mu m$).
- Fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm, tensile
  strength 1600 MPa).
- Tests: dog-bone direct tension ($0.2 mm/min$, displacement control), 28 d compressive
  strength (50 mm cubes), optical crack-width microscopy.
