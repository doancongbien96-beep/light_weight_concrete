## Summary
To maximize the autogenous self-healing speed and watertightness recovery rate of microcracks in high-ductility composites (PVA-ECC), Prof. Shunzhi Qian of Delft University of Technology and Prof. Victor Li of the University of Michigan precisely characterized the crack closure behavior, air/water permeability recovery rates, and re-tensile strength/strain-capacity recovery characteristics of nano-modified ECC blended with hydrophilic organic nanoclay (1.0 ~ 3.0 wt %), limestone powder, and slag under various healing environments (water immersion, wet-dry cycling, temperatures of $20 \sim 40 °C$). Nanoclay was shown to provide expansive gelation action and nucleation sites inside cracks, achieving **100 %** complete closure of $50 \mu m$ microcracks within 14 days of wet-dry cycling and recovering the permeability coefficient by 99 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the dual self-healing acceleration mechanism of nanoclay**: primary physical shielding through interlayer moisture absorption and swelling, plus secondary chemical healing induced by $CaCO_3$ crystal precipitation.
- **Establishment of a comparative envelope of recovery performance by healing environment**: wet-dry cycling ($12 h$ water / $12 h$ air) accelerated the healing crystal formation rate 1.8× compared with pure water immersion.
- **Achievement of a tensile strain capacity recovery of 90 % or more**: in re-tensile tests, new microcracks formed rather than reopened healed crack planes, fully preserving multiple cracking.
- **Establishment of maintenance-free waterproofing technology for underground structures and water treatment facilities**: immediate self-sealing performance upon crack formation.

## Methods
- Mix: OPC + blast-furnace slag (40 %) + limestone powder (20 %) + nanoclay (1.0, 2.0, 3.0 wt %), PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$).
- Pre-damage: introduction of $2.0 \%$ tensile strain under uniaxial tensile loading (average crack width $35 \mu m$, 35 cracks).
- Healing conditions: water immersion, wet-dry cycles, air exposure ($20 °C, 38 °C$).
- Evaluation: optical microscopy crack closure ratio, falling-head permeability test, re-tensile test.
