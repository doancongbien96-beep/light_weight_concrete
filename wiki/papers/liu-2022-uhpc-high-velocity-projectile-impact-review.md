# Penetration Resistance and Failure Mechanisms of Ultra-High Performance Concrete (UHPC) Protective Targets against High-Velocity Projectile Impact ($500\sim 1500\ \text{m/s}$): A Review of 60 % Depth-of-Penetration (DOP) Reduction and Functionally Graded (FGM) Multilayer Protective Design

## Summary

To provide counter-terrorism protection for military defense bunkers, nuclear containment buildings, and critical national infrastructure, this paper — the highest-authority landmark review origin source of International Journal of Impact Engineering 2022 by the research team of **Prof. Jian Liu (Guangzhou University) and Prof. Chengqing Wu (University of Technology Sydney, UTS)** — comprehensively analyzes **the high-velocity projectile impact (HVPI: High Velocity Projectile Impact, impact velocities $500\sim 1500\ \text{m/s}$) shock resistance of ultra-high performance concrete (UHPC: Ultra-High Performance Concrete) targets, the depth of penetration (DOP: Depth of Penetration), front cratering, and rear scabbing failure mechanics**, and consolidates the steel fiber volumetric ratio ($V_f \ge 2.0\%$), the effect of high-strength coarse aggregate incorporation, the ultra-high-pressure equation of state (EOS: Equation of State $P-\mu$), dynamic constitutive models (HJC, RHT, K&C), and functionally graded (FGM) multilayer protective design. The study demonstrated that UHPC **reduces penetration depth by $50\sim 70\%$ and suppresses the rear fragment scatter (spalling) area by more than $80\%$** compared with normal and high-strength concrete.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a database of UHPC penetration-resistance mechanics under high-velocity projectile impact ($500\sim 1500\ \text{m/s}$)**:
   - Quantitative comparison of projectile nose erosion, tunneling trajectories, and crater diameters of UHPC under armor-piercing (AP) rounds, rifle bullets, shaped charges, and high-velocity fragment impacts against normal concrete (NC) and high-strength concrete (HSC).
2. **Elucidation of the mechanism by which the critical steel fiber content ($V_f \ge 2.0\%$) suppresses front cratering and rear scabbing**:
   - The high-velocity pullout friction and crack bridging of steel fibers block at the source the rear-face fragment spallation driven by reflected tensile shock waves (spalling waves), preserving the residual protective thickness of the target.
3. **Demonstration of the projectile trajectory deflection effect of incorporating high-strength coarse aggregate**:
   - High-hardness aggregates such as basalt and corundum erode and deflect (Deflection) the rapidly penetrating warhead, reducing the depth of penetration (DOP) by an additional $20\%$.
4. **Presentation of a design framework for functionally graded multilayer protective targets (FGM Targets)**:
   - Completion of advanced structural design guidance achieving a $40\%$ saving in protective weight relative to a monolithic target through a front face (high-strength abrasion-resistant UHPC) + intermediate layer (energy-absorbing hybrid-fiber UHPC) + rear face (high-ductility fiber anti-scabbing layer) configuration.

## Methods

- **Dynamic failure mechanics and finite-element analysis modeling (Sections 2–5)**:
  - Strain-rate effects: evaluation in the high strain-rate regime of $10^2\sim 10^4\ \text{s}^{-1}$ using dynamic compression/tension amplification factors (DIF).
  - Impact hydrocode models: LS-DYNA and AUTODYN (HJC, RHT, and Karagozian & Case K&C models).
  - Effective equation of state (EOS): pore compaction (Compaction) and volumetric strain relations under ultra-high-pressure compression, $P = f(\mu)$.
- **Experimental protective performance metrics (Section 3)**:
  - Depth of penetration ($H$ or $\text{DOP}$), front crater diameter ($D_c$), rear scabbing diameter ($D_s$).
  - Ballistic limit velocity ($V_{50}$).

### 2. Interpretation of high-velocity impact absorption and damage suppression mechanisms

- **Shear resistance of the ultra-dense microstructure**: The dense nano C-S-H gel structure at W/B $\approx 0.18$ withstands the extreme hydrostatic pressure and hydrodynamic shear stress at the projectile tip, diverting the penetrator's penetration energy into plastic deformation and fragmentation of the warhead itself.
- **Suppression of reflected tensile shock waves**: When the compressive stress wave propagating into the target upon projectile impact reflects off the rear free surface and converts into a tensile wave, the high-strength steel fiber network traps the tensile stress, blocking at the source the scabbing phenomenon in which concrete fragments are ejected.

- **Overall conclusion**: By consolidating the dynamic material behavior of UHPC under high-velocity projectile impact, its failure modes, equations of state, and protective structural design techniques, this paper establishes state-of-the-art design guidance for military and civilian protective structures that reduces penetration depth by more than 60 % and blocks rear-face fragment scatter — the highest-authority landmark review origin paper in the field of impact engineering.
