# Material Strength and Strain-Hardening Behavior of an 80 MPa-Class High-Strength Cement Composite (UHDCC) with High Ductility

## Summary

To overcome the embrittlement limit imposed by rising matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) and realize ultra-high ductility in a high-strength cement matrix at the 80 MPa compressive-strength level (cement 75 % + GGBS 25 %, S/B 0.50), this study designed three composites (F12-35, F18-35, F12-30) taking as variables two types of high-strength polyethylene (PE) monofilament that differ in aspect ratio and tensile strength (PE12: diameter 16 µm, length 12 mm, strength 3030 MPa vs PE18: diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, strength 2700 MPa; fiber content 1.75 vol.%) and the water-to-binder ratio (W/B 0.30, 0.35), and characterized density, compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, tensile fracture toughness, and multiple-cracking characteristics (Prof. Bang Yeon Lee and Jeong-Il Choi, Chonnam National University; corresponding author Prof. Yun-Yong Kim, Chungnam National University). As a result, the F12-35 mix with PE12 fibers developed a 28-day compressive strength of **77.7 MPa** together with a direct tensile strength of 7.99 MPa, a tensile strain capacity of **7.90 %**, and a tensile toughness of **$0.46\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m/m}$**, successfully achieving ultra-high-strength and ultra-high-ductility performance simultaneously. In contrast, the F18-35 mix with PE18 fibers, whose aspect ratio is twice as large, showed higher first-cracking strength (5.30 MPa) and tensile strength (8.75 MPa), but the study elucidated micromechanically that its tensile strain capacity (4.42 %) fell 44 % below that of F12-35 owing to reductions in the strength performance index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$) and the fiber-bridging complementary energy ($J'_b$).

## Key Contributions

1. **World-class tensile strain capacity of 7.90 % achieved in an 80 MPa-class high-strength matrix**:
   - An extreme tensile strain capacity of 7.90 % — about 800 times that of normal concrete (0.01 %) and more than twice that of standard ECC (2–4 %) — realized in a high-strength matrix of 78 MPa compressive strength.
2. **Elucidation of the trade-off mechanism between PE fiber aspect ratio and PSH performance indices**:
   - **PE18 fibers ($L_f/d_f = 1500$)**: their high fiber strength and aspect ratio raised the first-cracking strength by 55 % ($f_i = 5.30\ \text{MPa}$), lowering the strength performance index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} = 1.65$) and suppressing the dispersion of multiple cracking.
   - **PE12 fibers ($L_f/d_f = 750$)**: a moderate first-cracking strength ($f_i = 3.42\ \text{MPa}$) combined with high frictional-slip pull-out maximized the strength performance index at **2.34**, inducing saturated fine multiple cracking and 7.90 % ultra-high ductility.
3. **Control of fiber-bridging complementary energy ($J'_b$) and strength characteristics through the W/B ratio**:
   - Lowering W/B from 0.35 to 0.30 in the F12-30 mix raised the compressive strength by 11 % to **86.3 MPa** while stably delivering a tensile strength of 8.66 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of **6.79 %**, demonstrating the optimal balance of ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility.

## Methods

- **Binder and material composition (Table 1)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (OPC, density $3.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$): 0.75.
  - Ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS, fineness $4030\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, density $2.91\ \text{g/cm}^3$): 0.25.
  - No. 7 silica sand ($d_{50} = 0.2\sim 0.3\ \text{mm}$) at 50 mass % of the binder (S/B 0.50).
  - Superplasticizer (SP), viscosity-modifying agent (VMA), defoamer.
- **Reinforcing fiber specifications (Table 2)**:
  - **PE12 fiber**: diameter 16 µm, length 12 mm, aspect ratio 750, tensile strength 3030 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
  - **PE18 fiber**: diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, aspect ratio 1500, tensile strength 2700 MPa, elastic modulus 88 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
  - Fiber volume fraction: **1.75 vol.%** common to all mixes.
- **Mix design (three mixes)**:
  - **F12-35**: W/B 0.35, S/B 0.50, PE12 1.75 vol.%, SP 0.002, VMA 0.001, defoamer 0.001.
  - **F18-35**: W/B 0.35, S/B 0.50, PE18 1.75 vol.%, SP 0.005, VMA 0.002, defoamer 0.001.
  - **F12-30**: W/B 0.30, S/B 0.50, PE12 1.75 vol.%, SP 0.009, VMA 0.001, defoamer 0.001.
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: KS L 5105 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens, 3 per mix, 28-day water curing).
  - Uniaxial direct tension: five JSCE standard dumbbell specimens (gage section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gage length 80 mm), 20 kN universal testing machine (displacement control at 0.1 mm/min), two LVDTs.
  - Crack pattern analysis: after the tension tests, precise determination of crack number, mean crack spacing, and crack width.

## Results

### 1. Density and 28-day compressive strength

| Mix ID | Fiber type | W/B | Measured density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F12-35 (reference)** | PE12 (12 mm) | 0.35 | 2.12 ± 0.01 | **77.7 ± 2.1** | optimal high-strength / ultra-high-ductility mix |
| **F18-35** | PE18 (18 mm) | 0.35 | 2.15 ± 0.01 | 79.1 ± 1.8 | high-aspect-ratio fiber mix |
| **F12-30** | PE12 (12 mm) | 0.30 | 2.18 ± 0.01 | **86.3 ± 2.5 (+11 %)** | **85 MPa-class ultra-high-strength mix** |

### 2. Uniaxial direct tensile performance and PSH strength index

| Mix ID | First-cracking strength (MPa) | Peak tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | Tensile toughness ($\text{MPa}\cdot\text{m/m}$) | PSH strength ratio ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F12-35** | 3.42 ± 0.45 | 7.99 ± 0.62 | **7.90 ± 0.75 %** | **0.46 ± 0.04** | **2.34 (highest)** |
| **F18-35** | **5.30 ± 0.52 (+55 %)** | **8.75 ± 0.71 (+9.4 %)** | 4.42 ± 0.58 % (−44 %) | 0.32 ± 0.03 | 1.65 |
| **F12-30** | 4.41 ± 0.48 (+29 %) | 8.66 ± 0.65 (+8.4 %) | **6.79 ± 0.61 %** | 0.45 ± 0.04 | **1.96** |

### 3. Surface multiple cracking and crack-width characteristics

| Mix ID | Relative crack number (%) | Mean residual crack width (µm) | Multiple-cracking behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F12-35** | **100 % (maximum multiple cracking)** | 124 µm | **saturated fine multiple cracking, 7.9 % strain capacity** |
| **F18-35** | 79 % (−21 %) | **84 µm (−32 %)** | high tensile strength mobilized, localization tendency |
| **F12-30** | 57 % (−43 %) | 178 µm (+44 %) | strain-hardening of the ultra-high-strength matrix |

- **Overall conclusion**: to secure extreme tensile ductility in a high-strength (80 MPa-class) matrix, the key is not an unconditionally larger fiber aspect ratio but a fiber design that lowers the first-cracking strength and maintains interfacial frictional slip so as to secure a strength performance index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} > 2.0$).
