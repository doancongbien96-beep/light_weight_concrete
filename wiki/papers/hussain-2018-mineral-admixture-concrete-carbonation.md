# Accelerated Carbonation Ingress Kinetics ($k\sqrt{t}$) and Mechanical Strength Enhancement in Mineral Admixture (Fly Ash and Slag) Concrete Across Water-to-Binder Ratios (W/B 0.35 to 0.65)

## Summary

To quantify the carbonation durability risks of supplementary cementitious materials replacing clinker—specifically fly ash (FA 25 wt.%) and ground granulated blast furnace slag (GGBS 40 wt.%)—Professor S. B. Singh and co-workers at BITS Pilani cast 9 concrete mixtures across three water-to-binder ratios (W/B = 0.35, 0.50, 0.65). Specimens were subjected to accelerated carbonation testing (temperature $20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, relative humidity $65\pm 5\%$, $\text{CO}_2$ concentration 4.0 %, up to 28 d continuous exposure) to evaluate carbonation penetration depth ($d_c$), carbonation rate coefficients ($k$), compressive strength, flexural strength, elastic modulus, and SEM microstructure evolution. In low-W/B (0.35) concrete, the dense matrix pore structure restricted 28 d accelerated carbonation depths to **$2.5\sim 3.0\ \text{mm}$**, maintaining parity with plain OPC control concrete (2.8 mm) despite high FA and GGBS replacement. However, increasing W/B to 0.65 caused carbonation depth to surge to **$18.5\ \text{mm}$** (a 65.2 % increase over OPC) due to the depletion of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ buffer capacity from pozzolanic reactions. Meanwhile, calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) precipitation caused an 11.0 % solid molar volume expansion that filled micro-capillary pores, increasing compressive strength by 5.1 ~ 8.6 % across all mixtures after 28 d carbonation.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of equivalent carbonation resistance in low-W/B (0.35) blended concretes**:
   - Proved that high-volume mineral admixture concrete (25 % FA or 40 % GGBS) achieves OPC-equivalent carbonation resistance ($d_c \le 3.0\ \text{mm}$) when designed at W/B = 0.35.
2. **Quantification of carbonation risks at high W/B (0.65)**:
   - Quantified that insufficient $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ buffer reserves at elevated W/B ratios accelerate carbonation rate coefficients ($k$) by up to 3.5-fold ($1.25 \rightarrow 4.35\ \text{mm/day}^{0.5}$).
3. **5~8 % mechanical strength increase via calcite pore-blocking mechanics**:
   - Clarified that the 11 % volume expansion during $\text{Ca(OH)}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3$ conversion clogs capillary pores, increasing compressive and flexural strengths.
4. **Validation of square-root-of-time diffusion model ($d_c = k \sqrt{t}$)**:
   - Confirmed that Fickian diffusion regression ($d_c = k\sqrt{t}$) reliably predicts accelerated carbonation depths ($R^2 > 0.98$).

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mixture proportions (Tables 2–3, $\text{kg/m}^3$)**:
  - Binders: Grade 43 OPC ($3250\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), Class F Fly Ash ($3800\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), GGBS ($4200\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - 3 W/B series: W/B = 0.35, 0.50, 0.65.
  - Replacement levels: Control (OPC 100 %), FA 25 wt.%, GGBS 40 wt.%.
  - Aggregates: Quartz river sand (fineness modulus 2.65), crushed gravel ($d_{max} = 20\ \text{mm}$).
- **Testing program (Section 3)**:
  - Accelerated carbonation chamber: $20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $65\pm 5\%$ RH, 4.0 % $\text{CO}_2$, exposed for 7, 14, and 28 d.
  - Carbonation depth: $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm}$ cubes split and sprayed with 1.0 % phenolphthalein.
  - Mechanical testing: Compression (ASTM C39), flexure (ASTM C78), modulus of elasticity (ASTM C469).
  - Microstructure: JEOL JSM-6390LV SEM/EDX.

### 2. Microstructural densification and degradation mechanisms
- **Pore refinement via solid volume expansion**: The molar volume of $\text{CaCO}_3$ ($36.9\ \text{cm}^3/\text{mol}$) exceeds that of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ ($33.1\ \text{cm}^3/\text{mol}$) by 11.5 %, clogging capillary pores and increasing matrix micro-hardness.
- **$\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ depletion in high-W/B matrices**: At W/B = 0.65, pozzolanic reactions consume $\text{CH}$ reserves in high-porosity networks, accelerating gaseous $\text{CO}_2$ penetration.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that W/B ratio is the governing factor for carbonation durability in blended cement concretes, and low-W/B designs ensure high carbonation resistance alongside mechanical strength gains.
