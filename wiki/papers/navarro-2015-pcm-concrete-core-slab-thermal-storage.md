# Proof of Concept of an Active–Passive Thermal Storage System Integrating Paraffin PCM (RT-21) into the Hollow Cores of a Concrete Core Slab: Heating Load Reduction of $50\%$ and Daytime Cooling Peak Mitigation of $38\%$

## Summary

To dramatically reduce building heating and cooling energy consumption and maximize indoor thermal comfort, the research team of **Prof. Luisa F. Cabeza of the University of Lleida, Spain** developed an **active–passive hybrid thermal storage slab system that integrates aluminum tubes filled with a paraffin-based phase change material (RT-21: melting point $21.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat capacity $134\ \text{kJ/kg}$) into the hollow channels of a concrete core slab**, and performed demonstration operation—winter heating thermal charging linked to a solar air collector and summer nighttime free cooling with ambient air—at a full-scale experimental cubicle in Puigverd de Lleida, Spain, **storing $13.2\ \text{kWh}$ of latent–sensible energy per day to reduce the nighttime heating load by $50\%$ and stabilizing summer daytime indoor temperature fluctuations within $\pm 1.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$**. This is a landmark 2015 original paper in Energy and Buildings.

## Key Contributions

1. **First demonstration of an active–passive fused thermal storage system combining a concrete core slab with PCM tubes**:
   - Organically coupling the high sensible heat storage capacity of concrete with the dense latent heat energy of the PCM converts the building structure itself into a large-capacity thermal energy storage (TES) device.
2. **Demonstration of winter solar-linked daily $13.2\ \text{kWh}$ clean heating thermal energy storage**:
   - During the day, warm air at $40\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$ supplied by the solar air collector charges the slab's interior PCM to a $100\%$ molten liquid state, and at night indoor air is circulated through the slab to replace $50\%$ of the heating load.
3. **38% reduction of the daytime cooling peak via summer nighttime free cooling with ambient air**:
   - Cool nighttime outdoor air ($<18\ ^\circ\text{C}$) is ventilated through the slab hollow cores to release the stored heat during the day and completely solidify the PCM, dispersing the daytime cooling electricity demand.
4. **Establishment of unsteady heat transfer and Biot number (Bi) modeling based on a network of 20 precision sensors**:
   - Fully quantifying the convective heat transfer coefficient ($h = 12.5\ \text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$) between the circulating air and the aluminum tubes embedded in the PCM together with the heat charge/discharge efficiency.

## Methods

- **Configuration of the demonstration system (Sections 2–3 & Figs. 1–6)**:
  - Test cubicle: an insulated envelope structure of $2.4 \times 2.4 \times 2.4\ \text{m}$.
  - Active slab: a hollow concrete slab ($2.0 \times 1.2 \times 0.25\ \text{m}$, mass $1200\ \text{kg}$, four hollow ducts).
  - PCM tubes: aluminum circular tubes ($\phi 50\ \text{mm}$, wall thickness 1.5 mm), filled with the paraffin Rubitherm RT-21 ($142\ \text{kg}$ filling, melting point $21\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $134\ \text{kJ/kg}$).
  - Coupled equipment: a solar air collector ($4.0\ \text{m}^2$), a variable-speed inverter blower fan, and 20-channel Pt-100 temperature sensors plus anemometers.
- **Experimental and operating modes (Section 3 & Figs. 7–8)**:
  - Mode 1 (winter charging): circulation of solar-heated warm air from 10:00 to 16:00 $\rightarrow$ latent-heat melting charge of the PCM.
  - Mode 2 (winter discharging): ventilation of indoor air through the slab from 18:00 to 06:00 $\rightarrow$ indoor radiative/convective heating release.
  - Mode 3 (summer cooling): introduction of nighttime ambient air from 00:00 to 07:00 $\rightarrow$ cold charging by solidification of the PCM.

### 2. Interpretation of the heat transfer enhancement and indoor comfort control mechanisms
- **Heat-conduction fin action of the aluminum tubes**: the low-conductivity organic paraffin ($0.2\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) is encapsulated in segments inside aluminum tubes ($200\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$), expanding the heat transfer area $3.5$-fold and resolving the charge/discharge lag.
- **Structural radiant heating effect**: the upper and lower faces of the slab release uniform low-temperature radiant heat ($22\ ^\circ\text{C}$) into the room, providing occupants with an optimal thermal comfort index (PMV $\approx 0$) without abrupt convective air currents.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the most authoritative landmark original paper in the fields of zero-energy building and structural thermal storage engineering, demonstrating—by applying a hybrid thermal storage system combining a concrete core slab with paraffin PCM to a real building—a 50% reduction of winter heating energy and a 38% mitigation of the summer peak cooling load.
