## Summary
To address the extreme logistics cost of sourcing fresh water and natural sand for island and marine infrastructure construction, a research team led by Prof. Jian-Guo Dai at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University investigated the polymerization reaction rate, chemical chloride immobilization as Friedel's salt ($3CaO\cdot Al_2O_3\cdot CaCl_2\cdot 10H_2O$), single PE fiber interfacial characteristics, and uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening behavior of a seawater-sea sand high-ductility geopolymer composite (SS-EGC) in which untreated natural sea sand (Unwashed Sea Sand) and seawater (Seawater, chloride concentration 3.2 %) were used directly and entirely (100 %) as mixing water and fine aggregate. Even under the condition of 100 % utilization of seawater and sea sand, a 28 d compressive strength of **54.8 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **7.2 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **8.2 %** were achieved.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of a fully proportioned (100 %) seawater-sea sand high-ductility geopolymer (SS-EGC) mix**: realizing zero fresh water use and a zero washing process.
- **Elucidation of the polymerization-accelerating role of $Na^+, Cl^-$ ions in seawater**: seawater activation caused 3 d early-age strength to develop 28 % earlier than in the fresh-water mix.
- **Achievement of a chemical chloride binding ratio of 85 %**: complete immobilization of free chloride ions within the dense C-A-S-H / N-A-S-H gel structure.
- **Demonstration of 8.2 % tensile strain capacity**: combined with non-corroding FRP reinforcement, establishing a construction technology for marine infrastructure with a 100-year service life.

## Methods
- Binder: slag 60 % + fly ash 40 %, alkali activator (dissolved and prepared with seawater, $W/B = 0.32$).
- Aggregate: unwashed natural sea sand ($d_{50} = 220 \mu m$, salt content 0.18 %).
- Fiber: UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm).
- Testing: dog-bone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain measurement), 28 d compressive strength, and chloride binding capacity by potentiometric titration analysis.
