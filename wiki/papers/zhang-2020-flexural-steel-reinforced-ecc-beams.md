## Summary
This study investigated the flexural behavior, load-deflection response, rebar-ECC bond interaction, and crack width development of steel reinforced engineered cementitious composite beams (R/ECC) through full-scale four-point bending experiments and nonlinear sectional analysis. Unlike ordinary RC beams, which lose stiffness prematurely after rebar yielding as tensile cracks localize and crack width surges beyond $1.0 mm$, R/ECC beams formed more than 100 fine cracks ($w < 0.05 mm$) across the entire tension zone, demonstrating improvements of 35 % and 180 % in ultimate load-carrying capacity and deflection ductility, respectively.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a nonlinear sectional analysis model for R/ECC flexural members**: formulation of a moment-curvature ($M-\phi$) relation integrating the strain-hardening envelope of the tension-side ECC with an elastoplastic rebar model.
- **Demonstration of post-rebar-yield deflection ductility index ($\mu_\Delta > 6.5$)**: the tension-side ECC carried load stably until crushing of the concrete compression zone.
- **Complete control of crack width under service load conditions**: the maximum crack width remained at or below $0.04 mm$ even at 70 % of the ultimate load, maximizing waterproofing and rebar corrosion protection performance.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $f_c = 54 MPa$, tensile strength $5.2 MPa$, $\epsilon_u = 4.2 \%$) and ordinary concrete ($f_c = 45 MPa$).
- **Specimens**: $150 \times 250 \times 2200 mm$ R/ECC and RC beams (tension reinforcement ratio $\rho = 0.8 \%, 1.4 \%, 2.1 \%$).
- **Measurements**: four-point bending test (span 1800 mm), rebar strain gauges, DIC optical displacement measurement.

## Results
- The $\rho = 1.4 \%$ R/ECC beam: peak flexural load $168 kN$ (a 34 % increase over the RC value of $125 kN$), deflection at failure $52 mm$ (a 2.9-fold increase over the RC value of $18 mm$).
- The post-yield plastic deformation zone of the tension reinforcement was confirmed to be uniformly distributed over the full beam length.
