## Summary
A study applying superabsorbent polymer (SAP) particles as an internal curing agent to fundamentally prevent the severe early-age autogenous shrinkage and microcracking that occur in high-performance ECC with a low water-to-binder ratio and high cement content. Through the absorption/release kinetics of SAP and mixing-water design, the water stored inside the SAP was made to be released gradually by the capillary negative pressure as cement hydration proceeded. Incorporation of SAP at 0.2 ~ 0.4 wt % reduced 28 d autogenous shrinkage by more than 80 % while, through increased degree of hydration and additional water supply, enhancing the tensile strain capacity ($> 4.5 \%$) of the PVA fiber composite and the microcrack self-healing efficiency.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a mechanism for complete control of early-age autogenous shrinkage in ECC**: supply of extra mixing water by SAP ($w_{ic}/b = 0.04 \sim 0.06$) dramatically reduced autogenous shrinkage strain from $650 \mu\epsilon$ to $95 \mu\epsilon$.
- **Densification of the matrix microstructure via internal curing**: the released water promoted secondary hydration of surrounding unhydrated particles, forming a dense C-S-H shell around the voids left by SAP.
- **Acceleration of microcrack self-healing performance**: upon crack occurrence, residual SAP particles re-absorbed/released moisture, accelerating calcium carbonate precipitation inside cracks.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $W/B = 0.24$, fly ash replacement ratio 60 %).
- **SAP specification**: cross-linked sodium polyacrylate particles (dry particle size $100 \sim 200 \mu m$, chloride absorption $18 g/g$, distilled-water absorption $120 g/g$).
- **Test items**: continuous autogenous shrinkage measurement with embedded corrugated gauges, uniaxial tensile testing, watertightness-recovery permeability testing, MIP porosity.

## Results
- ECC with 0.3 wt % SAP: 28 d autogenous shrinkage $110 \mu\epsilon$ (a reduction of $82 \%$ versus the reference mix), compressive strength maintained at $52.4 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $4.85 \%$.
- After introducing pre-cracks ($w = 40 \mu m$), 14 d underwater curing restored permeability-based watertightness by $98.5 \%$, demonstrating the self-healing synergy.
