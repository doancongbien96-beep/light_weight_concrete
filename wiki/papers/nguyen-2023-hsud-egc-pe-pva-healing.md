## Summary

To simultaneously realize high strength ($f_c > 80 MPa$), ultra-high ductility ($\epsilon_u > 8.0 \%$), and crack autogenous healing in eco-friendly geopolymer composites, the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University elucidated the hydration/geopolymerization reactions, the uniaxial tensile behavior, and the crack self-closure and tensile strength/ductility recovery rates of a high-strength ultra-ductile geopolymer composite (HSUD-EGC) incorporating ultrahigh-molecular-weight PE fiber and hydrophilic PVA fiber as a hybrid combination (PE 1.5 % + PVA 0.5 %) under various healing environments (water submersion, wet-dry cycling). The composite achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **84.5 MPa**, a tensile strength of **9.2 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **8.5 %**, and demonstrated a $50 \mu m$ fine-crack closure rate of **100 %** and a tensile ductility recovery rate of **92 %** after 28 days of underwater healing.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of a high-strength–ultra-ductile–self-healing triple-fusion hybrid EGC mix**: coupling the high-ductility pullout behavior of PE fiber with the interfacial nucleation action of PVA fiber.
- **Quantitative analysis of the self-healing products of a geopolymer composite**: XRD and SEM-EDS revealed that the products inside cracks are zeolite phases and N-A-S-H gel.
- **Demonstration of 8.5 % tensile strain capacity and complete crack closure**: fine cracks of $50 \mu m$ or less were 100 % healed after 14 days of water submersion.
- **Post-healing water permeability blocked 98 %**: securing the semi-permanent durability of structures exposed to harsh environments.

## Methods
- Binder: slag 60 % + silica fume 20 % + fly ash 20 %, with a high-concentration alkaline aqueous solution ($W/B = 0.22$).
- Fibers: PE fiber ($12 \mu m$, 1.5 vol %) + PVA fiber ($39 \mu m$, 0.5 vol %).
- Healing test: after imposing $3.0 \%$ pre-tensile strain, water submersion ($20 °C$) for 7, 14, and 28 days; optical-microscopy measurement of crack closure rate and re-tension testing.
