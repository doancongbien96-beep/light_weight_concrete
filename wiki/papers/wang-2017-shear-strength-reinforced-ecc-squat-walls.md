## Summary
This study elucidated the shear behavior of reinforced ECC (R/ECC) squat walls cast with PVA-ECC instead of conventional concrete, through cyclic lateral loading tests and a nonlinear truss model, to prevent the seismically brittle failure of squat walls whose low aspect ratio ($H/L = 1.0 \sim 1.5$) makes shear failure dominant. It demonstrated that R/ECC walls exclude brittle shear failure entirely because fiber bridging stress continues to transfer shear stress even after diagonal tension cracks develop, while exhibiting stable hysteresis loops with mitigated pinching and high shear strength ($V_n = 0.65 \sqrt{f_c'} A_w$).

## Key Contributions
- **Realization of a shear-ductile failure mode in squat walls**: conversion of the diagonal tension failure of conventional RC squat walls into an in-plane multiple-diagonal-microcrack shear-yielding mode of ECC.
- **Proposal of a shear strength prediction model for R/ECC**: establishment of a shear capacity equation integrating the concrete contribution ($V_c$), the horizontal reinforcement contribution ($V_s$), and the fiber bridging tensile contribution ($V_f = \sigma_0 A_w \cot\theta$).
- **Demonstration of a 3.2-fold improvement in energy dissipation capacity**: attainment of spindle-shaped hysteresis curves with dramatically alleviated pinching under cyclic loading.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $f_c = 48.5 MPa$, tensile strength $4.5 MPa$).
- **Specimens**: four full-scale R/ECC and RC squat walls ($1000 \times 1000 \times 100 mm$, aspect ratio 1.0, horizontal and vertical reinforcement ratios of 0.4 %).
- **Loading**: cyclic lateral loading test under constant axial load (drift angles of $\pm 0.25 \% \sim 3.5 \%$).

## Results
- R/ECC wall: peak shear capacity of $412 kN$ (a 66 % increase over the RC wall at $248 kN$) and a displacement ductility index of $\mu = 5.2$ (a 2.9-fold improvement over the RC wall at $\mu = 1.8$).
- Shear seismic safety above RC code requirements was secured even with the horizontal shear reinforcement quantity reduced by 50 %.
