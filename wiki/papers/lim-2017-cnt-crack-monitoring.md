# Crack Monitoring of Concrete Structures Using CNT/Cement Composites

## Summary

Paper that systematically establishes the relationship between the electrical conductivity of embeddable CNT/cement sensors and crack width/moisture state (Chonnam National University, Kim Hyung-Ki group). A serial resistance model was derived for three crack geometry cases, showing that under dry conditions the conductive network is permanently severed after cracking, whereas under saturated conditions moisture inside the crack provides the current path so that conductivity decreases gradually as a function of crack width.

## Key Contributions

1. Crack-conductivity analytical model (Eqs. 8~9): a parallel-plate (uncracked) + serial moisture-resistance (cracked) model. The relative conductivity σ_e/σ_c ranges over q_c·L/(q_c·L+2q_w·D) ~ q_c·L/(q_c·L+q_w·D) — upper/lower bounds depending on the crack geometry (opening-width distribution).
2. Dominant influence of moisture state: under drying, conductivity drops sharply regardless of crack width (CNT network severed by fiber pull-out, irreversible). Under saturation, the conductivity of water inside the crack (14~43 mS/m) acts as the path → 0.3 % CNT is strongly affected by water, whereas 0.6 % CNT has high intrinsic conductivity and stays stable — securing intrinsic conductivity is the key to improved reliability.
3. Design honoring the percolation threshold concentration: CNT 0.3/0.6 wt % (cement basis) = the critical range. Beyond it, electrical properties improve no further while strength/durability are degraded.
4. Measurement protocol: mechanical dispersion with 10 % silica fume, embedded stainless steel electrodes, DC 2-probe (polarization error minimal because the material is highly conductive), notch-controlled crack location, statistics over 30 specimens.

## Methods

MWCNTs (purity 98 %, diameter 12~40 nm, aspect ratio 250~800); w:c:sf:sp = 0.3:1:0.1:0.009. Cracks were induced in reinforced mortar beams (10×10×40 cm) by three-point bending; crack width averaged over 2~4 points with a portable microscope; stepwise width control by zip-tie tightening; simulation of prestressing tendon re-compression (axial compression 10 MPa).

## Results

- Saturated 0.6 % CNT shows a continuous decrease in relative conductivity over the crack-width range 0~0.5 mm — covering the crack-width ranges requiring repair (corrosion 0.4 mm, leakage 0.2 mm).
- Conductivity partially recovers even after crack closure (moisture path).
