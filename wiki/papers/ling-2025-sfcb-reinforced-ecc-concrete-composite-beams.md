# Mechanism of $165\%$ Amplification of Flexural Ductility and Control of Maximum Crack Width to $0.08\ \text{mm}$ in Composite Beams with Steel-FRP Composite Bars (SFCB) and Tension-Zone ECC Layer Replacement ($h_E/h$)

## Summary

To simultaneously overcome rebar corrosion deterioration in marine environments and harsh chloride exposure as well as the ductility-limited brittle failure of conventional FRP rebars, four-point flexural loading tests and **full-field strain mapping by Digital Image Correlation (DIC)** were performed on large composite beams ($150 \times 270 \times 2400\ \text{mm}$) that fuse a **Steel-FRP Composite Bar (SFCB) integrating a steel core with a glass-fiber (GFRP) jacket** and **layered ECC replacement of the lower tension zone (replacement height ratios $h_E/h = 0\%, 16.7\%, 33.3\%, 50\%$, PE-ECC $\varepsilon_u = 4.2\%$)**, demonstrating the mechanisms of **a $42\%$ increase in ultimate flexural load, a surge of $165\%$ in energy absorption capacity, and an $88\%$ reduction in maximum crack width ($0.65 \rightarrow 0.08\ \text{mm}$)** — a landmark original paper in Polymers 2025 by the research team of **Prof. Yue Cai and Yu Ling of Nanjing University of Science and Technology (NUST)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the perfect flexural synergy between the bilinear tensile behavior of SFCB and ECC strain hardening**:
   - Even after yielding of the steel core, the outer FRP jacket provides secondary elastic stiffness while the tension-side ECC confines crack opening, demonstrating stable multi-stage strain-hardening flexural behavior without load drop.
2. **Derivation of structural efficiency and the optimal thickness ($33.3\%$) as a function of ECC replacement height ratio ($h_E/h$)**:
   - Locally replacing only the bottom tension zone of $90\ \text{mm}$ ($h_E/h = 33.3\%$) achieves flexural performance equal to that of an all-ECC beam while cutting cost by $67\%$ compared with using ECC for the entire beam.
3. **Full visualization of multi-fine-crack evolution based on DIC two-dimensional strain mapping**:
   - Real-time measurement showed complete suppression of the single-large-crack concentrated failure of ordinary RC beams, with dense multiple cracks of $0.08\ \text{mm}$ or less uniformly dispersed over the entire pure flexural region.
4. **Securing post-yield stiffness and long-term chloride-exposure durability**:
   - The outer FRP shell blocks chloride penetration while the inner steel core shares ductility, laying the foundation for maintenance-free design of marine concrete structures.

## Methods

- **Material composition and composite mixtures (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Normal concrete (CC): 28-day compressive strength $46.7\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $33.85\ \text{GPa}$.
  - PE-ECC matrix: OPC 52.5R + fly ash (FA) + quartz powder (QP) + PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$).
    - ECC mechanical properties: 28-day compressive strength $45.2\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial ultimate tensile strength $4.85\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strain $4.20\%$.
  - SFCB rebar: diameter $12\ \text{mm}$ (steel core diameter 10 mm + GFRP jacket thickness 1 mm, nominal yield strength $450\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strength $820\ \text{MPa}$, secondary elastic modulus $55\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Beam specimen details and four-point flexural test (Section 2.2 & Figs. 3~4)**:
  - Beam dimensions: $150 \times 270 \times 2400\ \text{mm}$ (span 2100 mm, pure flexural region 700 mm).
  - Test variables: ECC replacement height ($h_E = 0, 45, 90, 135\ \text{mm}$), bottom tension-side SFCB reinforcement layout.
  - DIC measurement: Correlated Solutions 3D-DIC system (sampling at 2.0 Hz).

### 2. Strain compatibility between SFCB and ECC layers and flexural energy dissipation analysis
- **Maximized plastic stress lever arm**: The tensile strain hardening of ECC shifts the neutral axis upward, and the secondary elastic stiffness of SFCB continuously shares tensile force in the large-deformation regime, swelling the area under the load–deflection curve (energy absorption capacity) 2.65-fold.
- **Durability innovation through crack dispersion**: With the maximum crack width controlled to $0.08\ \text{mm}$, the ACI 224R allowable crack width ($0.3\ \text{mm}$) is overwhelmingly satisfied, hermetically blocking chloride-ion diffusion in marine splash zones.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original work in next-generation high-durability marine structural member design, simultaneously achieving a 42% improvement in flexural capacity, a 165% amplification of energy absorption, and confinement of the maximum crack width to $0.08\ \text{mm}$ through the optimal design combining SFCB composite reinforcement with 33.3% local layered ECC replacement of the tension zone.
