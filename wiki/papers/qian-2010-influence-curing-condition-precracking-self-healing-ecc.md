# Self-Healing Reliability of PVA-ECC by Precracking Age ($14\sim 56$ days) and Four Curing Environments: 95 % Flexural Stiffness Recovery Under Wet/Dry Cycles, Watertightness Restoration, and Calcite Crystal Mechanisms

## Summary

To establish the reliability of microcrack self-healing when engineered cementitious composites (PVA-ECC) experience cracks at various times and environments during service, this study precisely evaluated self-healing behavior as a function of **pre-cracking age (14, 28, 56 days; preload midspan deflection $1.5\ \text{mm}$, crack width $w \le 50\ \mu\text{m}$) and four curing environments (Condition B: ambient air, Condition C: high-concentration $\text{CO}_2$ gas, Condition D: repeated wet/dry cycles, Condition E: continuous water immersion)** through four-point flexural reloading tests, resonant frequency (RF) non-destructive stiffness recovery tracking, and environmental scanning electron microscopy (ESEM-EDS) analysis, demonstrating **under wet/dry and underwater conditions that even cracks created at the late age of 56 days recover 95 % flexural strength and fully reproduce 100 % of initial deflection ductility capacity, with complete sealing of $50\ \mu\text{m}$ cracks by secondary pozzolanic hydration products (C-S-H) of unhydrated cement/fly ash plus calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals**. This is the highest-authority landmark origin paper of Prof. Erik Schlangen and Dr. Jian Zhou of TU Delft, the Netherlands, with Prof. Shunzhi Qian (Southeast University, China, corresponding author), Construction and Building Materials, 2010.

## Key Contributions

1. **First worldwide quantification of self-healing limits and persistence versus precracking age ($14$, $28$, $56$ days)**:
   - Proved that even at ages up to 56 days, abundant unreacted latent hydraulic particles of fly ash enable perfect mechanical self-healing given sufficient water and $\text{CO}_2$ supply.
2. **Comparison of healing efficiency across four curing environments (wet/dry cycles identified as optimal)**:
   - Established that a 'wet/dry cycle' of 12 h immersion + 12 h drying promotes moisture and atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ supply alternately, maximizing calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystal growth beyond continuous immersion.
3. **Non-destructive tracking of dynamic modulus recovery above 90 % via resonant frequency (RF)**:
   - Quantified the process by which RF values that plunged at cracking recovered to 95 % of their original state after 28 days of wet/dry cycling.
4. **Establishment of standard guidelines for global smart self-healing concrete infrastructure design**:
   - Completed the decisive rationale for micromechanics-based design strictly controlling crack width to $50\ \mu\text{m}$ or below.

## Methods

- **Materials and specimen fabrication (Section 2 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Mix: standard M45 PVA-ECC (Type I cement + Class F fly ash $\text{FA/C} = 1.2$, W/B = 0.26, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ Kuraray PVA fiber).
  - Pre-damage: loading to $1.5\ \text{mm}$ midspan deflection in a four-point flexural rig then unloading ($w_{avg} = 30\sim 50\ \mu\text{m}$).
- **Self-healing curing and evaluation program (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~8)**:
  - Pre-loading ages: 14, 28, 56 days.
  - Curing environments (28-day healing): B (ambient 20℃, 50% RH), C (20 % $\text{CO}_2$), D (12h wet/12h dry cycles), E (water at 20℃).
  - Evaluation: four-point flexural reload test, ASTM C215 resonant frequency (RF), ESEM/EDS microstructure.

### 2. Interpretation of healing product chemistry and crack reopening upon reloading
- **Hybrid bridging of C-S-H gel and $\text{CaCO}_3$**: ESEM analysis showed secondary C-S-H gel bonding at the crack base while dense calcite crystals grew at the surface, forming a double seal.
- **Formation of new fine crack paths**: on flexural reloading the healed original crack lines did not fail; new multiple cracks formed in adjacent sound regions, re-expressing more than 100 % of the original deflection ductility.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is the top-level landmark origin paper of self-healing smart cementitious composite engineering, in which Prof. Shunzhi Qian and Prof. Erik Schlangen systematically established the effects of precracking age and four curing environments on PVA-ECC microcrack self-healing, demonstrating 95 % strength recovery even for 56-day late-age cracks under wet/dry cycling.
