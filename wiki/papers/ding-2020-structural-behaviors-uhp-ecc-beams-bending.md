# Flexural Behaviors of Ultra-High Performance Engineered Cementitious Composite (UHP-ECC, 125 MPa Compression, 15.5 MPa Tension, 8.5% Elongation) Reinforced Beams: 2.5-Fold Flexural Capacity Amplification, Bond Splitting Elimination, and Stirrup-Free Ductile Failure Mechanics

## Summary

To overcome premature tensile cracking, bond splitting failure, and brittle shear rupture in conventional RC beams, Professors Ke-Quan Yu of Tongji University and Shi-Lang Xu of Zhejiang University applied ultra-high-performance engineered cementitious composites (UHP-ECC, 2.0 vol.% PE microfibers)—exhibiting compressive strengths of $125.0\ \text{MPa}$, direct tensile strengths of $15.5\ \text{MPa}$, and tensile elongations of $8.50\%$—to full-scale reinforced beams (R-UHP-ECC: $1800 \times 120 \times 200\ \text{mm}$, longitudinal reinforcement ratio $\rho = 1.0\sim 2.5\%$). Through four-point bending tests, shear-flexure interaction investigations under stirrup-free conditions, and DIC crack mapping, the authors demonstrated that compared with normal concrete RC beams of identical reinforcement ratio, the first-cracking load increased by $180\%$, ultimate flexural capacity was amplified by $2.5$ times, service-load crack widths were restricted to below $0.03\ \text{mm}$, cover spalling and bond splitting were eliminated, and ductile flexural yielding was achieved without shear stirrups (stirrup-free).

## Key Contributions

1. **Structural realization of three extreme material properties (125 MPa compression, 15.5 MPa tension, 8.5% elongation)**:
   - Ultra-high ductile tensile strain-hardening was 100 % mobilized within full-scale reinforced beams, amplifying ultimate flexural capacity by $2.5$ times.
2. **Elimination of rebar-matrix bond splitting failure**:
   - Radial bursting stresses generated around high-strength deformed rebars were confined across $360^\circ$ by dense PE fiber bridging, preventing cover splitting cracks.
3. **Prevention of flexural-shear brittle failure under stirrup-free conditions**:
   - High shear-tensile carrying capacity of UHP-ECC eliminated shear failure without transverse stirrups, achieving ductile flexural failure via full tensile yielding of longitudinal steel bars.
4. **Tight control of service-load crack widths below $0.03\ \text{mm}$**:
   - Tensile deformation was distributed across over 100 fine multiple cracks, ensuring long-term durability for marine and bridge structures exposed to aggressive chloride environments.

## Methods

- **Materials and UHP-ECC mixture design (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binder: Portland cement + silica fume + fly ash (W/B = 0.18, polycarboxylate superplasticizer).
  - Aggregate: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.30).
  - Fiber: Ultra-high molecular weight PE microfiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 100\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Control group: Normal-strength concrete OPCC ($f_c = 38.5\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Beam design and flexural testing (Sections 2.3–2.5 & Figs. 4–7)**:
  - Dimensions: $1800 \times 120 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ (span $1500\ \text{mm}$, pure bending region $450\ \text{mm}$).
  - Variables: $\rho = 0.98\%$ (2-D12), $1.74\%$ (2-D16), $2.72\%$ (2-D20) / Stirrup configuration: with stirrups vs stirrup-free.
  - Loading: Four-point bending test (displacement control at 1.0 mm/min).

### 2. High-strength deformed bar bond stress and crack confinement analysis
- **Suppression of bond splitting failure**: Radial bursting stresses around rebar deformations were sustained by UHP-ECC PE fibers, maintaining integral composite bond into the strain-hardening plastic range beyond the rebar yield strength ($f_y = 460\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Tensile zone UHP-ECC moment contribution**: The entire UHP-ECC cross section below the neutral axis sustained a tensile capacity of $15.5\ \text{MPa}$, reducing required rebar cross-sectional area by $50\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: Applying UHP-ECC to reinforced concrete beams demonstrated a 2.5-fold flexural capacity enhancement, stirrup-free ductile failure, and elimination of bond splitting for advanced structural engineering.
