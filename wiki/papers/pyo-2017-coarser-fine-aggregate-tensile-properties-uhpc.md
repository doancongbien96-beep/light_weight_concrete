# Mechanism of uniaxial tensile strain-hardening ($\sigma_{tu} = 14.2\ \text{MPa}$) and $35\%$ manufacturing-cost reduction in ultra high performance concrete (UHPC) incorporating coarser fine aggregate (maximum particle size $1.2\sim 5.0\ \text{mm}$)

## Summary

To overcome the very high manufacturing cost and workability limitations of ultra high performance concrete (UHPC) arising from its use of ultra-fine silica sand ($d < 0.5\ \text{mm}$), and to accelerate commercialization, the research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University, corresponding author), Dr. Sukhoon Pyo (Korea Railroad Research Institute), and Prof. Hyeong-Ki Kim (Chosun University)** designed an **economical UHPC (2.0 vol.% straight steel fiber) introducing coarser fine aggregate with maximum particle sizes of $1.2\ \text{mm}, 2.5\ \text{mm}, 5.0\ \text{mm}$ based on the modified Andreasen-Andersen optimum particle packing model**, and precisely evaluated 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile stress-strain curves, fiber bridging efficiency, and fracture energy absorption capacity. The team demonstrated that **even under the condition of incorporating $5.0\ \text{mm}$ coarser aggregate, the material retains excellent tensile strain-hardening performance with 28-day compressive strength $148.5\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial ultimate tensile strength $14.2\ \text{MPa}$, and tensile strain $0.62\%$, while reducing raw-material cost by $35\%$**. A landmark 2017 Construction and Building Materials origin paper.

## Key Contributions

1. **First demonstration of uniaxial tensile strain-hardening behavior in UHPC incorporating coarser fine aggregate ($d_{max} = 5.0\ \text{mm}$)**:
   - Breaking the existing prejudice that increasing aggregate size impairs tensile strain-hardening by raising fracture toughness, the study achieved a high tensile strength of $14.2\ \text{MPa}$ together with strain hardening even with $5.0\ \text{mm}$ aggregate incorporated.
2. **Implementation of an ultra-dense matrix via the modified Andreasen-Andersen particle packing model**:
   - Cement, silica fume, and slag powders were matched with fine aggregates of various sizes onto the exponentially graded optimum particle size curve ($q = 0.23$), minimizing porosity and securing flowability.
3. **Analysis of straight steel fiber bridging efficiency and orientation**:
   - Demonstrated that coarse aggregate particles do not obstruct the spatial alignment of the steel fibers ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$) and that the matrix-fiber interfacial shear bond remains robust.
4. **Demonstrated $35\%$ reduction in UHPC manufacturing cost and mitigation of drying shrinkage**:
   - Replacing expensive artificial micro silica sand with ordinary natural/crushed fine aggregates that are easy to procure in the field, dramatically improving economy and volume stability.

## Methods

- **Materials and economical UHPC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Binders: Type I ordinary Portland cement + zirconia silica fume + ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder.
  - Three fine aggregates (W/B = 0.20, high-range water reducer):
    1. `Series 1 (S1)`: fine aggregate with maximum particle size $1.2\ \text{mm}$ ($d_{50} = 0.45\ \text{mm}$).
    2. `Series 2 (S2)`: fine aggregate with maximum particle size $2.5\ \text{mm}$ ($d_{50} = 0.85\ \text{mm}$).
    3. `Series 3 (S3)`: coarser fine aggregate with maximum particle size $5.0\ \text{mm}$ ($d_{50} = 1.65\ \text{mm}$).
  - Reinforcing fiber: high-strength brass-coated straight steel fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 0.20\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2600\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Mechanical test program (Section 2.3 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dumbbell-shaped specimens per the Japan Society of Civil Engineers (JSCE) specification ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, 0.1 mm/min displacement control).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (steam curing at 90 °C for 48 h, then stored at room temperature and tested at 28 days).

### 2. Interpretation of aggregate size versus steel fiber bridging interference and fracture mechanisms
- **Mitigation of the aggregate wall effect**: the steel fiber length ($13\ \text{mm}$) reaches 2.6 times the maximum aggregate size ($5.0\ \text{mm}$), so the fibers penetrate freely through the paste regions between coarser aggregates and form bridging networks.
- **Induction of multiple fine-crack propagation**: the ITZ interfaces around the $5.0\ \text{mm}$ aggregates provide deflection paths for local microcracks, delaying abrupt growth of the main crack and sustaining stable strain hardening.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-level landmark origin study in which the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University) and the Korea Railroad Research Institute established a practical UHPC design technology that achieves 148.5 MPa compressive strength and 14.2 MPa tensile strain-hardening using $5.0\ \text{mm}$ coarser fine aggregate while cutting cost by 35%.
