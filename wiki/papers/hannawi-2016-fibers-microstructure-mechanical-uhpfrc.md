# Effect of Fiber Types (Steel, Basalt, PVA, PP) on Microstructure, Gas Permeability, Ultrasonic P-Wave Velocity, and Acoustic Emission (AE) Cracking in UHPFRC Under Compressive Stress

## Summary

To systematically evaluate the role of fiber material (metallic steel, mineral basalt, synthetic PVA, PP, and BarChip) and geometry in ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC, W/B = 0.20, 28 d compressive strength 135~155 MPa), Professor Kinda Hannawi and co-workers at INSA-Rennes investigated matrix microstructure, apparent porosity, intrinsic gas permeability ($K_{int}$), ultrasonic P-wave velocity ($V_P$), and **real-time coupled gas permeability and acoustic emission (AE) crack evolution during uniaxial compressive loading**. Hydrophilic steel and basalt fibers formed dense interfacial transition zones (ITZ) with hydration products, maintaining ultra-low intrinsic gas permeability on the order of **$10^{-18}\ \text{m}^2$**, whereas hydrophobic synthetic fibers (PP, PVA) produced interfacial micro-cavities that raised permeability by 1 order of magnitude to **$10^{-17}\ \text{m}^2$**. Under compressive loading, a critical crack localization threshold occurred at 80 % of peak load ($0.8 f_c$), triggering a 100-fold surge in gas permeability. Fiber bridging by steel and PVA fibers effectively constrained crack opening, preventing catastrophic brittle bursting and promoting ductile plastic failure with sustained high AE energy emission.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantification of UHPFRC microstructure and gas transport ($K_{int}$) across fiber types**:
   - Demonstrated that steel and basalt fibers formed dense ITZs with $K_{int} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{m}^2$ and P-wave velocity $V_P \approx 5150\ \text{m/s}$, whereas synthetic fibers increased permeability 4~8-fold due to interfacial micro-voids.
2. **Coupled stress-permeability-acoustic emission real-time monitoring system**:
   - Monitored φ40 × 60 mm cylinders under uniaxial compression combined with differential-pressure nitrogen gas permeability and 500 kHz piezoelectric AE sensors, tracking crack initiation ($0.4 f_c$) $\rightarrow$ localization ($0.8 f_c$) $\rightarrow$ post-peak macro-cracking.
3. **Identification of permeability jump at $0.8 f_c$ and fiber bridging mechanics**:
   - While unreinforced UHPC failed catastrophically beyond $0.8 f_c$, steel and PVA fibers constrained crack widths below $20\ \mu\text{m}$ through pullout bridging, stably sustaining post-peak residual loads.

## Methods

- **Mixture proportions (Table 1, mass ratio)**:
  - Binders: CEM I 52.5 R cement 1.0, silica fume 0.25 (W/B = 0.20).
  - Aggregate: Fine quartz sand ($d_{50} = 250\ \mu\text{m}$) 1.10.
  - Superplasticizer: Polycarboxylate SP 0.035 (30 % solid content).
  - Five fiber types (fixed at $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    1. Steel: Straight high-tensile steel ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 2000 MPa).
    2. Basalt: Basalt micro-fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 13\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 2500 MPa).
    3. PVA: Polyvinyl alcohol fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa).
    4. PP: Polypropylene micro-fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 32\ \mu\text{m}$).
    5. BarChip: Macro-synthetic polyolefin fibers ($L_f = 48\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.8\ \text{mm}$).
- **Testing setup and instrumentation (Figures 1–3)**:
  - Gas permeability: Cembureau standard cell (nitrogen differential pressure $0.1\sim 0.5\ \text{MPa}$, Klinkenberg correction).
  - Ultrasonic P-wave velocity: 500 kHz piezoelectric transducers (LabVIEW, 50 ns resolution).
  - Real-time AE under stress: 200 kN servo frame (loading rate 150 N/s), 2-channel resonant AE sensors (40 dB threshold).

### 2. Stress-permeability coupling and non-destructive diagnostics
- **Permeability as damage index**: Permeability remained constant below $0.4 f_c$, increased 2~5-fold during microcrack initiation ($0.4\sim 0.8 f_c$), and surged 100-fold upon crack network percolation beyond $0.8 f_c$, providing an early warning precursor for structural collapse.
- **Superior bridging reliability of steel fibers**: Steel-reinforced UHPFRC demonstrated the lowest permeability rise and highest cumulative AE energy up to failure, making it ideal for nuclear containment and marine infrastructure.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work quantified the influence of diverse fiber types on UHPFRC microstructure, permeability, and acoustic emission damage evolution under compressive stress.
