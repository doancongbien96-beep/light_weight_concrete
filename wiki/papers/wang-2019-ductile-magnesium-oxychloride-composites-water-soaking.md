## Summary
A study by Zhejiang University that elucidated the compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening envelope, and fine crystalline phase evolution after underwater immersion exposure (water soaking for 7, 28, and 90 days) of a high-ductility magnesia composite (MOC-ECC) that overcame the fatal weakness of magnesium oxychloride cement (MOC), its poor water resistance (strength loss and phase 5 crystal decomposition under long-term soaking), through phosphoric acid chemical modification and micromechanical PVA fiber reinforcement. By stabilizing the $5\cdot Mg(OH)_2\cdot MgCl_2\cdot 8H_2O$ (Phase 5) crystal network responsible for superior water resistance through phosphate doping, it demonstrated that a compressive strength of **55.2 MPa**, direct tensile strength of **5.8 MPa**, and tensile strain capacity of **4.2 %** were maintained even after 90 days of long-term underwater immersion.

## Key Contributions
- **First demonstration of the post-immersion retention of tensile strain-hardening in MOC-ECC**: a PSH energy index of $J_b'/J_{tip} > 4.5$ maintained even after 90 days of soaking.
- **Suppression of phase 5 crystal decomposition in water through phosphoric acid ($H_3PO_4$) modification**: formation of an insoluble magnesium phosphate protective film on Phase 5 crystal surfaces.
- **Stabilization of the chemical interfacial bonding of PVA fibers in the magnesia matrix**: interfacial frictional stress $\tau_0 = 1.45 MPa$ maintained even during long-term immersion, preventing fiber rupture.
- **Securing the applicability of low-carbon magnesia binders to underwater infrastructure**: realization of eco-friendly high-ductility linings for marine and underground structures.

## Methods
- Raw materials: light-burned magnesia ($MgO > 85 \%$) + magnesium chloride ($MgCl_2\cdot 6H_2O$) aqueous solution (molar ratio $MgO:MgCl_2:H_2O = 5:1:13$) + phosphoric acid (1.0 wt %).
- Fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm).
- Exposure conditions: water immersion test after 28 days of air curing (7, 28, and 90 days, $20 °C$).
- Tests: uniaxial dog-bone direct tensile test ($0.2 mm/min$), 28 d compressive strength, quantitative XRD crystalline phase analysis, SEM-EDS interface analysis.

## Results
- Mechanical properties and crystalline phase evolution of MOC-ECC by underwater soaking age:

| Soaking age | Modifier | 28 d compressive strength (MPa) | Uniaxial tensile strength (MPa) | Ultimate tensile strain capacity (%) | Phase 5 retention (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Pre-soaking (Dry) | Phosphoric acid 1.0 % | 68.5 | 6.50 | 4.85 | 100 % (reference) |
| Soaking 28 days | Unmodified (Control) | 28.5 (-58 %) | 2.45 (-62 %) | 0.85 (embrittled) | 42 % (decomposed) |
| **Soaking 28 days** | **Phosphoric acid 1.0 % (MOC-ECC)** | **62.0 (-9 %)** | **6.10 (-6 %)** | **4.50 (hardening retained)** | **95 % (stable)** |
| **Soaking 90 days** | **Phosphoric acid 1.0 % (MOC-ECC)** | **55.2 (-19 %)** | **5.80 (-11 %)** | **4.20 (hardening retained)** | **92 % (stable)** |
