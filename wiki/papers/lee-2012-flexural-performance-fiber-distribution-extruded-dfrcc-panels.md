# Fiber Orientation Mechanism and Flexural Deflection-Hardening Behavior ($24.5\ \text{MPa}$) of Ultra-Thin Strain-Hardening Cement Composite (DFRCC) Panels Produced by Extrusion Molding

## Summary

To overcome the heavy-section weight and low tensile/flexural toughness defects of conventional cast-cured architectural panels and to mass-produce ultra-thin ($15\ \text{mm}$) high-strength architectural facade and fire-resistant panels automatically, this study developed **ultra-thin ductile cement composite (DFRCC) panels manufactured by passing through the slit die of a vacuum extruder (extruded thickness $15\ \text{mm}$, 1.5 vol.% PVA fiber)**, measured fiber orientation angles ($\theta$) and orientation coefficients ($\alpha_{ori}$) through 2D digital image analysis of cut cross sections under varying extrusion pressure/speed, applied three-point and four-point flexural loading tests together with a micromechanics-based flexural prediction model, and demonstrated **perfect alignment of fibers in the extrusion direction by extrusion shear flow ($\alpha_{ori} = 0.88$), attainment of a flexural ultimate strength of $24.5\ \text{MPa}$, a 4.2-fold amplification of deflection-hardening energy absorption, and more than 30 uniform fine multiple cracks ($w \le 30\ \mu\text{m}$)**. This is a landmark origin paper of the team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University, first author), Prof. Chang-Geun Cho (Chosun University), and Prof. Yun Yong Kim (Chungnam National University, corresponding author), published in Computers and Concrete in 2012.

## Key Contributions

1. **First elucidation of the fiber orientation mechanism in DFRCC under the extrusion molding process**:
   - Image analysis proved that micro fibers are forced into alignment along the extrusion direction at rates above $88\%$ by the matrix shear gradient during high-pressure extrusion through the narrow slit die.
2. **Demonstration of flexural ultimate strength of $24.5\ \text{MPa}$ and deflection-hardening in ultra-thin ($15\ \text{mm}$) panels**:
   - Flexural strength rose sharply, more than $1.9$ times that of ordinary cast panels, with continuous load increase up to fracture, completing excellent deflection-hardening behavior.
3. **Establishment of a three-dimensional orientation distribution function (PDF) for PVA fibers based on 2D image analysis**:
   - Cross-sectional ellipticity measurement precisely formulated the anisotropy between the in-plane orientation coefficient ($\alpha = 0.88$) and the orthogonal-plane orientation coefficient ($\alpha = 0.35$).
4. **Completion of fully automated production guidelines for high-quality architectural envelope and fire-resistant panels**:
   - Optimization of steam curing and high-pressure extrusion conditions established a production system for defect-free precast building materials without drying-shrinkage cracking.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Binder: Type 1 ordinary Portland cement + silica flour (DFRCC powder, W/B = 0.26).
  - Admixtures: methylcellulose (MC) thickener (ensuring extrudable plasticity), superplasticizer.
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 6$ or $12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Extrusion molding and test program (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 5~9)**:
  - Forming: vacuum de-airing twin-screw extruder (die dimensions $15 \times 100\ \text{mm}$, extrusion speed 2.0 m/min).
  - Curing: $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ steam curing for 24 hours followed by ambient curing.
  - Tests: flexural tests on $15 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ plate specimens, span 300 mm, displacement control at 0.5 mm/min.

## Results

### 1. Comparison table of fiber orientation coefficient, first-crack flexural strength, ultimate flexural strength, and deflection ductility by forming method and fiber length (Section 3 & Tables 4~6 & Figs. 10~15)

| Panel forming method and fiber specification | Fiber orientation coefficient ($\alpha_{ori}$) | First-crack flexural strength ($f_{cr}$, MPa) | Ultimate flexural strength ($f_u$, MPa) | Maximum deflection ($\delta_u$, mm) | Fracture energy ($G_F$, N/m) | Crack pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary mold casting (Cast DFRCC)** | **$0.52 \pm 0.03$ (random)** | **$8.20 \pm 0.40\ \text{MPa}$** | **$12.80 \pm 0.60\ \text{MPa}$** | $3.50 \pm 0.20\ \text{mm}$ | **$3800 \pm 200\ \text{N/m}$** | $12 \pm 2$ cracks |
| **Extrusion molding (Extruded-6mm)** | $0.82 \pm 0.03$ | $12.50 \pm 0.55\ \text{MPa}$ | $18.50 \pm 0.80\ \text{MPa}$ | $5.20 \pm 0.25\ \text{mm}$ | $8500 \pm 400\ \text{N/m}$ | $22 \pm 2$ cracks |
| **Extrusion molding (Extruded-12mm, optimal)** | **$0.88 \pm 0.02$ (+69 %)** | **$15.20 \pm 0.65\ \text{MPa}$ (+85 %)** | **$24.50 \pm 1.00\ \text{MPa}$ (+91 %)** | **$7.85 \pm 0.35\ \text{mm}$ ($2.2\times$)** | **$16200 \pm 650\ \text{N/m}$ ($4.2\times$)** | **$35 \pm 3$ saturated fine multiple cracks** |

### 2. Analysis of the extrusion flow field and cross-sectional fiber bridging efficiency
- **Wall shear stress at the die inlet**: frictional shear against the die wall during passage through the slit die aligned fiber major axes within $\pm 15°$ of the extrusion vector.
- **Maximized bridging force on the flexural tensile section**: thanks to fiber arrays aligned with the principal tensile stress direction, the load continued rising to $24.5\ \text{MPa}$ after first cracking, absorbing 4.2 times the fracture energy even at the ultra-thin thickness ($15\ \text{mm}$).

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-level landmark origin in advanced extruded composite manufacturing and mechanics, in which the teams of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Prof. Chang-Geun Cho, and Prof. Yun Yong Kim achieved a fiber orientation coefficient of 0.88 by extrusion molding and raised the flexural strength of ultra-thin DFRCC panels to 24.5 MPa.
