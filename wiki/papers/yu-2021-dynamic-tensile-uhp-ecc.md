## Summary
This study evaluated the dynamic direct tensile behavior of ultra-high strength-ultra-high ductility composites (UHP-ECC, static compressive strength $130 MPa$, tensile strength $14 MPa$) over a wide strain rate range ($\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4} \sim 50 s^{-1}$) using a high-speed hydraulic servo tensile testing machine. It was found that with increasing strain rate the dynamic increase factors (DIF) of initial cracking strength and ultimate tensile strength rose to 1.45 and 1.58, respectively, while the tensile strain capacity remained stably maintained at $7.5 \sim 8.5 \%$. Under high-speed tension, the increased interfacial frictional resistance of the PE fibers raised the dynamic tensile energy absorption density to $1.45 MJ/m3$, demonstrating outstanding performance as an impact-resistant and blast-proof structural material.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a dynamic tensile DIF model for UHP-ECC**: formulation of the expression $DIF = 1 + 0.052 \ln(\dot{\epsilon}/\dot{\epsilon}_0)$.
- **Elucidation of the strain capacity retention mechanism under high-speed tension**: compared with static conditions, the number of multiple microcracks increased by 25 % under high-speed tension with crack branching occurring.
- **Establishment of an energy absorption index for extreme protective structures**: completion of a dynamic bridging constitutive relation dissipating impact deformation energy.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: $W/B = 0.16$, silica fume 15 %, fly ash 40 %, UHMWPE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, strength 3000 MPa).
- **Testing equipment**: high-speed servo-hydraulic tensile testing machine, laser displacement sensors, and high-speed video cameras.

## Results
- $\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4} s^{-1}$: tensile strength $14.2 MPa$, strain capacity $8.45 \%$, energy absorption density $980 kJ/m3$.
- $\dot{\epsilon} = 45 s^{-1}$: tensile strength $22.5 MPa$ ($DIF = 1.58$), strain capacity $7.80 \%$, energy absorption density $1450 kJ/m3$.
