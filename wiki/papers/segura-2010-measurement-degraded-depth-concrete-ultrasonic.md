## Summary

To non-destructively and precisely measure the degraded depth ($d_c$) that develops in the surface layer of concrete members due to fire heating ($200 \sim 800 °C$) or chemical attack, this FraMCoS-7 paper from the UPC (Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya, Spain) – KAIST Prof. Jae-Hong Kim team developed a frequency-dependent penetration-depth (dispersion characteristic) analysis and a phase-velocity inversion algorithm for ultrasonic surface waves (Rayleigh waves) and validated them on full-scale fire-damaged concrete members. Through surface-wave dispersion curve analysis, it demonstrated that surface-layer degradation depths in the $5 \sim 80 mm$ range can be measured precisely within **5 %** error without destructive core extraction.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a frequency–penetration-depth dispersion model for Rayleigh surface waves**: formalizing the principle whereby high-frequency surface waves probe very shallow surface damage while low-frequency surface waves pass through deeper sound material.
- **Development of an inversion algorithm for the depth-wise elastic modulus profile ($E(z)$) of fire-damaged concrete**: Thomson–Haskell transfer-matrix inversion analysis for layered media.
- **Verified perfect agreement with measured core strengths**: a measurement accuracy of 95 % secured for carbonation/thermal-degradation depth.
- **Establishment of rapid post-fire safety diagnosis technology for tunnels and buildings**: automatic derivation of repair scope from non-destructive surface scanning alone.

## Methods
- Specimens: $150 \times 150 \times 600 mm$ concrete beam specimens, single-face heated in an electric furnace ($400, 600, 800 °C$, exposure times of 1, 2, and 4 h).
- Ultrasonic surface wave testing: broadband ultrasonic transducers ($50 kHz \sim 500 kHz$), two-channel phase-delay measurement system.
- Validation: cross-checked against stepwise core extraction, phenolphthalein carbonation depth, and ultrasonic longitudinal-wave velocity profiles.
