# Development of Ultra-Green Engineered Geopolymer Composites (RM-EGC) Utilizing 100 % Industrial Bauxite Residue (Red Mud) as Alkaline Activators: Elimination of Commercial Alkalis, Compressive Strength of $35.0\ \text{MPa}$, and Tensile Strain Capacity of $4.80\%$

## Summary

To permanently upcycle red mud (RM, $\text{pH} = 11.5\sim 12.8$, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 = 35.2\%, \text{Na}_2\text{O} = 8.5\%$)—a hazardous alkaline bauxite residue generated at hundreds of millions of tons annually during the Bayer process—and replace carbon-intensive commercial liquid activators (NaOH and $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$), Professor Lili Kan at USST and Professor Fei Wang at Tongji University developed 100 % solid-waste-based ductile composites (RM-EGC, reinforced with 1.5 vol.% PE microfibers). Utilizing the endogenous alkalinity and micro-minerals of red mud to activate ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) and silica fume, the authors evaluated 28 d compressive strength, direct tension, DIC strain fields, heavy metal leaching toxicity (TCLP), and embodied carbon. With zero chemical alkali additions (0.0), the RM-EGC achieved a **28 d compressive strength of $35.0\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strength of $4.20\ \text{MPa}$, a tensile strain capacity of 4.80 %, 100 % permanent heavy metal immobilization, and an 85 % carbon footprint reduction compared to standard cementitious ECC**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First 100 % replacement of commercial alkaline activators with bauxite residue (Red Mud)**:
   - Converted a corrosive industrial waste into the primary alkaline catalyst driving geopolymer polycondensation, realizing sustainable circular construction.
2. **Attainment of 4.80 % tensile strain capacity and strain-hardening without chemical activators**:
   - Red mud promoted slag dissolution and tailored interfacial fiber friction ($\tau_0 = 1.8\ \text{MPa}$), forming over 40 saturated multiple microcracks.
3. **Permanent encapsulation of toxic heavy metals ($\text{Cr, Pb, As}$) in 3D geopolymer nanocages**:
   - US EPA TCLP toxicity leaching confirmed that heavy metal concentrations remained well below drinking water standards.
4. **85 % carbon footprint reduction through 100 % solid-waste binders**:
   - Utilized solely industrial byproducts (GGBS + SF + RM) without cement clinker or manufactured chemical alkalis.

## Methods

- **Materials and RM-EGC mixture design (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Primary binders: GGBS ($50\sim 60\ \text{wt.}\%$) + silica fume SF ($5\sim 10\ \text{wt.}\%$).
  - Red mud: Dried RM powder (replacement ratios 0 %, 20 %, 40 %, 60 %, $d_{50} = 4.5\ \mu\text{m}$, $\text{pH} = 12.2$).
  - Activator source: Endogenous alkalis eluted from red mud + minimum mixing water (W/B = 0.32).
  - Aggregate: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fibers: High-strength PE microfibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa).
- **Testing program (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 4–6)**:
  - Direct tension: Dogbone specimens (0.5 mm/min).
  - Compressive strength: 50 mm cubes at 28 d and TCLP heavy metal leaching tests.

### 2. Red mud alkali release and microstructural densification
- **Activation via Bayer process residual alkalis**: Soluble $\text{NaOH}$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ in red mud dissociated to sever Si-O-Si and Al-O-Si bonds in slag, accelerating C-A-S-H gel precipitation.
- **Micro-filler pore refinement**: Ultrafine $4.5\ \mu\text{m}$ red mud particles densely packed gel pores, chemically fixing heavy metal ions and ensuring watertightness.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that hazardous red mud can completely substitute for commercial chemical activators, attaining a compressive strength of 35.0 MPa, a tensile strain capacity of 4.80 %, and an 85 % reduction in carbon emissions.
