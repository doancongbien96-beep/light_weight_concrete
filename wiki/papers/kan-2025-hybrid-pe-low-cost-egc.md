# Low-Cost EGC Hybridized with High- and Low-Modulus PE Fibers

## Summary

This study addresses the high cost barrier of engineered geopolymer composites (EGC) by replacing expensive high-modulus PE fibers (PE110: E 110 GPa, 3000 MPa) with lower-cost low-modulus PE fibers (PE40: E 40 GPa, 1500 MPa) across replacement ratios of 30~100 % at a total fiber content of 1.5 vol %. Even with 100 % PE40 replacement, the composite sustained a tensile strain capacity of 5.81 % while reducing unit cost to $343.9/m3.

## Key Contributions

1. **Hybrid ratio versus performance relationships**: Compressive strength increased by up to +17.1 % with increasing PE40 content (fewer fibers per unit volume reduced pore defects). Tensile strength decreased monotonically (−31.9 % at 100 % replacement), while tensile strain capacity peaked at 7.31 % (+7.3 %) at 50 % replacement.
2. **Interfacial frictional bond characterization**: Single fiber pullout yielded PE110 τ = 0.69 ± 0.16 MPa vs PE40 τ = 0.29 ± 0.06 MPa (less than half). PE110 provided initial crack bridging while PE40 accommodated subsequent crack opening.
3. **Two-stage cracking mechanisms**: PE110 dominated the stage 1 rapid multi-cracking phase, while both fibers behaved similarly in stage 2 gradual crack opening, explaining the strain capacity of high-modulus mixes.
4. **Crack width control equivalence**: PE40 controlled steady-state crack widths (94~128 μm) comparably to PE110, remaining smaller than standard ECC.
5. **Economic and environmental evaluation**: Minimized embodied carbon using zero NaOH and a low activator modulus of 1.1. Provided a comprehensive inventory of carbon, energy, and cost data (Table 5: PVA at $25.71/kg vs PE40 at $9.67/kg).

## Methods

- GGBS + silica fume binder (sub-micron SF particles of 0.138 μm for packing), waterglass + anhydrous sodium metasilicate (modulus 1.1), S/B = 0.3, W/B = 0.37.
- Curing: Heat cured at 80 °C for 24 h, followed by 4 d of air drying.
- Testing: Direct tension dogbone specimens (80 mm gauge length), single fiber pullout (5 mm embedment), and SEM.

## Results

- H0L10 (100 % PE40): Tensile strength 4.41 MPa, tensile strain capacity 5.81 %, 43 multiple cracks, average crack width ~108 μm.
- SEM analysis: Both fiber surfaces displayed matrix scratch marks and abrasions, confirming friction-dominated pullout with denser interfacial bonding around PE110.
