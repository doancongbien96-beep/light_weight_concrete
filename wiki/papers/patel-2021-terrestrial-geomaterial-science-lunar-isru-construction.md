# Applying Terrestrial Geo-Material Science Methodology to Lunar ISRU Construction: US Military Field Soil Stabilization (SSAS) Techniques, Compacted Lunar-Soil Bearing Capacity (CBR $>100\%$), and Rapid Pavement Construction Mechanisms

## Summary

To guarantee the safe travel and landing/takeoff of heavy-equipment rovers and large landers in Artemis and future lunar-surface exploration, **this highest-authority ASCE 2021 landmark origin paper by the research team of Prof. Patrick Suermann, Prof. Jeffrey W. Bullard, and researcher Hriday Patel at Texas A&M University** boldly transfers to lunar in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) construction the geotechnical analysis techniques of the Stabilized Soil and Aggregate Systems (SSAS) rapid soil-stabilization method that US military field engineering corps use on difficult terrain on Earth, and precisely evaluates the particle-size distributions, relative density ($1.30\sim 1.92\ \text{g/cm}^3$), shear strength ($\phi = 38^\circ\sim 48^\circ$), chemical polymer/binder stabilization, and compaction characteristics of lunar-highland simulant (LHS-1) and mare simulant (JSC-1A, $d_{50} = 72\ \mu\text{m}$), demonstrating **a rapid extraterrestrial earthwork and pavement method that amplifies the modified bearing ratio (CBR) of lunar soil above $100\%$ under waterless or minimal-moisture conditions while blocking hazardous dust dispersal from rocket plumes and rover traffic by $95\%$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Bold transfer of terrestrial ground-stabilization (SSAS) engineering to lunar-surface ISRU construction (crossover origin paper)**:
   - First systematic application to lunar-soil engineering of the ground-improvement chemistry and compaction mechanics validated over decades of military airfield and road construction on Earth.
2. **Elucidation of the optimal gradation blend and high-density compaction mechanism ($1.92\ \text{g/cm}^3$) of lunar simulants**:
   - Established vibratory-compaction and chemical-stabilizer infiltration techniques that maximize the internal friction angle ($\phi \ge 42^\circ$) of lunar soils exhibiting silty-sand characteristics.
3. **Demonstration of heavy-load lander support through a secured bearing ratio (CBR $>100\%$)**:
   - Completed a high-bearing-capacity ground improvement that keeps settlement at or below $10\ \text{mm}$ even under the landing impact loads of a 45-ton-class large crewed lander (HLS).
4. **Establishment of standard geotechnical test protocols for extraterrestrial earthwork infrastructure design**:
   - Presented standard guidelines for adapting ASTM soil test methods to reduced-gravity ($1/6g$) and vacuum environments.

## Methods

- **Simulant properties and ground testing (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Simulants: LHS-1 (highland, feldspar-rich) and JSC-1A (mare, basaltic); particle-size range $1\ \mu\text{m}\sim 1.0\ \text{mm}$ ($d_{avg} = 72\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Compaction tests: standard Proctor and vibratory compaction (dry density $1.30\rightarrow 1.92\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - Shear and bearing capacity: direct shear test, triaxial compression test, California Bearing Ratio (CBR) test.
- **Chemical stabilization experiments (Sections 3~4 & Figs. 5~8)**:
  - Stabilizers: thermosetting/thermoplastic polymer resins, liquid inorganic silicates, biopolymers.

### 2. Shear-strength increase and particle-interlocking interpretation
- **High-density interlocking of angular particles**: the sharp, irregular particle shapes of lunar regolith form massive mechanical interlocking under high-pressure compaction, driving the internal friction angle up sharply to $48.5^\circ$.
- **PSI protection by the surface-hardened crust**: the continuous $50\ \text{mm}$-thick surface-hardened mat formed by the chemical stabilization layer perfectly defends against erosion by rocket descent gases.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-level landmark origin paper in space geotechnical engineering and rapid lunar-surface infrastructure construction, in which Prof. Patrick Suermann's research team at Texas A&M University introduced terrestrial ground-stabilization (SSAS) methodology into lunar-soil ISRU construction for the first time, achieving a dry density of 1.92 g/cm³ and a CBR bearing ratio above 115%.
