# High-Performance Vibration-Damping Composite Combining a Polyurethane (PU) Matrix with Preplaced Aggregate: Simultaneous Attainment of a Damping Ratio of $18.1\%$ (12.7× That of Concrete) and a Flexural Strength of $8.2\ \text{MPa}$

## Summary

To fundamentally reduce floor impact noise in apartment buildings and harmful vibration in railway and bridge structures, and to overcome both the strength loss of conventional rubber-replaced concrete and the durability weakness of simple cushioning materials, **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University (corresponding author) and co-authors Kang Seok Lee, Jeong-Il Choi, Sang-Ku Kim, Bong-Ki Lee, and Jae-Seung Hwang** developed an **ultra-high-damping composite (PU-G-P) that couples a viscoelastic polyurethane (PU) resin with a two-stage preplaced coarse aggregate injection process**, and carried out damping-ratio measurement by the resonance frequency method (ASTM C215), compressive/flexural/tensile strength testing, and interfacial chemical analysis by contact angle and SEM-EDS. The paper establishes **the mechanism by which the composite achieves a damping ratio of $18.1\%$ — up to 12.7 times that of ordinary cement concrete ($1.43\%$) — together with a flexural strength of $8.2\ \text{MPa}$, while cutting the consumption of the expensive PU resin by $50\%$**: a landmark 2017 original paper in Construction and Building Materials.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of an ultra-high-damping composite with a preplaced-aggregate-based damping ratio of $18.1\%$**:
   - Achieves a damping ratio 12.7 times that of ordinary concrete ($1.43\%$) and even higher than that of neat PU resin ($17.1\%$), realizing a next-generation structural material that absorbs floor impact noise and earthquake/wind-induced vibrations.
2. **A 50 % cut in expensive resin usage and secured economy through the two-stage injection process**:
   - By first densely pre-packing coarse aggregate inside the mold and then infiltrating it with PU resin, the PU content was lowered to $50\%$ of the total volume while maximizing the viscoelastic shear energy at aggregate-to-aggregate contacts.
3. **Elucidation of the chemical bonding and interfacial adhesion mechanism between the hydrophilic PU resin and aggregates**:
   - Demonstrated the water contact angle of $51.3^\circ$ of the PU resin (hydrophilic character) and SEM-EDS elemental mapping showing formation of chemical bonding zones between Ca, Si, O and the PU organic chains, explaining high-strength flexural/tensile behavior without aggregate pull-out.
4. **Demonstration that the two-stage preplacement process (PU-G-P) outperforms ordinary mixing (PU-G-M) in vibration damping**:
   - Clarified that PU layers trapped in the micro-gaps between aggregates concentrate localized viscoelastic shear deformation under vibration, further improving damping efficiency by $5.87\%$.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix proportions (Tables 1~3 & Fig. 2)**:
  - Coarse aggregate: crushed stone (maximum size 25 mm, density $2.65\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - Fine aggregate: silica sand ($0.1\sim 0.3\ \text{mm}$).
  - PU resin: two-component polyurethane (base: polyol, hardener: MDI isocyanate, viscosity $1200\ \text{mPa}\cdot\text{s}$, density $1.05\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - Five comparison mixes:
    1. `C-G-P`: ordinary cement concrete control (W/C = 0.50, S/C = 2.0, G/C = 2.0).
    2. `PU-P`: neat polyurethane resin alone.
    3. `PU-G-P`: coarse aggregate preplaced, then vacuum-infiltrated with PU resin (two-stage process).
    4. `PU-G-M`: coarse aggregate and PU resin mixed conventionally (one-stage process).
    5. `PU-G-S-M`: combined mixing of coarse aggregate + silica sand + PU resin.
- **Test methods (Section 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 3~4)**:
  - Damping ratio and resonance frequency: $40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prisms, ASTM C215 impact resonance method (accelerometer + FFT analyzer, half-power bandwidth).
  - Mechanical tests: compression (50 mm cubes), three-point flexure ($40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$, span 100 mm), uniaxial tension (JSCE dumbbell-type specimens).
  - Interfacial microstructure: Phoenix 300 contact-angle analyzer, HITACHI S-4700 SEM-EDS.

### 2. Interpretation of the viscoelastic damping and interfacial shear dissipation mechanisms
- **Viscoelastic shear-hinge effect between aggregates**: In the preplaced process (PU-G-P), the aggregates form a three-dimensional skeleton with the PU resin confined in the narrow clearances around their contact points, so that under external vibration the minute relative displacements between aggregates are converted into high-viscosity shear-friction deformation of the PU resin, absorbing and dissipating up to $18.1\%$ of the vibration energy.
- **Prevention of interfacial debonding through hydrophilic bonding**: With its contact angle of $51.3^\circ$, the PU is hydrophilic and forms strong chemical cross-links with the silica/calcium components on the crushed-stone surface, sustaining stable damping performance under repeated vibratory loading without interfacial separation.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original work in composite materials engineering, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's team at Chonnam National University achieved ultra-high damping performance of $18.1\%$ — 12.7 times that of ordinary concrete — together with a flexural strength of $8.2\ \text{MPa}$ through a two-stage preplaced aggregate process that drastically reduces the amount of expensive polyurethane used, opening a new breakthrough for floor impact noise control in buildings and vibration control of structures.
