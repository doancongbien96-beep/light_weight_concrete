# Alkali Activation of Low-Calcium Class F Fly Ash and the N-A-S-H Gel Polymerization Reaction Mechanism: Attainment of Compressive Strength $60\sim 90\ \text{MPa}$ and Establishment of Geopolymer Hydration Reaction Theory

## Summary

To fundamentally address the massive $\text{CO}_2$ emissions and energy consumption generated in Portland cement (OPC) production, this paper systematically established for the first time in the world the hydration reaction theory of aluminosilicate inorganic polymers (geopolymers / AAMs), in which **an industrial by-product, low-calcium Class F fly ash, is activated with a high-concentration alkaline aqueous solution ($8\sim 12\text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ and sodium silicate $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$) to develop, within 24 hours under ambient/elevated-temperature ($65\sim 85\ ^\circ\text{C}$) curing conditions, a compressive strength of $60\sim 90\ \text{MPa}$**, and formulated a model of the microstructural evolution of amorphous three-dimensional networked N-A-S-H gel as functions of reaction temperature, activator concentration, and activation time: the greatest immortal landmark origin paper in the history of Cement and Concrete Research (CCR), 1999, by **Prof. Ángel Palomo of the Eduardo Torroja Institute of Construction Science, CSIC, Spain, and Prof. Michael W. Grutzeck of Penn State, USA**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first establishment of the reaction mechanism of cement-free alkali-activated fly ash binders (AAMs)**:
   - Proposed the Dissolution $\rightarrow$ Polymerization $\rightarrow$ Hardening model in which solid glassy fly ash particles dissolve, form oligomers by polymerization, and condense into an amorphous three-dimensional N-A-S-H gel network.
2. **Demonstration of ultra-high-strength development ($60\sim 90\ \text{MPa}$) within only 24 hours**:
   - Under thermal curing at $85\ ^\circ\text{C}$ with a sodium silicate activator, a compressive strength approaching $90\ \text{MPa}$ was achieved within 24 hours, completely proving industrial commercial viability as precast structural members.
3. **Optimization of activator type ($\text{NaOH}$ vs. $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3+\text{NaOH}$) and alkaline concentration (8~12M)**:
   - Established that sodium silicate solution containing dissolved silica ($\text{SiO}_2$) accelerates the reaction rate more than 3-fold relative to $\text{NaOH}$ alone and forms a dense crosslinked network.
4. **Clarification of the crystallographic difference between cement hydrate (C-S-H) and inorganic geopolymer gel (N-A-S-H)**:
   - First clarified by SEM/XRD the microstructure of a three-dimensional zeolite-precursor gel in which, in a system lacking $\text{CaO}$, $\text{Si-O-Al-O-Si}$ tetrahedral linkages are charge-compensated by alkali metal ions ($\text{Na}^+$).

## Methods

- **Materials and precursor chemical characteristics (Section 2 & Table 1)**:
  - Two fly ashes: Spanish low-calcium Class F fly ashes (`FA1`, `FA2`, $\text{SiO}_2 = 53.6\sim 55.4\%$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 24.5\sim 26.2\%$, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 = 7.5\sim 8.5\%$, $\text{CaO} < 3.5\%$).
  - Two alkaline activators:
    1. Solution A: $\text{NaOH}$ aqueous solution ($8\text{M}, 12\text{M}, 18\text{M}$).
    2. Solution B: liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 12.5\%$, $\text{SiO}_2 = 30.2\%$, $\text{Ms} = 2.4$) + $\text{NaOH}$ mixed solution ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ concentration $6\sim 10\ \text{wt.}\%$, $\text{Ms} = 1.0\sim 1.5$).
  - Liquid/solid ratio (L/S): $0.25\sim 0.35$ (securing excellent molding workability).
- **Curing and mechanical/microstructural testing (Section 2 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Curing temperatures: sealed chambers at $65\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $85\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (heating times $2, 5, 24\ \text{h}$).
  - Compressive strength: $10 \times 10 \times 60\ \text{mm}$ and $40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prismatic specimens (ASTM C109).
  - Microstructure: SEM (field-emission scanning electron microscopy) and wide-angle XRD analysis.

### 2. Interpretation of alkali-activation reaction kinetics and microstructural mechanisms
- **Crosslink promotion by dissolved silica**: the $\text{SiO}_4^{4-}$ monomers supplied by sodium silicate react immediately with the $\text{AlO}_4^{5-}$ dissolved out of the fly ash particles, forming ring-type and chain-type oligomers and thereby accelerating N-A-S-H gel formation 3-fold.
- **Overcoming activation energy through thermal curing**: thermal energy at $85\ ^\circ\text{C}$ exponentially increases the rate of severing the $\text{Si-O-Si}$ covalent bonds of the spherical glassy particles of solid fly ash, achieving a reaction degree above $70\%$ within 24 hours.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper, in which Prof. Ángel Palomo first established the alkali-activation reaction theory of low-calcium Class F fly ash together with the N-A-S-H gel microstructure formation model, and achieved a compressive strength of 90 MPa within 24 hours, opening the horizon of modern geopolymer and AAMs research, stands as the greatest and most authoritative landmark classic paper in history.
