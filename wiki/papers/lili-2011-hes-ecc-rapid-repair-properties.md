# High-Early-Strength Strain-Hardening Engineered Cementitious Composite (HES-ECC) for Emergency Repair: Concurrent Attainment of 4-Hour Compressive Strength $27.6\ \text{MPa}$ and Tensile Strain Capacity $3.5\%$ plus Bridge Deck Field Application

## Summary

For emergency concrete repair sites such as highway bridge decks and airport runways where traffic closure time must be minimized, this is a landmark 2011 original paper in the ACI Materials Journal by **Prof. Mo Li and Victor C. Li, University of Michigan**, in which a **high-early-strength engineered cementitious composite (HES-ECC: High-Early-Strength Engineered Cementitious Composites, PVA fiber 2.0 vol.%)** combining **Type III rapid-hardening Portland cement and an accelerating admixture (calcium-nitrite based)** was designed on a micromechanical basis to meet the **fast-track requirement of traffic reopening within 4 hours** while fundamentally solving the brittle failure and early-age cracking/spalling problems of conventional repair concrete, and the material's **compressive strength over ages from 4 hours to 60 days, uniaxial tensile (strain-hardening) behavior, three-point flexural (deflection-hardening) behavior, free drying shrinkage strain (RH = 0~93 %, 7 levels), and crack-width self-control characteristics** were systematically quantified. The results show that HES-ECC attained a **4-hour compressive strength of $27.6\ \text{MPa}$ (exceeding the NJDOT requirement of $20.7\ \text{MPa}$)**, a **4-hour tensile strength of $3.5\ \text{MPa}$ and tensile strain capacity of $3.4\%$ (crack width $< 100\ \mu\text{m}$)**, and a **28-day compressive strength of $55.6\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strength of $5.0\ \text{MPa}$, and MOR of $15.1\ \text{MPa}$**; the drying shrinkage strain is similar to that of same-cement-family HES-concrete, but crack-free robustness through strain-hardening toughness (elimination of Cracking Potential) was confirmed, and the material was successfully field-applied to Michigan DOT (MDOT) bridge deck patch repair.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first micromechanics-based development of ultra-early-strength ECC achieving 4-hour strain hardening**:
   - The Type III cement + calcium nitrite accelerator + PVA 2.0 vol.% combination simultaneously satisfies both the $J_b'/J_{tip}$ energy criterion and the $\sigma_0/\sigma_{cr}$ stress criterion even at ultra-early ages.
2. **Construction of an age-dependent property-evolution database for compressive, tensile, flexural, and shrinkage properties from 4 hours to 60 days**:
   - Tensile strain capacity: 4 hours at $3.4\% \rightarrow$ 24 hours at $3.5\% \rightarrow$ 28 days at $3.1\%$ (a slight decrease due to matrix densification with age).
   - Compressive strength: 4 hours at $27.6 \rightarrow$ 24 hours at $39.9 \rightarrow$ 28 days at $55.6\ \text{MPa}$ (rapid strength development).
3. **Quantification of the permeability suppression mechanism via crack-width self-control ($< 100\ \mu\text{m}$)**:
   - At crack widths of $100\ \mu\text{m}$ or less, the permeability coefficient equals that of the uncracked state ($\sim 10^{-11}\ \text{m/s}$), preventing rebar corrosion and freeze-thaw deterioration of repaired regions at the source.
4. **Report of a successful MDOT bridge deck field patch-repair application**:
   - After HES-ECC patch repair of the Grove Street bridge deck in Michigan, traffic was reopened within only 4 hours, with defect-free service confirmed for more than 2 years.

## Methods

- **HES-ECC mix (Table 1, $\text{kg/m}^3$)**:
  - Type III rapid-hardening Portland cement (ASTM C150), fly ash (ASTM C618 Class F, 1.2 times the cement mass), silica sand F110 ($d_{max} = 250\ \mu\text{m}$, S/C = 0.8).
  - W/C = 0.58, high-range water reducer (HRWR), calcium nitrite accelerator (DCI).
  - PVA fiber: Kuraray REC15, diameter $39\ \mu\text{m}$, length $8\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength $1620\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 42.8\ \text{GPa}$, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$.
  - Surface oiling (Oiling) of 1.2 % applied: suppresses interfacial bonding to induce the fiber pull-out mode.
- **Curing condition**: air curing ($23 \pm 3\ ^\circ\text{C}$, RH $50 \pm 5\%$) — simulating field repair conditions.
- **Test program (Sections 2~4 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Compression: $\phi 76.2\ \text{mm} \times 152.4\ \text{mm}$ cylinders, ASTM C39 (4 hours to 60 days).
  - Uniaxial tension: $228.6\ \text{mm} \times 76.2\ \text{mm} \times 12.7\ \text{mm}$ plate specimens, displacement rate $0.005\ \text{mm/s}$, LVDT gauge length $101.6\ \text{mm}$.
  - Three-point bending: $406.4\ \text{mm} \times 76.2\ \text{mm} \times 101.6\ \text{mm}$, span $304.8\ \text{mm}$, $0.02\ \text{mm/s}$.
  - Drying shrinkage: $200\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}$ prisms, 7-level RH control using saturated salt solutions (RH = 0, 12, 33, 66, 75, 85, 93 %), ASTM C157/C596.

### 2. Interpretation of Drying Shrinkage and Cracking Potential
- **Drying shrinkage of HES-ECC**: final drying shrinkage of about $1200\ \mu\varepsilon$ at RH = 50 % (a $50\%$ increase relative to HES-concrete at $800\ \mu\varepsilon$, attributable to the absence of aggregate and the high cement content).
- **Elimination of cracking potential**: the drying shrinkage strain of HES-ECC ($1200\ \mu\varepsilon$) amounts to only about $4\%$ of its tensile strain capacity ($31,000\ \mu\varepsilon$), so the cracking potential due to free shrinkage is virtually eliminated. In contrast, HES-concrete exhibits $800\ \mu\varepsilon$ of shrinkage, eight times its elastic tensile strain capacity ($\sim 100\ \mu\varepsilon$), making early-age shrinkage cracking inevitable.

- **Overall conclusion**: this immortal ACI landmark original paper reports that Prof. Victor C. Li's research team at the University of Michigan developed Type III rapid-hardening-cement-based HES-ECC for the first time in the world, simultaneously meeting the 4-hour fast-track emergency repair requirement and tensile strain-hardening durability, and successfully demonstrated its field application on an MDOT bridge.
