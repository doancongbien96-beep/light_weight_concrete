# Tensile Strain-Hardening Behavior ($\varepsilon_u = 4.85\%$) of Polyethylene (PE) Fiber-Reinforced Cementless Slag Composite under Varying W/B Ratio ($0.30\sim 0.38$) and Silica Sand Inclusion

## Summary

To overcome the brittle failure of cementless binders built on ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS), an industrial by-product serving as the primary precursor with no cement at all, and to develop a highly ductile eco-friendly composite material, this study designed a **cementless composite combining ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder with a powder-type binary activator ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$) and reinforcing it with 1.75 vol.% ultra-high-strength PE fiber**, and then precisely evaluated the 28-day compressive strength, the uniaxial direct tensile stress–strain curves, and the dispersion characteristics of multiple fine cracks within the gauge section as functions of the water-to-binder ratio (W/B = 0.30, 0.34, 0.38) and the presence or absence of fine aggregate (silica sand). It demonstrated that the aggregate-free mix at W/B = 0.34 (`M34`) achieved a 28-day compressive strength of $58.5\ \text{MPa}$, a uniaxial ultimate tensile strength of $7.85\ \text{MPa}$, and an ultimate tensile strain of $4.85\%$, and that excellent pseudo strain-hardening (PSH) of $4.20\%$ was maintained even under silica sand inclusion of $40\%$ (`M34-S`) — a **landmark 2015 origin paper in the Journal of the Korea Academia-Industrial cooperation Society by the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University, first author), Dr. Jung-Il Choi, and Prof. Young-Seok Kim (Chungnam National University)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Achieving tensile ductility of $4.85\%$ with 1.75% PE fiber alone in a cementless slag composite**:
   - While reducing the fiber volume fraction from the conventional PVA-fiber level of 2.0% down to 1.75%, it realized a high tensile strength of $7.85\ \text{MPa}$ together with a very large elongation capacity of $4.85\%$.
2. **Optimization of the fracture toughness and bridging strength of the cementless matrix across W/B ratios**:
   - It was established that the W/B = 0.30 mix exhibits a compressive strength of $68.5\ \text{MPa}$ yet suffers an elongation capped at $3.5\%$ owing to its excessive matrix fracture toughness, whereas the W/B = 0.34 mix forms the optimal PSH energy margin.
3. **Verification of economy and mechanical performance when fine aggregate (silica sand, S/B = 0.40) is incorporated**:
   - Even with silica sand added, the rise in matrix fracture toughness was fully supported by the PE fiber bridging capacity, stably securing a compressive strength of $55.0\ \text{MPa}$ and a tensile elongation capacity of $4.20\%$.
4. **Room-temperature curing and construction convenience secured through the powder-type binary activator**:
   - By premixing powdered slaked lime and sodium sulfate instead of liquid alkali, the field applicability of a one-part cementless highly ductile composite was demonstrated.

## Methods

- **Materials and PE cementless composite mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS, specific surface area $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{CaO} = 44.6\%$, 89.5 wt.%) + powdered slaked lime $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (7.5 wt.%) + sodium sulfate $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ (3.0 wt.%).
  - Reinforcing fiber: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE micro fibers ($V_f = 1.75\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 2700\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 88\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Four mixes:
    1. `M30`: W/B = 0.30 (no silica sand).
    2. `M34`: W/B = 0.34 (no silica sand).
    3. `M38`: W/B = 0.38 (no silica sand).
    4. `M34-S`: W/B = 0.34 + silica sand (S/B = 0.40).
- **Mechanical test program (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: JSCE dumbbell-type specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, displacement control at 0.2 mm/min).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (age of 28 days).

### 2. Interpretation of hydrophobic PE fiber bridging and micromechanics-based fracture mechanisms
- **Optimization of the interfacial frictional resistance of PE fibers**: slag hydration products (C-A-S-H gel) firmly confine the PE fiber surface, forming an appropriate frictional shear stress of $\tau_0 \approx 2.2\ \text{MPa}$ and inducing continuous pull-out without fiber rupture.
- **Saturation of multiple fine cracking**: at W/B = 0.34, the fiber bridging complementary energy $J_b'$ exceeds the matrix fracture toughness $J_{tip}$ by more than threefold, generating 32 fine cracks continuously across the entire gauge section.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark origin study in the field of sustainable highly ductile composites, demonstrating that the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University achieved a compressive strength of 58.5 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 4.85% in a cementless slag composite incorporating 1.75% PE fiber, and retained a ductility of 4.20% even when silica sand was included.
