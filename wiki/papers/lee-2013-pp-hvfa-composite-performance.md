# Performance enhancement techniques for PP-fiber high-volume fly ash cement composites

## Summary

A user-group paper with Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University as first author and Prof. Yun Yong Kim of Chungnam National University as corresponding author (Jin Wook Bang co-author). Unlike PVA or PE fibers, PP fiber had been used solely for preventing explosive spalling at high temperature rather than for achieving high ductility (its low tensile strength makes rupture easy, and its hydrophobicity gives weak interfacial bonding). This study proposes a performance enhancement technique that incorporates a high-strength PP fiber with about 40 % higher strength than conventional PP (diameter 12 µm, length 10 mm, tensile strength 850 MPa, elongation 21 %, density 0.91 g/cm³, elastic modulus 6 GPa) at 2 vol % in a high-volume fly ash matrix (replacement ratio 74 %, w/b 0.23~0.25), using an **antifoamer (suppressing unintended entrained air)** together with **polystyrene beads (4 mm-diameter artificial flaws)**. The key results are that the antifoamer alone raises compressive strength about 3× (structural-material level), and adding the beads on top secures about 3× tensile strain capacity. Two copy files of this paper exist under sources/ and were consolidated into a single note.

## Key Contributions

1. **Flaw size tailoring design framework**: Beyond the energy criterion (complementary energy of the bridging curve ≥ matrix fracture toughness, Marshall-Cox J-integral) and the strength criterion (maximum bridging stress σ₀ ≥ crack strength σfc) required for multiple-cracking strain-hardening, this work states explicitly that the number of cracks (deformation capacity) is governed by the flaw size distribution within the matrix. A distribution whose sizes exceed the critical flaw size cfc with a small standard deviation is ideal, and this is deliberately engineered with artificial flaws (beads) instead of natural flaws.
2. **Resolving the practical bottleneck of high-ductility PP fiber application**: It identifies the problem that hydrophobic, small-diameter PP fibers entrap large amounts of fine air during mixing, driving density 34 % below the theoretical value and dropping compressive strength to 15~19 MPa, forfeiting structural-material grade, and solves it by adding only an antifoamer to bring the density close to the theoretical value (~1.90 g/cm³).
3. **Division of roles between antifoamer and artificial flaw**: The antifoamer raises matrix strength (compressive ~3×, crack strength +25 %), securing structural performance, but harms ductility through increased fracture toughness. The beads deliberately lower this matrix strength (MP2-1 vs MP2-2: compressive −5 %, tensile −22 %) and homogenize flaw locations, lifting tensile deformation capacity about 3× — a mix strategy that secures strength and ductility simultaneously.
4. **Presentation of achievable performance ranges**: Ultimate tensile strain 3~6 %, tensile strength 2.2~2.8 MPa, compressive strength 15~45 MPa. Verifies that, beyond changing the water-binder ratio or binder type, the antifoamer·artificial-flaw combination serves as a means of controlling strength and ductility.

## Methods

- Five mixes (weight ratios): OPC : fly ash F (mean diameter 17.5 µm) = 1 : 2.8 (replacement ratio 74 %), PP fiber 2 vol % common. MP 1-1 baseline / MP 1-2 +beads 0.092 / MP 2-1 +antifoamer 0.0076 / MP 2-2 +antifoamer+beads / MP 3 antifoamer+beads+w/b 0.95 (other mixes 0.88).
- Mixing: forced-action mixer, dry mixing 3 min → water 3 min → superplasticizer·antifoamer·beads → fibers, 10~15 min total.
- Curing: demolded after 1 day at 23±3 °C and RH 50±5 %, then air-cured 28 days under identical conditions.
- Tests: slump and slump flow (KS F 2402, KS F 2594); density (in-air/in-water weights of 50 mm cubes); compressive strength (KS L 5105, 50 mm cubes, 3 per mix); uniaxial tension (200×75×12.5 mm plates with glued aluminum end plates 75×50 mm, displacement control 0.1 mm/min, LVDT gauge length 100 mm on both faces, 3 per mix).

## Results

- Flowability: slump 178~267 mm, slump flow 356~540 mm. The beads reduced flowability through water absorption and their angular shape; the antifoamer through removal of the ball-bearing effect of fine entrained air.
- Density: MP 1 series 1.22·1.31 g/cm³ (about 34 % below the theoretical values of 1.91·1.95 g/cm³ — due to uniformly distributed fine air voids), while the antifoamer-bearing MP 2·MP 3 came in at 1.68~1.78 g/cm³, close to the theoretical 1.90 g/cm³.
- Compressive strength: MP 1 series 15.8·18.7 MPa (unsuitable for structural use), while the antifoamer-bearing MP 2 series rose about 3×, securing structural-material level. MP 3 fell 16 % vs MP 2 (the w/b +7 % effect).
- Crack strength (first cracking): about 10 % decrease from bead addition, about 25 % increase from the antifoamer. MP 3 was similar to MP 2-2 — within this condition range, the beads (artificial flaws) affect matrix strength more than w/b does.
- Tension: final tensile strength decreased by 8.2 % (MP 1-2) and 22.9 % (MP 2-2) in the bead-containing mixes (degraded maximum fiber bridging stress). Ultimate tensile strain increased about 3× with beads in the MP 2 series, whereas the effect was marginal in the MP 1 series — adding beads to a matrix already rich in air voids leaves only the strength-reducing effect. There were also cases where artificial flaw introduction did not reduce the scatter of tensile performance (conclusion 4), so dependence on mix conditions is stated explicitly.
- Multiple fine-crack patterns observed in MP 1-1 specimens (Fig. 12).
