# Influences of Silicon Carbide Fineness on the Thermal Conductivity and Mechanical Properties of Cement Composites

## Summary

To maximize thermal transfer efficiency in radiant floor heating and geothermal heat exchanger systems, Professor Y. Du and co-workers at Liaoning Technical University systematically investigated the influence of median particle size of high-thermal-conductivity silicon carbide (SiC, 45.00 wt % replacement: CCM-1 at 94.0 µm, CCM-2 at 50.3 µm, CCM-3 at 21.5 µm, water-to-cement ratio $W/C = 0.32$, silica fume 6.00 wt %, superplasticizer 0.60 wt %) on the pore structure (MIP), crystalline hydration products (XRD), 28 d flexural and compressive strength, and thermal conductivity of cement composites. Compared to plain cement mortar (CM), thermal conductivity increased by **90.78 %** for CCM-1, **104.79 %** for CCM-2, and **125.34 %** for CCM-3 with fine SiC (21.5 µm). Coarse SiC (94.0 µm) in CCM-1 enhanced 28 d flexural and compressive strengths by **11.21 %** and **5.64 %**, respectively, relative to CM.

## Key Contributions

- **Elucidation of thermal conductivity enhancement mechanism across SiC particle fineness ($94.0\ \mu\text{m} \rightarrow 21.5\ \mu\text{m}$)**: The higher specific surface area and uniform dispersion of fine SiC (CCM-3) formed continuous heat-conduction pathways, achieving a 125.34 % increase in thermal conductivity.
- **Characterization of opposing mechanical strength trends by SiC particle size**: Coarse SiC (CCM-1) enhanced flexural strength (+11.21 %) and compressive strength (+5.64 %) through physical packing effects, whereas fine SiC (CCM-3) induced air-entraining effects that reduced flexural strength by 36.43 % and compressive strength by 15.28 % relative to CCM-1.
- **MIP and XRD microstructural analysis**: Confirmed that SiC incorporation mitigated ettringite (AFt, 9.02°2$\theta$) and $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ peak intensities while refining micropore volumes.
- **Design guidelines for high-efficiency cementitious composites in radiant heating and energy piles**: Established particle size selection criteria balancing mechanical strength and thermal conductivity trade-offs.

## Methods

- Mix details (Table 1):
  - **CM (Control)**: $W/C = 0.32$, SiC 0 wt %, silica fume 6.00 wt %, polycarboxylate superplasticizer 0.60 wt %
  - **CCM-1**: $W/C = 0.32$, SiC 45.00 wt % ($d_{50} = 94.0\ \mu\text{m}$), silica fume 6.00 wt %, superplasticizer 0.60 wt %
  - **CCM-2**: $W/C = 0.32$, SiC 45.00 wt % ($d_{50} = 50.3\ \mu\text{m}$), silica fume 6.00 wt %, superplasticizer 0.60 wt %
  - **CCM-3**: $W/C = 0.32$, SiC 45.00 wt % ($d_{50} = 21.5\ \mu\text{m}$), silica fume 6.00 wt %, superplasticizer 0.60 wt %
- Test methods: Flexural and compressive strengths at 3, 7, and 28 days, TC3000E transient hot-wire thermal conductivity measurements, SEM-EDS, XRD, and MIP pore-structure analysis.
