# High-Temperature Resistance and Fire Performance ($200\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) of Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC): Ceramic Sintering at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and 135 % Residual Strength Recovery

## Summary

To overcome the dehydration decomposition of calcium hydrates ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ and C-S-H) and severe strength loss suffered by conventional cementitious concretes (OPC/ECC) under fire and extreme thermal shock, Professor Lili Kan at USST and Professor Fei Wang at Tongji University investigated cementless Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC) reinforced with 2.0 vol.% fibers. The authors conducted high-temperature electric furnace exposure tests from ambient temperature ($20\ ^\circ\text{C}$) to $200\ ^\circ\text{C}, 400\ ^\circ\text{C}, 600\ ^\circ\text{C}, 800\ ^\circ\text{C}, 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$, isolating the independent contributions of key components (GGBS/FA ratio, activator modulus, aggregate type, fiber melting). The study demonstrated **100 % elimination of explosive spalling via fiber melting channels, ceramic phase transformation via high-temperature sintering above $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ in fly-ash-rich matrices, and a remarkable residual strength gain reaching 135 % of ambient compressive strength**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First systematic isolation of component effects in ultra-high-temperature ($1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) EGC**:
   - Quantified the independent contributions of binder chemistry (GGBS vs FA), alkaline activator concentration, aggregate type, and polymeric fibers to high-temperature microstructural stability.
2. **Attainment of 135 % residual strength recovery via ceramic sintering at $800\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$**:
   - Proved that unlike degrading cement concrete, amorphous fly-ash geopolymer gels undergo viscous sintering into crystalline leucite and nepheline ceramics at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, elevating compressive strength from $45.0\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 60.8\ \text{MPa}$.
3. **Zero explosive spalling (0.0) and complete volumetric thermal stability**:
   - PVA/PE fiber melting channels provided steam pressure relief that, combined with the low thermal expansion of geopolymers, preserved structural geometry up to $1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
4. **Formulation of refractory composite design guidelines**:
   - Established that adjusting the GGBS/FA ratio to 0.3~0.5 simultaneously secures room-temperature tensile ductility and ultra-high-temperature ceramic strength.

## Methods

- **Materials and EGC series (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binders: Class F fly ash + GGBS (GGBS/FA ratios = 0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0).
  - Activator: Liquid $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{NaOH}$ (modulus $M_s = 1.0\sim 1.5$, W/B = 0.35).
  - Fibers: Kuraray REC15 PVA and high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
- **Thermal exposure and testing protocols (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 4–7)**:
  - Heating: Electric furnace at $5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$ to target temperatures (200, 400, 600, 800, 1000 °C), held for 2 h and furnace-cooled.
  - Characterization: 50 mm cube residual compressive strength, TGA/DSC, XRD, MIP, and water absorption (ASTM C20).

### 2. High-temperature sintering and microstructural analysis
- **Fly ash geopolymer sintering mechanics**: Above $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the aluminosilicate network underwent viscous sintering, closing microcracks and transforming into dense crystalline ceramic phases that boosted compressive strength to 135 % of its room-temperature value.
- **Influence of GGBS dosage**: High GGBS content promoted room-temperature strength but suffered calcium phase decomposition at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$; thus an FA/GGBS ratio of 3:1 was optimal for refractory performance.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that fly-ash-based EGC undergoes ceramic sintering at $800\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$, elevating its compressive strength to 135 % of room-temperature levels while completely preventing explosive spalling.
