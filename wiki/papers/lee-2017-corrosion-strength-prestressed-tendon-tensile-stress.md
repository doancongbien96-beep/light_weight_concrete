# Electrochemical Impressed-Current Corrosion Behavior and Residual Tensile Fracture Strength Degradation Mechanism of Prestressed PC Strands under Sustained High Tensile Stress ($0\sim 60\%\ f_{pu}$)

## Summary

To quantitatively assess the risk of stress corrosion cracking (SCC) and abrupt brittle fracture that arises when PC strands carrying sustained high tensile prestress (Prestress Tensile Stress) during construction and service of marine bridges and PSC infrastructure structures are exposed to chlorides, the research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University (first author), Dr. Kyung-Taek Koh of the Korea Institute of Civil Engineering and Building Technology (KICT), and Prof. Seung-Jun Kwon of Hannam University (corresponding author)** conducted **impressed current accelerated corrosion experiments (24 h acceleration) in a 3.5 wt.% NaCl electrolyte while sustaining tensile loads at four levels ($0\%, 20\%, 40\%, 60\%\ f_{pu}$) on standard 7-wire PC strands (outer diameter $15.3\ \text{mm}$, ultimate tensile strength $f_{pu} = 1860.0\ \text{MPa}$)**, and carried out measurements of the accumulated impressed-current charge, cross-sectional loss after removal of iron oxide rust, local pitting corrosion depth, and uniaxial residual tensile fracture tests, demonstrating **a stress-induced corrosion acceleration mechanism in which the corrosion current surges to $193.9\%$ as the tensile stress level rises to $60\%\ f_{pu}$, an abrupt $45\%$ loss of the residual ultimate fracture load, and an analytical model for the degradation of fracture strain as a function of pit depth**, in a landmark 2017 origin paper published in Advances in Materials Science and Engineering.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first quantification of the interaction between sustained tensile stress and electrochemical corrosion acceleration**:
   - Demonstrated that corrosion rate accelerates $1.94$× under tensile stress ($0.6 f_{pu}$) relative to stress-free strands, owing to metal lattice deformation and breakdown of the passive film.
2. **Derivation of a deterministic correlation between local pitting corrosion depth and residual tensile capacity**:
   - Established that local pit depth ($d_{pit}$), rather than the simple cross-sectional reduction ratio, is the dominant factor producing stress concentration and premature brittle fracture.
3. **Proposal of a limiting corrosion allowance criterion for PSC strands**:
   - Warned that when the mass loss ratio reaches $5.0\%$, strand elongation plunges by more than $50\%$, creating a risk of sudden brittle collapse of PSC structures.
4. **Establishment of a standard for life-cycle durability assessment and residual service life prediction of marine PSC bridges**:
   - Established a chloride corrosion diagnostic guideline that reflects the actual stress state of the tendons.

## Methods

- **Test apparatus and strand specification (Section 2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Strand specification: KS D 7002 SWPC 7B 7-wire strand (diameter $15.3\ \text{mm}$, $f_{pu} = 1860\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $195\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Loading frame: steel support frame + hydraulic oil jack + load cell (sustained tensile stress levels: $0\%, 20\%, 40\%, 60\%\ f_{pu}$).
  - Accelerated corrosion cell: $3.5\ \text{wt.}\% \text{NaCl}$ aqueous solution + platinum-coated electrode + DC constant-current source (Impressed Current DC $1.0\ \text{A}$).
- **Corrosion and residual strength evaluation (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Impressed-current duration: 24 h continuous acceleration.
  - Rust removal: cleaning with 10 % ammonium citrate (ASTM G1).
  - Tensile testing: tensile fracture test in a 1000 kN universal testing machine.

### 2. Interpretation of the stress-induced corrosion and brittle rupture mechanism

- **Anodic reaction acceleration by lattice expansion**: when a high tensile stress of $60\%\ f_{pu}$ acts on the wire, the crystal lattice of the steel elongates, lowering the activation energy for dissolution of iron ions ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) and making the corrosion rate surge twofold.
- **Elevation of the local pit-tip stress concentration factor ($K_t$)**: severe stress concentration develops at the bottom of deep pits of $0.95\ \text{mm}$, inducing pre-yield early brittle cleavage fracture (Cleavage Fracture) of the strand.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a top-tier landmark origin study in the field of corrosion durability and safety diagnosis of prestressed concrete structures, in which the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Dr. Kyung-Taek Koh, and Prof. Seung-Jun Kwon elucidated the mechanism of electrochemical corrosion acceleration of PC strands under sustained tensile stress and the abrupt $45\%$ drop in residual tensile strength.
