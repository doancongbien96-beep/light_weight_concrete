## Summary
To push the expensive fiber cost, the largest factor blocking commercialization of strain-hardening composites, to its limit, this study elucidated the nano-scale matrix tailoring (Matrix Tailoring) mechanism of an ultra-low-fiber high-ductility geopolymer composite (Ultra-Low Fiber EGC) that retains uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening behavior ($\epsilon_u = 1.8 \sim 3.2 \%$) even when the PE fiber content is lowered extremely to **0.2 ~ 0.6 vol %**, one tenth of the conventional mix ($2.0 \%$); the research team of Prof. Jian-Guo Dai at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University carried out the work. With the matrix fracture toughness lowered radically to $K_m = 0.22 MPa\sqrt{m}$, a 28 d compressive strength of 42.5 MPa and a direct tensile strain capacity of **2.85 %** were achieved with merely **0.4 vol %** fiber content.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the mechanism for reaching the strain-hardening limit at ultra-low fiber contents (0.2 ~ 0.6 vol %)**: extreme control of the matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ to around $0.20 MPa\sqrt{m}$ satisfied the PSH energy criterion.
- **Demonstration of an 80 % fiber cost saving**: economic viability was secured for large-scale civil infrastructure application of commercial high-ductility composites.
- **Realization of a direct tensile strain capacity of 2.85 % (0.4 vol % fiber)**: ductility improved more than 180-fold relative to ordinary concrete.
- **Demonstration of multiple fine cracking**: more than 20 finely distributed cracks formed even under the 0.4 % fiber condition.

## Methods
- Binder: slag-fly ash composite precursor + ultrafine nano calcium carbonate, alkali activator ($W/B = 0.35$).
- Fiber: ultra-high-strength PE fiber ($V_f = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 1.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm).
- Testing: dogbone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, full-field DIC strain), three-point bending fracture toughness test, 28 d compressive strength.
