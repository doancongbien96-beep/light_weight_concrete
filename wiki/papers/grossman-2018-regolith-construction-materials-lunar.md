# Regolith-Derived Construction Materials for Lunar and Martian Infrastructure: High-Temperature Thermal Sintering (262 MPa) and Molten Regolith Electrolysis (MRE)

## Summary

To achieve self-sufficient in-situ construction for permanent lunar and Martian outposts without importing binders from Earth (100 % ISRU), Dr. K. Grossman investigated the thermal sintering mechanics, phase evolution, and structural properties of basaltic lunar simulant (JSC-1A, particle size $0.05\sim 1.0\ \text{mm}$) and Martian simulant (JSC-Mars-1A) across sintering temperatures of $1050\sim 1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$, alongside Molten Regolith Electrolysis (MRE) at $1600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to simultaneously extract breathable oxygen and structural Fe-Si metal alloys. Sintering at an optimum window of $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ induced viscous sintering of plagioclase and pyroxene minerals, reducing porosity to 0.8 % and achieving an ultra-high compressive strength of **262.0 MPa ($38,000\ \text{psi}$)**. Sintering temperatures exceeding $1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ caused iron oxide bloating, while MRE extracted over 90 % of theoretical oxygen alongside ductile Fe-Si structural reinforcement.

## Key Contributions

1. **Production of 262 MPa (38,000 psi) ultra-high-strength structural ceramic blocks via thermal sintering**:
   - Discovered that fine-fraction regolith (< 45 µm) compacted under 50 MPa and sintered at $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ achieved a density of $2.95\ \text{g/cm}^3$ and compressive strength of 262.0 MPa without additives.
2. **Coproduction of oxygen and metals via Molten Regolith Electrolysis (MRE)**:
   - Induced electrolytic reactions in molten regolith at $1600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to reduce $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3, \text{SiO}_2, \text{TiO}_2$, yielding high-purity oxygen and structural Fe-Si-Ti alloys.
3. **Identification of sintering temperature window ($1050\sim 1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and particle size effects**:
   - Quantified that high-specific-surface fines (< 45 µm) initiate densification 50 °C lower than coarse fractions, and established that over-sintering above $1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ releases gas from iron oxide reduction.

## Methods

- **Raw simulants and mineralogy (Chapter 3)**:
  - Lunar simulant: JSC-1A ($d_{50} = 75\ \mu\text{m}$, chemical composition $\text{SiO}_2$ 47.7 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 15.0 %, $\text{FeO/Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ 10.8 %, $\text{CaO}$ 10.4 %, $\text{MgO}$ 9.3 %, $\text{TiO}_2$ 1.6 %).
  - Size grading: Fine (< 45 µm), medium (45~150 µm), and coarse (> 150 µm).
- **Thermal sintering and MRE systems (Chapters 4–5)**:
  - Sintering furnace: Vacuum and inert argon atmosphere at $1000\sim 1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (heating rate 5 °C/min, 2~4 h isothermal hold, cooling at 2 °C/min).
  - Uniaxial compaction: Cold pressing at 10~50 MPa ($25\ \text{mm}$ cylinders and $50\ \text{mm}$ cubes).
  - MRE: Platinum (Pt) or iridium anodes and tungsten (W) cathodes in an alumina crucible at $1600\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Testing program**:
  - Compressive strength: MTS 810 servo-hydraulic test frame following ASTM C1424.
  - Microstructural analysis: XRD, FE-SEM/EDS, Archimedes density and porosity.

### 2. Sintering mechanisms and closed-loop ISRU implications
- **Optimum sintering temperature ($1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**: Viscous flow of plagioclase and pyroxene reduced porosity to 0.8 %, maximizing strength to 262 MPa, whereas temperatures above $1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ triggered bloating.
- **Fe-Si structural alloy recovery**: Metals recovered from MRE can be utilized for structural reinforcement and anchor bolts.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the thermal sintering protocols for fabricating 262 MPa structural blocks from lunar/Martian regolith and demonstrated oxygen-metal coproduction via molten regolith electrolysis.
