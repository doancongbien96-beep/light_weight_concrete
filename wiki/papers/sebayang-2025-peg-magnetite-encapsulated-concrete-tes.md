## Summary

To dramatically improve the heat-charging/heat-release rates of solar-thermal power generation and building thermal energy storage systems, this most recent 2025 study investigates a magnetic nanocomposite thermal energy storage concrete in which polyethylene glycol (PEG 6000, latent heat $\Delta H = 175 J/g$), a phase-change material, is hybrid-bonded with magnetic magnetite ($Fe_3O_4$, nano particle size 25 nm) nanoparticles and encapsulation-embedded in a high-strength cement matrix, characterizing the concrete's thermal conductivity, photothermal-to-magnetic thermal conversion efficiency, latent heat capacity, and mechanical compressive strength. Through the high thermal conduction pathways formed by magnetite nanoparticles, the composite concrete's thermal conductivity was tripled from $0.55 W/(m\cdot K)$ to **1.65 W/(m·K)**, and the heat-charging cycle time was cut by **65 %**.

## Key Contributions

- **Development of a PEG/magnetite composite-encapsulated nano thermal storage system**: high latent heat ($145 J/g$) and high thermal conductivity secured simultaneously.
- **Demonstration of a thermal-conduction percolation network formed by magnetic nanoparticles**: completely overcoming the phase-change heat-transfer bottleneck.
- **Stability demonstrated over 1000 consecutive thermal charge/discharge cycles**: PCM leakage rate below 0.1 % and latent heat retention of 98.5 %.
- **A 28 d compressive strength of 42 MPa secured**: enabling integral thermal-storage construction in structural concrete walls and floor slabs.

## Methods

- Synthesis: ultrasonic dispersion to prepare PEG-6000/$Fe_3O_4$ (1 ~ 5 wt %) nanofluids, followed by spherical epoxy encapsulation.
- Concrete mix: OPC ($W/C = 0.40$), with replacement embedding in fine and coarse aggregates (10, 20, 30 vol %).
- Testing: Hot Disk thermal constant analyzer, DSC latent heat measurement, VSM magnetic analysis, and an electric-furnace thermal cycling tester.
