## Summary
A classic CCR landmark paper in which Professor C.P. Pantelides's research team at the University of Kentucky clarified the forced vibration frequency response function (FRF), modal damping ratio $\zeta$, loss coefficient ($\eta$), and dynamic elastic modulus of polyolefin fiber reinforced concrete (P-FRC) reinforced with structural macro synthetic polyolefin fibers ($V_f = 0.5 \sim 2.0 \%$, length 50 mm), aiming at resonance vibration control and dynamic fatigue life extension of long-span bridge decks and high-rise buildings. It was demonstrated that macro polyolefin fiber incorporation increases the material damping ratio by up to **120 %** over plain concrete through viscoelastic energy absorption of polymer chains and fine friction at fiber-matrix interfaces.

## Key Contributions
- **Dynamic vibration damping mechanism of polyolefin FRC established**: precise extraction of modal damping ratios based on the half-power bandwidth method.
- **Damping ratio-natural frequency envelopes established by fiber content ($V_f$) and vibration mode**: first flexural mode damping ratio $\zeta_1 = 1.15 \% \rightarrow 2.55 \%$ attained.
- **Post-cracking damping surge phenomenon clarified**: at fine crack opening, fiber pullout friction multiplies the damping ratio fivefold up to 5.8 %.
- **Seismic and wind-vibration control structural design guidelines presented**: resonance response amplification factor (dynamic magnification factor) reduced by 50 %.

## Methods
- Mixes: OPC ($W/C = 0.40$), macro polyolefin fibers (diameter 0.65 mm, length 50 mm, $V_f = 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$).
- Vibration tests: $100 \times 100 \times 760 mm$ beam specimens, sinusoidal sweep excitation with an electromagnetic shaker ($10 \sim 1000 Hz$), acceleration sensor measurement.
