## Summary
A paper in which the research team of Prof. F.U.A. Shaikh at Curtin University, Australia comparatively established the mechanical correlation between the uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening behavior and the four-point flexural deflection-hardening behavior of strain-hardening geopolymer composites (PE-EGC) reinforced with high-strength ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) fibers ($V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$), together with the PSH energy/strength criteria and multiple fine crack propagation behavior. Leveraging the proper interfacial frictional slip characteristics of hydrophobic PE fibers ($\tau_0 = 1.65 MPa$), the PE 2.0 vol % mix demonstrated world-class ultra-high ductility: direct tensile strength **6.8 MPa**, direct tensile strain capacity **6.5 %**, flexural strength **18.5 MPa**, and ultimate flexural deflection **12.5 mm**.

## Key Contributions
- **Established a 1:1 mechanical linkage model between direct tensile strain-hardening and flexural deflection-hardening of PE-EGC**: successfully predicted the moment-deflection curve through section integration starting from the uniaxial tensile constitutive law.
- **Demonstrated the superiority of PE fiber over PVA fiber**: complete pullout without fiber rupture in the alkaline matrix, extending tensile strain capacity to 2.2 times that of PVA.
- **Maximized the PSH performance indices**: energy index $J_b'/J_{tip} = 8.5$ and strength index $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} = 2.4$ secured.
- **Demonstrated fine crack width control**: average crack width maintained below $25 \mu m$ up to tensile failure.

## Methods
- Binder: Class F fly ash 80 % + slag 20 %, alkaline activator ($NaOH 10 M$ + sodium silicate solution $Ms = 2.0$), $W/B = 0.32$.
- Fiber: Spectra PE fiber ($V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 \%$, diameter 38 µm, length 12.7 mm, tensile strength 2600 MPa).
- Tests: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$), four-point bending test ($100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$), 28 d compressive strength.
