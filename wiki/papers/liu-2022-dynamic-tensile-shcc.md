## Summary

Using a split Hopkinson tension bar (SHTB) and a hydraulic servo testing machine, this study elucidated the dynamic direct tensile behavior of strain hardening cementitious composites (SHCC) at intermediate-to-high strain rates ($\dot{\epsilon} = 10^{-4} \sim 100 s^{-1}$). The dynamic increase factor (DIF) of tensile strength, the change in tensile strain capacity, multiple crack formation density, and the fiber rupture fraction on fracture surfaces were analyzed with a high-speed camera and electron microscopy as the strain rate increased. A rate-dependent fiber bridging damage model was established, providing an analysis framework for SHCC structures under dynamic impact and blast loading.

## Key Contributions

- **Implementation of SHTB direct tension testing in the ultra-high-rate tensile regime ($\dot{\epsilon} = 100 s^{-1}$)**: acquired pure tensile stress-strain curves corrected for inertial effects by applying pulse shaping and specimen alignment techniques.
- **Demonstration of dramatic rate enhancement of tensile strength and energy absorption capacity**: at $\dot{\epsilon} = 85 s^{-1}$, a dynamic tensile strength $DIF = 2.15$ and a 3.2-fold increase in tensile energy absorption density.
- **Dynamic fine-crack propagation and branching analysis**: established that higher strain rates drive crack-tip propagation speed close to the material's Rayleigh wave speed, accelerating crack branching and sharply multiplying the number of cracks.

## Methods

- **Material mix**: high-strength PE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$), $W/B = 0.20$, silica fume and fly ash composite matrix.
- **Test techniques**:
  - Quasi-static and intermediate rates: hydraulic high-speed actuator ($10^{-4}, 10^{-2}, 1.0 s^{-1}$).
  - High-speed impact: aluminum SHTB apparatus ($20 \sim 100 s^{-1}$), DIC strain measurement.

## Results

- Tensile properties by strain rate:
  - $10^{-4} s^{-1}$: tensile strength $8.2 MPa$, strain capacity $6.8 \%$, energy absorption $480 kJ/m3$.
  - $1.0 s^{-1}$: tensile strength $12.4 MPa$ ($DIF = 1.51$), strain capacity $6.2 \%$, energy absorption $690 kJ/m3$.
  - $85 s^{-1}$: tensile strength $17.6 MPa$ ($DIF = 2.15$), strain capacity $5.4 \%$, energy absorption $1540 kJ/m3$.
