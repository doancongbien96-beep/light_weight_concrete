## Summary
A 2024 study that precisely characterized the flexural load-deflection envelopes, post-yield plastic hinge formation, distributed crack widths, and energy dissipation capacity of full-scale reinforced beams (R/HS-ECC beams, $150 \times 250 \times 2200 mm$) using a high-strength high-ductility composite of the 100 MPa compressive-strength class (HS-ECC, direct tensile strength 10 MPa, tensile strain capacity $\epsilon_u > 6.0 \%$), and constructed a nonlinear moment-curvature ($M-\phi$) theoretical prediction model based on the section layered-integration method. Relative to conventional high-strength RC beams, the flexural capacity was increased by **42 %** and the displacement ductility index by **185 %**, while crack width was confined to **0.03 mm** or less over the entire loading range.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of precise nonlinear moment-curvature prediction equations for R/HS-ECC beams**: material tensile strain-hardening and compressive softening envelopes linked directly to section integration.
- **Complete prevention of brittle crushing of the compression zone in high-strength beams**: ductile tension of the tensile-side HS-ECC layer relieved stress concentration in the compression zone.
- **Achievement of a deflection ductility index of $\mu_\Delta = 6.2$**: excellent plastic energy dissipation realized even under over-reinforced rebar ratios.
- **Provision of practical flexural design charts and simplified design equations**: fundamental data supplied for revisions of ACI 318 and Eurocode 2.

## Methods
- Eight full-scale beams ($150 \times 250 \times 2200 mm$, span 1900 mm, tensile reinforcement ratio $\rho = 1.0, 1.8, 2.6 \%$).
- Materials: normal high-strength concrete (HSC 100 MPa) versus HS-ECC ($f_c = 105 MPa$, $f_t = 10.2 MPa$, $\epsilon_u = 6.5 \%$).
- Four-point flexural loading test, LVDT deflection measurement, full-field DIC strain tensor mapping.
