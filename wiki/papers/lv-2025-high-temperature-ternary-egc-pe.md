# High-Temperature Behavior of a Ternary (GGBS+FA+SF) Heat-Resistant EGC

## Summary

An EGC incorporating 1.5 vol % ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers in a GGBS:FA:SF = 0.445:0.445:0.11 binder was heated from 20~800 °C (5 °C/min, 2 h hold at the target temperature) and then evaluated for mechanical properties and microstructure. In the low-temperature range (20~150 °C), residual geopolymer reactions become activated so that the mechanical properties rather improve; above 600 °C the strength decreases due to collapse of the Si-O-Al network, yet even at 800 °C the composite retains a residual compressive strength of 21.5 MPa without explosive spalling.

## Key Contributions

1. Quantification of the temperature dependence of tensile behavior (Table 4): first-crack strength, tensile strength, and strain all peak at 80 °C (4.10 MPa, 5.53 MPa, and 11.03 %, respectively). Mechanism: thermally activated unreacted Al2O3-SiO2 reaction forms an additional three-dimensional Si-O-Al network, and moderate moisture evaporation strengthens the fiber-matrix interfacial bond.
2. Identification of the thermal-decomposition limit temperature of PE fibers: about 150 °C. At 170 °C tensile softening is completely lost (strain 0.09 %, single cracking). The multiple-cracking count also peaks at 32~36 over 80~130 °C → drops to 1 at 170 °C.
3. Compressive strength curve: 54.4 MPa at 20 °C → peak of 68.8 MPa (+26.5 %) at 150 °C → 56.64 MPa at 400 °C (maintaining the room-temperature level) → 41.66 MPa at 600 °C → 21.5 MPa at 800 °C. Compared with a peer study (Humur et al., 1 h exposure), the residual strength at 600/800 °C is markedly higher while the exposure time is twice as long.
4. Multiple-cracking and crack-width characteristics at high temperature: the high GGBS content increases autogenous and drying shrinkage, so crack widths (204~276 μm) are larger than in the existing EGC literature. The trade-off between high reactivity/densification and failure to control shrinkage cracking is stated explicitly.
5. Denseness indicator (ASTM C20 boiling-water test): apparent porosity decreases from 26.41 % (20 °C) to 21.35 % (150 °C), then increases to 34.93 % (800 °C). Pore refinement proceeds even up to 400 °C — the physical basis for mid-temperature strength retention.

## Methods

Direct tension (dog-bone, gauge 80 mm, 15×30 mm, 2 mm/min, LVDTs on both sides), compression (50 mm cubes), boiling-water test (ASTM C20-00), FT-IR (KBr pellet), SEM. Curing: demolded after 24 h at 20 °C RH 75 % → oven at 80 °C for 24 h → 7 days at room temperature. W/B 0.29, S/B 0.44, Na2SiO3/NaOH activator (SiO2/Na2O molar ratio 3.3).

## Results

- FT-IR: the 1118 cm−1 Si-O-T band remains stable up to 400 °C, while the 659 cm−1 M-O band drops sharply after 600 °C (Ca-O decomposition).
- Mass loss: 14.13 % up to 150 °C (physically adsorbed water and part of the bound water), 18.73 % up to 400 °C (PE evaporation + OH loss from C-A-S-H/N-A-S-H), 21.77 % at 800 °C (Si-O-Al collapse + CaCO3 decomposition).
- Fracture morphology at high temperature: even at 800 °C a through crack forms but no spalling occurs — potential for application to fire-resistant structures.

## Related Notes

- The combination of the PE fiber thermal weakness (150 °C) and the heat resistance of the geopolymer matrix (800 °C), together with the low-temperature data of [[han-2022-egc-impact-cold-temperature]], constitutes the temperature-environment performance envelope of EGC.
- The effect of matrix toughness enhancement factors (residual reactions, strengthened interfacial bonding) on tensile softening can be interpreted through the J_tip perspective of [[pseudo-strain-hardening-criteria]] — the 80 °C optimum is the balance point between the rise in σ_c and the rise in σ_0.
- Low-porosity high-strength systems (UHPC) are instead vulnerable to vapor-pressure spalling → mitigated by PP fibers at 0.6 vol% ([[abbas-2016-uhpc-mechanical-performance-durability-sustainability]]) — an opposite-polarity picture of EGC vs UHPC high-temperature behavior.
- Extending the series of fiber thermal-weakness temperatures: PE ~150 °C, PVA melting point 230 °C and thermal decomposition above 400 °C ([[baloch-2023-hot-bonded-scc-ecc-uhpc-fire]]) — an axis for comparing the post-heating residual performance of organic fiber types.
