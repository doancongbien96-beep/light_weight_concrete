## Summary
This study evaluated the quasi-static cyclic hysteretic behavior of a sandwich-type prefabricated composite wall consisting of high-ductility ECC face layers and a lightweight concrete core, in order to dramatically improve the seismic energy dissipation and crack resistance of prefabricated wall systems for high-rise buildings in earthquake-prone regions. Under repeated seismic lateral loading, the outer ECC layers carried the high-strain tensile/shear zones through multiple fine cracks, preventing compressive crushing and shear failure at the wall base; the wall achieved a displacement ductility ratio $\mu > 5.5$ together with high energy absorption capacity.

## Key Contributions
- **Proposal of an integrated prefabricated composite wall structure combining high-ductility ECC faces with a lightweight core**: secured more than twice the lateral displacement ductility of a conventional RC wall while reducing self-weight by 35 %.
- **Suppression of damage in the plastic hinge region at the wall base**: the high compressive strain capacity ($> 0.6 \%$) and tensile bridging resistance of ECC fundamentally prevented spalling of the cover concrete.
- **Verification of the safety of the precast wall joint (grout splice sleeve)**: demonstrated a buffering action against stress concentration at the connection.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $f_c = 55 MPa$, $\epsilon_u = 3.8 \%$) and lightweight concrete ($f_c = 28 MPa$, density $1600 kg/m3$) were used.
- **Specimen**: a half-scale prefabricated composite wall ($1200 \times 2400 \times 160 mm$) was tested.
- **Loading test**: a quasi-static cyclic lateral loading test was conducted (axial load ratio $n = 0.15$, maximum drift angle $3.0 \%$).

## Results
- A maximum lateral load of $285 kN$ and a displacement ductility ratio of $\mu = 5.8$ were achieved.
- Even at a drift angle of $2.5 \%$, more than 80 fine cracks with widths of $0.06 mm$ or less were distributed without concentration into a dominant main crack.
