# Compressive Strength and Tensile Properties of High-Volume Slag Cement Composite Containing Phase Change Material

## Summary

To maximize the thermal energy storage performance of building envelopes, reduce heating and cooling loads, and lower cement clinker usage, Professor Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author) and Dr. Jung-Il Choi at Chonnam National University, along with Professor Su-Tae Kang at Daegu University, formulated a high-volume slag cementitious composite (50 wt.% GGBS replacement of OPC) directly incorporating a **paraffin-based phase change material (PCM: n-Heptadecane $\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{36}$, phase change temperature $22\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat of fusion $200\ \text{kJ/kg}$)**. Reinforced with ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (PE) fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$), the authors investigated the effects of a **calcium sulfoaluminate (CSA) expansive agent (Hauyne-free lime-gypsum composite) and an alkaline activator ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$)** on hardened density, 28 d compressive strength, JSCE dogbone uniaxial tensile stress-strain behavior, ultimate tensile strength and strain capacity ($\varepsilon_{tu}$), tensile toughness, and multiple microcracking patterns. Despite direct incorporation of liquid PCM into the matrix, the optimal CSA-modified formulation (P-EA, 0.05 % CSA) achieved **a 28 d compressive strength of $48.5\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strength of $6.25\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strain capacity of $4.20\pm 0.45\%$, and a tensile toughness of $210.5\ \text{kJ/m}^3$**. Under direct tension, over 20 saturated multiple microcracks developed with an average crack width of $45\ \mu\text{m}$, proving the compatibility between latent thermal storage and structural strain-hardening ductility.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of 4.2 % ultra-high tensile strain-hardening in high-volume slag composites with PCM ($200\ \text{kJ/kg}$)**:
   - Overcame strength and bond degradation caused by paraffinic PCM inclusions via optimal PE fiber bridging and slag hydration, securing 48.5 MPa compressive strength and 4.2 % strain capacity.
2. **Restoration of matrix-fiber interfacial bonding via CSA expansive agent**:
   - Ettringite crystal growth compensated for drying shrinkage, densified micro-voids around PCM droplets, and reinforced interfacial frictional confinement.
3. **Quantification of multiple microcracking ($w \le 45\ \mu\text{m}$, 20 cracks) under JSCE direct tension**:
   - Characterized slip-hardening pullout of hydrophobic PE fibers during displacement-controlled tensile loading.
4. **Establishment of low-carbon building envelope technology with 50 % cement reduction**:
   - Integrated room-temperature ($22\ ^\circ\text{C}$) latent heat thermal regulation with structural-grade seismic toughness.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: Type I Portland cement (OPC) 50 wt.% + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, specific surface area $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$) 50 wt.%.
  - Phase change material (PCM): Paraffinic n-Heptadecane ($\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{36} > 97\%$, melting point $22\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $200\ \text{kJ/kg}$, density $0.88\ \text{g/cm}^3$ solid / $0.77\ \text{g/cm}^3$ liquid).
  - Expansive agent: CSA expansive agent ($3\text{CaO}\cdot 3\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\cdot\text{CaSO}_4 + \text{CaO} + \text{CaSO}_4$).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Ultra-high-strength PE fibers (diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, modulus 88 GPa, $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - Fine aggregates and admixtures: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 100\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40), polycarboxylate SP, HPMC thickener, defoamer.
  - Four experimental mixtures:
    - P (Control): PCM + binder.
    - P-A: PCM + binder + activator ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ 0.035 %).
    - P-EA: PCM + binder + CSA expansive agent (0.05 %).
    - P-A-EA: PCM + binder + activator (0.07 %) (no expansive agent).
- **Testing protocols (Section 2.3 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Compressive strength: KS L 5105 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes, 28 d water curing at $23\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Direct tension: JSCE dogbone specimens ($330\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, 80 mm gauge length, 0.1 mm/min displacement control, dual 25 mm LVDTs).

### 2. Bridging mechanics and expansion densification
- **Pore refinement via CSA expansion**: In the P-EA mix, needle-like ettringite crystals formed during CSA hydration filled the porous interfaces surrounding PCM droplets, maintaining compressive strength at 48.5 MPa and maximizing fiber pullout resistance.
- **Multiple microcracking and load-reloading cycles**: Interfacial frictional slip-hardening of hydrophobic PE fibers generated over 20 consecutive microcracks under displacement control, driving load re-ascensions that delivered 4.2 % tensile strain capacity.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that incorporating PCM into 50 % slag composites alongside optimized CSA expansive agents concurrently achieves 48.5 MPa compressive strength and 4.2 % tensile strain-hardening.
