# Flexural Performance of RC Beams Strengthened with SHCC and High-Strength Reinforcing Steel Bars

## Summary

This paper proposes a strengthening technique combining surface chipping with a 60 mm thick SHCC layer and high-strength reinforcing steel (HSRS, yield strength 1200 MPa) bars. The method was validated across 4 specimen types (RC-P, SHCC-0, SHCC-3, SHCC-5) via four-point bending tests and layered sectional nonlinear analysis (Prof. Bang Yeon Lee and Prof. Chang-Geun Cho's group). Applying SHCC alone tightly controlled crack widths within 60~200 μm (spreading multiple microcracks without widening individual cracks), while adding 3 or 5 HSRS bars increased ultimate moment capacity by 18 % and 27 % relative to the plain RC control, respectively.

## Key Contributions

- **Experimental validation of hybrid SHCC+HSRS retrofitting**: Established a complementary design strategy where SHCC provides crack width control and durability, while HSRS bars supply high load-bearing capacity, overcoming the limited strength enhancement of plain HPFRCC overlays.
- **Crack behavior quantification**: SHCC-strengthened beams resisted loads through stable multiple microcracks without crack widening even beyond rebar yielding. First-cracking moment in SHCC-0 was 1.21-fold that of RC-P, increasing further with HSRS bar count (SHCC-5 reached 52.0 kN·m).
- **Layered section nonlinear analysis model**: Idealized curvature distributions across cracking, yielding, and ultimate stages, deriving closed-form expressions for end rotations and midspan deflections. Prediction errors were −2.1~+2.9 % for yield load and −0.3~+4.0 % for ultimate load.
- **Analysis of structural constraints**: Identified that minor overestimations in SHCC strength contribution stemmed from strain incompatibility caused by SHCC drying shrinkage and substrate restraint.

## Methods

- SHCC mixture: W/B 45 %, S/B 83 %, GGBS 20 %, FA 20 % (binder mass ratios), VMA 0.22 %, HRWRA 0.14 %, oil-coated PVA fibers (diameter 40 µm, length 12 mm) at 2 vol %. Tensile strain capacity 2.1 %, tensile strength 1.6 MPa(?), compressive strength 29.2 MPa.
- HSRS bars: 6 mm diameter indented bars, yield strength 1200 MPa, elastic modulus 186 GPa. Longitudinal steel D19 ($f_y = 450\ \text{MPa}$, fy 450 MPa), stirrups D10@200 ($f_y = 300\ \text{MPa}$, fy 300 MPa).
- Specimens: 3400 mm span, $500 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ cross-section (500×300 mm). Concrete compressive strength $f_{cu} = 26.1\ \text{MPa}$ (26.1 MPa). Surface chipping at 7 d age $\rightarrow$ HSRS placement $\rightarrow$ SHCC casting $\rightarrow$ 5-week moist curing. Tested on a 2500 kN UTM under four-point loading (800 mm pure bending zone).

## Results

- Yield load: RC-P 334 kN $\rightarrow$ SHCC-0 341 kN (1.02-fold) $\rightarrow$ SHCC-3 370 kN (1.11-fold) $\rightarrow$ SHCC-5 376 kN (1.13-fold). Peak load: 345 kN $\rightarrow$ 357 kN (1.03-fold) $\rightarrow$ 425 kN (1.23-fold) $\rightarrow$ 448 kN (1.30-fold).
- Ultimate moment: SHCC-0/3/5 achieved 1.04/1.18/1.27-fold gains over RC-P (222 kN·m). Flexural stiffness at first cracking increased with SHCC thickness and HSRS bar count.
- Failure modes: All specimens failed by compressive concrete crushing. SHCC-0 exhibited no delamination, whereas SHCC-3 and SHCC-5 showed localized debonding near ultimate failure.
- Crack width: Maintained within 60~200 μm throughout the SHCC layer without widening post-yielding (contrasting sharply with rapid crack opening in RC-P).
