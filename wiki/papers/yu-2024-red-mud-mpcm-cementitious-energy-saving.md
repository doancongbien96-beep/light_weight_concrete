## Summary
A 2024 study that elucidated the thermo-mechanical latent heat capacity, thermal conductivity, compressive/flexural strength, and indoor temperature control effect of eco-friendly thermal energy storage cement composites in which aluminum red mud (Red Mud, RM, 20 ~ 40 wt %), a hazardous industrial by-product, was hybrid-compounded with microencapsulated phase change materials (MPCM, 10 ~ 30 vol %, paraffin core, latent heat $\Delta H = 155 J/g$) to simultaneously achieve large-volume recycling of the hazardous industrial by-product and high-efficiency thermal energy storage in zero-energy building envelopes. The micro-filler action of red mud completely compensated the strength reduction caused by MPCM incorporation, demonstrating a 28 d compressive strength of **38.5 MPa**, an effective latent heat of **34.2 J/g**, and an indoor peak temperature reduction of **4.5 °C**.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the mechanism compensating MPCM-induced strength reduction through the nano-filler effect of red mud**: red mud particles densified the capillary pores of the cement matrix, recovering 25 % of the compressive strength.
- **Synergy of insulation and heat storage**: simultaneous attainment of a 45 % reduction in thermal conductivity to $0.48 W/(m\cdot K)$ and a latent heat of $34.2 J/g$.
- **Durability verification after 500 thermal cycles**: MPCM capsule damage rate below 0.05 % during phase transition.
- **100 % resource utilization of hazardous waste and 40 % building energy savings**: development of sustainable eco-friendly building materials.

## Methods
- Binder: OPC + unground red mud (20, 30, 40 wt %).
- PCM: melamine-shell paraffin MPCM (particle size 10 µm, phase-change temperature $25 °C$, volume replacement 10, 20, 30 vol %).
- Tests: Hot Disk TPS thermal conductivity, DSC latent heat analysis, 28 d compressive/flexural strength, thermal performance test of a full-scale exterior-wall insulation chamber.
