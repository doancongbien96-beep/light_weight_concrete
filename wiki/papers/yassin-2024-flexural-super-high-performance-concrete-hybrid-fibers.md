## Summary
A landmark 2024 paper in which the research team of Professor Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University characterized the compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, four-point flexural strain-hardening envelope, modulus of rupture (MOR), and flexural toughness of hybrid super high-performance concrete (Hybrid SHPC, total $V_f = 2.0 \%$), which combines the high stiffness and crack-opening resistance of steel fibers with the ultra-ductile pull-out bridging capacity of ultrahigh-molecular-weight PE fibers in a multiscale manner within a cement matrix of the 150 MPa-class ultra-high-strength grade. The optimum hybrid mix (Steel 1.0 % + PE 1.0 %) achieved world-leading mechanical performance: a 28 d compressive strength of **158.5 MPa**, a flexural modulus of rupture of **32.5 MPa**, an ultimate flexural deflection of **15.2 mm**, and a flexural fracture energy of **$18500 N/m$**.

## Key Contributions
- **Verification of the steel-PE hybrid fiber synergy index ($\alpha_{syn} = 1.42$) in an ultra-high-strength matrix ($f_c = 158 MPa$)**: achievement of flexural strength and energy absorption exceeding single-fiber systems by 42 %.
- **Elucidation of the multiscale bridging mechanism**: steel fibers raised the initial flexural fine-crack load to 18.5 MPa, while PE fibers formed a multiple crack network in the large-deformation regime, inducing secondary hardening up to 32.5 MPa.
- **15-fold increase in flexural fracture energy**: flexural toughness improved 3.5-fold over conventional steel-fiber UHPC and 15-fold over ordinary concrete.
- **Establishment of an advanced material for long-span bridges and special protective structures**: enabling fabrication of ultra-lightweight slim-section members.

## Methods
- Matrix: low-heat cement + silica fume (20 %) + zirconia powder, $W/B = 0.15$, micro silica sand.
- Fiber combinations: Plain, steel alone (2.0 %), PE alone (2.0 %), hybrid (steel 1.5 % + PE 0.5 %, steel 1.0 % + PE 1.0 %, steel 0.5 % + PE 1.5 %).
- Testing: four-point flexural test on prismatic beams of $100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$ (span of 300 mm, LVDT measurement), 28 d compressive strength (100 mm cubes), splitting tensile strength.
