# Lunar Construction Research: Early Structural Concepts, Radiation Shielding, and In-Situ Materials

## Summary

This pioneering technical report, conducted under the auspices of ASCE and NASA during the early stages of the Apollo program in the 1960s, investigated structural concepts and construction techniques on the lunar surface. To minimize payload transportation mass from Earth, the report formulated foundational principles for utilizing processed lunar regolith and basalt rocks as shielding and structural media. Design requirements were systematically derived for subterranean shielding structures and pressurized habitat modules capable of withstanding lunar 1/6 gravity, high vacuum ($10^{-12}\ \text{Torr}$), extreme temperature fluctuations (-150 ~ +130 °C), cosmic radiation, and micrometeorite bombardment.

## Key Contributions

- **Definition of extreme lunar environmental load criteria**: Formulated design load equations accounting for reduced structural self-weight under 1/6 gravity, severe thermal stresses, and radiation shielding requirements (minimum 2 ~ 3 m regolith overburden).
- **Proposals for in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) construction methods**: Established early engineering frameworks for regolith sintering, cast basalt, and waterless binders.
- **Formulation of pressurized shell and shielding interfaces**: Proposed dual structural systems decoupling internal 1 atm tensile pressure containment from external impact/radiation shielding envelopes.

## Methods

- Structural mechanics analysis based on extreme space environmental parameters.
- Shielding thickness evaluation for cosmic rays (GCR) and solar particle events (SPE).
- Thermal balance and heat flux analysis across vacuum-insulated lunar habitat zones.

## Results

- Subterranean burial using an overburden layer of 2.5 m or more was proven optimal to mitigate severe thermal fatigue from solar surface gradients, stabilizing interior temperatures at approximately -20 °C.
- Established early recognition that waterless binder routes and thermal fusion are essential to overcome the impossibility of conventional hydraulic cement hydration in water-scarce space environments.
