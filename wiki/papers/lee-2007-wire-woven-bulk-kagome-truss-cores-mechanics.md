# Compressive Mechanics and Plastic Energy Absorption of Wire-Woven Bulk Kagome (WBK) Porous Metal Truss Cores: Maximizing Specific Buckling Strength and 4.5-Fold Impact Absorption

## Summary

To overcome the bending-dominated weakness of 2D honeycomb and foam metal cores as well as the manufacturing limitations of multilayer laminated bonding, Prof. Ki-Ju Kang and Prof. Insu Jeon at Chonnam National University developed the Wire-Woven Bulk Kagome (WBK) method. This approach mass-produces defect-free, 3D periodic multilayer Kagome truss cores by **weaving continuous helical metal wires along 6 symmetrical axes followed by node brazing**. Through uniaxial compression testing, theoretical strut buckling/plastic hinge analysis, and specific strength measurements across relative densities ($\bar{\rho} = 0.02\sim 0.08$), the authors demonstrated **ideal stretch-dominated deformation modes, a 3.2-fold enhancement in specific strength over random metal foams, a 4.5-fold amplification in gravimetric energy absorption under steady plateau stress, and robust failure resistance under multi-axial shear-compression loading** (Acta Materialia, 2007).

## Key Contributions

1. **First invention and automated mass manufacturing of 3D Wire-Woven Bulk Kagome (WBK) metal truss cores**:
   - Replaced tedious layer-by-layer bonding with continuous 6-directional wire weaving to produce monolithic multilayer truss cores.
2. **Maximization of specific strength and stiffness via stretch-dominated deformation**:
   - Leveraged the moment-free pin-jointed characteristics of Kagome lattices to elevate compressive strength per unit relative density to levels exceeding octet trusses.
3. **Plateau stress stability and 4.5-fold amplification of impact energy absorption**:
   - Maintained steady square-wave plateau resistance without stress drop up to $60\%$ compressive strain, demonstrating ideal crashworthiness characteristics.
4. **Analytical elastoplastic strut buckling model**:
   - Derived closed-form equations incorporating wire slenderness ($r/a$) and node braze fixity, matching experimental data with high fidelity ($R^2 > 0.98$).

## Methods

- **WBK truss manufacturing process (Section 2, Figs. 1–5)**:
  - Wire materials: High-strength carbon steel / stainless steel wires ($d_w = 0.5\sim 2.0\ \text{mm}, \sigma_y = 450\sim 850\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Weaving process: Helical pitch interlocking along 6 symmetrical axes $\rightarrow$ node paste coating (Cu/Ni) $\rightarrow$ vacuum furnace brazing ($1050\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Geometry: Strut angle $54.7^\circ$, relative density $\bar{\rho} = 2\sqrt{2}\pi (r/a)^2 = 0.02\sim 0.08$.
- **Compression and energy absorption testing (Sections 3–4, Figs. 6–10)**:
  - Static uniaxial compression at 1.0 mm/min up to $70\%$ strain.
  - Volumetric energy absorption: $W_v = \int_0^{\varepsilon_D} \sigma(\varepsilon) \, d\varepsilon$.

### 2. Multi-layer Kagome lattice deformation and energy dissipation
- **Plateau stress retention**: Sustained uniform plastic resistance across compressive strains from $10\%$ to $60\%$ without softening, achieving high energy absorption efficiency ($\eta = 0.92$).
- **Multifunctional applications**: Established utility not only as sandwich cores for ultra-lightweight structural panels, but also for fluid heat exchangers, cooling channels, and blast-resistant armor.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper established the manufacturing science and mechanics of wire-woven bulk Kagome truss cores, demonstrating stretch-dominated buckling and plateau energy absorption for ultra-lightweight structures and impact mitigation.
