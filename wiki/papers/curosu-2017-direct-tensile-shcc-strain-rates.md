# Tensile behavior and single-fiber micromechanics of a 140 MPa-class ultra-high-strength strain-hardening cementitious composite (HS-SHCC) reinforced with high-performance polymer fibers (HDPE, aramid, PBO)

## Summary

A landmark origin paper from Prof. Mechtcherine's group at TU Dresden that systematically investigated how single fiber–matrix interfacial bond characteristics and wettability govern the tensile strain-hardening behavior of a composite, using four types of high-performance organic polymer short fibers (HDPE, aramid Technora, PBO-AS, and PBO-HM; fiber length 6 mm, embedded at 2.0 vol.%) in one identical 130~145 MPa-class ultra-dense high-strength cement matrix (W/B 0.18, silica fume substitution, maximum aggregate size 0.2 mm); the program combined single-fiber pull-out tests at 2 mm embedment, contact-angle measurement (Wilhelmy technique), uniaxial direct tension tests, and fine multiple-cracking analysis. The study demonstrated micromechanically that the composite with hydrophobic, non-polar HDPE fibers (M-HDPE) exhibited stable frictional slip pull-out without chemical adhesion, achieving the highest tensile strain capacity (**3.9 %**) and the maximum fracture energy (**$227\ \text{kJ/m}^3$**), whereas the composites with hydrophilic, chemically affine aramid (M-ARA) and PBO fibers raised the first-cracking strength (5.5~6.3 MPa) and tensile strength (**9.8 MPa**) drastically through strong interfacial bonding ($\tau_0 > 3.8\sim 4.4\ \text{MPa}$) and controlled the mean crack width to an ultra-fine **14.6 µm**, but their strain capacities were limited to 1.4~1.6 % owing to increased fiber rupture.

## Key Contributions

1. **Correlation between polymer-fiber surface chemistry/contact angle and interfacial micromechanics**:
   - **HDPE fiber**: the non-polar olefin structure (water contact angle $\theta = 100^\circ$) carries no chemical adhesion, inducing complete pull-out slip at an interfacial shear strength of $\tau_0 = 2.20\ \text{MPa}$ $\rightarrow$ the highest strain capacity of 3.9 % and gentle softening behavior.
   - **Aramid fiber (Technora)**: surface polar groups and a coating layer form a chemical bond with the matrix ($\tau_0 > 3.78\ \text{MPa}$) $\rightarrow$ suppression of micro-flaws maximizes the initial cracking strength at 6.3 MPa; fiber rupture dominates.
   - **PBO fiber (Zylon AS/HM)**: built on an ultra-high tensile strength of 5800 MPa and high elastic moduli (180~270 GPa), delivering the maximum interfacial strength ($\tau_0 = 4.43\ \text{MPa}$) and the highest composite tensile strength (**9.8 MPa**).
2. **Attainment of extreme crack-width control (14.6 µm) in ultra-high-strength HS-SHCC**:
   - Through their high elastic moduli and powerful interfacial confinement, aramid and PBO fibers suppressed the mean residual crack width after load removal to 14.6~14.8 µm (one third or less of PVA-ECC), maximizing durability against moisture and ion ingress.
3. **Establishment of the PSH-bridging correlation between single-fiber pull-out and composite tensile behavior**:
   - Experimentally verified that the frictional pull-out behavior (HDPE) versus interfacial debonding behavior (aramid/PBO) identified in the 2 mm-embedded single-fiber pull-out curves correspond exactly to the softening slope and energy-absorption toughness of the composite tensile stress–strain curves.

## Methods

- **HS-SHCC matrix mix (unit quantities $\text{kg/m}^3$, Table 2)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (CEM I 42.5 R-HS) 1464, silica fume 293, water 316 (W/B 0.18).
  - Fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 0.06\sim 0.20\ \text{mm}$) 146, polycarboxylate superplasticizer (SP) 37.
  - High-shear mixing for 8 min in a 3-liter Hobart mixer.
- **Reinforcing polymer fiber specifications (fiber length 6 mm, 2.0 vol.%, Table 1)**:
  - **HDPE (Dyneema SK60)**: diameter 20 µm, tensile strength 2500 MPa, elastic modulus 80 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$, elongation 3.5 %.
  - **Aramid (Technora T202)**: diameter 12 µm, tensile strength 3400 MPa, elastic modulus 73 GPa, density $1.39\ \text{g/cm}^3$, elongation 4.6 %.
  - **PBO-AS (Zylon AS)**: diameter 13 µm, tensile strength 5800 MPa, elastic modulus 180 GPa, density $1.54\ \text{g/cm}^3$, elongation 3.5 %.
  - **PBO-HM (Zylon HM)**: diameter 13 µm, tensile strength 5800 MPa, elastic modulus 270 GPa, density $1.56\ \text{g/cm}^3$, elongation 2.5 %.
- **Test equipment and procedures**:
  - Single-fiber pull-out test: custom-built pull-out rig based on a micro-tensiometer, embedded length 2.0 mm, pull-out velocity 0.01 mm/s.
  - Surface wettability: Wilhelmy contact-angle measurement on single fibers.
  - Compressive strength: $40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}$ cubic specimens, 3000 kN universal testing machine.
  - Uniaxial direct tension test: dumbbell-shaped specimens (gauge cross-section $24\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 100 mm), displacement control at 0.05 mm/s, two LVDTs.
  - Crack-pattern analysis: digital optical microscopy-based precise determination of the number, spacing, and width of cracks in the unloaded state.

## Results

### 1. Single-fiber pull-out at 2 mm embedment and interfacial bond properties (Table 4)

| Fiber type | Nominal diameter (µm) | Fiber break stress (MPa) | Interfacial bond strength ($\tau_0$, MPa) | Dominant interfacial failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HDPE** | 20 | – (no fracture) | **2.20 ± 0.15** | **100 % pure frictional slip pull-out** |
| **Aramid** | 12 | 2523 ± 562 | **> 3.78** | abrupt fiber tensile fracture after debonding |
| **PBO-AS** | 13 | **3106 ± 474** | **4.43 ± 0.71 (maximum)** | fiber tensile fracture after partial pull-out |
| **PBO-HM** | 13 | 2757 ± 506 | 3.41 ± 0.61 | fibril damage and fiber fracture |

### 2. Uniaxial tensile performance and fracture energy of the HS-SHCC composites (Table 3)

| Composite ID | 28-day compressive strength (MPa) | First-cracking stress (MPa) | Maximum tensile strength (MPa) | Ultimate tensile strain capacity (%) | Fracture energy ($\text{kJ/m}^3$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M-HDPE** | 133.5 ± 2.5 | 3.8 ± 0.4 | 7.6 ± 0.8 | **3.9 ± 0.9 % (maximum)** | **227 ± 64 (maximum)** |
| **M-ARA** | **144.8 ± 1.4** | **6.3 ± 0.4 (maximum)** | 9.4 ± 0.6 | 1.4 ± 0.4 % | 118 ± 40 |
| **M-PBO-AS** | 140.3 ± 4.0 | 5.5 ± 0.6 | **9.8 ± 0.9 (highest)** | 1.4 ± 0.3 % | 113 ± 33 |
| **M-PBO-HM** | 142.9 ± 3.1 | 4.2 ± 0.2 | 8.4 ± 0.1 | 1.6 ± 0.1 % | 109 ± 13 |

### 3. Fine multiple-cracking control characteristics (Table 3)

| Composite ID | Mean residual crack width (µm) | Mean crack spacing (mm) | Multiple cracking and durability assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M-HDPE** | 34.6 ± 9.6 | 2.3 ± 0.3 | sound strain-hardening, maximum strain capacity |
| **M-ARA** | **14.6 ± 4.3 (minimum)** | **1.3 ± 0.1 (shortest)** | **highest-density multiple cracking, extreme watertightness/shielding** |
| **M-PBO-AS** | 14.8 ± 4.5 | 1.7 ± 0.1 | high strength realized, ultra-fine crack width |
| **M-PBO-HM** | 20.3 ± 5.2 | 2.4 ± 0.3 | high stiffness, fine crack control |

- **Overall conclusion**: establishes that HDPE-SHCC is the optimal choice for seismic and impact-resistant structures where energy absorption capacity and tensile ductility come first, while aramid/PBO-SHCC is the ideal solution for environments that demand extreme durability through delayed initial cracking and control of ultra-fine crack widths below 15 µm.
