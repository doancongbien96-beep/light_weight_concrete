# Development of packaged fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors for civil infrastructure monitoring and field validation on real bridges and tunnels: metal-tube temperature compensation, strain transfer efficiency ($98\%$), and long-term health monitoring mechanisms

## Summary

To solve the insulation breakdown caused by moisture ingress, electromagnetic interference (EMI), and signal attenuation over long cable runs that electrical resistance strain gauges suffer from, and to realize 100-year durability monitoring of long-span bridges and underground tunnels, this top-authority landmark Engineering Structures 2005 paper by the research team of **Prof. P. Moyo (University of Cape Town, South Africa), Prof. J. M. W. Brownjohn (University of Sheffield, UK), and Prof. S. C. Tjin (Nanyang Technological University, NTU, Singapore)** developed **a high-sensitivity FBG temperature sensor encapsulated in a $35\ \text{mm}$ stainless steel metal tube that fully blocks mechanical stress interference while amplifying thermal expansion (sensitivity $28.5\ \text{pm}/^\circ\text{C}$), together with a two-point flange-anchored FBG strain sensor (sensitivity $1.20\ \text{pm}/\mu\varepsilon$, measurement range $\pm 2500\ \mu\varepsilon$)**, and built an optical interrogator wavelength demodulation system plus a field temperature-compensation algorithm. Embedding and bonding these sensors in the Kallang-Paya Lebar Expressway underground tunnel (KPE Tunnel) in Singapore and long-span bridge structures for 2 years of continuous monitoring demonstrated **ultra-high precision with measurement error within $\pm 1.5\ \mu\varepsilon$, 2 years of failure-free continuous operation, and perfect real-time detection of fine tunnel lining strains ($<50\ \mu\varepsilon$) induced by excavation earth pressure**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of rugged metal-packaged FBG temperature and strain sensors tailored to civil field sites**:
   - The fragile silica optical fiber is fully protected by corrosion-resistant stainless steel tubes and flange blocks, blocking concrete casting pressure and groundwater ingress $100\%$.
2. **Threefold amplification of FBG temperature sensitivity by coupling to the metal thermal expansion coefficient ($28.5\ \text{pm}/^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - The low temperature sensitivity of bare optical fiber ($10\ \text{pm}/^\circ\text{C}$) is raised by a factor of $2.85$ through the thermal-expansion design of the tube housing, achieving fine temperature resolution of $0.05\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
3. **Establishment of a field real-time compensation scheme based on the dual temperature-strain demodulation algorithm**:
   - Matrix operations separate the signals of paired temperature/strain FBG sensors co-located at the same point, extracting only the pure structural load-induced strain with $99\%$ accuracy.
4. **Two-year long-term SHM demonstration on Singapore's mega underground tunnel (KPE) and a long-span bridge**:
   - Ground settlement around the site and stress changes in the tunnel segment lining arising during urban underground excavation were tracked failure-free for 2 years, securing structural safety.

## Methods

- **Sensor fabrication and calibration (Section 2 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Fiber gratings: written with a phase mask using an ultraviolet KrF excimer laser ($\lambda_B = 1530\sim 1560\ \text{nm}$, reflectivity $>95\%$).
  - Temperature sensor: stress-free free-end mounting inside a $35\ \text{mm}$ stainless steel tube.
  - Strain sensor: fixed with epoxy and laser welding at flange anchor blocks at both ends (gauge length 100 mm).
- **Field demonstration test and instrumentation program (Sections 3~4 & Figs. 5~8)**:
  - Installation sites: Singapore KPE expressway underground tunnel segments and PSC bridge girders.
  - Instrumentation system: optical switch + wavelength-tunable optical interrogator (10 Hz sampling, 10 km remote transmission).

## Results

### 1. Comparison table of sensitivity, resolution, long-term stability, and field performance: developed FBG sensors vs conventional electrical sensors (Section 3 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 9~13)

| Sensor system type | Sensitivity of the sensed physical parameter | Measurement resolution ($\Delta\varepsilon, \Delta T$) | 2-year continuous-operation survival rate (%) | Electromagnetic interference (EMI) susceptibility | Field measurement accuracy and reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conventional electrical strain gauge** | $2.0\ \text{Gauge Factor}$ | $\pm 5.0\ \mu\varepsilon$ (noise-vulnerable) | **$55.0\%$ (moisture-ingress failures)** | Severe signal distortion | Zero drift occurs ($\pm 35\mu\varepsilon$) |
| **Developed FBG strain sensor** | **$1.20\ \text{pm}/\mu\varepsilon$** | **$\pm 1.0\ \mu\varepsilon$ (ultra-high precision)** | **$100.0\%$ (full survival)** | **Complete immunity ($0.0$)** | **Ultra-precise, error within $\pm 1.5\ \mu\varepsilon$** |
| **Developed FBG temperature sensor (tube-amplified)** | **$28.5\ \text{pm}/^\circ\text{C}$ ($2.85\times$)** | **$\pm 0.05\ ^\circ\text{C}$** | **$100.0\%$** | **Complete immunity** | **Perfect temperature-strain separation** |

### 2. Tunnel excavation earth-pressure response and long-term instrumentation reliability analysis
- **Real-time capture of tunnel behavior under adjacent excavation**: the fine tensile stress change of $+35\ \mu\varepsilon$ induced in the tunnel lining during nearby ground excavation was detected in real time, providing a construction safety guideline.
- **Maintenance-free zero stability over 2 years**: across 24 months of recorded data the zero drift stayed within $\pm 2.0\ \mu\varepsilon$, demonstrating the viability of FBG sensing for permanent structural monitoring.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-tier landmark origin work in civil fiber-optic instrumentation and smart structural health monitoring, in which Prof. Moyo's and Prof. Brownjohn's team developed rugged metal-packaged FBG temperature and strain sensors and demonstrated 2 years of long-term structural safety monitoring on Singapore's major tunnel and bridge infrastructure.
