# Mechanical Properties and Micromechanical Mechanisms (Fracture Toughness and Interfacial Bond) of Highly Ductile Cementitious Composites (HDCC) Incorporating Limestone Powder

## Summary

To reduce $\text{CO}_2$ emissions from cement manufacturing and micromechanically optimize the tensile strain performance of highly ductile cementitious composites (HDCC/ECC), Professor Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University and Professor Yun Yong Kim at Chungnam National University replaced cement with non-calcined limestone powder (LP, $d_{50} = 15\ \mu\text{m}$, fineness $3800\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$) at 0 wt.%, 15 wt.%, 30 wt.%, and 45 wt.% (W/B = 0.45, PVA fibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$). The authors evaluated compressive strength (7, 14, and 28 d), JSCE dogbone uniaxial tension, **single-fiber pullout tests (chemical debonding energy $G_d$, frictional shear strength $\tau_0$, slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$), and wedge-splitting matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) tests**, establishing the correlation between macro-mechanical behavior and the micromechanical energy performance index ($J_b'/J_{tip}$). Increasing LP content lowered matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) from $0.46 \rightarrow 0.33\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{0.5}$ (reducing crack-tip fracture energy $J_{tip}$) and moderated excessive chemical interfacial bonding ($G_d$) from $2.14 \rightarrow 1.25\ \text{J/m}^2$, suppressing premature fiber rupture and promoting full fiber pullout. Consequently, the **energy performance index ($J_b'/J_{tip}$) increased by 57 % from $2.45 \rightarrow 3.85$**, enhancing uniaxial tensile strain capacity by **61.5 % from 2.6 % to 4.2 %**, while the 45 % replacement mixture (HDCC45) maintained a structural 28 d compressive strength of **31.2 MPa** and tensile strength of **3.5 MPa**.

## Key Contributions

1. **61.5 % enhancement in HDCC tensile ductility (2.6 % to 4.2 %) via limestone powder substitution**:
   - Replaced 45 % of cement with eco-friendly limestone powder while raising tensile strain capacity to 4.2 %, maximizing multiple microcracking dispersion.
2. **Micromechanical validation via single-fiber pullout and wedge-splitting fracture tests**:
   - Quantified a 28 % reduction in matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ ($0.46 \rightarrow 0.33\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{0.5}$) and a 42 % reduction in chemical debonding energy $G_d$ ($2.14 \rightarrow 1.25\ \text{J/m}^2$), satisfying steady-state flat crack propagation criteria.
3. **57 % expansion of the energy performance index ($J_b'/J_{tip}$: $2.45 \rightarrow 3.85$)**:
   - Expanded the strain-hardening safety margin well beyond the threshold ($J_b'/J_{tip} > 1.0$), ensuring robust multi-crack saturation.
4. **Preservation of structural compressive strength (31.2~48.8 MPa) and stress performance index ($\ge 1.3$)**:
   - Maintained a high strain-hardening stress ratio ($\sigma_{tu}/\sigma_{fc} = 1.28 \sim 1.35$) across all replacement levels.

## Methods

- **Mixture design and material specifications (Tables 1–2, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: OPC Type I + Limestone Powder (LP, $\text{CaCO}_3$ 98 %, $d_{50} = 15\ \mu\text{m}$, Blaine fineness $3800\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, specific gravity 2.70).
  - Fine aggregates: Silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Water-to-binder ratio: W/B = 0.45 (SP admixture 0.005).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray PVA fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 40\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa, modulus 40 GPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - 4 mix groups: HDCC0 (0 % LP), HDCC15 (15 % LP), HDCC30 (30 % LP), HDCC45 (45 % LP).
- **Testing program (Figures 1–2)**:
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C39 ($\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylinders, 7, 14, 28 d).
  - Uniaxial tension: JSCE dogbone specimens ($330\ \text{mm} \times 60\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm, $0.2\ \text{mm/min}$, dual LVDTs).
  - Single-fiber pullout: $1.0\ \text{mm}$ embedment in acrylic molds ($0.2\ \text{mm/min}$, calculating $G_d, \tau_0, \beta$).
  - Matrix fracture toughness: Wedge-splitting method ($75\ \text{mm} \times 75\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ specimens, clip-gauge CMOD, $0.001\ \text{mm/s}$).

### 2. Micromechanical mechanisms and material optimization
- **Chemical bond moderation**: Inert LP particles diluted $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ accumulation along PVA fiber interfaces, suppressing premature fiber rupture and maximizing complementary bridging energy ($J_b'$).
- **Matrix toughness reduction**: Lowering $K_m$ reduced crack-tip fracture energy ($J_{tip}$), allowing fiber bridging to capture microcracks and transfer loads into adjacent matrix zones.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work proved that substituting cement with limestone powder moderates interfacial chemical bonding and matrix fracture toughness, expanding the energy performance margin to achieve 4.2 % tensile ductility in sustainable HDCC.
