## Summary
A study evaluating the residual compressive strength, tensile mechanical behavior, microstructural phase transformations, and thermal stability of fly ash and metakaolin based strain-hardening engineered geopolymer composites (EGC) after elevated-temperature exposure ($200 \sim 800 °C$). Unlike conventional OPC-based ECC, which suffers matrix collapse at high temperature due to dehydration decomposition of $Ca(OH)_2$ ($450 °C$), it found that calcium-free/low-calcium geopolymer matrices maintain stable zeolite/ceramic binding phases even at $800 °C$. It demonstrated excellent fire resistance in which the matrix crack width remains stably maintained even after PVA fiber melting, preserving 75 % of the room-temperature compressive strength even after exposure to $600 °C$.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the ultra-high-temperature thermal stability of geopolymer matrices**: identified the mechanism whereby amorphous N-A-S-H gel sinters above $T > 600 °C$, transforming into crystalline ceramic phases of nepheline and leucite with renewed strength gain.
- **Analysis of residual tensile strength and crack modes after elevated-temperature exposure**: tensile strain-hardening maintained up to $200 °C$ ($\epsilon_u > 3.0 \%$); above $400 °C$, pseudo strain-hardening is lost due to fiber melting, yet a residual tensile strength of $2.5 MPa$ or more is secured.
- **Complete verification of steam-explosive-spalling resistance**: confirmed that explosive spalling does not occur at all even under $800 °C$ rapid heating exposure, thanks to the combined effect of porous geopolymer nanopores and fiber melting channels.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: Class-F fly ash 80 % + metakaolin 20 %, alkaline solution ($Na_2SiO_3 + NaOH 10 M$), PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15).
- **Heating test**: heating at $5 °C/min$ up to $200 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C, 800 °C$ with a 2 h soak at target temperature.
- **Analyses**: XRD crystalline phase analysis, SEM-EDS fracture surface microstructure observation, and 28 d residual compression and uniaxial direct tension tests.

## Results
- Residual compressive strength: room temperature $54.2 MPa \rightarrow 200 °C$: $58.5 MPa$ (increase from moisture drying) $\rightarrow 600 °C$: $41.8 MPa$ ($77 \%$ retained) $\rightarrow 800 °C$: $32.4 MPa$.
- Demonstrated unrivaled applicability as members for fire-resistant structures at elevated temperature and tunnel linings.
