## Summary
To evaluate the suitability of natural basalt fiber, which offers excellent fire resistance and corrosion resistance (basalt fiber: diameter 12 µm, tensile strength 1773 MPa, elastic modulus 89 GPa, density 2.65 g/cm³), as a reinforcement in highly ductile cement composites, this 2015 Materials paper by Dr. Choi Jeong-Il and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University comparatively analyzed, against commercial PVA and PE fibers, the interfacial bond characteristics of a single basalt fiber in an alkali-activated slag (AAS, compressive strength 42 MPa) matrix (chemical bond strength $G_d$, frictional bond strength $\tau_0$, slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$) along with the reduction rate of apparent fiber tensile strength and the strength reduction coefficient ($f'$) at fiber inclination angles of 0°, 30°, 45°, and 67.5°. Basalt fiber developed a chemical bond strength of **2.59 J/m², which is 87.6 % higher than PVA fiber**; however, its strength reduction coefficient $f'$ = **1.535** with increasing inclination angle came out **9 times and 3 times** higher than PVA ($f'$ = 0.171) and PE ($f'$ = 0.475), respectively, quantitatively establishing its brittle fracture character, in which the tensile strength drops abruptly **from 1773 MPa to 302 MPa, an 83 % loss**, at an inclination of 67.5°.

## Key Contributions
- **Precise extraction of the micromechanical interfacial bond parameters of a single basalt fiber**: chemical bond strength $G_d$ = 2.59 ± 0.20 J/m², frictional bond strength $\tau_0$ = 1.08 ± 0.19 MPa, and slip-hardening coefficient $\beta$ = 0.0054 ± 0.0005 derived.
- **Index modeling of the decay of apparent tensile strength with fiber inclination angle (0°, 30°, 45°, 67.5°)**: regression analysis on $\sigma_{fu}(\theta) = \sigma_{fu}\, e^{-f'\theta}$ proved the vulnerable transverse shear sensitivity of basalt fiber ($f'$ = 1.535).
- **Construction of a one-to-one comparison database of interfacial and fracture behavior against PVA and PE fibers**: identified the premature splitting brittleness of basalt fiber relative to the stable slip-pullout of PVA ($f'$ = 0.171).
- **Micromechanics-based prediction of the fiber-bridging stress–crack opening displacement ($\sigma-\delta$) response of basalt fiber**: clarified that the initial bridging stress is excellent but the stress decays abruptly as the crack extends.

## Methods
- Fiber types:
  - **Basalt**: diameter 12 µm, tensile strength 1773 MPa, density 2.65 g/cm³, elastic modulus 89 GPa, length 12 mm
  - **PVA**: diameter 40 µm, tensile strength 1202 MPa, density 1.30 g/cm³, elastic modulus 41 GPa, length 12 mm
  - **PE**: diameter 12 µm, tensile strength 2757 MPa, density 0.97 g/cm³, elastic modulus 110 GPa, length 12 mm
- Matrix: alkali-activated slag (binder 1.0, water 0.34, silica sand 0.4, SP 0.01, defoamer 0.0002; 28-day compressive strength 42 MPa).
- Tests: single-fiber pullout test (embedded depth 1.0 mm) and single-fiber tensile fracture tests at inclined angles (0°, 30°, 45°, 67.5°).
