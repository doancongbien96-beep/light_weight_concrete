# Dynamic Compressive Behavior of High-Strength EGC and Fiber Length Effects

## Summary

Investigated the effect of PE fiber length (6/12/18 mm, all at 2 vol %) on the static and dynamic compressive behavior of EGC using a Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB). Dynamic compressive strength, DIF, and impact toughness were evaluated over strain rates of 78~218 s⁻¹, and a prediction equation modifying the CEB-FIP model with a fiber aspect ratio term was proposed.

## Key Contributions

1. Inverted-U effect of fiber length: dynamic strength, DIF, and impact toughness all peak at 12 mm (dynamic strength 157.2 MPa, maximum DIF 1.88). Mechanism — at 6 mm insufficient embedment length limits energy dissipation; at 18 mm lower workability increases agglomeration and pore defects.
2. Static-dynamic inversion elucidated: static compressive strength actually decreases with fiber addition (PE hydrophobicity and absence of chemical bonding form weak interfacial zones + increased porosity), whereas under dynamic conditions fibers greatly enhance strength (crack propagation speeds up and bypasses weak zones → fiber bridging becomes operative).
3. Strain-rate-dependent failure mode transition: at low rates (≤91 s⁻¹) short fibers (numerous and well dispersed) favor bridging across crack faces; at high rates (≥160 s⁻¹) the larger embedment depth of long fibers favors energy absorption through fracture and pull-out.
4. Proposal of a DIF prediction model: the CEB-FIP model overestimates EGC, and trends also disagree with the equations of Luo (fiber-free geopolymer) and Khan (FRGC) → a dedicated PE-EGC equation (Eq. 8) introducing an aspect-ratio-based modification coefficient a.
5. Reconfirmed the observation that the elastic modulus of EGC is roughly half that of ECC — suggesting latent potential for dynamic load resistance.

## Methods

FA:GGBS = 7:3, activator 10 M NaOH+sodium silicate (modulus 2.3), W/B 0.25, S/B 0.16 (quartz sand D50 126 μm), BaCl2 retarder. Water curing 28 days. SHPB diameter 100 mm, specimens Ø100×50 mm, stress equilibrium ensured with brass pulse shapers, strain rate defined from the slope of the uniformly deformed region.

## Results

- Static compressive strength 86.7~107 MPa (high-strength class), decreasing as fiber length increases.
- Dynamic strength: PE6/12/18 at 143/157/139 MPa respectively (≈195 s⁻¹), +30~63 % relative to static.
- Impact toughness (area up to 2 % strain): PE12 highest at 1.82 J/cm3.
