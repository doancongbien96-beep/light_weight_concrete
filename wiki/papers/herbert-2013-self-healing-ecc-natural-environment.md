# Microcrack Self-Healing, Resonant Frequency (RF) Stiffness Recovery, and Tensile Reloading Behavior of Engineered Cementitious Composites (PVA-ECC) Under Outdoor Natural Weathering

## Summary

To validate whether engineered cementitious composites (PVA-ECC) achieve robust self-healing in real outdoor environments with four-season temperature fluctuations ($-11.7\ ^\circ\text{C} \sim +32.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$), natural rainfall, snow, and erratic wet-dry cycles, Professor Victor C. Li and Emily N. Herbert at the University of Michigan (Ann Arbor) induced tensile plastic pre-strains of 0.5 % and 1.0 % (mean crack widths of $24\ \mu\text{m}$ and $30\ \mu\text{m}$) at 7 d and exposed specimens to outdoor weathering for 12 months (1 year). Tracking non-destructive ASTM C215 resonant frequency (RF) and performing uniaxial tensile reloading after 1 year, the authors demonstrated that natural precipitation and ambient humidity **fully restored the resonant frequency of 0.5 % pre-cracked specimens to 105 % of their initial uncracked state (100 % recovery)**, while 1.0 % pre-cracked specimens regained 87 % stiffness. In subsequent direct tensile reloading after 1 year of natural exposure, healed cracks retained matrix-level tensile capacity, **recovering 100 % of first-crack strength and exhibiting 3.0 % secondary multi-cracking strain-hardening ductility**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First demonstration of 100 % mechanical self-healing in ECC under 12 months of natural outdoor weathering**:
   - Replaced artificial laboratory immersion with natural rain, dew, and snow, fully restoring dynamic modulus in ECC damaged to 0.5 % strain ($24\ \mu\text{m}$ crack width).
2. **Quantification of seasonal healing kinetics via non-destructive resonant frequency (RF)**:
   - Documented that RF, which dropped to 40~60 % immediately after pre-cracking, surged during spring rains (6~8 months of exposure) to reach saturation recovery.
3. **Recovery of strain-hardening tensile capacity ($\varepsilon_{tu} \ge 3.0\%$) upon reloading**:
   - Proved that calcite precipitation and secondary C-S-H growth fully bonded healed fracture faces, causing reloading cracks to initiate at adjacent sound matrix locations rather than reopening existing cracks.
4. **Correlation between tight crack width control ($w < 30\ \mu\text{m}$) and healing robustness**:
   - Established that keeping crack widths below $30\ \mu\text{m}$ via micromechanical optimization is the critical prerequisite for reliable autogenous healing in outdoor environments.

## Methods

- **ECC mix design and materials (Table 1, mass ratio)**:
  - Cement: OPC Type I 1.0.
  - Fly ash: Class F fly ash 2.2 (high volume FA/C = 2.2).
  - Aggregate: Fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$) 1.16.
  - Water-to-binder ratio: W/B = 0.27 (water 0.85).
  - Superplasticizer: Polycarboxylate HRWRA 0.01.
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray PVA fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elastic modulus 42 GPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, mass ratio 0.067).
- **Pre-damaging and outdoor exposure (Sections 2.2–2.3)**:
  - Specimen geometry: $305\ \text{mm} \times 76\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$ coupon plates, water cured for 7 d and pre-loaded in direct tension (MTS 25 kN at $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Pre-strain levels: $\varepsilon_{pre} = 0.5\ \%$ (5~8 cracks, mean width $24\ \mu\text{m}$) and $\varepsilon_{pre} = 1.0\ \%$ (12~16 cracks, mean width $30\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Outdoor site: Ann Arbor, Michigan (September 2011 to September 2012, 12 months full exposure).
- **Evaluation protocols**:
  - Longitudinal resonant frequency (RF): ASTM C215 (accelerometer, impact hammer, dynamic signal analyzer measured 1~2 times per month).
  - Uniaxial tensile reloading: 180 mm gauge length with 2-channel LVDTs after 12 months of outdoor exposure.

### 2. Natural self-healing mechanisms and infrastructure sustainability
- **Pozzolanic buffering in HVFA matrices**: Unhydrated fly ash (FA/C = 2.2) reacted with rainwater over long-term exposure, providing secondary C-S-H gel that bonded crack faces.
- **Elimination of maintenance costs**: Autonomous self-healing under natural weathering extends the maintenance service life of outdoor infrastructure (bridge decks, tunnel linings) by 2~3-fold.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark 1-year study proved that ECC microcracks undergo 100 % autogenous healing under real outdoor natural weather, fully restoring elastic modulus and tensile strain-hardening ductility.
