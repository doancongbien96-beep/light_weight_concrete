## Summary
A classic study that quantitatively elucidated, by means of stereological techniques, the spatial inhomogeneity of fibers arising during casting, compaction, and gravitational settling of steel fiber reinforced concrete (SFRC), the vertical segregation of material, and the anisometric orientation imposed by formwork boundary surfaces. Specimen cross-sections were cut and polished layer by layer, and the fiber count density per unit area ($N_A$) and cross-sectional orientation angles were measured with magnetic/optical techniques. Owing to the excluded volume effect of coarse aggregate and the formwork wall effect, fibers become strongly aligned into a two-dimensional plane near the top and bottom surfaces, and the mechanism producing top-to-bottom fiber density differences of up to 40 % was modeled mathematically.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of stereological fiber orientation tensors and dispersion indices**: a technique for back-calculating the three-dimensional orientation distribution function ($\alpha, \beta$) from the major/minor axis ratios of fiber ellipses on cut sections.
- **Quantification of gravitational settling and the formwork wall effect**: formulation of the transition zone of two-dimensional planar orientation as a function of the ratio of member depth to fiber length ($h/L_f$).
- **Analysis of anisotropic flexural capacity scatter across structural member types**: elucidation of the variability of structural performance depending on whether the casting direction coincides with the tensile stress direction.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: steel fiber SFRC ($V_f = 1.0 \sim 2.0 \%$, lengths 30 mm, 50 mm, diameter 0.5 mm).
- **Stereological measurement**: continuous cutting of vertical and horizontal sections from large block specimens, followed by X-ray radiography and image analysis.

## Results
- In horizontally cast flat plate members, the topmost 20 % zone exhibits low fiber density due to bleeding and settlement, while the bottom region and the 25 mm zone adjacent to formwork walls show 35 % higher fiber density with two-dimensional orientation ($\eta_\theta > 0.8$).
- Demonstrated that the spatial non-uniformity of fibers is the primary cause of the scatter in flexural failure strength of structural members (CV > 25 %), and proposed rheological criteria for uniform mixing.
