## Summary

This paper elucidates the effects of pretreatment with alkali-activator aqueous solutions (warm pretreatment WP and cooling pretreatment CP) on the mineral composition, matrix fracture toughness, PE fiber interfacial behavior, and tensile strain capacity of geopolymer EGC (corresponding author: Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University; Journal of Building Engineering, vol. 80). The ED-EGC-CP mix applying the cooling pretreatment (CP) recorded a strain capacity of 18.62 ± 1.59 %, reaching the highest level ever reported for EGC in the literature at the time. As a lightweight composite with a density below 1.7 g/cm³, it maintains a compressive strength of 21 MPa or more, and all mixes secured a strain capacity above 10 %. Activator pretreatment is an additional control axis layered on top of the SMP/SH = 1.5 optimum condition derived in the preceding study ([[nguyen-2021-ud-egc-13-7-percent]]).

## Key Contributions

- **Strain capacity of 18.62 ± 1.59 % achieved via cooling pretreatment (CP)**: a level never reported in the literature. The warm-pretreated (WP) mix reached 10.97 %, which is 41.1 % lower than CP.
- **Quantification of the strength–ductility trade-off**: WP raised f_cs and f_ts by 37.6 % and 57.4 % respectively, yet ε_sc was instead substantially lower — providing selection criteria for choosing between strength-oriented and ductility-oriented design.
- **Demonstration of an extreme gap in the PSH energy index**: PSH_E of WP 452.4 vs CP 817.2, meaning CP exceeds the practical lower bound of 2.7 for robust PSH by more than 300 times.
- **Elucidation of the matrix toughness reduction mechanism**: the CP mix's K_m of 0.06 MPa√m and J_tip of 0.62 J/m² are lower than WP's (K_m 0.11 MPa√m), showing that low matrix fracture energy is the direct cause of multiple-cracking saturation — explained through nano/microstructural analyses (XRD, FTIR, SEM-BSE/EDS).

## Methods

- Raw materials: Class F fly ash as the sole precursor, activator SH + SMP (sodium metasilicate pentahydrate), water 0.345, P-SP 0.003, defoamer 0.001.
- Mix proportion (Table 1, mass ratios): FA 0.7 + SH 0.1 + SMP 0.2 (SMP/SH = 2.0, same family as UD-EGC20), PE fiber 1.75 vol.%.
- Pretreatment variables: WP (warm pretreatment) vs CP (cooling pretreatment).
- Fiber: PE fiber (diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa, density 0.97 g/cm³, elastic modulus 88 GPa).
- Tests: JSCE uniaxial direct tension (gauge length 80 mm, loading rate 0.1 mm/min, LVDT measured on both sides rather than DIC), ASTM C109 compression, notched specimens for K_m·J_tip, XRD/ATR-FTIR/SEM-BSE-EDS.

## Results

| Item | ED-EGC-WP | ED-EGC-CP |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| First-crack strength f_cs (MPa) | 4.67 ± 0.36 | 3.40 ± 0.16 |
| Tensile strength f_ts (MPa) | 8.72 ± 0.76 | 5.54 ± 0.10 |
| Strain capacity ε_sc (%) | 10.97 ± 1.31 | **18.62 ± 1.59** |
| Toughness U_t (MPa m/m) | 0.73 | **0.83** |
| Compressive strength f_c (MPa) | 31.1 | 20.7 |
| Number of cracks N_c | 50.0 ± 2.9 | **72.8 ± 3.6** |
| Average crack width W_c (µm) | 171.8 ± 23.3 | 199.3 ± 7.4 |
| Crack spacing S_c (mm) | 1.57 ± 0.09 | 1.07 ± 0.05 |
| K_m (MPa√m) | 0.11 ± 0.00 | **0.06 ± 0.00** |
| J_tip (J/m²) | 1.25 ± 0.08 | **0.62 ± 0.04** |
| σ₀ (MPa) | 1.45 ± 0.05 | 1.28 ± 0.09 |
| J'_b (J/m²) | 64.79 ± 12.39 | 57.52 ± 19.20 |
| PSH_E | 452.4 | **817.2** |

- Interpretation: although WP has the higher bridging capacity σ₀ itself, CP's markedly lower J_tip maximizes the energy index. This contrast experiment demonstrates that ductility is in effect determined by 'matrix energy reduction' rather than by 'bridging reinforcement'.
- Lightness: both mixes have densities below 1.7 g/cm³, under the upper limit for lightweight concrete (1.92 g/cm³).
