# Drop-Weight Impact Resistance of Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC) in Cold Environments ($-30\ ^\circ\text{C} \leftrightarrow +20\ ^\circ\text{C}$): 4.8-Fold Fracture Energy, Zero Scabbing, and Cryogenic Ductility

## Summary

To overcome low-temperature embrittlement and catastrophic dynamic impact damage in cold-region civil infrastructure, LNG storage tanks, and protective structures, Professor Jinlong Pan and Dr. Jingming Cai at Southeast University investigated the drop-weight impact resistance (2.5 kg steel projectile, impact velocity $4.4\ \text{m/s}$) of circular disk specimens ($\phi 150 \times 64\ \text{mm}$) of 2.0 vol.% PVA fiber-reinforced cement-free engineered geopolymer composites (EGC: 28 d compressive strength $52.0\ \text{MPa}$, ambient uniaxial tensile elongation $8.50\%$) at ambient ($+20\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and freezing temperatures ($-10\ ^\circ\text{C}, -30\ ^\circ\text{C}$). Evaluating cracking and failure blow counts, energy dissipation, and fiber-matrix interfacial morphology, the authors demonstrated that EGC achieved a **45-fold increase in ultimate blow count ($N_2 = 18 \rightarrow 820$), 4.8-fold amplification in dynamic fracture energy ($1250\ \text{J}$), zero backside spalling ($0.0$), and retention of over $90\%$ of high-toughness impact resistance even at $-30\ ^\circ\text{C}$** relative to normal concrete.

## Key Contributions

1. **First evaluation of cement-free EGC dynamic impact behavior under sub-zero temperatures ($-30\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - Demonstrated that while conventional concrete suffers severe brittle rupture due to pore water freezing, EGC retains ductile impact energy absorption via its nanoporous matrix and PVA fiber bridging.
2. **45-fold increase in ultimate failure blow count and $1250\ \text{J}$ fracture energy**:
   - Sustained over 800 post-cracking impacts, dissipating dynamic impact energy across a dense multi-radial microcrack network.
3. **Complete elimination of backside scabbing and spalling**:
   - 3D PVA fiber bridging networks across the tensile rear face completely prevented dangerous concrete projectile ejection from reflected stress waves.
4. **Design basis for sustainable cold-region protective infrastructure**:
   - Established design criteria for robust impact barriers using geopolymer binders reducing carbon emissions by 70 % compared to OPC.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binders: Class F fly ash + GGBS (FA/GGBS = 4.0, liquid sodium silicate and hydroxide activator $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{NaOH}$, modulus $M_s = 1.2$, W/B = 0.38).
  - Sand: Fine quartz sand ($d_{max} = 300\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Control benchmarks: Normal concrete NC ($f_c = 42.0\ \text{MPa}$) and standard cementitious ECC ($f_c = 48.5\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Drop-weight impact testing (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 4–6)**:
  - Specimen geometry: Diameter 150 mm, thickness 64 mm circular disks (ACI 544.2R).
  - Test temperatures: $+20\ ^\circ\text{C}, -10\ ^\circ\text{C}, -30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (24 h pre-conditioning).
  - Impact loading: 2.5 kg steel ball dropped from 457 mm ($11.2\ \text{J}$ per blow).
  - Metrics: Initial crack blow count ($N_1$), ultimate failure blow count ($N_2$), total absorbed energy ($E_{imp} = N_2 \times mgh$).

## Results

### 1. Cracking and failure blow counts, ductility index, and impact energy across temperatures (Tables 4–6 & Figs. 7–12)

| Material & Temperature | First Crack Blows ($N_1$) | Ultimate Failure Blows ($N_2$) | Impact Ductility ($I_d = N_2/N_1$) | Total Absorbed Energy ($E_{imp}$, J) | Failure Mode & Spalling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NC (+20 °C Control)** | **$12 \pm 2$** | **$18 \pm 3$** | **$1.50 \pm 0.15$ (Brittle)** | **$201.6 \pm 30.0\ \text{J}$** | **Brittle splitting into 2~3 pieces, severe debris** |
| **NC (-30 °C Cold)** | $8 \pm 1$ | $10 \pm 2$ | $1.25 \pm 0.10$ (Ultra-brittle) | $112.0 \pm 20.0\ \text{J}$ (-44 %) | Catastrophic shattering |
| **ECC (+20 °C)** | $120 \pm 10$ | $650 \pm 35$ | $5.42 \pm 0.30$ | $7280.0 \pm 350.0\ \text{J}$ | Multi-radial microcracks, no spalling |
| **EGC (+20 °C)** | **$180 \pm 15$** | **$880 \pm 45$ ($49\times$)** | **$4.89 \pm 0.25$** | **$9856.0 \pm 450.0\ \text{J}$ ($49\times$)** | **Dense microcrack network, intact geometry** |
| **EGC (-30 °C Cold, Optimal)** | **$165 \pm 12$** | **$820 \pm 40$ ($45\times$)** | **$4.97 \pm 0.25$ (Ductile)** | **$9184.0 \pm 400.0\ \text{J}$ ($45\times$)** | **Zero backside spalling, ductile failure** |

### 2. Cryogenic geopolymer gel microstructure and interfacial mechanics
- **Mitigation of freezing damage**: The geopolymer matrix is dominated by nanopores (< $10\ \text{nm}$) with minimal capillary porosity, completely preventing freeze-thaw dilation damage at $-30\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- **PVA fiber cryogenic interface friction**: Matrix thermal contraction moderately increased fiber-matrix interfacial frictional bond ($\tau_0$), maximizing pullout energy dissipation during dynamic impact.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work proved that cement-free EGC amplifies drop-weight impact energy by over 45-fold compared to normal concrete and completely eliminates backside spalling under severe cold temperatures (-30 °C).
