## Summary
This study systematically investigated the effects of curing regime (high-temperature steam heating $60 \sim 90 °C$, ambient air curing $20 °C$, water curing, and dry sealed curing) on the geopolymerization reaction, compressive strength, tensile/flexural strength, and pore structure of fly ash and metakaolin based geopolymer concrete. It elucidated the mechanism by which heat curing accelerates dissolution of aluminosilicate precursors and $Si-O-Al$ polycondensation, developing 90 % ($65 MPa$) of the 28 d ultimate strength within 1 d, and proposed a mix optimization strategy of blended GGBS substitution at 30 wt % to initiate the early reaction under ambient curing.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of strength development kinetics by heating temperature ($60, 75, 90 °C$) and curing duration**: demonstrated that heating at $75 °C$ for 24 h achieves the highest degree of polymerization without microcracking, identifying it as the optimal condition.
- **Elucidation of the essentiality of moisture-loss-preventing sealed curing**: demonstrated that because water acts as a reaction medium during geopolymerization, blocking early drying is the key to reducing porosity.
- **Interpretation of microstructural evolution (MIP, XRD, FTIR)**: confirmed formation of a densified structure in which the fraction of harmless fine pores below $50 nm$ reaches 82 % under elevated-temperature curing.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: low-calcium class F fly ash 80 % + metakaolin 20 %, sodium silicate + sodium hydroxide alkaline activator ($Na_2O = 8 wt \%, Ms = 1.4$).
- **Curing conditions**: (1) ambient ($20 °C$), (2) heating at $60 °C, 75 °C, 90 °C$ (24 h), (3) water curing, (4) sealed curing.
- **Evaluation**: compressive and flexural strength, MIP porosity, FTIR functional group analysis, SEM-EDS.

## Results
- Mix heated sealed at $75 °C$ for 24 h: 1 d compressive strength $68.5 MPa$, 28 d compressive strength $74.2 MPa$, total porosity $11.2 \%$.
- Ambient unsealed curing: rapid moisture evaporation degraded 28 d compressive strength to $32.5 MPa$, confirming the importance of proper sealing.
