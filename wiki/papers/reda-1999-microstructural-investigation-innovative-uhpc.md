# Surface chemical reaction of calcined bauxite aggregate and void-free ITZ densification mechanism in 200 MPa-class innovative ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC)

## Summary

To raise the compressive strength of ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC) beyond 200 MPa and fundamentally overcome the defects of the weak aggregate-paste interfacial transition zone (ITZ), this study designed **a low-water-binder-ratio (W/B = 0.16) cement-silica fume composite incorporating calcined bauxite aggregate and limestone fine aggregate**, and rigorously evaluated SEM-EDS elemental mapping, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), and splitting tensile/compressive strength under hot-water and high-temperature steam curing ($90\ ^\circ\text{C}$) conditions, demonstrating **an extremely densified microstructure in which the alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) constituents on the calcined bauxite aggregate surface undergo a pozzolanic chemical reaction with the cement matrix during high-temperature curing, eliminating the ITZ entirely and delivering a 28-day compressive strength of $215\ \text{MPa}$ and a total porosity of $2.8\%$** — a 1999 landmark origin paper in Cement and Concrete Research (CCR) by the research team of **Prof. N. G. Shrive, Prof. J. E. Gillott, and Dr. M. M. Reda at the University of Calgary, Canada**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First elucidation of the high-temperature chemical reaction mechanism between calcined bauxite aggregate and cement matrix**:
   - Unlike inert aggregates, at high temperature ($90\ ^\circ\text{C}$) the calcined bauxite surface supplies dissolved $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ions, precipitating C-A-S-H gel directly at the interface and perfectly sealing ITZ pores.
2. **28-day compressive strength of $215\ \text{MPa}$ and ultra-high elastic modulus ($58\ \text{GPa}$)**:
   - Stable attainment of strength beyond 200 MPa through the Mohs hardness of bauxite (9.0) and perfect integration with the extremely densified matrix.
3. **MIP nano-pore structure analysis (total porosity $2.8\%$, zero harmful pores)**:
   - Demonstrates an ultra-low-porosity nanostructure in which capillary pores above $10\ \text{nm}$ are completely eliminated and only ultrafine gel pores ($<5\ \text{nm}$) remain.
4. **Optimization of aggregate volume fraction and binder paste savings**:
   - While the limestone-aggregate mix requires a massive paste content of $60\sim 70\%$ to reach equivalent strength, the bauxite mix secures 200 MPa-class performance with only $45\%$ paste.

## Methods

- **Materials and UHPC mix composition (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ASTM Type I ordinary Portland cement + zirconia silica fume (SF/C = 0.25).
  - Two aggregates:
    1. `Calcined Bauxite`: $d_{max} = 2.36\ \text{mm}$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 85\%$, $\text{SiO}_2 = 7\%$, Mohs hardness 9.
    2. `Limestone`: $d_{max} = 2.36\ \text{mm}$, $\text{CaCO}_3 = 96\%$.
  - W/B ratio: 0.16 (naphthalene/polycarboxylate-based superplasticizers applied).
- **Curing and microstructure testing (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Curing conditions: 1) standard water immersion at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 2) hot-water curing at $90\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (48 h), 3) steam curing at $90\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (48 h).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (7-day and 28-day ages).
  - Microstructure: SEM backscattered electron imaging (BSE), EDS line analysis, Micromeritics MIP ($414\ \text{MPa}$ high-pressure mercury intrusion).

### 2. Analysis of ITZ interfacial chemical reaction and crack propagation mechanism
- **Complete elimination of the ITZ defect**: BSE-SEM and EDS line analysis show that the $\text{Ca/Si}$ ratio transitions continuously from the bauxite aggregate boundary into the matrix, and the oriented $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ rim and porous gap observed in conventional concrete are excluded $100\%$.
- **Simultaneous induction of aggregate fracture**: upon application of failure load, the crack does not detour along the weak ITZ but penetrates directly through the ultra-high-hardness bauxite aggregate (transgranular fracture), maximizing compressive and shear resistance.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark origin paper of UHPC nano-microstructure design that fused the chemical reactivity of calcined bauxite aggregate with steam curing at 90 °C to achieve a compressive strength of 215 MPa and an ultra-low porosity of 2.8 %, eliminating ITZ defects at their source.
