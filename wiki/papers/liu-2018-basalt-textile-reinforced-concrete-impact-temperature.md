# Dynamic Energy Absorption and Tensile Membrane Failure Behavior of Basalt Textile Reinforced Concrete (BTRC) under Impact Velocities ($1.15\sim 4.62\ \text{m/s}$) and Extreme Temperatures ($-25\sim 100\ ^\circ\text{C}$)

## Summary

To improve the impact resistance of thin-plate concrete structures exposed to severe climatic environments (deep winter sub-zero cold of $-25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ through summer heat of $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and accidental impact loads, this landmark 2018 Construction and Building Materials origin paper from the research team of **Prof. Deju Zhu and Chair Professor Caijun Shi at Hunan University** fabricated a **textile reinforced concrete (BTRC: Basalt Textile Reinforced Concrete)** laminated with 4 and 6 layers of inorganic basalt-fiber 2D grid mesh (Basalt Textile), and using a drop-weight impact tester and a 1.0 MHz ultra-high-speed data acquisition system characterized the dynamic impact force ($F_{max}$), deflection displacement, fracture energy absorption capacity ($E_{abs}$), and interfacial debonding failure mechanisms as functions of impact velocity ($1.15, 2.31, 3.46, 4.62\ \text{m/s}$) and temperature condition ($-25, 0, 25, 50, 75, 100\ ^\circ\text{C}$). The results demonstrated that **the 6-layer textile reinforcement (BTRC-6) sustains a maximum impact force of $12.5\ \text{kN}$ and raises energy absorption capacity to $48.5\ \text{J}$, a 3.2-fold increase, and that even under high-velocity drop-weight impact at $4.62\ \text{m/s}$ the panel undergoes no perforation failure, repelling the impactor through two-dimensional tensile membrane action — outstanding impact-protection behavior**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstrated 3.2-fold increase in impact energy absorption with basalt textile ply count (4 layers vs. 6 layers)**:
   - As the number of textile layers increases from 4 to 6, the absorbed impact energy surges from $15.2\ \text{J} \rightarrow 48.5\ \text{J}$, completely blocking spall-fragment ejection from the back face.
2. **Characterization of dynamic impact sensitivity across the extreme temperature range ($-25\ ^\circ\text{C} \sim 100\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - Demonstrated that at $-25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ deep sub-zero temperature, freezing of pore water inside the matrix raises the peak impact force by $18\%$ while deepening embrittlement, whereas at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ high temperature, softening of the polymeric sizing coating strengthens ductile pull-out behavior.
3. **Establishment of impact velocity dependence and the tensile membrane (Membrane Action) resistance mechanism**:
   - As impact velocity increases from $1.15 \rightarrow 4.62\ \text{m/s}$, strain-rate effects raise the dynamic resistance linearly, and after matrix fragmentation the textile mesh behaves as a tensile membrane, preventing perforation (Perforation).
4. **Resolution of the fine-scale failure process within 10 ms based on high-speed data sampling (1.0 MHz)**:
   - Precisely instrumented the four-stage impact failure process spanning elastic contact $\rightarrow$ primary matrix cracking $\rightarrow$ textile tensile bridging $\rightarrow$ fiber pull-out/rupture.

## Methods

- **Materials and specimen fabrication (Section 2.1 & Table 1)**:
  - Matrix: high-flow non-shrink cement mortar (OPC + silica fume + silica sand + SP, 28-day compressive strength $65.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Basalt textile: 2D basalt grid mesh (grid size $10 \times 10\ \text{mm}$, filament tensile strength $2100\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $85\ \text{GPa}$, epoxy-coated treatment).
  - Specimen dimensions: $100 \times 100 \times 15\ \text{mm}$ thin flat plates, laminated with 4 layers (BTRC-4, thickness 15 mm) and 6 layers (BTRC-6, thickness 15 mm).
- **Drop-weight impact and environmental test equipment (Section 2.2 & Fig. 2)**:
  - Drop-weight tester: hemispherical steel hammer ($\phi 20\ \text{mm}$, mass $3.5\ \text{kg}$), pneumatic fixing jig ($\phi 40\ \text{mm}$ circular support opening).
  - Impact velocity: adjusted by drop height ($1.15, 2.31, 3.46, 4.62\ \text{m/s}$).
  - Environmental chamber: precision temperature control from $-25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (dry-ice cooling) to $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (infrared heating), followed by immediate impact.

### 2. Impact absorption and failure morphology interpretation
- **Tensile membrane (Membrane) action of the two-dimensional mesh**: even after localized pulverization of the cement mortar, the six plies of basalt textile distribute load in all directions as a three-dimensional net, absorbing the residual kinetic energy of the falling hammer through elastic tensile deformation and rebounding the hammer.
- **Temperature stability of basalt fibers**: basalt fiber itself does not degrade up to $-200\sim 600\ ^\circ\text{C}$, so fiber rupture strength is retained even at high temperature; hot-temperature impact absorption capacity does not deteriorate and performance remains excellent.

- **Overall conclusion**: this landmark origin paper experimentally established that basalt textile reinforced concrete (BTRC) provides full protection against high-velocity drop-weight impact at $4.62\ \text{m/s}$ while absorbing high energy of $48.5\ \text{J}$ even under deep sub-zero and elevated temperature environments, establishing protective-cladding technology for harsh environments and impact-resistant tunnel lining design.
