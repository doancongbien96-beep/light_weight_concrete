## Summary
This study experimentally and theoretically elucidated the decisive role of the size distribution and number density of initial flaws inside the matrix on multiple microcrack saturation and tensile strain capacity in the micromechanics-based tensile strain-hardening behavior of engineered cementitious composites (ECC). By quantitatively introducing artificial flaws (artificial porous microspheres, diameter $0.5 \sim 2.0 mm$) into the matrix to control the first-crack strength ($\sigma_{fc}$) and lower the crack-tip toughness ($J_{tip}$), crack saturation was maximized. It was demonstrated that at the optimal flaw size ($1.0 mm$) and content of 1.5 vol %, single-crack localization was completely suppressed and the tensile strain capacity increased 93 %, from $2.8 \%$ to $5.4 \%$.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the interaction between matrix flaw size and the PSH strength/energy criteria**: demonstrated the micromechanics principle that increasing flaw size lowers the first-crack strength $\sigma_{fc}$ and thereby raises the strength performance index $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$.
- **Derivation of the strength-degradation limit under excessive flaws**: identified the critical threshold beyond which, when the flaw size exceeds $2.0 mm$ or flaws are added in excess, the ultimate tensile strength drops sharply due to the reduced fiber-bridging cross-sectional area.
- **Establishment of a manufacturing technology for ultra-high-ductility composites via artificial flaw introduction**: developed a mixing technology that induces a uniform flaw distribution using foaming agents and hollow microspheres.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.28$).
- **Artificial flaw variables**: polystyrene beads (diameter 0.5 mm, 1.0 mm, 2.0 mm; content 0.5 %, 1.0 %, 1.5 %, 2.0 vol %).
- **Test items**: uniaxial dogbone direct tension test, matrix fracture toughness test, and image analysis of cross-sectional voids.

## Results
- Reference mix (no flaws added): tensile strength $5.2 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $2.85 \%$, 22 cracks.
- Optimal flaw mix ($1.0 mm$ beads at 1.5 vol %): tensile strength $4.85 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $5.42 \%$, 58 cracks (a 90 % increase in ductility).
- $2.0 mm$ flaw-size mix: tensile strength dropped sharply to $3.1 MPa$, confirming the loss of bridging capacity.
