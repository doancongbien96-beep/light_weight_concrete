# Drop-Weight Impact Resistance of High Strength-High Ductility Concrete (HSHDC): Zero Back-Face Spalling Under $4.5\ \text{kJ}$ Impact and Demonstrated Damage Resistance Versus US Army Cor-Tuf

## Summary

To fundamentally block extreme impact failure and back-face scabbing/spalling of military protective structures and infrastructure, drop-weight impact tests with a $150\ \text{kg}$ hammer ($1.5\sim 4.5\ \text{kJ}$ impact energy) were performed on $457 \times 457 \times 51\ \text{mm}$ panels made of **165 MPa-class high strength-high ductility concrete (HSHDC / HS-ECC: 28-day compressive strength $165\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial tensile strength $14.5\ \text{MPa}$, tensile ductility $4.5\%$, PE fiber $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$)** and the **US Army standard high-strength protective concrete (Cor-Tuf: 28-day compressive strength $210\ \text{MPa}$, straight steel fiber $V_f = 3.0\%$)**, precisely comparing impact load-time histories, back-face spall damage area, permanent residual deflection, and three-dimensional crack failure modes. While Cor-Tuf produced large back-face spalling ($320\ \text{cm}^2$) and brittle perforation cracks at $1.5\ \text{kJ}$ impact, HSHDC achieved a back-face spall area of $0.0\ \text{cm}^2$ (completely spall-free) even under triple ultimate impact of $4.5\ \text{kJ}$, absorbing 100 % of the impact energy through permanent flexural dome deformation — elucidating this protective mechanism. This is the landmark origin paper of the joint team of Prof. Victor C. Li (University of Michigan), Prof. Ravi Ranade (University at Buffalo), and Dr. William F. Heard (US Army ERDC), ASCE Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering, 2017.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of overwhelming impact superiority of HSHDC over US Army standard protective concrete (Cor-Tuf)**:
   - While 210 MPa-class strain-softening UHPC with 3 % steel fiber (Cor-Tuf) shed large back-face fragments from reflected tension waves, 1.5 % PE fiber HSHDC attained a back-face spall damage area of $0.0\ \text{cm}^2$.
2. **Complete prevention of perforation and shear punching under $4.5\ \text{kJ}$ ultimate drop-weight impact**:
   - Even under high-energy impact at a hammer velocity of $7.7\ \text{m/s}$, energy was dissipated through full-section plastic flexural hinge formation without local shear cone failure.
3. **Elucidation of impact energy absorption rate and plastic dome deformation mechanisms**:
   - HSHDC panels formed extensive concentric multiple microcracks around the impact point through ultra-ductile tensile strain-hardening of $4.5\%$, accommodating ductile dome deformation up to $18.5\ \text{mm}$.
4. **Establishment of spall-free criteria for anti-ballistic/anti-blast protective wall design**:
   - Set quantitative experimental design criteria for next-generation protective composites preventing secondary injury to personnel/equipment from back-face fragmentation.

## Methods

- **Materials and composite mixes (Section 3 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - `HSHDC mix`: OPC + silica fume + micro silica sand (W/B = 0.22; compressive strength $165\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strength $14.5\ \text{MPa}$, tensile ductility $4.5\%$; Spectra PE fiber $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - `Cor-Tuf mix (US Army ERDC)`: OPC + silica fume + silica flour (W/B = 0.15, 90℃ steam curing; compressive strength $210\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strength $11.0\ \text{MPa}$, strain-softening behavior; brass-coated straight steel fiber $V_f = 3.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 0.20\ \text{mm}$).
- **Drop-weight impact test program (Sections 3.3~3.5 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Panel dimensions: $457 \times 457 \times 51\ \text{mm}$ (simply supported steel frame, effective span $406\ \text{mm}$).
  - Drop tower: $150\ \text{kg}$ steel hammer ($\phi 100\ \text{mm}$ hemispherical nose); drop heights $1.0\ \text{m}$ ($1.5\ \text{kJ}$), $2.0\ \text{m}$ ($3.0\ \text{kJ}$), $3.0\ \text{m}$ ($4.5\ \text{kJ}$).
  - Instrumentation: piezo impact load cell, ultra-high-speed video camera ($10000\ \text{fps}$), laser displacement sensors, 3D laser scanner (damage area and residual deflection).

### 2. Interpretation of impact stress wave reflection and fiber pull-out bridging mechanisms
- **Plastic energy absorption of reflected tensile waves**: when the compressive impact wave reflects from the back free surface as a tension wave, dense PE fibers inside HSHDC absorb enormous plastic deformation energy through their $4.5\%$ tensile elongation, fundamentally blocking brittle separation (spalling) of concrete fragments.
- **Conversion to ductile flexural mechanism**: peak impact force was mitigated by $25\%$ while extending impact duration, realizing a shear-to-flexure conversion that distributes load over the whole panel.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is the top-level landmark origin paper that changed the paradigm of military protection and critical-facility impact-resistant design, demonstrating through one-to-one comparative drop-weight testing against the US Army standard protective material Cor-Tuf that 165 MPa HSHDC achieves zero back-face spalling and fully non-perforating plastic deformation even under 4.5 kJ ultimate impact.
