## Summary
To drastically reduce the usage of expensive ultra-high molecular weight PE fiber to half or less ($V_f = 0.8 \sim 1.2 \%$) while fully preserving ductile strain-hardening performance ($\epsilon_u > 4.5 \%$), this study applied the design-driven micromechanics optimization approach (Design-Driven Micromechanics Approach) developed by the research team of Prof. Jian-Guo Dai at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (PolyU) to a fly ash-slag geopolymer matrix. Through the chemical composition of the alkali activator and the addition of ultrafine limestone powder, the matrix fracture toughness was relaxed extremely to $K_m \le 0.30 MPa\sqrt{m}$, and at a low fiber content of merely **1.0 vol %** a 28 d compressive strength of **56.5 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **5.8 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **5.2 %** were achieved while cutting the fiber cost by **50 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a design framework for high-ductility EGC with ultra-low fiber content ($V_f = 1.0 \%$)**: establishment of an inverse-design mechanism that controls matrix brittleness ($K_m$) in proportion to the reduction of the fiber volume fraction.
- **Maintenance of the PSH energy index $J_b'/J_{tip} > 5.0$**: despite the halved fiber amount, the frictional interface characteristics ($\tau_0 = 1.8 MPa$) were maximized to induce stable multiple fine cracking.
- **Material cost reduction of 45 % and improved environmental performance**: the high fiber cost problem, the greatest commercialization obstacle of conventional ECC/EGC, was completely resolved.
- **Demonstration of multiple fine crack control**: more than 50 fine cracks formed in the 1.0 % fiber mix (average crack width $35 \mu m$).

## Methods
- Binder: Class F fly ash 60 % + GGBS 30 % + finely ground limestone powder 10 %, liquid sodium silicate activator ($W/B = 0.30$).
- Fiber: UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa).
- Testing: dogbone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, full-field DIC strain measurement), three-point bending fracture toughness test, 28 d compressive strength.
