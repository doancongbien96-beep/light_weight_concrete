## Summary
To overcome the thermal conductivity bottleneck of cementitious composites for building thermal energy storage (PCM-integrated cementitious composites), namely the delayed heat storage/release caused by the low thermal conductivity of phase change materials ($0.15 \sim 0.25 W/(m\cdot K)$), this study incorporated two-dimensional highly conductive graphite nanoplatelets (GNP, thickness 5 ~ 10 nm, plate diameter 15 µm) as a thermally conductive nanofiller (1 ~ 5 wt %) into shape-stabilized paraffin thermal energy storage cement, and characterized the effective thermal conductivity, latent heat storage capacity, thermal response rate, and mechanical strength. Through formation of a 2D GNP percolation network, the thermal conductivity of the composite was raised to **2.15 W/(m·K)**, a 4.2-fold improvement, and the heat charging time was shortened by **70 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Identification of the thermal conduction percolation threshold of 2D graphite nanoplatelets (GNP) ($\phi_c = 2.5 wt \%$)**: minimization of the contact thermal resistance between GNPs to form continuous high-speed heat transfer pathways.
- **4.2-fold enhancement of thermal conductivity ($0.52 \rightarrow 2.15 W/(m\cdot K)$)**: acceleration of the phase-change heat absorption/release response rate by a factor of 3.3.
- **Demonstrated thermal property stability after 1000 phase-transition cycles**: leakage rate below 0.05 % and latent heat capacity retention of 99.1 %.
- **Preservation of 28 d compressive strength**: the 28 d compressive strength of 38.5 MPa was retained owing to the nanofilling effect.

## Methods
- PCM: paraffin/expanded vermiculite shape-stabilized composite (latent heat $\Delta H = 135 J/g$, incorporation ratios of 15, 25 wt %).
- Thermally conductive nanofiller: GNP (1, 2, 3, 5 wt %), dispersed by ultrasonication with surfactant.
- Testing: Hot Disk TPS thermal constant analyzer, DSC latent heat analysis, thermal response rate measurement with an infrared thermal imaging camera, and 28 d compressive strength.
