# Mechanical Behavior and Heavy-Weight Shielding Mechanisms of Sustainable Concrete Incorporating Steel Mill Scale Iron Oxide Byproducts as Fine Aggregate Replacement: 46.5 MPa Compressive Strength

## Summary

To prevent river ecosystem degradation from unsustainable natural sand mining and recycle millions of tons of high-density iron oxide waste generated by the steel industry, Professors P. Ganeshprabhu and T. Felixkala at Karunya Institute of Technology and Sciences investigated sustainable concrete (M30 grade) incorporating steel mill scale ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 = 88.5\%$, specific gravity $4.85\ \text{g/cm}^3$) from hot-rolling operations as a replacement for natural river sand at 0~50 wt.%. Evaluating slump workability, 28 d compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, flexural strength, static modulus of elasticity, ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), and drying shrinkage, the authors demonstrated that a **20 wt.% mill scale replacement amplified compressive strength by 21.5 % ($38.2\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 46.5\ \text{MPa}$), achieved a flexural strength of $5.85\ \text{MPa}$, and increased concrete unit weight by 15 %** to provide enhanced radiation and acoustic shielding.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of feasibility for steel mill scale as natural sand replacement**:
   - Proved that high-density (4.85) angular iron oxide particles establish strong mechanical interlocking with cement paste.
2. **Coordinated enhancement of compressive strength (46.5 MPa, +21.5 %) and tensile properties at 20 wt.% replacement**:
   - The intrinsic particle strength and micro-filler pore refining effects of mill scale simultaneously enhanced splitting tensile strength ($4.10\ \text{MPa}$) and flexural strength ($5.85\ \text{MPa}$).
3. **Economical fabrication of heavy-weight radiation shielding concrete**:
   - Increased concrete density to $2.75\ \text{g/cm}^3$ without expensive baryte or iron ore aggregates, providing cost-effective radiation shielding for nuclear and medical facilities.
4. **Zero-landfill industrial upcycling and riverbed preservation**:
   - Established sustainable mixture design standards that alleviate natural river sand exhaustion and eliminate industrial landfill costs.

## Methods

- **Materials and concrete mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binders: Portland pozzolana cement (PPC, IS 1489 Part 1 standard, W/C = 0.45).
  - Fine aggregates: Natural river sand (specific gravity 2.65, fineness modulus 2.85, Zone II).
  - Replacement: Steel mill scale powder (0 %, 10 %, 20 %, 30 %, 40 %, and 50 % replacement, specific gravity 4.85).
  - Coarse aggregates: Crushed granite ($d_{max} = 20\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 2.70).
- **Testing program (Sections 2.2–2.5 & Figs. 4–6)**:
  - Fresh concrete: Slump cone test.
  - Hardened mechanical testing: Compressive strength (150 mm cubes), splitting tensile strength ($\phi 150 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ cylinders), flexural strength ($100 \times 100 \times 500\ \text{mm}$ prisms).
  - Non-destructive evaluation: UPV (ultrasonic pulse velocity, IS 13311 Part 1).

### 2. Microstructural interlocking and ultrasonic wave propagation
- **Mechanical mechanism of 20 % optimal replacement**: Angular mill scale particles developed high interfacial shear friction with cement paste and formed an optimized particle size distribution, producing ultra-dense concrete with UPV reaching $4.78\ \text{km/s}$.
- **Workability reduction beyond 30 %**: Excess high-density mill scale increased mix plastic viscosity, leading to compaction defects unless compensated by superplasticizers.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that steel industry mill scale byproducts serve as effective replacements for natural sand, achieving 46.5 MPa compressive strength and enhanced heavy-weight shielding properties.
