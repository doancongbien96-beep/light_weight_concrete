# 2.35× Enhancement of the Dynamic Damping Ratio ($1.85\% \rightarrow 4.35\%$) and Modal Analysis of Concrete with Waste-Tire Rubber Powder (#10/#40) Fine-Aggregate Replacement

## Summary

To reduce the seismic and wind-induced vibration responses of buildings and improve inter-story noise insulation performance, this landmark 2010 original paper by the research team of Professor George C. Yao and Ching-Yao Lin at National Cheng Kung University (NCKU), Taiwan, published in the Journal of Asian Architecture and Building Engineering, precisely evaluates the 28-day compressive strength, ultrasonic dynamic modulus of elasticity, natural frequency, and the **structural damping ratio ($\xi$) evolution behavior based on the Logarithmic Decrement** obtained from impact-hammer free-vibration cantilever modal tests as a function of the **fine-aggregate replacement ratio of waste-tire rubber powder (#10 coarse 2 mm, #40 fine 0.4 mm) (0 %, 2 %, 4 %, 6 %, 8 %, 10 %, 15 %, 20 %)**, elucidating the **viscoelastic absorption mechanism whereby the damping ratio surges 2.35-fold from $1.85\% \rightarrow 4.35\%$ at 15 % rubber replacement**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of a 2.35× increase in damping ratio ($1.85\% \rightarrow 4.35\%$) through rubber powder fine-aggregate replacement**:
   - Established a high-damping structural material technology that amplifies the damping ratio by more than $135\%$ relative to ordinary concrete, enabling rapid dissipation of the dynamic vibration energy of building structures.
2. **Elucidation of the correlation between rubber powder particle size (#10 vs. #40) and damping and mechanical performance**:
   - Proved that fine #40 rubber powder disperses uniformly inside the matrix, delivering higher damping-improvement efficiency than coarse #10 while minimizing the strength reduction.
3. **Derivation of the optimal replacement ratio (6~8 %) satisfying both structural strength (25 MPa) and high damping ($\xi \ge 3.2\%$)**:
   - Analyzed the trade-off between the compressive-strength reduction and the damping-ratio increase with increasing rubber incorporation rate, quantitatively presenting the optimal design threshold ($6\sim 8\%$) applicable to structural members.
4. **Establishment of a precise modal damping-ratio measurement scheme based on impact hammer and band-pass filtering**:
   - In the acceleration signal analysis of cantilever specimens, nonlinear torsional noise was excluded and the logarithmic decrement was rigorously computed over signal segments of $30\ \text{gal}$ or higher.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Sections 2~3 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Control concrete: OPC + natural fine aggregate + coarse aggregate (W/C = 0.48, target compressive strength $34.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Rubber powder:
    - `#10 Rubber`: $1.2\sim 2.0\ \text{mm}$ (passing No. 8~16 sieve).
    - `#40 Rubber`: $0.3\sim 0.6\ \text{mm}$ (passing No. 30~50 sieve).
  - Fine-aggregate weight replacement ratio at 8 levels: 0%, 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, 10%, 15%, 20%.
- **Dynamic modal test (Section 3 & Figs. 3~5)**:
  - Specimen dimensions: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ prismatic beam (300 mm cantilever support).
  - Loading and instrumentation: impact-hammer strike $\rightarrow$ measurement at the beam end with a high-sensitivity accelerometer $\rightarrow$ 5~55 Hz band-pass filtering $\rightarrow$ logarithmic decrement $\delta = \frac{1}{n} \ln(A_0/A_n) \rightarrow \xi = \frac{\delta}{2\pi}$.

### 2. Interpretation of the impact-vibration absorption mechanism of viscoelastic rubber particles
- **Hysteretic loss through viscoelastic shear deformation**: rubber particles whose elastic modulus is more than 100× lower than that of the cement hydration matrix intensively absorb local shear deformation under dynamic vibration, converting mechanical kinetic energy into heat.
- **Wave scattering due to acoustic impedance mismatch**: the enormous elastic-wave velocity difference across the rubber–cement paste interface causes stress waves to undergo multiple refraction and scattering at the interface, suppressing vibration transmission.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original study in the field of eco-friendly high-damping building structural materials, raising the structural damping ratio of concrete by 2.35× up to $4.35\%$ using waste-tire rubber powder and establishing the optimal replacement ratio ($6\sim 8\%$) that satisfies structural strength requirements.
