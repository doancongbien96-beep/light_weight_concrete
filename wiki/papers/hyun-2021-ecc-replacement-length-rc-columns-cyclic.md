# Influence of ECC Replacement Length ($L_R = 3.6d \sim 5.4d$) in the Plastic Hinge Zone on Cyclic Lateral Loading Behavior, Ductility Ratio, and Cumulative Energy Dissipation of RC Columns

## Summary

To prevent cover concrete spalling, longitudinal rebar buckling, and rapid strength degradation in the critical plastic hinge zone of reinforced concrete (RC) column-footing connections during earthquakes, a collaborative research team from Chonnam National University (Professor Bang Yeon Lee), Chungnam National University (Professor Yun Yong Kim), Ssangyong E&C (Jung-Hwan Hyun), and Dongyang Corp (Dr. Jin-Wook Bang) evaluated RC columns with plastic hinge concrete partially replaced by strain-hardening ECC (PVA fibers 2.0 vol.%, tensile strain capacity $3.55\%$, tensile strength $5.47\ \text{MPa}$) across lengths of $3.6d$ ($900\ \text{mm}$) and $5.4d$ ($1350\ \text{mm}$) relative to column depth ($d = 250\ \text{mm}$). Under an axial load ratio of 10 % (axial load $20\ \text{kN}$) and displacement-controlled cyclic lateral loading, the authors compared hysteretic responses, crack distributions, ductility ratios, and cumulative energy dissipation. Relative to the plain RC control, ECC composite columns increased peak lateral capacity by **21.4 % ($32.2 \rightarrow 39.1\ \text{kN}$)**, extended ultimate lateral displacement by **39.7 % ($49.6 \rightarrow 69.3\ \text{mm}$)**, enhanced displacement ductility ratio by **19.4 % ($2.88 \rightarrow 3.44$)**, and boosted cumulative energy dissipation by up to **47.7 %**, confirming that ECC strain-hardening microcracking effectively prevents cover spalling and longitudinal rebar buckling.

## Key Contributions

1. **Investigation of RC composite columns across varying ECC replacement lengths ($L_R$)**:
   - Compared $L_R = 3.6d$ and $5.4d$ to establish quantitative design guidelines ($L_R \ge 3.6d$) for optimal replacement length.
2. **Mitigation of plastic hinge damage via ECC multiple microcracking**:
   - While the RC control experienced major shear cracks and cover spalling within $1.0d$, ECC columns distributed damage into dozens of microcracks across $>3.6d$, relieving stress concentrations.
3. **Seismic retrofit economics via 47.7 % energy dissipation gain**:
   - Identified $L_R = 3.6d$ as the economical optimum since ductility saturated beyond $3.6d$, with $5.4d$ providing only marginal additional energy dissipation.

## Methods

- **ECC mixture proportions (Table 2, $\text{kg/m}^3$)**:
  - Water 362, OPC 411, fly ash 1152, SP admixture 0.494, HPMC thickener 0.165, defoamer 0.041, PVA fibers 2.0 vol.%.
  - Fiber specifications: Diameter $39\ \mu\text{m}$, length $12\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength $1600\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $40\ \text{GPa}$, density $1.3\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
  - Tensile properties: First crack strength $4.21\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strength $5.47\ \text{MPa}$, strain capacity $3.55\%$.
- **Concrete mixture proportions (Table 3)**:
  - W/B = 53.8 %, cement 216, GGBS 47, fly ash 47, fine aggregate 892, coarse aggregate 931 ($d_{max} = 15\ \text{mm}$), chemical admixture 2.2 $\text{kg/m}^3$.
  - 28 d compressive strength $24.2\ \text{MPa}$.
- **Column specimens (Figs. 3–4, Table 4)**:
  - Section: $250\ \text{mm} \times 250\ \text{mm}$, clear shear span height $1540\ \text{mm}$.
  - Longitudinal steel: 8-D13, transverse ties: D10 @ 100 mm.
  - Test series: RC (Control), ECC3.6d ($L_R = 900\ \text{mm}$), ECC5.4d ($L_R = 1350\ \text{mm}$).
- **Cyclic testing protocols (Figs. 5–6)**:
  - Axial load: 300 kN hydraulic actuator maintaining constant $20\ \text{kN}$ (10 % axial capacity).
  - Lateral displacement: 500 kN actuator applying 2 cycles per drift step.
  - Instrumentation: Column-top LVDT, strain gauges on longitudinal rebar.

### 2. Crack patterns and failure mechanisms
- **RC column**: Severe diagonal shear cracking within $1.0d$ ($250\ \text{mm}$) of the base $\rightarrow$ cover spalling $\rightarrow$ rebar buckling $\rightarrow$ rapid brittle strength drop.
- **ECC3.6d / ECC5.4d columns**: Dozens of tight flexural microcracks ($< 0.1\ \text{mm}$) formed across the ECC zone, suppressing shear crack opening, preventing cover spalling and rebar buckling, and maintaining stable hysteretic loops.
- **Ductility convergence**: Displacement ductility factors for $3.6d$ and $5.4d$ were virtually identical (3.44 vs 3.40), proving that ductility benefits saturate at $L_R = 3.6d$, with $5.4d$ contributing an additional 10 % in energy dissipation.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that an ECC replacement length of $3.6d$ in the plastic hinge zone optimizes seismic performance, enhancing lateral capacity, displacement ductility, and energy dissipation while eliminating cover spalling.
