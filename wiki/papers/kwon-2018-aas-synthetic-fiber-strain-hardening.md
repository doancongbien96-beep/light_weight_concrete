# Tensile Strain-Hardening Behaviors, Stress Performance Indices, and Saturated Crack Spacing of Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Composites with Synthetic Fibers (PP, PE, PBO) and W/B Ratios ($0.35\sim 0.55$)

## Summary

In cementless alkali-activated slag (**AAS**) matrices designed to drastically reduce carbon dioxide emissions, this study systematically analyzes the effects of 3 synthetic fiber types (**PP, PE, PBO; each at $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$**) and water-to-binder ratios (**W/B = 0.35, 0.45, 0.55**) on compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening response (first crack strength, ultimate tensile strength, tensile strain capacity), stress performance index ($\sigma_{tu}/\sigma_{fc}$), and multiple cracking saturation characteristics (crack count and crack spacing $x_d$ over an 80 mm gauge length). Conducted by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), Seong-Joon Kwon, Jeong-Il Choi, and Huy Hoàng Nguyễn at Chonnam National University (Computers and Concrete, 2018), results demonstrated that the **PE fiber reinforced W/B 0.35 mixture (PE-0.35) achieved an ultimate tensile strength of $12.37\ \text{MPa}$ and ultra-high tensile strain capacity of $5.98\%$ with saturated multiple microcracking**. The **PBO fiber mixture (PBO-0.35) delivered a peak tensile strength of $15.22\ \text{MPa}$ and an ultra-dense crack spacing of 0.86 mm**, while the economical PP fiber mixture (PP-0.45) secured a tensile strain capacity of $3.35\%$.

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive 1:1 benchmark database of 3 synthetic fibers (PP/PE/PBO) in AAS matrices**:
   - Quantified the isolated influences of fiber elastic modulus ($8.5\sim 270\ \text{GPa}$) and tensile strength ($850\sim 5800\ \text{MPa}$) on tensile strain-hardening and crack spacing across W/B ratios of 0.35, 0.45, and 0.55.
2. **Simultaneous achievement of $12.37\ \text{MPa}$ tensile strength and $5.98\%$ strain capacity in PE-0.35**:
   - Synergized hydrophobic PE fiber frictional pullout with high-strength AAS matrix densification, yielding a stress performance index of 3.12 and saturated crack spacing of 1.66 mm.
3. **Identification of ultra-high strength ($15.22\ \text{MPa}$) and ultra-fine crack spacing (0.86 mm) in PBO composites**:
   - The high elastic modulus ($270\ \text{GPa}$) and strong matrix bond of PBO fibers elevated first crack strength to $6.48\ \text{MPa}$ and minimized crack opening displacements, driving multiple microcrack proliferation.
4. **Optimization of W/B for fiber dispersion and composite ductility**:
   - For PE and PBO fibers, lowering W/B elevated matrix density and composite ductility, whereas lower-modulus PP fibers attained peak ductility ($3.35\%$) at W/B = 0.45 due to optimal workability-bond balance.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3)**:
  - Binder: Ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBFS, Blaine fineness $4380\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Alkaline activator: NaOH powder + liquid sodium silicate (waterglass), $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ equivalent concentration 4.0 % of binder, Ms = 1.0.
  - Water-to-binder ratios: W/B = 0.35, 0.45, 0.55 (3 levels).
  - 3 synthetic fiber types (fixed volume fraction $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    1. **PP fiber**: Diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 12 mm, strength $850\ \text{MPa}$, modulus $8.5\ \text{GPa}$.
    2. **PE fiber**: Diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, strength $2900\ \text{MPa}$, modulus $88\ \text{GPa}$.
    3. **PBO fiber**: Diameter $13\ \mu\text{m}$, length 12 mm, strength $5800\ \text{MPa}$, modulus $270\ \text{GPa}$.
- **Testing procedures (Section 2.2, Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 $50\ \text{mm}$ cubes cured for 28 d.
  - Direct uniaxial tension: JSCE dumbbell specimens ($30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, $80\ \text{mm}$ gauge length) loaded at $0.2\ \text{mm/min}$.
  - Crack pattern characterization: Total count and average crack spacing $x_d$ measured across the 80 mm gauge length.

### 2. Fiber strain-hardening and cracking mechanics
- **Mechanism of ultra-high ductility ($5.98\%$) in PE composites**: Combines balanced interfacial friction and high fiber strength ($2900\ \text{MPa}$) to enable extensive fiber pullout slip without premature rupture.
- **Mechanism of dense cracking (0.86 mm spacing) in PBO composites**: High elastic modulus ($270\ \text{GPa}$) minimizes crack opening elongation per crack, shedding load rapidly to uncracked matrix zones and maximizing crack density.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's group systematically evaluated synthetic fiber reinforcement in cementless AAS matrices, demonstrating $5.98\%$ tensile strain capacity with PE fibers and $15.22\ \text{MPa}$ peak strength with PBO fibers.
