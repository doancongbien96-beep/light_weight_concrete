# Mechanical Behavior of Thin-Laminated Cementitious Composites Incorporating Cenosphere Fillers

## Summary

To produce lightweight thin-laminated cementitious composites (ferrocement/LCC), fine sand in the matrix was replaced with fly ash cenospheres (FAC, density 720 kg/m3) at 40/50/60 wt %, with flexural performance evaluated using hybrid reinforcement combining 1~2 layers of galvanized iron (GI) welded wire mesh or fiberglass (FG) mesh with 0.5 % PVA fibers. The composite satisfied structural lightweight concrete requirements and exhibited superior specific strength compared to conventional lightweight fillers.

## Key Contributions

1. **FAC replacement efficiency**: Every 10 wt % increment of FAC reduced density by ~8 %. At 40 % FAC, density decreased by −33 % (1458 kg/m3) with only a 12 % reduction in compressive strength, outperforming conventional lightweight aggregates such as expanded vermiculite or glass. Maximum specific strength reached 26.65 kPa/(kg/m3).
2. **Matrix strength reduction facilitating strain-hardening**: Post-cracking flexural strength gains in FAC mixes were larger than in sand mixes (e.g., +50~83 % in single-layer FG with FAC vs +37 % with sand). Lower $\sigma_c$ effectively engaged fiber and mesh tensile capacity, extending PSH margin logic to structural laminates.
3. **Inverse relationship between mesh layers and deformation**: Two-layer reinforcement doubled flexural strength but reduced ultimate failure strain due to reduced effective depth, indicating potential sudden failure risks.
4. **Re-evaluation of ACI 318 lightweight factor $\lambda$**: The standard $\lambda = 0.75$ underestimated elastic modulus in FAC mixes; $\lambda = 0.90$ was proposed.
5. **Multiple cracking**: Sustained multiple cracking across the central 1/3 flexural span without mesh delamination.

## Methods

- Binders: OPC 52.5 + 10 % silica fume, W/CM = 0.30~0.45, target flow 160~200 mm.
- Testing: 3-point bending (span 300 mm, loading zone 100 mm), ASTM C469 static elastic modulus, and conversion of flexural to equivalent tensile strength via Eq. 7.

## Results

- Matrix properties: Compressive strength and modulus were 38.9 MPa/13.1 GPa at 40 % FAC and 22.4 MPa/11.3 GPa at 60 % FAC, compared to 72.5 MPa/26.7 GPa in the sand control.
- Composite properties: Maximum flexural strength reached 34.2 MPa (sand + 2 layers GI mesh), with energy absorption up to 96 kJ/m2.
