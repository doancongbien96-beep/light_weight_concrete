# Unprecedented Simultaneous Realization of Ultrahigh Damping and High Stiffness in a Cement–Exfoliated Graphite (Graphite Network) Composite: Loss Modulus $E'' = 7.5\ \text{GPa}$ (1100 times rubber), Loss Tangent $\tan\delta = 0.81$ Mechanism — Immortal Classic

## Summary

To fundamentally break through the chronic trade-off suffered by traditional vibration damping materials (rubbers and polymers have high vibration damping $\tan\delta$ but extremely low stiffness $E'$ and energy dissipation coefficient $E''$ and therefore cannot support structural loads, whereas cast irons and metal composites have high stiffness but very low $\tan\delta$), the research team of **Deborah D. L. Chung, State University of New York at Buffalo (SUNY Buffalo)** developed a **cement–graphite nano-network composite formed by combining an interpenetrating three-dimensional network of fine exfoliated graphite worms ($34\sim 54\ \text{vol.}\%$ by volume) with micro carbon fibers inside an ordinary Portland cement matrix**, and measured its dynamic viscoelastic behavior by dynamic mechanical thermal analysis (DMA, $0.2\sim 2.0\ \text{Hz}$); the measurements demonstrated **the unprecedented coexistence of ultrahigh stiffness and ultrahigh damping in the history of world materials engineering — a storage modulus of $E' = 9.3\ \text{GPa}$, a loss tangent of $\tan\delta = 0.81$ (exceeding the rubber value of $\tan\delta = 0.67$), and a loss modulus of $E'' = 7.5\ \text{GPa}$ (1120 times the $6.7\ \text{MPa}$ of conventional rubber and 2.5 times the $3.0\ \text{GPa}$ of shape-memory alloys and metal composites)** — the most authoritative immortal landmark classic paper of Carbon 2010.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first complete shattering of materials engineering's long-standing stiffness–damping trade-off**:
   - Achieved a vibration damping ratio ($\tan\delta = 0.81$) exceeding that of rubber together with a record-high loss modulus ($E'' = 7.5\ \text{GPa}$) while maintaining a high elastic modulus ($E' = 9.3\ \text{GPa}$).
2. **Elucidation of the interlayer sliding nanoscale energy dissipation mechanism of exfoliated graphite**:
   - Demonstrated that the weak van der Waals bonding acting between graphite basal planes produces reversible frictional sliding under dynamic loading, dissipating vibration energy into heat essentially instantaneously.
3. **Creation of smart concrete integrating the structural support function with active/passive vibration control**:
   - Demonstrated applicability as foundation pads absorbing seismic and machinery vibrations, floor slabs for ultraprecision factories, and bridge bearing dampers.
4. **Establishment of the theoretical apex of global multifunctional smart cement composite materials**:
   - Presented the paradigm of integrally designing structural load support and energy-absorbing damping within a single inorganic composite.

## Methods

- **Materials and composite fabrication (Section 2 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Matrix: Type I ordinary Portland cement paste ($W/C = 0.35$, methylcellulose dispersant).
  - Graphite network: thermally exfoliated graphite oxide (Exfoliated Graphite: expansion ratio about 150-fold, compression-molded into worm-form three-dimensional networks, $V_g = 34\sim 54\ \text{vol.}\%$).
  - Comparison group: plain concrete, natural rubber, cast iron, aluminum–metal composite, NiTi shape-memory alloy.
- **Dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) program (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Three-point bending dynamic loading (frequencies $0.2, 1.0, 2.0\ \text{Hz}$, amplitude $10\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Instrumentation: storage modulus ($E'$), loss modulus ($E''$), loss tangent ($\tan\delta = E''/E'$).

## Results

### 1. Comparison table of storage modulus ($E'$), loss tangent ($\tan\delta$), and loss modulus ($E''$) for various engineering materials versus the cement–graphite network composite (Section 3 & Tables 1~2 & Figs. 8~12)

| Material type | Storage modulus ($E'$, GPa) | Loss tangent ($\tan\delta$, vibration damping ratio) | Loss modulus ($E''$, GPa, energy dissipation capacity) | Vibration-energy attenuation classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Natural rubber** | **$0.010\ \text{GPa}$ ($10\ \text{MPa}$, extremely low stiffness)** | **$0.67 \pm 0.05$ (high damping)** | **$0.0067\ \text{GPa}$ ($6.7\ \text{MPa}$, lowest)** | **High damping / cannot support load** |
| **Plain concrete** | $30.0 \pm 1.5\ \text{GPa}$ | $0.015 \pm 0.002$ (extremely low damping) | $0.45 \pm 0.05\ \text{GPa}$ | High stiffness / brittle vibration transmission |
| **Cast iron** | $120.0 \pm 5.0\ \text{GPa}$ | $0.005 \pm 0.001$ | $0.60 \pm 0.05\ \text{GPa}$ | High stiffness / insufficient damping |
| **NiTi shape-memory alloy (SMA)** | $70.0 \pm 3.0\ \text{GPa}$ | $0.040 \pm 0.003$ | $2.80 \pm 0.15\ \text{GPa}$ | Excellent metallic damping material |
| **Metal-matrix composite (MMC)** | $100.0 \pm 4.0\ \text{GPa}$ | $0.030 \pm 0.002$ | $3.00 \pm 0.18\ \text{GPa}$ | High-stiffness damping material |
| **Cement–graphite composite (developed in this work)** | **$9.3 \pm 0.4\ \text{GPa}$ (high stiffness retained)** | **$0.81 \pm 0.04$ (surpassing rubber)** | **$7.50 \pm 0.35\ \text{GPa}$ ($1120\times$ rubber)** | **World-best integration of ultrahigh stiffness and ultrahigh damping** |

### 2. Analysis of graphite interlayer shear dissipation and frequency dependence

- **Nanoscale sliding dissipation**: under DMA cyclic loading, the frictional viscoelastic hysteresis loops generated at the contact faces of the exfoliated graphite three-dimensional network absorb more than $85\%$ of the $1.0\ \text{Hz}$ vibration energy per cycle.
- **Frequency stability**: ultrahigh damping performance of $\tan\delta \ge 0.75$ is perfectly maintained over the entire band of seismic and structural vibrations, $0.2\sim 2.0\ \text{Hz}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper, in which the research team of University Professor Deborah Chung formed a three-dimensional exfoliated graphite network within a cement matrix and demonstrated an unprecedented ultrahigh-stiffness, ultrahigh-damping composite material simultaneously exhibiting a storage modulus of 9.3 GPa and a loss modulus of 7.5 GPa (1100 times rubber), is the immortal supreme landmark classic of carbon composite materials and smart vibration-protection engineering.
