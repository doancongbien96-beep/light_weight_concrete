# Assessment of Crack Elongation Performance in RC Beams Repaired by UHP-SHCC

## Summary

This study evaluates the crack elongation and reflection cracking behavior of ultra-high-performance strain-hardening cementitious composites (UHP-SHCC, $f_c >$ 120 MPa, tensile strain $> 2.5\%$) applied as a tensile repair overlay on cracked reinforced concrete (RC) beams under four-point bending. While conventional non-shrink mortar repairs suffered direct crack penetration and premature debonding, the UHP-SHCC overlay maintained high bridging stress at the crack tip, dispersing the single macrocrack into multiple branching microcracks ($w < 50\ \mu\text{m}$) and significantly enhancing flexural load capacity and energy dissipation.

## Key Contributions

- **Reflection cracking suppression mechanism**: Diverted stress concentrations at existing RC substrate crack tips into a distributed microcrack network within the UHP-SHCC repair layer, preventing reflective crack propagation.
- **Enhanced flexural capacity and ductility**: Increased post-yield ultimate bending moment by 38 % over conventional mortar repairs while maintaining monolithic composite action without debonding until failure.
- **Visualization of crack branching via digital image correlation**: Measured multiple crack propagation where individual crack widths remained tightly controlled within $30 \sim 45\ \mu\text{m}$.

## Methods

- **Material proportions**:
  - UHP-SHCC: Low-heat Portland cement + silica fume + silica sand powder, $W/B = 0.20$, high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 1.5\%$, diameter 12 µm, length 6 mm, tensile strength 2700 MPa).
  - 28 d properties: Compressive strength 135 MPa, tensile strength 9.8 MPa, tensile strain capacity 2.8 %.
- **Specimen dimensions**: 150 x 200 x 1800 mm RC beams (longitudinal reinforcement ratio 0.8 %).
- **Repair method**: Chipping 40 mm of concrete cover from the tension face, followed by UHP-SHCC overlay casting and 4-point bending testing (1500 mm span).

## Results

- Conventional mortar repaired beam: Substrate crack penetrated the repair overlay at 32 kN, reaching a peak load of 58.4 kN and losing load capacity rapidly at a deflection of 12.5 mm.
- UHP-SHCC repaired beam: Multiple microcracking initiated at 45 kN, sustaining stable ductile flexural response up to a peak load of 81.2 kN and 28.0 mm deflection (2.4-fold higher energy absorption).
