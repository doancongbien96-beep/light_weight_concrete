# Static, impact, and residual flexural performance of RC beams strengthened with various HPFRCCs

## Summary

Four HPFRCCs with distinct mechanical characteristics (UHPC, HPSHCC, ECC, URSHCC) were applied as 50 mm strengthening layers on the tension side of large RC beams (300 × 450 mm section, 2700 mm long), and the strengthening effect was evaluated through static four-point flexure · drop-weight impact (30 kJ kinetic energy) · post-impact residual flexural testing. The UHPC- and HPSHCC-strengthened beams achieved the highest static ultimate capacities at about +8 % (343.1 → 371.6~372.5 kN), yet UHPC with its low tensile strain capacity (0.80 %) lost ductility to crack localization while HPSHCC with 9.08 % strain capacity retained it. Under impact, all strengthened beams increased their ultimate reaction force (largest for URSHCC at +7.4 %), and the high-speed-camera-corrected maximum deflection was smallest for S-UHPC at 71.5 mm. After impact damage, the residual flexural strength of the unstrengthened beam remained similar to its pre-impact value, whereas the UHPC- and URSHCC-strengthened beams lost capacity upon exceeding their tensile strain limits.

## Key Contributions

1. **Systematic comparison of four HPFRCCs at identical thickness and fiber volume fraction**: isolating how the tensile strength–strain capacity spectrum (ECC 3.85 MPa/4.28 % up to UHPC 17.28 MPa/0.80 %, with URSHCC 8.44 MPa/6.09 % and HPSHCC 11.13 MPa/9.08 % in between) shapes strengthening performance. Only UHPC and HPSHCC raised static ultimate capacity effectively (+8 %); ECC, whose tensile strength resembles NSC's 2.50 MPa, instead gave −4 % (329.4 kN).
2. **Quantifying the strength–ductility trade-off**: S-UHPC had a deflection capacity of 11.7 mm (−35 % against NSC's 17.9 mm), degrading early through crack localization and local rebar straining, whereas S-HPSHCC held 16.9 mm and preserved ductility — PE-fiber bridging with a strain capacity 11× UHPC's shares the tensile demand even after yielding.
3. **Demonstrating that strengthening-layer interfacial debonding ties back to strain capacity**: the UHPC (0.80 %) and ECC (4.28 %) layers partially debonded from the substrate concrete at the ultimate state, while HPSHCC and URSHCC suppressed debonding. Debonding makes bottom-face displacement readings overestimate deflection, and correction through high-speed-camera Quadro-marker image analysis is required (NSC maximum deflection 73.3 mm; the bottom-face sensor value of 66.3 mm is in fact an underestimate — overestimation from the debonded layer and sensor-position effects are mixed).
4. **Strengthener-by-strengthener differences in impact response**: ultimate reaction rose in every strengthened beam (S-URSHCC 1021.8 → 1097.2 kN, +7.4 %; S-UHPC +2.8 %; S-HPSHCC +1.3 %; S-ECC +0.7 %). Impact flexural stiffness peaked at S-UHPC, 1828.4 kN/mm (NSC 1303.3), while absorbed energy peaked at S-URSHCC, 1582.0 (table unit J; the body text labels it kJ — J is the consistent choice against the 30 kJ input energy, a notation inconsistency in the original) — the stiffest S-UHPC absorbed the least at 826.3, since fast early failure shortens the energy-dissipation window.
5. **Material dependence of residual performance**: the unstrengthened beam kept residual flexural strength similar to its pre-impact state even after the 30 kJ impact (its impact rebar strain never reached the work-hardening initiation strain), but its stiffness and ultimate deflection dropped markedly. The UHPC- and URSHCC-strengthened beams exceeded their tensile strain capacities under impact deformation and entered tension softening, losing bridging capability and hence residual capacity — when choosing a strengthening material, the strain-capacity margin relative to the impact energy decides residual performance.
6. **Mode shift in impact cracking behavior**: the HPFRCC-strengthened beams developed fewer flexural cracks concentrated at midspan (rigid-body-rotation-like deformation), and 30 kJ exceeds the energy dissipation capacity of every HPFRCC strengthening layer, inducing crack localization. The ECC- and HPSHCC-strengthened beams additionally produced a new failure mode of top-surface cracks driven by support-zone tension during rebound.

## Methods

- **Specimens**: five RC beam types of 2700 × 300 × 450 mm (static, impact, and residual evaluation; the conclusion reports 10 beams cast and tested in total). Tension reinforcement D16 (rebar ratio ρ = 0.0057, near the minimum ratio), compression bars and stirrups D10, stirrup spacing 100 mm — designed to prevent shear failure and force flexural failure. Rebar properties (ASTM A370): D10 yield 461 MPa / tensile 587 MPa, D19 480/604 MPa, E_s 200 GPa.
- **Strengthening method**: the 50 mm cover below the tension rebar was removed → surfaces roughened by hammer-drill chipping → coated with a latex-based interface agent → cast with HPFRCC. Casting moved along the beam axis in a floating container to avoid single-point casting and ensure uniform compaction.
- **Mix proportions** (W/B): NSC 35.9 MPa (design target 30 MPa), ECC 0.284 (OPC + FA 2.2, PVA 2 %), UHPC 0.2 (OPC + SF 0.25 + silica flour filler, steel fiber 2 %), URSHCC 0.192 (ultra-rapid-hardening cement + BFS 0.5 + LSP 0.25 + CKD 0.1 + SF 0.2, PE 2 %), HPSHCC 0.162 (OPC + BFS 0.5 + SF 0.25, PE 2 %). Aggregate: fine silica sand 0.2~0.3 mm, no coarse aggregate.
- **Fiber properties**: PVA (diameter 38 µm, length 8 mm, aspect ratio 210, tensile 1560 MPa, E 24 GPa); steel fiber (200 µm, 19 mm, 95, 2580 MPa, 200 GPa); UHMWPE (30 µm, ~18 mm, aspect ratio 562.5~600, 3000 MPa, 100 GPa).
- **Material tests**: compression on Ø100 × 200 mm cylinders, 3000 kN UTM. Direct tension per JSCE dog-bone recommendation (crack-induced section 13 × 30 mm, gauge length 80 mm), LVDTs on both faces, n ≥ 6.
- **Static test**: 1500 kN actuator, four-point flexure, 3 deflection transducers at 400 mm spacing, rebar strain gauges.
- **Impact test**: drop-weight rig rated for up to 3 t from 15 m. 3 t hammer, drop height 1020 mm giving 30 kJ kinetic energy, three-point bending support. Support top plates were fixed by four steel rods each to prevent uplift and overturning (restraint force measured with donut load cells); impact force through 4 load cells between hammer and hammer head, reactions through 2 load cells on the floor anchorage blocks. 5 accelerometers (10 000 g), 9 laser displacement sensors (100 mm range), 3 rebar and 6 stirrup strain gauges, sampling at 1 MHz, 2 high-speed cameras.
- **Residual test**: the impact-damaged beams were reloaded in the same four-point configuration as the static test.

## Results

Material properties (direct tension):

| Material | f'c (MPa) | Tensile strength (MPa) | Strain capacity (%) | Fiber |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSC | 35.9 | 2.50 | – | – |
| ECC | 59.4 | 3.85 | 4.28 | PVA 2 % |
| UHPC | 140.3 | 17.28 | 0.80 | steel fiber 2 % |
| URSHCC | 107.8 | 8.44 (28 d) | 6.09 | PE 2 % |
| HPSHCC | 110.3 | 11.13 | 9.08 (energy density 824.76 kJ/m³) | PE 2 % |

Static flexural results:

| Specimen | P_y (kN) | Δ_y (mm) | P_u (kN) | Δ_u (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSC | 329.5 | 10.8 | 343.1 | 17.9 |
| S-ECC | 327.9 | 11.2 | 329.4 | 11.6 |
| S-UHPC | 363.4 | 10.8 | 372.5 | 11.7 |
| S-URSHCC | 328.1 | 10.4 | 348.9 | 16.8 |
| S-HPSHCC | 358.2 | 11.9 | 371.6 | 16.9 |

Impact flexural results (ultimate reaction basis):

| Specimen | P_u,impact (kN) | Δ_u,impact (mm) | EI_impact (kN/mm) | E_impact (J) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSC | 1021.8 | 0.784 | 1303.3 | 1174.6 |
| S-ECC | 1028.6 | 0.752 | 1367.8 | 1383.6 |
| S-UHPC | 1050.5 | 0.575 | 1828.4 | 826.3 |
| S-URSHCC | 1097.2 | 0.649 | 1689.4 | 1582.0 |
| S-HPSHCC | 1034.9 | 0.637 | 1624.5 | 1041.2 |

- Peak impact force exceeds the peak reaction because of inertial effects, and the reaction oscillates after reaching about 912~974 kN. NSC and S-ECC posted the lowest maximum reactions, about 912 kN.
- Deflection at ultimate load decreased in every strengthened beam: S-UHPC −26.7 %, S-HPSHCC −18.7 %, S-URSHCC −17.2 %, S-ECC −4.1 %.
- High-speed-camera Quadro-marker-corrected maximum deflections: NSC 73.3 mm, S-UHPC 71.5 mm (smallest), followed by S-HPSHCC and then S-ECC. Bottom-face sensor measurements overestimate deflection owing to strengthening-layer debonding and require separate correction.
- Residual performance: the impact-damaged NSC beam kept residual flexural strength similar to its pre-impact state, though flexural stiffness and ultimate deflection decreased. S-UHPC and S-URSHCC fell short of their pre-impact ultimate capacities after impact (having entered tension softening), and S-HPSHCC could not be compared on peak load because of an actuator rotation problem, leaving only stiffness comparison possible.
- Cracking: NSC showed numerous flexural cracks distributed out to the supports (parabolic deformation shape); the strengthened beams showed fewer cracks concentrated at midspan. S-UHPC had the fewest cracks (reflecting the brittle limit of its energy dissipation).
