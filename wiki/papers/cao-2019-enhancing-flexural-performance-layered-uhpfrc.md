# Maximizing the flexural performance of ultra-high performance concrete via a functionally graded layered structure (Layered UHPFRC): $40\%$ steel-fiber savings, flexural strength of $32.5\ \text{MPa}$, and an interfacial monolithization mechanism

## Summary

A landmark Composites Part B: Engineering (2019) origin paper by the team of **Prof. Q. L. Yu, Prof. H. J. H. Brouwers, and Dr. Y. Y. Y. Cao at Eindhoven University of Technology (TU Eindhoven), the Netherlands**. To overcome the inefficiency of conventional practice, which uniformly mixes expensive straight steel fibers through the entire UHPFRC member, and to maximize material economy, they designed **functionally graded layered UHPFRC beams (Layered UHPFRC, cross-section $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$)** that place unreinforced or low-fiber UHPC ($V_f = 0.0\sim 0.5\ \text{vol.}\%$) on the flexural compression side while concentrating high-content steel-fiber UHPC ($V_f = 2.0\sim 3.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) only on the flexural tension side; through theoretical derivation of the critical layer thickness ratio ($\beta_c$), four-point bending tests, full-section DIC strain-field measurement, and precise evaluation of interlayer interface shear behavior they demonstrated **a flexural modulus of rupture (MOR $32.5\ \text{MPa}$) and deflection ductility indices fully equivalent to uniform 2 % steel-fiber UHPFRC while slashing total steel-fiber usage by $40\%$, together with completely monolithic behavior free of interlayer delamination (Delamination)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Derivation of the theoretical formula for the optimal layer thickness ratio ($\beta_c = \sqrt{\alpha}/(1+\sqrt{\alpha})$) of UHPFRC flexural members**:
   - Mathematically completes the design of the critical layer thickness ratio, which places the neutral axis exactly at the layering interface so that the interface receives no tensile-debonding stress.
2. **$40\%$ reduction in steel fiber usage and twofold maximization of fiber utilization efficiency**:
   - Removes unnecessary fiber placement in the compression region and strategically deploys fiber only in the extreme tension region, more than doubling flexural fracture energy per identical fiber content.
3. **Complete elimination of interlayer interface defects through wet-on-wet continuous casting**:
   - The upper compression layer is cast within 15 min of the lower tension-layer pour, securing matrix-monolithic interface shear strength through nanoscale C-S-H interlocking.
4. **Presents design guidelines for next-generation eco-friendly economical precast slabs/panels**:
   - Radically lowers the expensive unit material cost of high-strength fiber concrete, substantially lowering the commercialization threshold toward large-scale civil engineering and building structures.

## Methods

- **Materials and layered mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Matrix mix: CEM I 52.5R cement + micro silica fume + crushed quartz sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$, W/B = 0.18, 28-day compressive strength $140.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Fibers: brass-coated micro straight steel fibers ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2500\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Specimen series (cross-section $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$, span $300\ \text{mm}$):
    1. `Mono-2.0%`: uniform 2.0 % steel-fiber monolayer (reference specimen).
    2. `Layered-2.0/0.0%`: bottom 50 mm (2.0 % fiber) + top 50 mm (fiber-free, total fiber content 1.0 %).
    3. `Layered-3.0/0.0%`: bottom 40 mm (3.0 % fiber) + top 60 mm (fiber-free, total fiber content 1.2 %).
    4. `Layered-2.5/0.5%`: bottom 50 mm (2.5 % fiber) + top 50 mm (0.5 % fiber, total fiber content 1.5 %).
- **Flexural testing and instrumentation (Sections 2.3~2.5 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Four-point bending test (displacement control 0.1 mm/min).
  - Instrumentation: 2D-DIC full-section strain measurement, LVDT deflectometers, interlayer interface slip gauges.

### 2. Layered-interface stress continuity and fiber bridging mechanism analysis
- **Interface placement above the neutral axis**: the `Layered-3.0/0.0%` specimen designed at the critical thickness ratio $\beta = 0.40$ keeps the interface permanently within the compressive stress zone, preventing shear splitting cracks at their source.
- **High-density 3 % fiber bridging in the tension bottom layer**: the increased tensile-side fiber density fully constrains early fine-crack opening, achieving a higher flexural modulus of rupture ($32.5\ \text{MPa}$) than the monolayer.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the landmark origin work of sustainable ultra-high-performance concrete design in which Prof. Q. L. Yu's team applied the functionally graded layering concept to UHPFRC for the first time, proving that total steel-fiber content can be cut by 40 % while delivering a flexural strength of 32.5 MPa with equal or better deflection toughness.
