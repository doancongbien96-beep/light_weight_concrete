# Effects of PE fiber dosage and length on high-tensile-strength EGC

## Summary

A study of ambient-cured PE fiber reinforced EGC systems by the Nottingham Ningbo China group (corresponding author Bo Li). With a FA:GGBS 1:1 geopolymer matrix, PE fiber length (6/12/18 mm) and dosage (1.5/1.75/2.0 vol %) were taken as systematic variables to evaluate workability, compression, tension, cracking, and environmental impact. Key conclusion: increasing fiber length (6→18 mm) benefits tensile strength (+42.0 %) and strain (+148.0 %), but air entrapment by the 18 mm fibers costs compressive strength — a length-dependent trade-off; the optimal combination is 18 mm at 1.5 %.

## Key Contributions

1. Length dependence of fiber dosage effects clarified: for 12 mm fibers, raising dosage from 1.5→2.0 % improves 28 d compressive strength +9.0 % and tensile strength +12.7 % through bridging, whereas for 18 mm fibers the same increase causes air entrapment with compressive −3.8 % and tensile −3.6 %. In overdosed long-fiber combinations (2.0 %), fiber clustering is the main cause of tensile strength loss (the 18 mm mix at 1.5 % exceeds the 1.75 % and 2.0 % mixes by 12.0 % and 3.7 % respectively).
2. Fiber length effect quantified: at 2 % dosage, lengthening from 6→18 mm raises tensile strength +42.0 % and ultimate strain +148.0 % (maximum of 11.3 % at PE18-2.0 %, +50.9 % over 12 mm). Crack control also improves: the slope of the mean crack width-strain linear relation (r = 0.917~0.999) decreases with length and dosage (at 18 mm and 9 % strain, 250→161 μm when dosage rises from 1.5 to 2.0 %).
3. λ calibration of the Naaman (2008) tensile strength prediction model: in σ_pc = λ·τ·V_f·(L_f/d_f), λ regressed as a linear function of V_f(L_f/d_f) (λ = −0.0761·V_f(L_f/d_f) + 1.7985, r² = 0.9154). Measured/predicted ratio averaged 1.03, conservatively valid also against other studies' data (Kan, Kumar et al., Table 4).
4. Environmental impact quantified comparatively (cradle-to-gate MSI): EGC with 2 % PE has embodied energy 55 % lower than HTS-ECC (Yu & Leung 2020) and 22 % lower than M45 ECC; embodied carbon is 80 % lower than HTS-ECC and 53 % lower than M45.

## Methods

- Matrix: FA(GGBS) 1:1 by weight, alkali dose Na₂O/binder 4.5 %, silicate modulus 2.25, W/B 0.33, S/B 0.30 (silica sand max 250 μm, mean 100 μm). PE fibers d = 24 μm, E = 110 GPa, tensile strength 3000 MPa, elongation at break 2~3 %.
- Curing: sealed ambient curing at 23±2 °C and RH 95±5 %.
- Workability GB/T 2419-2005 mini-slump; density ASTM C642; compression BS EN 1015-11 (40 mm cubes, 0.6 MPa/s, 3·7·28 d); direct tension JSCE 2008 dumbbell (LVDT gauge 80 mm, 0.5 mm/min); cracks via white painting + DIC (Nikon D5200, every 10 s, pixel 50 μm); single fiber pullout (embedded 2 mm, Lin-Li equation) measured frictional bond τ = 0.72±0.21 MPa; SEM (reaction stopped by 24 h isopropanol immersion).

## Results

- Workability: fiber incorporation lowers flow through skeleton formation. Raising dosage from 1.75→2.0 % reduces spread by −3.5 % for 12 mm and −13.4 % for 18 mm; relative to 6 mm, −4.1 % for 12 mm and −14.5 % for 18 mm.
- Compression: 12 mm shows positive 28 d strength response to dosage (bridging-governed); 18 mm negative (air entrapment-governed). At 2 %, 18 mm is 28 d compressive −12.4 % versus 12 mm.
- Tension (strain hardening and multiple cracking confirmed in all mixes): PE18-2.0 % tensile strength 7.8 MPa, strain 11.3 % (Table 6). Under DIC, the 12 and 18 mm-2 % mixes show saturated multiple cracking while 6 mm retains low-strain residual segments.
- SEM: 6 mm fails by pullout (smooth fiber surface); 12 and 18 mm show fiber rupture plus clustering — long fibers resist stress effectively.
- Recommended mix: for superior tensile performance, 18 mm at 1.5 % (avoiding fiber clustering).
