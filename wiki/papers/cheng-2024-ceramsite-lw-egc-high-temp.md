# High-Temperature Compressive Performance of Lightweight EGC by Ceramsite Type

## Summary

Evaluated the 20–800 °C high-temperature uniaxial compressive performance of LW-EGC incorporating three lightweight aggregate types (fly ash ceramsite FACM, clay ceramsite CC, shale ceramsite SC). Established that aggregate type governs the thermal stability and strength-degradation path of lightweight composites at densities of 1407–1653 kg/m3, and confirmed that PVA fiber melting operates as an anti-spalling mechanism.

## Key Contributions

1. Strength-gain regime up to 200 °C: common to all mixes, peak stress increased on heating from 20 to 200 °C (LW-EGC-R 33.37→42.34 MPa). Free-water removal and matrix densification effect — a low-to-medium temperature strengthening pattern similar to the 80 °C peak of [[lv-2025-high-temperature-ternary-egc-pe]].
2. Residual strength ranking by aggregate type (800 °C): LW-EGC-R (micro-spherical FAC, 15.32 MPa) > CC (11.41 MPa) > M (10.25 MPa) > SC (6.85 MPa). SC is the worst owing to its irregular shape and large ITZ. The intrinsic cylinder compressive strength of the aggregate itself (FACM 13.1 MPa >> CC 3.5 · SC 3.2 MPa) predicts residual performance.
3. Anti-spalling role of PVA fiber melting (200–400 °C): melted-out fiber channels release internal moisture → reduced vapor pressure → spalling prevented. PVA high-temperature behavior data contrasting with the PE-based [[lv-2025-high-temperature-ternary-egc-pe]].
4. Catalog of high-temperature microstructural changes: matrix dehydration, shrinkage, and cracking; nephelite formation; calcite decomposition increasing porosity. Yet the geopolymer three-dimensional network itself remains stable.
5. Hybrid reinforcement system: mix design with combined incorporation of MWCNT 0.15 % + PVA 2 %.

## Methods

Precursor FA 800+GGBFS 240+MK 60+SF 60 kg/m3, FAC (fly ash micro-sphere) 200 kg/m3, activator water glass (modulus adjusted 3.24→1.4), held at the target temperature for 120 min after constant-rate heating, then GB/T50081 uniaxial compression (Ø100×200 mm, LVDT) after natural cooling.

## Results

- Mass loss: largest for the CC mix (23.12 % at 800 °C), smallest for the SC mix (20.37 %).
- Peak strain increased monotonically with rising temperature (0.45→0.83 %) — ductilization and softening.
- The initial elastic stage was similar regardless of aggregate type; differences appeared in the stress-strengthening and yielding stages.

## Related Notes

- High-temperature performance data contrasting with the room-temperature lightweight-aggregate EGC of [[nematollahi-2017-lightweight-aggregate-egc]]. Provides selection criteria for lightweight aggregates (intrinsic strength, shape, ITZ).
- The PVA melt-channel mechanism adds a high-temperature perspective to fiber selection alongside the CBF heat-resistance discussion in [[ralegaonkar-2018-basalt-fiber-mortar-review]].
