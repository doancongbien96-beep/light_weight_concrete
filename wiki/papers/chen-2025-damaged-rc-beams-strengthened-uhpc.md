# Flexural performance of damaged RC beams strengthened with UHPC: Coupling effect of load-induced cracking and chloride corrosion

## Summary

A study that elucidates the flexural behavior of damaged RC beams strengthened with a 30 mm UHPC layer through an 8-specimen experimental program (1 control + 1 unstrengthened damaged + 6 strengthened) combining load-induced pre-cracking (crack widths 0.2/0.4 mm) with electrochemical chloride corrosion (mass loss rates 5/10/15 %), and proposes an ultimate flexural strength analytical model reflecting residual strain and corrosion degradation. UHPC strengthening recovered ultimate capacity by +63~84 %, stiffness by +23.8~72.3 %, and crack resistance 3~5-fold, but reduced ductility by 64.2~79.1 %; relative to single damage, coupled damage caused a −20 % cracking load and −5~14 % stiffness. All beams exhibited conventional flexural failure (compression-zone concrete crushing after tensile rebar yielding) without interfacial peeling.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantification of coupled damage beyond single-damage studies**: Prior work addressed either mechanical load damage or environmental attack alone; this paper designed the experiment as a two-variable matrix of pre-cracking level × corrosion level. The coupling effect was identified as a mutually amplifying mechanism: (i) pre-cracks serve as pathways for aggressive species that accelerate corrosion, and (ii) expansive stress from corrosion products promotes crack reopening and widening.
2. **Quantified range of the UHPC strengthening effect**: Compared with the unstrengthened damaged beam (CB₀.₂₋₁₀), strengthened beams achieved cracking loads P_cc +267~400 % (stated as 217~400 % in the conclusions section — an internal numerical inconsistency in the paper) and ultimate loads +63~84 %. The cracking load is most sensitive to pre-cracking level (UB₀.₂₋₁₀ shows P_cc −20 % against U_B₀.₀₋₁₀ and UB₀.₂₋₀).
3. **Discovery of an atypical benefit from slight corrosion (5 %)**: UB₀.₂₋₅ showed the smallest crack widths at equal load with modestly improved ductility and crack resistance. Interpreted as pore filling by corrosion products and enhanced rebar-concrete bond from increased surface roughness at low corrosion levels (≤5 %), consistent with the single tensile rebar strain data.
4. **Residual-strain-based effective strength concept**: Because residual strain under secondary reloading does not resist the external load, the strain field at preloading is reconstructed using the Berkeley model (compressive residual strain ε_cp) together with the crack-width-rebar-strain relation of JTG D62-2004, and the compression-zone contribution is discounted through an effective concrete strength of the form f_ce = f_c·[1−(1−ε_cp/ε₀)²].
5. **Strain-compatibility model accounting for bond degradation of corroded-beam rebars**: The CEB-FIP bond-slip curve is simplified into a bifold (mean bond strength 0.5τ_max, residual bond 0.4τ_max); combining Kemp & Wilhelm effective bond strength with the Saifullah & Clark corrosion reduction formula (A₁=0.769, A₂=−0.012), strain compatibility is deemed satisfied when T_c^max < T_max, and otherwise the tensile rebar strain is relaxed through the strain coordination coefficient C_η. The corrosion-based property discounts are A_cs=A_s(1−η), f_y^c=(1−1.98η/100)f_y, E_s^c=(1−1.13η/100)E_s.
6. **Model validation**: the experimental-to-calculated ultimate load ratio P_u,exp/P_u,cal = 0.922~1.052 (error <10 %). The slight overprediction for strengthened beams arises from the assumptions of perfect interfacial bonding and uniform corrosion versus actual interfacial slip.

## Methods

- **Materials**: NC (C50 mix, cement:aggregate:sand:water = 372:1270:646:110 kg/m³) compressive strength 62.3 MPa, elastic modulus 37.6 GPa. UHPC (binder:SP:steel fiber:water = 2000:13.4:174.7:178.3 kg/m³, Portland 42.5 + silica fume + fly ash + quartz sand/quartz powder) compressive 135.8 MPa, tensile 9.5 MPa, elastic modulus 47.5 GPa. Steel fibers: length 13 mm, diameter 0.2 mm, tensile strength 2780 MPa, elastic modulus 200 GPa. Rebar HRB400: Φ10 yield 435 MPa / ultimate 602 MPa / E 195 GPa, Φ16 yield 417 / 585 MPa / E 189 GPa.
- **Specimens**: eight 2300×300×200 mm RC beams. Tensile reinforcement 3Φ16, compression bars 2Φ10, stirrups Φ8@100 mm in the shear spans. The UHPC layer is 30 mm thick with four Φ10 bars embedded in it, connected to the substrate through Φ8 L-shaped dowels. Interface treatment: rust removal → hand-tool chipping (average depth 2.5 mm) → dust removal → wetting followed by casting; moist curing for 28 days at 30 ± 3 °C.
- **Damage imposition**: (1) Preloading to reach target maximum crack widths of 0.2/0.4 mm — initial cracking load ≈45 kN with the rebars kept elastic, and residual strain remaining after unloading (raising the target width 0.2→0.4 mm required +50 % reloading load and produced +40 % residual strain). (2) Electrochemical corrosion — a 5 % NaCl tank with direct current of 1.53 mA/cm²; Faraday's law set target mass loss rates of 0/5/10/15 %. The stirrups were epoxy-insulated yet picked up part of the current and corroded somewhat, so measured corrosion levels ran systematically below the theoretical values with deviations within 10 % (measured: CB₀.₂₋₁₀ 9.02 %, UB₀.₂₋₅ 4.71 %, UB₀.₂₋₁₀ 9.61 %, UB₀.₂₋₁₅ 13.92 %, UB₀.₀₋₁₀ 10.59 %, UB₀.₄₋₁₀ 10.69 %). Pitting corrosion concentrated between L/4~3L/4 of the span.
- **Specimen naming**: CB (undamaged control), CB₀.₂₋₁₀ (damaged unstrengthened), UB₀.₀₋₁₀, UB₀.₂₋₀, UB₀.₂₋₁₀, UB₀.₂₋₅, UB₀.₂₋₁₅, UB₀.₄₋₁₀ (subscripts = crack width-corrosion level).
- **Flexural test**: four-point loading. 5 kN increments before cracking, then 10 kN increments, switching to displacement control at 1 mm/min after tensile rebar yield. Load, strain, deflection, and crack width instrumented.

- K is the post-cracking-to-yield stiffness (K = P_y/δ_y); μ is the ductility index (the table lists μ=δ_y/δ_u but the values correspond to δ_u/δ_y — presumed notation error in the source).
- Effect of damage alone: CB vs CB₀.₂₋₁₀ stiffness −6.4 %, ductility +67.5 %, cracking load −25 %.
- Effect of UHPC strengthening: vs CB₀.₂₋₁₀, cracking load +267~400 %, ultimate load +63~84 %, stiffness +23.8~72.3 %, ductility reduced to 35~60 % of the CB beams.
- Coupled vs single damage: cracking load −20 %, stiffness −5~14 %, ductility +6~36 % (accelerated cracking increases section rotation and ultimate deflection).
- Crack behavior: short, closely spaced cracks in the UHPC layer (fiber bridging relieves stress concentration at crack tips); two-stage crack development maps the 0.2 mm crack-width threshold onto the region beyond 80 % of P_u (multiple-crack formation stage → crack acceleration stage).
- Strain distribution: corroded beams show rebar strain lag (slip accompanying bond degradation), violating the sectional equilibrium assumption — the UHPC layer mitigates this lag, so the UHPC-RC composite section largely satisfies the plane-section assumption.
