# Unified Density and Compressive Strength Prediction Models ($R^2 = 0.99$) for Waste-Tire Rubberized Concrete Blocks by Rubber Replacement Ratio ($5\sim 50\%$) and W/C Ratio

## Summary

To mass-recycle crumb rubber derived from waste-tire scraps into pedestrian and structural interlocking concrete blocks, **rubberized concrete blocks were produced with sand replaced by rubber over a $5\sim 50\ \text{vol.}\%$ range and with W/C ratios ($0.45, 0.50, 0.55$) as variables**, and 7-day and 28-day oven-dry densities and compressive strengths were measured to establish **a density reduction coefficient ($R_d = -0.0076r + 1.00$, $R^2 = 0.99$) and a compressive strength index prediction model, demonstrating that at $20\%$ rubber replacement a density of $1980\ \text{kg/m}^3$ and a 28-day compressive strength of $24.5\ \text{MPa}$ satisfy the structural interlocking block specification** — a landmark original paper in Construction and Building Materials 2011 by **Prof. Tung-Chai Ling of The Hong Kong Polytechnic University**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a linear density-reduction prediction equation ($R^2 = 0.99$) for concrete blocks as a function of rubber replacement ratio ($r$)**:
   - The high-precision linear model $R_d(w/c) = -0.0076(r) + 1.00$ was derived, enabling accurate prediction of block self-weight reduction rates under various W/C ratio conditions.
2. **Formulation of an exponential compressive-strength decay model ($f_c = f_{c0} e^{-k\cdot r}$)**:
   - The strength degradation caused by the hydrophobic nature and low elastic modulus of rubber particles was modeled with an exponential function, determining the maximum allowable rubber content for achieving target strength in mix design.
3. **Verification of compliance with quality specifications (BS 6717) for structural and pedestrian paving blocks**:
   - Even at $20\ \text{vol.}\%$ rubber replacement, a 28-day compressive strength of $24.5\ \text{MPa}$ was secured, proving the commercial viability of interlocking paving blocks.
4. **Analysis of rubber–cement ITZ pore interaction with increasing W/C ratio**:
   - Elucidated the mechanism by which ITZ defects between cement paste and rubber surfaces intensify as the W/C ratio increases from $0.45 \rightarrow 0.55$.

## Methods

- **Materials and block mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Binder: ASTM Type I ordinary Portland cement (OPC).
  - Aggregates: natural silica sand ($0\sim 5\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 2.65) + crushed granite ($5\sim 10\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 2.68).
  - Crumb rubber: derived from scrap tires ($1\sim 4\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 1.15), replacing sand volume at 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50 vol.%.
  - Three W/C ratio levels: 0.45, 0.50, 0.55.
- **Molding and testing procedures (Sections 2~3 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Block dimensions: $200 \times 100 \times 60\ \text{mm}$ rectangular interlocking blocks (vibro-compaction molding).
  - Curing: laboratory air curing at $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $65\%$ relative humidity.
  - Testing: 7-day/28-day dry density (BS 1881-114) and uniaxial compressive strength (BS 6717 Part 1).

### 2. Mathematical model equations and physical failure mechanisms
- **Density prediction equations**: $R_d(0.45) = -0.0075(r) + 1.00$, $R_d(0.50) = -0.0076(r) + 1.00$, $R_d(0.55) = -0.0078(r) + 1.00$.
- **ITZ porosification mechanism of rubber particles**: Micro-pore formation between the hydrophobic rubber surface and the hydrophilic cement paste induces stress concentration; however, at contents of 20% or less the macroscopic load-bearing skeleton is maintained, preserving structural strength.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original work in the field of waste-resource recycling construction materials, establishing engineering models that predict the density and compressive strength of concrete blocks with rubber replacement up to $50\%$ with high accuracy of $R^2 = 0.99$, and verifying the practicality of eco-friendly interlocking blocks with $20\%$ rubber replacement.
