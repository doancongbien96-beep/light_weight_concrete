# Carbonation Characteristics and Microstructural Degradation of Alkali-Activated Slag (AAS) Mortar

## Summary

In this paper from the Journal of the Korea Concrete Institute by Professor Keun-Hyeok Yang (Kyonggi Univ) and Professors Keum-Il Song, Bang Yeon Lee, and Jin-Kyu Song (Chonnam National Univ), the authors investigated the initial flow, pre- and post-carbonation compressive strength development, carbonation depth, and microstructural decomposition behavior of cementless alkali-activated blast-furnace slag (AAS: GGBS fineness $\text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, specific gravity 2.93, basicity coefficient 1.81) mortars incorporating powdered sodium silicate activator at 6 %, 10 %, and 14 % by binder mass (equivalent sodium oxide ratio $\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{SM} = 0.03, 0.05, 0.07$, mixes G3, G5, G7, W/B = 0.50, S/B = 2.45) after 7 days of air curing followed by accelerated carbonation chamber exposure (CO2 concentration 5 ± 0.5 %, 20 ± 0.5 °C, RH 60 ± 2 %) for 7, 14, and 21 days. The results showed that whereas OPC mortar exhibited a **40.5 % increase in 28 d compressive strength from 32.1 MPa to 45.1 MPa** due to $\text{CaCO}_3$ precipitation, AAS mortars suffered substantial strength reductions—G3 (14.9 $\rightarrow$ 7.4 MPa, -50.3 %), G5 (30.4 $\rightarrow$ 15.2 MPa, -50.0 %), G7 (42.5 $\rightarrow$ 28.9 MPa, -32.0 %)—caused by decalcification and pore coarsening of C-A-S-H gels.

## Key Contributions

- **Identification of initial flow enhancement mechanism in AAS binders**: Initial flow increased relative to OPC (170 mm) to 185 mm for G3, 195 mm for G5, and 190 mm for G7 due to the ball-bearing effect of dissolved sodium silicate.
- **Quantification of contrasting pre- and post-carbonation compressive strength behavior between OPC and AAS**: OPC gained strength through pore filling by hydration products, whereas AAS lost strength rapidly through structural collapse of C-A-S-H gels.
- **Evaluation of carbonation resistance as a function of activator dosage ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{SM} = 0.03 \rightarrow 0.07$)**: Higher activator concentration (G7) densified the initial matrix, mitigating the strength loss rate (-32.0 %) and reducing carbonation depth.
- **Identification of carbonation products via XRD analysis**: Confirmed that due to the absence of $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ in AAS, C-A-S-H gel decomposed directly into calcite and vaterite phases.

## Methods

- Materials: Type 1 ordinary Portland cement ($\text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), ground granulated blast-furnace slag ($\text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{SiO}_2$ 33.58 %, CaO 42.09 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 12.70 %), powdered sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ 50.2 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 45.0 %), Jumunjin standard sand (S/B = 2.45, W/B = 0.50).
- Mixture proportions:
  - **OPC**: 100 % OPC
  - **G3**: GGBS 94 % + sodium silicate 6 % ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{SM} = 0.03$)
  - **G5**: GGBS 90 % + sodium silicate 10 % ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{SM} = 0.05$)
  - **G7**: GGBS 86 % + sodium silicate 14 % ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}/\text{SM} = 0.07$)
- Testing: Mortar flow (KS L 5105), compressive strength at 14, 21, and 28 days, accelerated carbonation test (KS F 2584, $\text{CO}_2$ 5 %, exposures for 7, 14, and 21 days), carbonation depth measurement via 1 % phenolphthalein solution.
