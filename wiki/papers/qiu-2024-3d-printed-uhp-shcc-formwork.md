# Enhancing the flexural performance of concrete beams with 3D-printed UHP-SHCC permanent formwork via graded fiber volume fraction

## Summary

This paper proposes composite beams made by filling U-shaped 3D-printed UHP-SHCC (ultra-high-performance strain-hardening cementitious composite) permanent formwork with normal concrete (NC), and shows through four-point bending tests (with DIC) that grading the PE fiber volume fraction stepwise through the formwork thickness yields higher flexural performance with less fiber than uniform distribution. The design rests on the stress-distribution logic that fiber is used more efficiently the more it is concentrated in the tension-dominated zone below the neutral axis: lowering the mean fiber content from 2.0 % to 1.5 % (a 25 % fiber saving and a 12.8 % material-cost saving) still achieved up to +65.6 % peak load or +77.4 % maximum deflection through graded design. The filename had truncated the title, but the full bibliographic record was confirmed from the paper body (corresponding author Ye Qian, HKU, with co-author Jian-Guo Dai, CityU — not the user's group).

## Key Contributions

1. Three graded fiber-distribution designs (FGF1~FGF3, mean 1.5 %) vs the uniform-distribution baseline (UF-2 %): FGF1 = bottom plate 30 mm (2.5 %) + web 90 mm (0.5 %); FGF2 = bottom plate 30 mm (2.5 %) + web 45 mm (1.0 %) + web 45 mm (fiber-free); FGF3 = bottom plate 15 mm (2.5 %) + bottom plate 15 mm (2.0 %) + web 45 mm (1.0 %) + web 45 mm (0.5 %).
2. Flexural strength: FGF3 reached the highest peak load of 81.4 kN (+65.6 % vs UF), FGF2 66.0 kN (+34.2 %), UF-2 % 49.1 kN, FGF1 44.8 kN (about −8.8 %, yet comparable performance under the 25 % fiber-saving condition).
3. Ductility: maximum deflection was highest overall for FGF1 at +77.4 %, FGF3 +33.4 %, and FGF2 similar to UF (within ±10 %). Energy absorption (ASTM C1609 deflection levels L/200~L/50): FGF2 +25.7~44.8 %, FGF3 +54.0~58.6 %, FGF1 −8.8~−14.2 %. That is, the two-stage design specializes in ductility and the four-stage design in strength and energy.
4. Cracking performance: all beams failed in a deflection-hardening mode, localizing cracks after multiple cracking. In the stepped-distribution beams, interlayer cracks formed between filaments of different fiber content (interlayer adhesion degraded by the ~20 min layer stacking interval). FGF1 showed early cracking and widening in the low-content Region C (0.5 %) of the upper web; FGF2 and FGF3 mitigated this by reallocating fiber to that zone. Crack number correlated positively with maximum deflection (FGF1 was best in crack number and spacing, with crack count +94.8 %).
5. Introduction of a fiber efficiency concept: η_X = X/V_f (X = peak load, maximum deflection, energy absorption). Load-based fiber efficiency ranged 29.9~54.3 kN/% against 24.6 kN/% for the uniform beam (+21.4~120.7 %), deflection-based efficiency +22.8~148.2 % (FGF1 at 2.48× the uniform case), and energy-based efficiency +14.4~111.5 %.
6. Cost analysis: PE fibers account for 51.1 % of material cost. Lowering the mean content from 2.0 % to 1.5 % cuts the material cost per beam from 31.0 to 27.1 HKD (−12.8 %). A five-dimensional (peak load, maximum deflection, energy absorption, crack number, cost) radar-chart evaluation is proposed; from a practical standpoint the three-stage design (FGF2) is recommended as the balance point between performance and construction complexity.

## Methods

- Specimens: four 480 × 120 × 120 mm composite beams. NC was filled into U-shaped UHP-SHCC permanent formwork (bottom plate 30 mm thick, web 20 mm thick). The formwork interior was divided into four equal-area zones (A~D) with fiber content assigned per zone.
- UHP-SHCC mix (by weight): cement:silica fume:fly ash = 0.60:0.10:0.30, water-binder ratio 0.16, quartz sand 0.20 (particle size 0.180~0.425 mm), nanoclay 0.5 % (improving printability via cohesion, static yield stress, and thixotropy), superplasticizer 1.6 %, PE fibers 0~2.5 % (length 12 mm, diameter 24 µm, tensile strength above 3000 MPa).
- NC: water-cement ratio 0.58, maximum coarse aggregate size 10 mm, 28-day compressive strength 41.6 MPa.
- Printing: 6-axis robot arm + progressive cavity pump + circular nozzle (diameter 12.3 mm). Printing paths were laid parallel to the beam's flexural tensile stress direction to induce fiber alignment. The layer interval between layers of different fiber content was about 20 min. After printing: 24 h ambient curing → 7-day water curing → NC filling → 28-day curing.
- Testing: four-point bending over a 400 mm span (central 133.3 mm), displacement control at 0.05 mm/min, midspan deflection measured with 2 LVDTs, and DIC (industrial camera + Vic2D) for full-field deformation and crack tracking.
- Reference: a prior study by the same group had already reported that 3D-printed UHP-SHCC formwork beams gain +131.0 % flexural strength, +188.2 % deformation capacity, +68.1~556.2 % energy absorption, and +137.5 % crack number over cast-in-place formwork beams (fiber alignment effect of the custom nozzle).

## Results

- The load-deflection curves of all specimens showed a deflection-hardening shape: elastic stage → hardening stage → softening stage. Even as cracks developed in the UHP-SHCC formwork and the infill concrete during hardening, the load kept rising through fiber bridging.
- Side effect of the stepped distribution: interlayer adhesion between filaments of different fiber content is relatively weak, so interlayer cracks appear and interrupt the continuous development of vertical cracks. Long layer intervals (20 min in this study) are identified as the cause — surface moisture loss and increased voids degrade interlayer adhesion.
- Problem of the fiber-free zone: Region D of FGF2 (upper web, fiber-free) turns into a tension zone once deflection grows and the neutral axis rises, but with no fibers to carry it, softening set in earlier than in FGF3 (softening soon after reaching 66.0 kN vs continued load rise for FGF3).
- Problem of the low-content web: in Region C (0.5 %) of FGF1, early cracking and width growth appeared first, and after localization the tensile load was transferred to the high-content Regions A and B, which sustained large curvature and maximum deflection — the mechanism of lowest strength yet highest deformation capacity.
- Overall, fiber grading favors strength and energy the better it aligns with the stress distribution (four-stage > three-stage > two-stage), while simpler layouts ease construction. As the ultimate solution, a printing process that automatically adjusts fiber content where needed is posed as follow-up work.
