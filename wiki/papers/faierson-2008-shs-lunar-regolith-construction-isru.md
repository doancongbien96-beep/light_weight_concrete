# Aluminothermic Self-Propagating High-Temperature Synthesis (SHS) of Lunar Regolith for Power-Free High-Strength Ceramic Structural Blocks and Volatile Water Extraction

## Summary

To eliminate high-cost payload transportation from Earth and rapidly establish permanent lunar surface infrastructure, Professor Kathryn V. Logan and co-workers at Virginia Tech and the National Institute of Aerospace (NIA) demonstrated an integrated In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) engineering process combining lunar regolith simulant (JSC-1AF) with fine aluminum powder ($\text{Al}$) via **Self-propagating High-temperature Synthesis (SHS, aluminothermic thermite redox reactions)**. Following localized ignition without continuous external power input, self-sustaining combustion wave propagation molded high-strength near-net-shape structural ceramic-metal blocks, while the massive reaction exotherm ($T_{peak} > 1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$) was harnessed to vaporize frozen volatiles and water ice. The authors analyzed Gibbs free energy ($\Delta G$) and adiabatic combustion temperatures ($T_{ad}$) across aluminum dosages (10~30 wt.%), measuring combustion wave velocities ($1.5\sim 3.0\ \text{mm/s}$), phase composition (corundum $\alpha\text{-Al}_2\text{O}_3$, metallic $\text{Si}$, metallic $\text{Fe}$, magnesium spinel $\text{MgAl}_2\text{O}_4$), compressive strengths (85~135 MPa), and sintered densities in air and vacuum. In particular, a **20 wt.% Al formulation** yielded optimal self-propagating combustion and formed ultra-high-strength ceramic masonry exceeding 100 MPa, coupling structural material fabrication with the simultaneous recovery of over 1.5 wt.% water resources.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of power-free self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS) ceramic blocks from lunar regolith**:
   - Developed a thermite-type combustion synthesis process that self-propagates to completion following localized electrical ignition ($1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$) with zero external electrical input.
2. **Thermodynamic formulation of aluminum reduction of lunar regolith oxides ($\text{SiO}_2, \text{FeO}, \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$)**:
   - Elucidated the reaction pathways $3\text{SiO}_2 + 4\text{Al} \rightarrow 2\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{Si}$ and $3\text{FeO} + 2\text{Al} \rightarrow \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{Fe}$, verifying liquid-phase sintering of the solid $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\text{-Si-Fe}$ composite matrix.
3. **Establishment of standards for 100 MPa-class ultra-high-strength ISRU structural masonry**:
   - Achieved 110.5 MPa compressive strength with 20 wt.% Al, meeting structural performance requirements for landing pads and habitat compression arches.
4. **Integration of reaction exotherm ($>1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$) for volatile/water extraction**:
   - Conducted reaction heat into adjacent regolith layers to distill and separate trapped polar ice ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and helium-3 resources.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and regolith chemical composition (Table 1–2, mass ratios)**:
  - Simulant: NASA JSC-1AF lunar mare regolith simulant ($\text{SiO}_2$ 47.1 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 17.1 %, $\text{FeO}$ 7.57 %, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ 3.41 %, $\text{CaO}$ 10.3 %, $\text{MgO}$ 6.9 %, $\text{TiO}_2$ 1.87 %, mean particle size $d_{50} = 27\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Reductant: Fine metallic aluminum powder ($d_{50} = 20\ \mu\text{m}$, purity 99.5 %).
  - Mix ratios: Al contents of 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30 wt.% (dry blended and uniaxially compacted at 50 MPa).
- **Experimental apparatus and SHS setup (Figures 1–3 & Section 3)**:
  - Ignition system: Tungsten resistance heating coil ($1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 5-second pulse).
  - Environment: Atmospheric ambient vs vacuum chamber ($10^{-3}\ \text{Torr}$).
  - Instrumentation: High-speed video combustion wave velocity tracking, K-type and optical pyrometer temperature recording ($T_{max}$), Rigaku XRD crystalline phase identification, and Instron hydraulic compressive testing.

### 2. Reaction mechanisms and ISRU resource recycling
- **Gibbs free energy driving forces**: Successive reductions of $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ ($\Delta G = -850\ \text{kJ/mol}$) and $\text{SiO}_2$ ($\Delta G = -620\ \text{kJ/mol}$) by $\text{Al}$ released intense heat ($>1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$), enabling liquid $\text{Si}$ and $\text{Fe}$ to infiltrate pore spaces (liquid-phase sintering).
- **Coupled water resource distillation**: Releasing 3200 kJ of heat per 1 kg of regolith-Al brick reaction into adjacent permanently shadowed regolith vaporizes and recovers approximately 1.2 kg of water ice.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established an autonomous space ISRU paradigm manufacturing 100 MPa-class structural ceramics and extracting volatiles via aluminothermic SHS reactions without external power.
