# Quantitative Failure Mechanism Comparison of Ultra-High-Strength (UHPFRC, $142\ \text{MPa}$) vs Ultra-High-Ductility (HDFRC, $\varepsilon_u = 5.8\%$) Protective Composites under High-Velocity Projectile Impact ($v = 800\ \text{m/s}$): Penetration Resistance and Scabbing Suppression Synergy

## Summary

To advance anti-terrorism protection for defense infrastructure and critical civilian structures, Prof. Gyu-Yong Kim (Chungnam National University), Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), and Prof. Jeong-Il Choi fabricated **ultra-high-performance fiber-reinforced concrete (UHPFRC: 28 d compressive strength $142.0\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strength $12.5\ \text{MPa}$, steel fibers $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) and high-ductility fiber-reinforced concrete (HDFRC / ECC: 28 d compressive strength $30.5\ \text{MPa}$, direct tensile strain capacity $5.80\%$, PE microfibers $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) panels ($300 \times 300 \times 50, 75, 100\ \text{mm}$)**. Under static mechanical characterization and high-velocity projectile impact ($7.62\ \text{mm}$ armor-piercing bullets, impact velocity $v = 800 \pm 20\ \text{m/s}$), digital image binarization, penetration depth ($d_p$), and rear scabbing limit thickness ($l_s$) were evaluated. Results showed that **UHPFRC restricted frontal crater area by $80\%$ and limited penetration depth to $28.5\ \text{mm}$ due to its high matrix density, whereas HDFRC completely suppressed ($100\%$) rear-face concrete scabbing through its $5.80\%$ tensile strain-hardening capacity**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First 1:1 comparative demonstration of high-velocity projectile impact responses in UHPFRC vs HDFRC**:
   - Experimentally clarified the divergent failure modes between high-strength (4.7-fold compressive strength) and high-ductility (7.6-fold tensile ductility) composites.
2. **Superior frontal penetration resistance of UHPFRC (penetration depth 28.5 mm)**:
   - Dense nano-microstructure and 3D steel fiber shear bridging dissipated projectile kinetic energy via localized surface pulverization.
3. **100 % suppression of rear-face scabbing (Scabbing Limit Thickness $l_s$) in HDFRC**:
   - Reflected tensile stress waves were fully bridged by $> 40$ multiple microcracks and PE fiber pullout, completely arresting rear fragment ejection ($100\%$).
4. **Design guidelines for layered protective composite panels (UHPFRC front + HDFRC rear)**:
   - Formulated an optimal functionally graded defense system combining frontal high-strength penetration resistance with rear ductile scabbing mitigation.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–4 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - UHPFRC: Cement + silica fume + zirconia powder (W/B = 0.20, brass-coated straight steel fibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 2500\ \text{MPa}$).
  - HDFRC: Cement + fly ash (W/B = 0.45, ultra-high-molecular-weight PE microfibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$).
- **High-velocity projectile impact test program (Sections 2.3–2.5 & Figs. 4–7)**:
  - Ballistic firing system with optical laser velocity gates ($v = 800\ \text{m/s}$).
  - Projectile: 7.62 mm NATO standard round (mass 9.6 g).
  - Measurements: Frontal crater area (image thresholding), penetration depth ($d_p$), scabbing mass loss, and cracking patterns.

### 2. High-velocity shockwave propagation and material fracture mechanics
- **Compressive resistance of UHPFRC**: The ultra-dense matrix resisted gigapascal-level hydrostatic shock pressures during projectile penetration, arresting bullet progression at $28.5\ \text{mm}$.
- **Tensile wave attenuation in HDFRC**: When compressive shockwaves reached the rear free surface and reflected as tensile waves, the $5.80\%$ ductile bridging network absorbed 100% of the tensile energy, preventing fragmentation.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated the complementary protection mechanisms of UHPFRC (minimizing penetration to 28.5 mm) and HDFRC (eliminating rear scabbing), establishing the foundation for dual-layer composite defense structures.
