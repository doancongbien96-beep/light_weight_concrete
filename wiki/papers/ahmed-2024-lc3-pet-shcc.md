# Preserving the strain-hardening response of PET-fiber SHCC with limestone and calcined clay (SH-LC3-PET)

## Summary

Targeting SHCC application of PET fibers — which degrade by alkaline hydrolysis in alkaline
environments — a TU Dresden (Mechtcherine group) study designed a tailored low-alkalinity,
low-strength matrix (SH-LC3) replacing 75 wt% of Portland cement with limestone plus calcined
clay, achieving and sustaining strain-hardening with untreated virgin PET fibers alone. While
direct exposure to pore solution (pH 12.5) reduced PET tensile strength by about 25 % up to
60 days, tensile performance inside the matrix was retained through 60 days. Strain capacity
exceeded 4 % at all ages; the authors state this is the first report of strain-hardening with
virgin PET alone.

## Key Contributions

1. SH-LC3 matrix design concept: (i) lower alkalinity via clinker reduction (protecting PET),
   (ii) capped matrix strength to satisfy the strength criterion with low-strength compliant
   PET fibers, (iii) limited clinker and portlandite for early hydration stabilization
   (property plateau after 14 days). The key move is using LC3 raw-material composition as a
   strain-hardening design tool.
2. Matrix-internal versus external exposure contrast: direct immersion in pH 12.5 pore
   solution cut PET tensile strength −7.6 % (7 d)/−11.7 % (14 d)/−13.6 % (28 d)/−24.7 % (60 d)
   with surface pitting (nano-craters) appearing between 28 and 60 days — acting as stress
   concentrators. Inside the matrix, chemical bond formation (rising Gd) coexists with pullout
   behavior, preserving performance through 60 days.
3. Non-monotonic interface development: single-fiber pullout shows frictional bond τ stable at
   0.26–0.29 MPa across ages, while chemical bond Gd peaks at 28 days — 0.11 J/m2 (7 d)
   → 3.52 (14 d) → 4.43 (28 d) → 1.16 J/m2 (60 d). (Limited) alkaline hydrolysis lowers
   hydrophobicity creating chemical bonding, but by 60 days diameter reduction weakens the bond again.
4. Tensile properties: first-cracking strength 1.4→2.3 MPa, tensile strength 2.0→3.3 MPa,
   E 4.3→9.7 GPa (7→60 d), strain capacity 5.0/4.4/4.8/5.8 % (7/14/28/60 d) — above 4 %
   throughout. PSH strength index σpc/σcc = 1.43/1.40/1.39/1.43, always above 1.2.
5. Mechanism of the 60-day strain rebound: reduced chemical bonding → pullout-dominated rather
   than rupture → higher complementary energy → saturation at crack density 297 /m with crack
   spacing 3.5 mm. Crack width control remains the weakness (mean width ~213 μm at ultimate
   strain, roughly twice PE-SHCC and four times PVA-SHCC) — flagged as a durability task.

## Methods

- Mix (kg/m3): cement (CEM I 52.5 R-SR3/NA) 286, limestone powder 282, calcined clay 564,
  gypsum 9 (3 wt% of binder), quartz sand 257 (s/b=0.23), PET fiber 34 (2.5 vol%, 18 mm),
  water 458 (w/b=0.4), PCE superplasticizer 8, VMA 4. The calcined clay is low-grade kaolinite
  (processed content ≤25 %, amorphous ~65.7 % after calcination).
- Two experimental tracks: (a) external pore-solution exposure — test solution prepared by
  diluting binder composition (pH 12.5±0.1), single fibers immersed 7/14/28/60 days then
  tension-tested (Zwick/Roell 1445, 0.05 mm/s) with ESEM observation; (b) matrix-internal
  aging — sealed curing then at the same ages compression (40 mm cubes), single-fiber pullout
  (4 mm embedment), and dog-bone tension with DIC/ARAMIS at 2 Hz.
- Pullout interpretation: τ = PB/(π df Le), Gd = 2(PA−PB)²/(π² Ef df³), pullout energy Wp by
  integration (Eqs. 1–3).

## Results

- Compressive strength 17.1 MPa (7 d) → 29.7 MPa (14 d, +73.7 %) then plateauing (30–32 MPa
  at 28 d) — in this highly diluted system (effective w/c ≈ 1.60) the clinker fully hydrates
  within 14 days.
- Pullout: at 14 and 28 days some fibers ruptured with sharp load drops (PVA-like chemical-
  bond behavior); by 60 days pullout returned. Wp stayed stable at 1.5–1.9×10^-4 J regardless
  of age.
- Maximum crack width ordered by strongest chemical bond: 28 d (249 μm) < 14 d < 60 d < 7 d
  (355 μm) — stronger bonds increase fiber rupture on localization planes, actually trimming
  strain capacity (consistent with Wang & Li's complementary-energy logic).
- Application proposal: given the crack-width limits, protective overlays (reinforcement) for
  dynamic rather than static loading are favored — PET's marked plastic deformation and the
  low-density matrix combine with strain-rate sensitivity. Future plans: LC3-50/-35 strength
  upgrades, carbonation curing, steel-fiber and carbon-textile hybrids.
