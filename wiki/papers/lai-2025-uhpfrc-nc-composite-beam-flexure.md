# Flexural Strength and Toughness Performance of UHPFRC-NC Composite Beams

## Summary

This experimental and theoretical study investigated composite beams pairing a tensile-zone UHPFRC layer (UC) with an unreinforced normal concrete layer (NC containing only a small fiber dosage) in the compression zone without conventional rebar reinforcement. The authors proposed a 3-stage (yield, peak, ultimate cracking) nonlinear equivalent stress block analytical model and conducted sensitivity analyses on UC layer thickness and NC compressive strength to establish section design guidelines. Flexural moment capacity gains taper once UC thickness exceeds 3 cm, and properly proportioned NC-UC2 composite beams achieve flexural performance comparable to full-depth UC1 sections.

## Key Contributions

- **Nonlinear equivalent stress block model maintaining closed-form moment equations**: Integrated a 3-segment generalized tensile stress block (Nguyen et al. 2017) with a linear compressive stress block (Momin et al. 2021) incorporating a calibration factor A=0.405 based on neutral axis location (NC vs UC layer).
- **Sensitivity analysis of UC thickness (0~7.5 cm)**: Peak flexural capacity $M_{pc}$ increased up to 4.7-fold at 3 cm thickness relative to plain NC, with diminishing returns beyond 3 cm identifying an optimal thickness threshold. The neutral axis remained within the top 1 cm of the NC layer across thicknesses until full-depth UC was reached.
- **Sensitivity to NC compressive strength (27.5/54.9/82.4 MPa)**: Increasing NC compressive strength enhanced moment capacity up to a threshold, beyond which gains leveled off, providing guidelines to avoid material over-specification.
- **Experimental validation**: NC-UC2 exhibited the lowest residual strength loss at L/150 deflection (68.9 % reduction across 5 specimens) and reached a peak load of 31.5 kN (exceeding the 23.5 kN of solid UC1), quantitatively confirming that expensive full-depth UHPFRC can be replaced by composite sections.

## Methods

- NC mix: Type 1 Portland cement, w/c 0.35, hooked steel fibers (diameter 0.5 mm, length 30 mm) at 0.5 vol %. UC mix: w/c 0.25, 50 % GGBS replacement of 450 kg/m³ cement, silica fume 225 kg/m³, silica sand ~940 kg/m³, micro straight steel fibers (diameter 0.2 mm, length 12.5 mm) at 1 % (UC1) and 2 % (UC2).
- Testing per ASTM C1609: 3-point bending on $30 \times 7.5 \times 7.5\ \text{cm}$ (30×7.5×7.5 cm) beams at $0.004\ \text{mm/s}$ (0.004 mm/s) displacement control using a 100 ton MTS machine with LVDT deflection tracking. Composite beams were cast with 30 mm UC + 45 mm NC.
- Material properties: Compressive strengths NC 79.5 MPa, UC1 135.9 MPa, UC2 137.7 MPa. Tensile peak strengths $f_{pc}$ (fpc) were 2.65, 6.19, and 9.98 MPa (a 1.6-fold gain from doubling fiber fraction from 1 to 2 vol %).

## Results

- Peak loads: NC 13.2 kN, NC-UC1 16.8 kN, UC1 23.5 kN, NC-UC2 31.5 kN, UC2 35.8 kN. Toughness from yield to peak increased from 0.43 kJ to 10.9 kJ (25.4-fold) for NC-UC2 and from 0.53 kJ to 16.1 kJ (30.4-fold) for UC2.
- Residual strength reduction at L/150 deflection: NC 75.0 %, NC-UC1 68.0 %, UC1 70.9 %, NC-UC2 68.9 %, UC2 70.5 %, demonstrating composite efficacy in maintaining post-peak capacity.
- Cracking patterns: UC2-based specimens developed significantly more multiple microcracks than UC1. NC-UC2 displayed $45^\circ$ (45°) inclined shear cracks preceding flexural yield, highlighting the need for shear verification.
- Nonlinear analytical solutions accurately reproduced strain-hardening capacity increases, capturing lower yield and higher peak/cracking moments.
