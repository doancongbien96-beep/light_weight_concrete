## Summary

This study experimentally evaluated, jointly with NASA Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC), the physico-mechanical performance, durability, and vacuum sublimation behavior of non-hydrated sulfur concrete and geopolymer composite materials for lunar base construction. Using lunar simulant (JSC-1) and refined sulfur as binders, it established a molten casting process at $135 \sim 145 °C$ and carried out exposure tests simulating extreme lunar environments ($10^{-6} Torr$ high vacuum and 50 cycles of thermal cycling over $-150 \sim +120 °C$). It demonstrated that sulfur concrete develops a compressive strength of $35 \sim 45 MPa$ and a flexural strength of $8.5 MPa$ within 1 h of cooling, and that with appropriate silane surface sealing the vacuum sublimation mass loss rate is suppressed to 0.2 % or below.

## Key Contributions
- **Derivation of the optimal mix proportion of lunar simulant (JSC-1) sulfur concrete**: the highest compressive strength ($42.8 MPa$) and fracture toughness were secured with the 35 wt % sulfur + 65 wt % JSC-1 mix.
- **Verification of resistance to lunar thermal cycling ($-150 \sim +120 °C$)**: analysis of the aggregate-sulfur interface shear behavior under abrupt phase-transformation stress and suppression of thermal cracking through addition of the modifier (DCPD).
- **Proposal of vacuum sublimation kinetics and surface protection schemes**: quantification of the sublimation mechanism of unprotected sulfur concrete in vacuum environments and demonstration of the shielding performance of thin-film aluminum deposition/silicone coatings.

## Methods
- **Materials**: JSC-1 lunar simulant (basaltic chemical composition, fineness modulus 2.2), high-purity elemental sulfur, DCPD polymerization modifier (2 wt %).
- **Fabrication**: heated mixing of aggregate/sulfur ($140 °C$), casting into preheated molds followed by pressure compaction, then air cooling at room temperature.
- **Test items**: compressive and flexural strength, coefficient of thermal expansion (TMA), and mass loss rate measurement in a Thermal Vacuum Chamber.

## Results
- Compressive strength of $42.5 MPa$ and modulus of rupture of $8.2 MPa$ after 1 h of room-temperature curing.
- Residual compressive strength of $36.8 MPa$ ($86 \%$ retention) after exposure to 50 thermal-vacuum cycles.
