# Fresh-State and Hardened Mechanical Flexural-Toughness Behavior of Waste-Tyre Rubber Particle Substitution Concrete

## Summary

For eco-friendly recycling of waste tyre rubber and the development of impact-absorbing, lightweight concrete, this study (a leading circular-resource composites paper by Prof. M. A. Aiello's research team at the University of Salento, Italy) produced rubcrete in which coarse aggregate (12.5–20 mm) and fine aggregate (10–12.5 mm) were partially replaced at 0 %, 25 %, 50 %, and 75 % by volume with shredded waste-tyre rubber pieces (scrap tyre rubber shreds), and quantitatively compared slump workability, unit weight, 28-day compressive strength, four-point flexural strength, and post-cracking load-bearing capacity (post-cracking ductility / energy absorption). The results showed that incorporating nonpolar rubber particles reduced surface friction resistance, improving slump from 180 mm (class S4) to 220 mm (class S5, highly flowable) and reducing unit weight by up to 8.8 % (2372 → 2163 kg/m3). Compressive strength decreased owing to the weakness of the rubber–cement interface, and the study established that the strength loss with fine-aggregate replacement (28–37 % loss) is far smaller than with coarse-aggregate replacement (54–62 % loss). Furthermore, flexural fracture tests demonstrated that, unlike the complete brittle failure of plain concrete, the tensile bridging effect of the rubber particles sustains considerable residual flexural capacity beyond the peak load while dissipating plastic-deformation energy.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantification of flowability and unit-weight changes under waste-tyre rubber particle substitution**:
   - The hydrophobic surface characteristics of the shredded rubber particles exclude free-water absorption, raising slump from 180 mm to 215–220 mm and improving constructability without increasing the superplasticizer dosage.
   - Unit weight decreases by up to 8.8 %, from 2372 kg/m3 to 2163 kg/m3 at 25–75 % substitution, securing the feasibility of application as a lightweight structural material.
2. **Differentiation of compressive-strength degradation mechanisms by replaced-aggregate size**:
   - **Coarse replacement**: coarse rubber particles act as large defects (macro-voids), collapsing strength by **54.4 %** at 50 % substitution (45.8 → 20.9 MPa).
   - **Fine replacement**: fine rubber particles disperse uniformly within the paste, containing the strength loss at 50 % substitution to only **28.3 %** (42.1 → 30.2 MPa).
3. **Increased post-cracking flexural toughness and impact-energy absorption**:
   - Whereas plain concrete drops abruptly toward zero after brittle fracture, rubcrete maintains a stable ductile load-descent curve up to ultimate deflection through the elastic deformation and split-bridging action of the rubber particles.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mix design (unit content kg/m3, Tables 3 and 4)**:
  - Binder: ordinary Portland cement (CEM II/A-LL 42.5 R) 335 kg/m3, mixing water 174~200 L/m3 (W/C = 0.52~0.60), plasticizer 3.35 L/m3.
  - Natural aggregates: coarse aggregate 12.5~20 mm, fine aggregate 10~12.5 mm, coarse sand 2~4 mm, fine sand 1~2 mm.
  - Waste-tyre rubber particles:
    - Coarse rubber shreds (12.5~20 mm; tensile strength 18.5 MPa, elongation at break 420 %, elastic modulus 2.1 MPa).
    - Fine rubber shreds (10~12.5 mm; specific gravity 1.15).
  - Replacement volumetric rates: 0 % (C1, C2), 25 % (RA), 50 % (RB/RC), and 75 % (RC/RD) of the coarse or fine aggregate volume.
- **Test equipment and test methods**:
  - Fresh concrete: slump test (UNI EN 12350-2), unit weight (unit mass).
  - Compressive strength: 150 mm × 150 mm × 150 mm cube specimens (UNI EN 12390-3, 28 days).
  - Flexural strength and load–deflection curves: four-point bending of 150 mm × 150 mm × 600 mm prismatic beams (UNI EN 12390-5, 450 mm span, LVDT deflection measurement).

### 2. Fracture behavior and application-suitability analysis
- **Strength-retention optimum**: mixes replacing 25–50 % of the fine aggregate with rubber (RA2, RC2) retain compressive strengths of 30–36 MPa and flexural strengths of 4.0–4.6 MPa, satisfying structural specifications.
- **Brittleness mitigation**: rubber particles delay crack propagation at crack tips and elastically absorb impact energy, delivering optimal performance for road kerbs, pavement base layers, railway pads, and noise barriers.

- **Overall conclusion**: recycling waste tyre rubber proves to be a sustainable circular-resource concrete technology that, when applied as fine-aggregate substitution, minimizes compressive-strength loss while securing unit-weight reduction, improved workability, and excellent post-cracking flexural plastic toughness.
