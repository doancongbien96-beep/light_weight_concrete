# Mechanism of Resonant-Frequency-Based Dynamic Modulus ($E_d$) Reduction and 2.25-Fold Enhancement of the Dynamic Damping Ratio ($1.75\% \rightarrow 3.95\%$) in Self-Compacting Rubberised Concrete (SCRC)

## Summary

To control vibration transmission in structures while dissipating floor impact sound and seismic energy and securing no-compaction constructability, **self-compacting rubberised concrete (SCRC: Self-Compacting Rubberised Concrete) was designed by replacing natural fine and coarse aggregates with scrap-tire crumb rubber (CR: $0.5\sim 4\ \text{mm}$) by weight (5 %, 10 %, 15 wt.%)**, and the dynamic elastic modulus ($E_d$), dynamic shear modulus ($G_d$), quality factor ($Q$), and **structural damping ratio ($\xi$) evolution behavior** were precisely evaluated using slump flow (SF), 28-day compressive strength, four-point flexural load–deflection curves, ASTM C1018 flexural toughness indices ($I_5, I_{10}, I_{20}$), ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), and a resonant frequency tester (ERUDIT); **at 5 wt.% rubber replacement the material preserved a structural strength of $42.5\ \text{MPa}$ while raising the damping ratio from $1.75\% \rightarrow 3.95\%$, a 2.25-fold improvement, and amplifying flexural toughness by more than a factor of 2** — a landmark original-source paper by **Dr Khalid B. Najim and Prof. Matthew R. Hall of the University of Nottingham, UK**, in Construction and Building Materials 2012.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of the optimal flowability–mechanical balance between self-compacting concrete (SCC) and waste rubber powder**:
   - Combined fly ash (PFA) with a high-range water-reducer (SP) to control the viscosity increase induced by rubber particle addition, perfectly maintaining a self-compacting slump flow of $650\sim 750\ \text{mm}$.
2. **Increase of the dynamic damping ratio by a factor of 2.25 ($1.75\% \rightarrow 3.95\%$) and quantification of vibration damping capacity**:
   - Demonstrated that the quality factor ($Q$) derived by the resonant-frequency half-power bandwidth method plunges from $57.1 \rightarrow 25.3$ (damping capacity $1/Q$ up about 2.3-fold), absorbing vibration energy rapidly.
3. **Demonstrated leap in the ASTM C1018 flexural toughness indices ($I_5, I_{10}, I_{20}$)**:
   - At 5 wt.% fine rubber replacement the flexural toughness index $I_{10}$ surges by $102\%$ relative to the reference SCC, excluding brittle post-cracking failure and realizing stable quasi-ductile deflection.
4. **Construction of a dynamics model according to rubber particle size (fine-aggregate replacement FR vs. coarse-aggregate replacement CR)**:
   - Demonstrated that fine rubber powder (FR), compared with coarse rubber (CR), suppresses void formation within the cement matrix, minimizing the compressive-strength loss while exerting a more homogeneous elastic-wave scattering effect.

## Methods

- **Materials and SCRC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Binder: OPC 42.5R + fly ash (PFA, 25 wt.%, activity index 84 %).
  - Aggregates: natural silica sand (0~2 mm) + limestone coarse aggregate (4~10 mm) + polycarboxylate SP.
  - Crumb rubber: sourced from scrap tires (density $1050\ \text{kg/m}^3$).
  - Replacement combinations:
    1. `SCC-Control`: 0 % rubber (reference mix).
    2. `5FR, 10FR, 15FR`: fine aggregate replaced with rubber at 5, 10, 15 wt.%.
    3. `5CR, 10CR, 15CR`: coarse aggregate replaced with rubber at 5, 10, 15 wt.%.
- **Mechanical and dynamic test program (Sections 3~4 & Figs. 3~9)**:
  - Compressive strength and splitting tension: $100\ \text{mm}$ cubes and $\phi 100 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylinders.
  - Flexural toughness: $100 \times 100 \times 500\ \text{mm}$ beams (four-point bending, span 300 mm).
  - Resonant-frequency dynamics test: ERUDIT resonance tester (BS 1881-5 longitudinal/torsional vibration) $\rightarrow f_0, f_1, f_2 \rightarrow Q = f_0/(f_2 - f_1), \xi = 1/(2Q)$.

### 2. Analysis of the viscoelastic cushioning and elastic-wave damping mechanisms

- **Flexible spring action of the rubber particles**: rubber particles whose elastic modulus is about 100 times lower than that of the cement matrix undergo localized shear elastic deformation under dynamic external loading, absorbing kinetic energy and attenuating the vibration response peaks.
- **Wave scattering by acoustic impedance discontinuity**: the large difference in density and elastic-wave velocity between rubber and the concrete matrix scatters the resonant impact waves at the interface, reducing the quality factor $Q$ to below one half.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark original-source study that established high-performance smart vibration-absorbing self-compacting structural material technology, raising the damping ratio to $3.51\%$ (more than double) while maintaining a slump flow of $710\ \text{mm}$ and a compressive strength of $42.5\ \text{MPa}$ by incorporating waste-tire rubber powder into self-compacting concrete.
