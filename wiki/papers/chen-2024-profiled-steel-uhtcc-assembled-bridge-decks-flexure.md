# Flexural behavior of profiled steel-ultra-high toughness cementitious composite (Profiled Steel-UHTCC) assembled composite bridge decks: $45\%$ self-weight reduction, 2.4-fold amplification of flexural capacity, and a $0.03\ \text{mm}$ crack-width control mechanism

## Summary

To simultaneously address the chronic deficiencies of steel bridge decks (fatigue cracking and excessive self-weight), this landmark Journal of Constructional Steel Research 2024 original research article by the team of Chair Professor Shi-Lang Xu and Professor Qing-Hua Li of Zhejiang University (China) and Professor Wei Gao of UNSW (Australia) develops **a novel ultra-lightweight composite bridge deck ($2400 \times 600 \times 120\ \text{mm}$) assembled integrally with shear studs from a closed-section cold-formed profiled steel plate (thickness $4\sim 14\ \text{mm}$) and a 2.0 vol.% PVA ultra-high toughness cementitious composite (UHTCC: 28-day compressive strength $52.0\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial ultimate tensile strength $3.30\ \text{MPa}$, tensile ductility $4.20\%$)**. Static four-point flexural loading tests, DIC strain mapping of shear sections, steel-UHTCC interface slip analysis, and a nonlinear plastic flexural capacity model demonstrate **a $45\%$ self-weight reduction versus conventional flat-plate/normal-concrete top decks, a $140\%$ rise in first flexural cracking load, 2.4-fold amplification of ultimate flexural capacity, serviceability crack widths constrained at or below $0.03\ \text{mm}$, and complete suppression of local buckling of the steel plate**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First implementation of a geometrically and mechanically complementary assembly system of profiled steel plate and UHTCC**:
   - Fuses the high second moment of area of the profiled steel plate with the ultra-ductile tensile bridging resistance of UHTCC, slimming the deck thickness to $120\ \text{mm}$ while achieving a breakthrough $45\%$ self-weight reduction.
2. **Complete prevention of out-of-plane local buckling of the profiled steel plate**:
   - The upper UHTCC layer continuously restrains the compression flange and web of the profiled plate elastically, so the steel expresses $100\%$ of its strength without buckling until reaching the plastic yield stress ($f_y = 355\ \text{MPa}$).
3. **Serviceability crack widths fully controlled at or below $0.03\ \text{mm}$ with watertightness secured**:
   - Tensile stresses from vehicular wheel loads on the bridge are dispersed into more than 30 multiple fine cracks in the UHTCC, blocking chloride and rainwater penetration at the source.
4. **Establishment of a dedicated plastic moment design equation for profiled steel-UHTCC assembled decks**:
   - A flexural design equation derived considering the profiled-section shape factor and the tensile softening-hardening relation of UHTCC is shown to match experimental measurements with high accuracy ($R^2 = 0.97$).

## Methods

- **Specimen design and mix (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Deck geometry: $2400 \times 600 \times 120\ \text{mm}$ (span $2000\ \text{mm}$, profiled plate height $70\ \text{mm}$, upper UHTCC thickness $50\ \text{mm}$).
  - Plate thickness variables: $t_s = 4, 10, 12, 14\ \text{mm}$ (Q355B structural steel, $f_y = 385.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - UHTCC mix: cement + fly ash (FA/C = 2.5, W/B = 0.25, Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$).
- **Flexural test program (Sections 2.3~2.5 & Figs. 5~8)**:
  - Four-point bend loading tests (0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Instrumentation: 2D-DIC imaging of shear sections, five LVDT deflection gauges, stud interface relative-slip sensors.

### 2. Shear flow and plastic energy dissipation analysis of the profiled section
- **Three-dimensional shear stiffness of the profiled ribs**: the folded profile supports transverse torsion and out-of-plane shear, amplifying deck deflectional stiffness 2.8-fold compared with an ordinary flat plate.
- **Perfect deformation compatibility between UHTCC and steel**: the $4.2\%$ strain capacity of UHTCC synchronizes perfectly with plastic deformation of the steel, forming a stable ductile plastic hinge up to ultimate load without shear fracture of the interface studs.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-tier landmark original research article in the field of steel-concrete composite structures: the Chair Professor Shi-Lang Xu team developed a novel composite bridge deck assembling a profiled steel plate with ultra-high-toughness UHTCC, demonstrated a 45 % self-weight reduction, 2.4-fold amplification of ultimate flexural capacity, and crack-width control at or below 0.03 mm, and established the design equations.
