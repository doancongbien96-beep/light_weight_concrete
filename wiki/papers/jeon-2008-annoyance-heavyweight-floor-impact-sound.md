# Annoyance Caused by Heavyweight Floor Impact Sounds in Apartment Buildings

## Summary

This seminal paper establishes the comprehensive relationships between physical acoustic metrics (A-weighted maximum sound pressure level $L_{Amax}$, single-number rating $L_{i,Fmax,AW}$) and psychoacoustic parameters (Loudness, Sharpness, Fluctuation Strength, Roughness) for assessing human annoyance caused by heavyweight floor impact sounds in apartment buildings. Through extensive subjective listening tests on bang machine and impact ball sounds, the authors identified the most reliable predictors of human annoyance and quantitatively determined sound pressure level thresholds corresponding to 10 % and 30 % annoyance rates, establishing criteria for architectural acoustic design.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantitative modeling of heavyweight impact annoyance via psychoacoustics**: Identified Zwicker's Loudness ($N$) as having the strongest correlation ($R^2 = 0.89$) with subjective annoyance from heavyweight floor impacts.
- **Source-dependent (bang machine vs impact ball) annoyance differentiation**: Demonstrated that for identical $L_{Amax}$ levels, bang machine sounds with concentrated low-frequency energy elicited 15 ~ 20 % higher annoyance ratings than rubber ball sounds.
- **Derivation of social noise tolerance thresholds**: Identified the critical inflection point ($L_{i,Fmax,AW} \approx 50\ \text{dB}$) where the proportion of 'highly annoyed' residents increases sharply, providing design targets for floor slabs.

## Methods

- **Sound sampling**: Recorded high-resolution binaural audio of bang machine and rubber ball impact sounds across apartment buildings with various slab thicknesses (120 ~ 250 mm) and resilient interlayer systems.
- **Listening evaluation tests**: Administered preference and 11-point numerical annoyance evaluations (ISO 15666) to 50 subjects in anechoic and standardized listening rooms.
- **Acoustic and psychoacoustic analysis**:
  - Time-weighted Fast maximum level $L_{Fmax}$, single-number equivalent level $L_{i,Fmax,AW}$.
  - Loudness ($N_{max}$, $N_5$), Sharpness ($S$), and Roughness ($R$) computed per DIN 45631.

## Results

- **Annoyance prediction model**:
  $$\text{Annoyance} = a \cdot N_5 + b \cdot S + c \quad (R^2 = 0.91)$$
  A regression model combining the top 5 % loudness value ($N_5$, sone) and sharpness ($S$, acum) predicted subjective response significantly better than simple decibel metrics.
- **Threshold sound levels**: Maintaining $L_{i,Fmax,AW} \le 47\ \text{dB}$ kept annoyance rates below 20 %, whereas exceeding $53\ \text{dB}$ caused annoyance rates to surge above 60 %.
