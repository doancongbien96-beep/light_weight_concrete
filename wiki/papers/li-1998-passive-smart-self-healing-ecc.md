# Development of a Passive Smart Self-Healing Cementitious Composite (PSS-ECC) Embedding Hollow Glass Fibers and a Chemical Healing Agent, with Demonstrated Stiffness Recovery Ratio of $140\%$

## Summary

A 1998 Composites Part B: Engineering landmark original paper of immortal stature by the joint research team of University of Michigan endowed chair professor Victor C. Li, Yonsei University professor Yun Mook Lim, and Yin-Wen Chan of National Taiwan University, which proposed for the first time in the world the concept of a **passive smart self-healing cementitious composite (PSS-ECC: Passive Smart Self-Healing ECC)**—a material that senses damage and performs self-healing on its own, without any external power source or human intervention, when overloads and cracks occur in a structure—and which integrated **hollow glass fibers (Hollow Glass Fiber, SAC) filled with an air-curing healing resin (Ethyl Cyanoacrylate)** into the ECC matrix, demonstrating through repeated 4-point bending tests the full-cycle self-healing process of microcrack formation $\rightarrow$ hollow fiber rupture $\rightarrow$ capillary resin release $\rightarrow$ complete crack sealing and hardening, thereby achieving a **post-damage flexural stiffness recovery ratio of $140\%$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first establishment of the Passive Smart Self-Healing concrete concept**:
   - Construction of a fully autonomous materials-level smart system that operates without external sensors or control equipment, using the rupture of brittle hollow fibers as the 'sensor' and the self-release of the internal chemical agent as the 'actuator'.
2. **Demonstration of the synergy between the ECC crack-width control property and capillary release from hollow fibers**:
   - Whereas the healing resin leaks and is lost at the large localized cracks of ordinary concrete, the fine multiple crack widths ($< 100\ \mu\text{m}$) of ECC provide strong capillary suction so that the resin uniformly fills the entire crack and forms bridging bonds.
3. **Quantitative proof of a stiffness recovery ratio of 140 % via repeated 4-point bending load tests**:
   - After first-crack damage, with the load held at 0.2 kN while the healing agent cured, reloading restored the flexural stiffness to a level equivalent to the initial undamaged state.
4. **Visualization of the self-healing mechanism via optical dye tracing and cross-sectional SEM observation**:
   - Captured the tensile and shear rupture morphology of the hollow fibers at the crack tip and demonstrated the process by which the released resin bonds with matrix particles and bridging fibers to restore watertightness.

## Methods

- **Smart material composition (Section 2 & Fig. 3)**:
  - Matrix: Spectra PE fiber-based ECC ($V_f = 1.5\sim 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}$).
  - Healing agent carrier: hollow borosilicate glass fibers (SAC: inner diameter $0.5\sim 0.6\ \text{mm}$, outer diameter $0.8\sim 1.0\ \text{mm}$, length 150 mm).
  - Healing resin: 1-part low-viscosity ethyl cyanoacrylate (Ethyl Cyanoacrylate) + red fluorescent tracing dye.
- **Specimens and loading test (Section 3 & Figs. 7~8)**:
  - Specimen dimensions: $38.1 \times 38.1 \times 152.4\ \text{mm}$ prism beams.
  - SAC fiber placement: embedded at the center of the tensile region, 5 strands per layer in 1~2 layers.
  - 4-point bending test: displacement-controlled loading $\rightarrow$ hold at $0.2\ \text{kN}$ upon load drop (waiting for resin curing) $\rightarrow$ reload test.

### 2. Interpretation of the three-stage sensing-actuation-healing mechanism

- **Precision sensing rupture mechanism**: when the matrix crack opening displacement reaches $200\ \mu\text{m}$, the shear/tensile stress concentrated on the glass fiber surface induces instantaneous brittle rupture, releasing the internal pressure so that the resin is immediately expelled.
- **Capillary penetration and solid bridging**: the narrow crack gaps ($< 100\ \mu\text{m}$) of ECC form a powerful meniscus capillary pressure that spreads the resin across the entire crack cross-section, and its reaction with moisture in the air forms a rapidly polymerizing polymer matrix, structurally re-bonding the crack interface.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the most celebrated immortal landmark original paper in the field of intelligent construction materials (Smart Materials), in which Victor C. Li and Prof. Yun Mook Lim collaborated to realize $140\%$ flexural stiffness self-healing by exploiting the material's own microcracks as the sensor and the embedded hollow fibers as the actuator, entirely without external control.
