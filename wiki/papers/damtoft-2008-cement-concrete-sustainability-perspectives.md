# Carbon-Mitigation Technology Strategies of the Cement and Concrete Industry for Sustainable Development and Climate Change: A Review

## Summary

An authoritative global review by leading researchers of Aalborg Portland (Denmark) and Lafarge (France) — Damtoft, Herfort, Gartner et al. — comprehensively analyzing the four core mitigation strategies pursued by the cement manufacturing and concrete construction industry, which accounts for roughly 5 % of worldwide anthropogenic greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, toward 2050 net-zero and climate-change response: curbing raw-meal decarbonation, developing low-energy clinkers, maximizing blended cements/SCMs, and lifecycle recarbonation capture in concrete. The paper quantifies the structure of the roughly 0.87 tonnes of $\text{CO}_2$ released per tonne of clinker produced (0.53 tonnes of raw-material $\text{CO}_2$ from limestone decarbonation + 0.34 tonnes from calcination fuel), and systematically lays out belite-based and calcium sulfoaluminate (CSA) low-energy, low-calcium clinker technologies, expansion of slag/fly ash/limestone ternary blended cements, permanent $\text{CO}_2$ sequestration through natural recarbonation of crushed concrete aggregate, and a roadmap for building heating/cooling energy savings exploiting the high thermal mass of concrete.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantitative decomposition of the cement-production $\text{CO}_2$ emission mechanism ($0.87\ \text{kg }\text{CO}_2/\text{kg}$)**:
   - **Raw-material emissions (raw material $\text{CO}_2$)**: the unavoidable chemical release arising from decarbonation of limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) ($\text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow$) amounts to about **$0.53\ \text{kg/kg-clinker}$**, some 60 % of the total.
   - **Fuel emissions (fuel $\text{CO}_2$)**: emissions attributable to the $1450\ ^\circ\text{C}$ calcination heat source and to electricity consumption amount to about **$0.34\ \text{kg/kg-clinker}$**.
2. **Systematization of innovative low-calcium, low-energy clinker technologies**:
   - **Belite-rich clinker ($\text{C}_2\text{S}$)**: lowers the $\text{CaO}$ requirement and reduces the calcination temperature to $1350\ ^\circ\text{C}$, cutting raw-material and fuel $\text{CO}_2$ by 15~20 % simultaneously.
   - **Calcium sulfoaluminate (CSA / BCTCS)**: calcination at $1250\sim 1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$ on an alumina/sulfate basis cuts $\text{CO}_2$ emissions by up to 30 %.
3. **Blended cement and clinker-factor reduction roadmap**:
   - establishes a pathway for cutting the global clinker factor from 0.80 to 0.60 or below by applying multi-component composite cements combining ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS), fly ash (FA), silica fume (SF), and limestone filler.
4. **Demonstration of lifecycle $\text{CO}_2$ natural uptake through concrete carbonation (recarbonation)**:
   - scientifically establishes that concrete structures re-react with atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ during their service period and during demolition, crushing, and recycling, permanently re-absorbing (as a sink) **20~40 %** of the original limestone-decarbonation emissions.

## Methods

- **Global cement industry database and scenario analysis**:
  - analysis of World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD) Cement Sustainability Initiative (CSI) indicators.
  - world cement production trend (1.57 billion tonnes in 2000 $\rightarrow$ projection exceeding 3 billion tonnes by 2020).
- **Analysis of technical mitigation axes (four domains)**:
  1. Alternative fuels and raw materials: scrap tires, biomass, refuse-derived fuel (RDF) from municipal solid waste, steel slag as kiln feed.
  2. New clinker thermodynamics: Belite-Ye'elimite-Ferrite (BYF), Calcium Sulfoaluminate (CSA).
  3. Concrete admixture technology: maximizing binary/ternary SCM replacement ratios.
  4. Lifecycle carbon uptake model: estimating concrete-carbonation $\text{CO}_2$ sequestration on the basis of Fick's law of diffusion.

## Results

### 1. Stage-wise comparison of $\text{CO}_2$ emissions in cement manufacture and mitigation potentials

| Mitigation lever | Key technologies and means | $\text{CO}_2$ reduction potential | Technical challenges and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fuel substitution** | biomass, waste-derived fuels (RDF) | 10 ~ 20 % | kiln combustion control and flue-gas environmental standards |
| **Raw-material substitution** | non-carbonate calcium sources (slag, etc.) | 5 ~ 15 % | limits on raw-material availability and chemical-composition constraints |
| **Low-energy clinker** | Belite, CSA, BCT clinkers | 15 ~ 30 % | securing early strength, inclusion in standards |
| **Blended cement (SCM)** | combined substitution of GGBS, FA, and limestone | **20 ~ 50 %** | **the most immediate and powerful practical solution** |
| **Recarbonation** | carbonation of recycled crushed concrete aggregate | **absorbs 20~40 % of raw-material $\text{CO}_2$** | optimization of particle-size grinding and open-stockpile exposure conditions |

### 2. Concrete thermal mass and lifecycle sustainability
- **Building energy savings**: the high heat capacity of concrete buffers indoor temperature fluctuations, cutting peak summer cooling and winter heating energy by 10~15 %.
- **Demolished-concrete circulation**: when demolished concrete is converted into recycled aggregate, its specific surface area increases sharply, so it rapidly absorbs atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ within months and offsets the environmental burden.

- **Overall conclusion**: establishes that the future sustainability of the cement and concrete industry is attained not through any single technology but through a whole-lifecycle integrated approach of "multi-component SCM blended cements + low-carbon clinkers + service-life extension through durability design + post-demolition recarbonation capture".
