# Influence of Aggregate Type and Size on ECC Ductility

## Summary

Replacing the microsilica sand (maximum 200 µm) of standard ECC with ordinary crushed dolomitic limestone sand and gravel sand (each with a maximum of 1.19 and 2.38 mm) and taking the fly ash replacement ratio (FA/C = 1.2, 2.2, 4.2 by mass) as the study variable, this study evaluated mechanics, ductility, and drying shrinkage. It demonstrated that under high FA content even ordinary sand can retain standard-ECC-level tensile ductility, providing grounds for expanding the local-material applicability and economy of ECC.

## Key Contributions

1. Mechanism by which high FA relaxes the aggregate size constraint: increasing FA lowers the chemical bond at the PVA fiber/matrix interface and the matrix toughness while raising interfacial frictional bonding, favoring strain-hardening. In addition, FA's low density increases paste volume, improving fiber dispersion, together with the ball-bearing effect of its spherical particles.
2. Result data: at FA/C = 4.2, tensile strain capacity of 1.96~3.23 % (28 days) and tensile strength of 3.57~5.13 MPa. Residual crack widths remained below 60 µm across all mixes. Compressive strength at FA/C 4.2 also exceeded 30 MPa.
3. Subtle effect of aggregate type: gravel sand showed slightly higher ductility than crushed sand of the same size — the superior aggregate-paste bonding of crushed sand raises matrix toughness, acting unfavorably against the J_tip criterion. An uncommon observation that aggregate surface roughness is reflected in the tensile behavior of ECC.
4. The effect of MAS 1.19→2.38 mm was unexpectedly small — the increase in matrix toughness with increasing aggregate size offsets the reducing effect of high FA.
5. Drying shrinkage reduction: a maximum decrease of 20 % from FA/C 1.2→2.2, with an additional decrease at MAS 1.19→2.38 mm (aggregate restraint). Crushed sand mixes were the lowest.

## Methods

15 mixes with w/cm fixed at 0.27 and PVA fibers at 2 vol % (39 µm diameter, 8 mm length, strength 1620 MPa, 1.2 % oil coating). Direct-tension coupons (203×76×13 mm, 0.005 mm/s), four-point bending (span 305 mm), 50 mm compression cubes, drying shrinkage per ASTM C157 (90 days).

## Results

- First-cracking strength decreased from 4.0 to 3.0 MPa with increasing FA/C, while ultimate tensile strength was up to 30 % higher at FA/C 1.2 — a clear strength-ductility trade-off.
- Flexural strength (MOR) ranged 8.14~12.75 MPa, reaching 65 % of the FA/C 1.2 value at FA/C 4.2. Deflection capacity increased up to 6.51 mm with increasing FA/C.
- Unlike ordinary concrete, the compressive strength of ECC was nearly unaffected by aggregate surface roughness and MAS.

## Related Notes

- The FA-induced reduction of interfacial parameters (G_d↓, τ↑, J_tip↓) is an experimental demonstration of the backward tailoring logic of [[li-2023-ecc-micromechanics-critical-review]].
- Symmetric to the S/B ratio findings of [[kumar-2022-egc-repair-sand-binder-ratio]]: increasing sand/aggregate raises σ_c and reduces ductility, but this can be compensated through binder design (FA ratio, fiber length).
- The textbook origin of the general principles by which aggregate gradation, shape, and MAS affect concrete properties is Ch8 of [[newman-2003-advanced-concrete-technology-materials]].
