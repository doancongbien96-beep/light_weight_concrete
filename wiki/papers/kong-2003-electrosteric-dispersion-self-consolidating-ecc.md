# Development of Self-Consolidating PE-ECC (SC-ECC) via Electrosteric Dispersion/Stabilization Rheological Control and Tensile Strain-Hardening Behavior

## Summary

In engineered cementitious composites reinforced with hydrophobic high-modulus polyethylene (PE) fibers (PE-ECC), micromechanical constraints governing extreme tensile strain-hardening (tensile strain capacity 5~6 %, over 10-fold higher than the 0.5 % of standard UHPC/Ductal) require strictly fixed matrix parameters (W/C = 0.27~0.30, interfacial frictional shear strength $\tau_0 = 0.5\sim 0.7\ \text{MPa}$, fiber volume fraction $V_f = 1.0\ \text{vol.\%}$). To develop **Self-Consolidating PE-ECC (SC-ECC)** capable of flowing and filling congested reinforcement under self-weight without external vibration while preserving these micromechanical constraints, Prof. Victor C. Li and Dr. Hyun-Joon Kong (Univ. of Michigan, Ann Arbor) established an **electrosteric rheological design approach** combining electrostatic dispersion via strong polyelectrolytes with steric stabilization via non-ionic water-soluble polymers. Testing L-box passing ability ($L = 1.0$), fresh deformability indices ($\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2$), and uniaxial tensile behavior of unvibrated cast specimens demonstrated that sequential addition of superplasticizer (MFS) and hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) achieved zero bleeding (0.0 %), full self-consolidation ($L = 1.0$), and **tensile strain capacity of 5.0 ~ 6.0 %** with tensile strength of **5.2 MPa** under complete omission of vibratory compaction.

## Key Contributions

1. **Orthogonal integration of micromechanics and electrosteric rheology**:
   - Established a design paradigm maximizing fresh flowability purely through polymer admixture interactions while holding fixed the W/C (0.30) and fiber fraction ($V_f = 1.0\%$) required for 5 % tensile ductility.
2. **Electrosteric dispersion and anti-sedimentation mechanisms**:
   - Combined zeta potential charging via polyelectrolytes (MFS/PCE) to break agglomerates with non-ionic HPMC steric barrier layers to prevent particle segregation and slump loss.
3. **Achievement of complete L-Box passing ratio ($L = 1.0$, 100 % pass)**:
   - Eliminated mechanical fiber jamming through congested 35 mm rebar clearance gates via optimized plastic viscosity.
4. **Demonstration of 6 % ultimate tensile ductility without vibration compaction**:
   - Maintained 3D uniform fiber dispersion even after 10 min rest prior to unvibrated placement, stably realizing saturated multiple microcracking and **5.5 % tensile elongation**.

## Methods

- **Materials and SC-ECC mixture proportions (Section 3 & Table 1)**:
  - Binder: ASTM Type I cement (density $3.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$, $d_{50} = 15\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Aggregate: Fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/C = 1.0).
  - Admixture system:
    1. Superplasticizer (SP): Sulfonated melamine formaldehyde (MFS, anionic polyelectrolyte, dosage $0.5\sim 1.5\ \text{wt.\%}$).
    2. Viscosity modifier (VMA): HPMC (non-ionic hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, dosage $0.05\sim 0.20\ \text{wt.\%}$).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Spectra-900 high-strength hydrophobic PE fibers ($L_f = 12.7\ \text{mm}, d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 2600 MPa, elastic modulus 73 GPa, $V_f = 1.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - Water-to-cement ratio: W/C = 0.30 (cement volume fraction $\phi_{cement} = 0.51$).
- **Fresh property and self-consolidation characterization (Section 3.2)**:
  - Mortar flow cone deformability index: $\Gamma_1 = (d_1 + d_2)/2d_0$ ($d_0 = 100\ \text{mm}$).
  - Large slump cone ECC deformability: $\Gamma_2 = (D_1 + D_2)/2D_0$ ($D_0 = 200\ \text{mm}$, measured initially and after 10 min rest).
  - Scaled L-Box test: Height passing ratio $L = H / (H_0 / 2)$ across 35 mm clear bar obstacles.
- **Uniaxial tensile testing of hardened composite (Section 3.2.4)**:
  - Plate specimens ($150\ \text{mm} \times 75\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$) cast without vibration after 10 min rest.
  - MTS servohydraulic testing at $0.005\ \text{mm/s}$ displacement rate (50 mm gauge length LVDTs).

### 2. Processing rheology and micromechanics compatibility
- **Suppression of fiber jamming**: Hydrophobic PE fibers naturally tend to agglomerate, but the optimized matrix plastic viscosity serves as a lubricating layer, preventing mechanical interlocking at clearance obstacles.
- **Omission of vibration compaction**: Omitting vibration prevented fiber settlement and preferential reorientation, establishing an isotropic 3D bridging network that yielded 5.5 % tensile ductility.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that electrosteric polymeric control enables simultaneous achievement of self-consolidation ($L = 1.0$) and 5.5 % tensile strain capacity in dense W/C = 0.30 PE-ECC.
