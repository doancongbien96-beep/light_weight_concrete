# Physical and Mechanical Characterization of 10 Global Lunar Soil Simulants (LSS) from Civil and Building Materials Perspectives: Particle Size Distribution, Direct Shear Coulomb Friction Angles ($\phi = 38\sim 51^\circ$), and Angles of Repose

## Summary

To support lunar outpost construction and large-scale in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) of concrete and composite matrices, this study comprehensively evaluates 10 leading global lunar soil simulants (**LSS**: 5 North American and 5 European products, including JSC-1A, LHS-1, LMS-1, DNA-1, EAC-1, TUBS-M, CAS-1, and others) from civil and building engineering viewpoints. The authors characterized **dry specific gravity, loose and 50 Hz vibrated bulk densities ($1.52\sim 2.05\ \text{g/cm}^3$), sieve/laser diffraction particle size distributions (median diameter $D_{50}$, uniformity coefficient $C_u$, curvature coefficient $C_c$), direct shear Coulomb internal friction angles ($\phi$) and cohesion ($c$), funnel drop angles of repose (AoR), and optical particle morphology**. Test results demonstrated broad variations depending on chemical mineralogy and crushing processes: **internal friction angles spanned $38.5^\circ \sim 51.2^\circ$, angles of repose ranged from $34.2^\circ \sim 48.5^\circ$, and specific gravities varied between $2.75\sim 3.12\ \text{g/cm}^3$**. Highlands simulant LHS-1 and mare simulant LMS-1 exhibited superior gradation ($C_u > 15$) and angular particle shapes, providing optimal mechanical interlocking for lunar concrete aggregates and 3D printing binders.

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive civil engineering database for 10 global lunar soil simulants (LSS)**:
   - Mapped 8 core physical and mechanical parameters (density, gradation, shear strength, angle of repose) under identical standardized testing protocols.
2. **Quantification of Coulomb shear parameters ($\phi = 38.5^\circ \sim 51.2^\circ$, $c = 0.5\sim 5.2\ \text{kPa}$) via direct shear testing**:
   - Formulated internal friction resistance and apparent cohesion as functions of crushed particle angularity and fine dust content.
3. **Vibratory compaction and packing density enhancement mechanisms ($1.52 \rightarrow 2.05\ \text{g/cm}^3$)**:
   - Demonstrated that continuous gradation simulants with high uniformity coefficients ($C_u$) achieve over 30 % density gains under 50 Hz vibration.
4. **Design guidelines for angles of repose ($34^\circ\sim 49^\circ$) in lunar earthworks**:
   - Standardized friction parameters for regolith embankments and excavation slope stability analyses under lunar low gravity ($1/6 g$).

## Methods

- **Evaluated lunar soil simulants (10 types, Table 1)**:
  - North American (5 types): JSC-1A (Orbitec/NASA), LHS-1 (Exolith Lunar Highlands), LMS-1 (Exolith Lunar Mare), DNA-1, OPRH2N.
  - European (5 types): EAC-1 (ESA European Astronaut Centre), TUBS-M (TU Braunschweig Mare), CAS-1, AGK-2019, ER-1.
- **Testing equipment and characterization protocols (Section 2 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Density: Helium pycnometry, loose bulk density, and 50 Hz vibratory table compaction density.
  - Gradation analysis: Sieve and laser diffraction ($D_{10}, D_{30}, D_{50}, D_{60}$, $C_u = D_{60}/D_{10}$, $C_c = D_{30}^2/(D_{10}D_{60})$).
  - Direct shear testing: ZAN AB-2a shear box (normal stresses $\sigma_n = 50, 100, 200\ \text{kPa}$, shear rate $0.05\ \text{mm/min}$, Coulomb envelope $\tau_f = \sigma_n \tan\phi + c$).
  - Angle of repose: 100 mm fixed-height funnel drop apparatus with digital optical angle measurement.
  - Micro-morphology: SMZ1500 stereomicroscope and high-resolution imaging (50~540 magnification).

### 2. Particle interlocking and geotechnical mechanics
- **High friction angle mechanics ($\phi > 48^\circ$)**: Unlike rounded terrestrial sands ($\phi \approx 30^\circ\sim 35^\circ$), the sharp, angular edges of high-energy crushed basaltic and anorthositic grains generate intense shear interlocking.
- **Concrete aggregate packing**: With $C_u > 15$, LHS-1 and LMS-1 provide dense packing across particle size ranges, minimizing matrix porosity and binder consumption.

- **Overall conclusion**: This 2023 comparative study established standard physical and mechanical baselines across 10 global lunar soil simulants for space civil engineering and lunar concrete construction.
