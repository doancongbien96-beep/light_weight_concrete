# Development of a High-Strength Lightweight Strain-Hardening Cementitious Composite (HFSLCC) Using Glass Bubbles and PVA-PE Hybrid Fibers, with Micromechanics-Based Optimal Design

## Summary

A landmark origin paper on lightweight strain-hardening composites from the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chungnam National University and Chonnam National University). To simultaneously achieve a dramatic reduction of structural self-weight together with high strength (45~60 MPa) and ultra-ductile tensile strain-hardening behavior (tensile strain 3.5~4.8 %), hollow glassy microspheres known as glass bubbles (Glass Bubble, density $0.6\ \text{g/cm}^3$, particle size $30\ \mu\text{m}$) and fine silica sand ($130\ \mu\text{m}$) were adopted as fillers to precisely control the matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$), and hydrophilic PVA fibers were combined with ultra-high-strength hydrophobic PE fibers (total $V_f = 1.5\sim 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) in a high-strength lightweight composite (HFSLCC: Hybrid Fiber-reinforced Semi-Lightweight Cementitious Composite). Its micromechanics bridging analysis ($\sigma-\delta$), three-point bend notched fracture toughness, single-fiber pull-out, 28-day compressive strength, and uniaxial direct tension tests were systematically carried out. The results showed that incorporating glass bubbles at 25~35 wt.% lowered the unit mass of the composite to $1580\sim 1690\ \text{kg/m}^3$, a **reduction of more than 30 % versus ordinary concrete**, while sharply reducing the crack-tip energy ($J_{tip}$) and satisfying the PSH toughness ratio ($R_T = J'_b/J_{tip} \ge 3.0$). Furthermore, the PVA(1.5 %) + PE(0.5 %) hybrid mix simultaneously demonstrated outstanding high-strength, lightweight, ultra-ductile performance: 28-day compressive strength **52.4 MPa**, uniaxial direct tensile strength **6.2 MPa**, and tensile elongation **4.2 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Unit mass reduction and optimal control of matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) using glass bubbles**:
   - Introducing hollow spherical particles with a density of $0.6\ \text{g/cm}^3$ lightened the unit mass to $1580\ \text{kg/m}^3$ while lowering the matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ from $0.31\ \text{MPa}\cdot\sqrt{\text{m}}$ to **$0.20\ \text{MPa}\cdot\sqrt{\text{m}}$**, maximizing satisfaction of the PSH energy criterion.
2. **Micromechanical quantification of the PVA-PE hybrid fiber bridging synergy**:
   - **Hydrophilic PVA fibers (12 mm)**: induce nucleation of numerous fine cracks on the initial crack plane, maximizing crack dispersion.
   - **Hydrophobic PE fibers (15 mm, tensile strength 2500 MPa)**: restrain crack opening on the major crack plane through their high elastic modulus (75 GPa) and frictional slip resistance, amplifying the complementary energy ($J'_b$).
3. **Demonstration of the mechanical performance of the high-strength lightweight strain-hardening composite (HFSLCC)**:
   - While maintaining structural-grade high strength at a 28-day compressive strength of **45 ~ 58 MPa**, it completely overcomes the brittle failure of ordinary lightweight concrete, realizing a uniaxial tensile elongation of **3.5 ~ 4.8 %** and saturated fine multiple cracking.

## Methods

- **Raw material and filler properties (Table 1, 2, 3)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (Type I) + ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (Slag/Cement = 0.25).
  - Micro silica sand: mean particle size $130\ \mu\text{m}$, density $2.65\ \text{g/cm}^3$ (Sand/Cement = 0.80).
  - Glass bubble (GB): density $0.60\ \text{g/cm}^3$, mean particle size $30\ \mu\text{m}$, substituted at 15 %, 25 %, and 35 % of cement mass.
  - Mix proportions: W/C = 0.45 and 0.55, polycarboxylate SP 0.5~3.0 %, thickener HPMC 0.2 wt.%.
  - Reinforcing fibers (Table 3 & 4):
    - PVA fiber: diameter $40\ \mu\text{m}$, length 12 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elastic modulus 39 GPa, density $1.30\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
    - PE fiber: diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, tensile strength 2500 MPa, elastic modulus 75 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
    - Hybrid combinations (total $V_f = 1.5\sim 2.0\ \%$): Mix 1 (PVA 1.0 + PE 0.5), Mix 2 (PVA 1.0 + PE 1.0), Mix 3 (PVA 1.5 + PE 0.5), Mix 4 (PVA 1.75 + PE 0.25).
- **Test equipment and test methods**:
  - Matrix fracture toughness: three-point bending fracture test on center-notched prismatic specimens of $40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 160\ \text{mm}$ ($K_m$).
  - Single-fiber pull-out test: micro pull-out jig and precision 10 N load cell ($0.2\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Uniaxial direct tension test: dogbone specimens preventing end failure ($30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm} \times 80\ \text{mm}$ gauge section, 10 kN UTM, strain measurement with two LVDTs).
  - Unit mass (KS F 2409) and 28-day compressive strength (KS F 2403, $\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$).

### 2. Micromechanics design and fracture behavior analysis
- **Toughness ratio and multiple-cracking saturation**: thanks to low $K_m$ values ($0.20\sim 0.26\ \text{MPa}\cdot\sqrt{\text{m}}$), the GB 25~35 % mixes comfortably exceed the $R_T > 3.0$ and $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} > 2.0$ criteria, producing tensile elongation above 4 % and dense multiple cracking.
- **Practicality as a lightweight structural material**: securing compressive strength above 45 MPa at a unit mass of $1580\ \text{kg/m}^3$ delivers optimal performance for building curtain walls, slabs, and seismic retrofit panels.

- **Overall conclusion**: the micromechanical combination of hollow glass bubbles and PVA-PE hybrid fibers was proven to be an innovative lightweight, high-toughness composite design technology that reduces concrete self-weight by more than 30 % while simultaneously achieving 50 MPa-class high strength and ultra-high-ductility tensile strain-hardening above 4 %.
