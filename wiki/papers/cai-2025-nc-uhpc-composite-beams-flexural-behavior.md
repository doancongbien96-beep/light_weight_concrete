# Flexural behavior of NC-UHPC composite beams with embedded thin UHPC layers: +55 % flexural capacity, +92 % cracking load, and an anti-debonding theoretical model

## Summary

To economically overcome the cracking vulnerability of hogging-moment zones over continuous-
bridge supports while minimizing expensive UHPC usage, Prof. Zhi-Qi He's and Dr. Tianyu Cai's
team (Southeast University, China) designed NC-UHPC composite beams ($2400 \times 150 \times
300\ \text{mm}$) with a strategically embedded ultra-thin steel-fiber UHPC layer (thickness
$30$, $50\ \text{mm}$; 2.0 vol.% straight fibers; compressive strength $130.0\ \text{MPa}$)
cast integrally only within the tensile stress concentration zone of ordinary concrete members
(NC: $38.5\ \text{MPa}$). Static four-point bending tests, full-field DIC strain measurement,
interfacial slip monitoring, and two-dimensional interface stress modeling demonstrated a
first-cracking load $92\%$ higher, ultimate flexural capacity $55\%$ higher, service-load crack
widths tightly held at $\le 0.06\ \text{mm}$, and fully composite action without interface
debonding versus the plain NC reference beam — a 2025 landmark in Case Studies in Construction
Materials.

## Key Contributions

1. **First proposal of the embedded-thin-UHPC-layer strengthening concept**: to overcome the
   environmental-exposure debonding defects of external overlay methods, the UHPC layer is cast
   integrally inside the rebar cover, maximizing interface confinement.
2. **75 % less UHPC with 55 % capacity amplification simultaneously**: UHPC is placed only in
   a layer comprising 15 % of the beam section — economics and mechanics maximized together.
3. **Service-load crack width strictly held ≤ 0.06 mm**: UHPC fibers densely bridge tensile
   cracks around the main rebars, blocking harmful crack growth at its source.
4. **Interface shear analysis and anti-debonding design equations established**: an interfacial
   shear failure criterion combining cohesion ($c$) and friction angle ($\phi$) was derived,
   matching experiments with $R^2 = 0.98$.

## Methods

- **Specimen design and mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Beam dimensions: $2400 \times 150 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ (span $2000\ \text{mm}$; main
    rebar 2-D18, compression rebar 2-D12).
  - Series (six full-scale beams total):
    1. `NC-Ref`: plain NC reference beam.
    2. `NC-UHPC-30`: 30 mm embedded UHPC layer.
    3. `NC-UHPC-50`: 50 mm embedded UHPC layer.
    4. `NC-UHPC-50-R`: 50 mm UHPC layer with through shear reinforcement.
  - UHPC mix: W/B = 0.18, 2.0 vol.% straight fiber ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.2\
    \text{mm}$).
- **Flexural test program (Sections 2.3~2.5 & Figs. 5~8)**:
  - Four-point loading (displacement control 0.5 mm/min).
  - Instrumentation: 2D-DIC full-section strains, five LVDT deflectometers, interface-slip
    transducers.

### 2. Interface stress redistribution and crack-confinement mechanisms
- **Tension-band effect of the embedded layer**: lower tensile stress redistributes broadly
  across the whole UHPC layer, opening fine multiple cracks instead of one large crack.
- **Confining pressure from surrounding NC**: the embedded layer is triaxially confined by the
  NC matrix, suppressing interlayer shear slip ($<0.05\ \text{mm}$) and preventing premature
  debonding outright.

- **Overall conclusion**: this top-tier landmark paper proves that embedded ultra-thin UHPC
  layers deliver 55 % capacity amplification and 92 % higher first-cracking loads with minimal
  UHPC usage and fully composite interfaces, complete with theory.
