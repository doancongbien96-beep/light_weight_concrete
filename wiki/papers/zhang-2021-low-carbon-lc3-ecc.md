## Summary
A study by Professor Victor Li's research team at the University of Michigan clarifying the micromechanics PSH design, single PVA fiber interfacial friction characteristics, direct tensile strain-hardening envelopes, and full life-cycle carbon assessment (LCA) of a low-carbon ductile composite (LC3-ECC) applying limestone calcined clay cement entirely as binder (LC3: clinker 50 % + calcined clay 30 % + ground limestone 15 % + gypsum 5 %), in order to realize sustainable low-carbon construction infrastructure. Based on the low fracture toughness of the LC3 matrix ($K_m = 0.35 MPa\sqrt{m}$), a 28 d compressive strength of **52.0 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **5.8 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **5.5 %** were achieved while cutting cement clinker consumption and carbon emissions by **45 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Micromechanics of the eco-friendly ductile composite based on LC3 binder completed**: high-ductility ECC realized with clay and limestone even in regions short of fly ash/slag supply.
- **PSH energy index $J_b'/J_{tip} = 6.8$ secured**: 28 d direct tensile strain capacity of 5.5 % with mean crack widths controlled to at most $25 \mu m$.
- **Chloride diffusion blocked through carboaluminate hydrate formation**: chloride diffusion coefficient cut by 65 %, doubling durability.
- **LCA carbon footprint reduction of 45 % demonstrated**: global carbon-neutral construction standard established.

## Methods
- Binder: LC3 composite binder (clinker 50 %, metakaolin calcined clay 30 %, limestone powder 15 %, gypsum 5 %), $W/B = 0.28$.
- Fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm).
- Tests: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), 28 d compressive strength, XRD crystalline phase analysis, SimaPro 9.0 LCA analysis.
