# Theoretical and Experimental Analysis of the Flexural Behaviour of Prestressed NC-UHPC Composite Beams: $85\%$ Amplification of Ultimate Flexural Capacity, Suppression of Crack Width to $0.04\ \text{mm}$, and Sectional Safety-Factor Design

## Summary

To radically reduce the sectional self-weight of long-span bridge girders and fundamentally block crack opening in the lower tension zone, **large-scale prestressed composite beams ($3200 \times 150 \times 300\ \text{mm}$, clear span 2900 mm) were fabricated by placing normal concrete (NC: compressive strength $45.0\ \text{MPa}$) in the upper compression zone and casting a steel-fiber-reinforced prestressed ultra-high-performance concrete layer (PS-UHPC: tensile strength $12.5\ \text{MPa}$, compressive strength $140.0\ \text{MPa}$) monolithically in the lower zone where ultimate tensile and flexural tensile stresses concentrate**, and four-point flexural load testing together with a nonlinear strain-integration sectional theory model and precise analysis of interfacial shear behavior demonstrated **a first flexural cracking load $140\%$ higher than an ordinary RC beam of the same section ($55.0\ \text{kN} \rightarrow 132.0\ \text{kN}$), an ultimate flexural capacity amplified $85\%$ ($165.0\ \text{kN} \rightarrow 305.0\ \text{kN}$), service-load crack widths confined ultra-tightly below $0.04\ \text{mm}$, and a $40\%$ reduction in steel cost** — a landmark original paper in MDPI Materials 2023 by the research team of **Prof. P. Lin of Harbin Institute of Technology, China, and Prof. J. Ma of Southeast University**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a sectional mechanics theory and nonlinear analysis model for prestressed NC-UHPC composite girders**:
   - Developed an algorithm that predicts the moment–curvature ($M-\phi$) relationship with $98\%$ accuracy by introducing a two-stage constitutive equation covering UHPC tensile strain hardening and strain softening.
2. **Elucidation of the dual crack-confinement synergy between prestressing and UHPC steel-fiber bridging**:
   - The residual compressive stress field generated by tendon jacking force, combined with the three-dimensional pullout bridging force of micro steel fibers, delayed first flexural cracking up to the $43\%$ level of the ultimate load.
3. **Demonstration of complete shear integrity at the cast interface between dissimilar NC-UHPC materials**:
   - Even without artificial shear studs, wet-contact casting alone secured an interfacial shear bond strength of $3.8\ \text{MPa}$, preventing interfacial delamination $100\%$ up to ultimate flexural failure.
4. **Presentation of an economic ultra-lightweight design standard for large highway and railway bridge girders**:
   - By locally placing expensive UHPC only in the lower 1/3 of the tension section, material cost was halved while realizing flexural performance on par with a full-section UHPC girder.

## Methods

- **Specimen design and mix proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Composite beam dimensions: $3200 \times 150 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ (upper NC thickness 180 mm, lower UHPC thickness 120 mm).
  - UHPC mix: cement + silica fume + fly ash ($W/B = 0.18$, brass-coated micro steel fiber $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$).
  - Prestressing: two 7-wire strands with an outer diameter of $15.2\ \text{mm}$ (initial jacking force $0.7 f_{ptk} = 186\ \text{kN}$).
- **Flexural load test and instrumentation (Sections 2.2~3.2 & Figs. 6~10)**:
  - Four-point flexural loading: pure flexural span 800 mm, shear span 1050 mm (1000 kN loading frame, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Instrumentation: LVDT deflection transducers, electrical-resistance strain gauges over the section height, and optical-microscope crack-width measurement.

### 2. Analysis of neutral-axis migration and lower-tension contribution
- **Suppression of neutral-axis rise**: Because the lower UHPC layer continued to sustain a tensile stress of $8.5\ \text{MPa}$ even after yielding of the main reinforcement, it prevented the abrupt rise of the sectional neutral axis and thereby avoided premature crushing of the top NC.
- **Zero interfacial shear slip**: Owing to the fine silica-sand interlock across the joint plane and the vertical confinement provided by the tendons, relative interfacial slip remained perfectly controlled at or below $0.02\ \text{mm}$ even at the ultimate load ($305\ \text{kN}$).

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-tier landmark original work in high-performance hybrid bridge structural engineering, in which the Harbin Institute of Technology and Southeast University teams elucidated the flexural behaviour of prestressed NC-UHPC composite girders theoretically and experimentally, demonstrating an 85% amplification of flexural capacity, confinement of crack width to 0.04 mm, and delamination-free interfacial integrity.
