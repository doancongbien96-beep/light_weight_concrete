## Summary
This is the most authoritative review paper by Professor F.U.A. Shaikh of Curtin University, Australia, which comprehensively compared and analyzed the compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, flexural deflection-hardening behavior, fracture toughness, and elevated-temperature/fire durability of short-fibre reinforced geopolymer composites (Short Fibre Reinforced Geopolymer Composites, FRC/EGC), obtained by reinforcing short fibers (Short Fibers: steel fiber, PVA fiber, carbon fiber, polypropylene fiber, basalt fiber) into fly ash and slag based alkali-activated geopolymer matrices. It systematized the interfacial chemical bond characteristics by fiber type and the micromechanical conditions for exhibiting PSH strain-hardening in the alkaline matrix environment of geopolymers.

## Key Contributions
- **Comprehensive comparison of geopolymer reinforcement efficiency by fiber type (steel, PVA, carbon, PP, basalt)**: steel fibers are excellent for improving compressive/flexural strength, while PVA/PE fibers are optimal for achieving deflection-hardening ductility ($\epsilon_u > 3.0 \%$).
- **Identification of the interfacial bonding mechanism of PVA fibers in an alkaline environment**: since the high alkali concentration of geopolymers increases the chemical bonding ($G_d$) of PVA fibers and can thereby induce fiber rupture, oil coating and matrix optimization are essential.
- **Consolidation of the elevated-temperature fire resistance ($800 °C$) of geopolymer composites**: unlike Portland cement, there is no $Ca(OH)_2$ decomposition or spalling, so more than 60 % of the residual strength is maintained even after exposure to $800 °C$.
- **Establishment of mix design guidelines for eco-friendly low-carbon high-ductility construction materials**: achieving a reduction in $CO_2$ emissions of 80 %.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of a database of more than 120 global research publications.
- Analysis of variables: binders (fly ash, metakaolin, slag), activators ($NaOH, Na_2SiO_3$), fibers ($V_f = 0.5 \sim 2.5 \%$).

## Results
- Comparison envelope of the mechanical performance of geopolymer composites by fiber type:

| Fiber type | Volume fraction ($V_f$) | 28 d compressive strength (MPa) | Modulus of rupture MOR (MPa) | Tensile behavior characteristics | $800 °C$ high-temperature resistance |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| Unreinforced geopolymer | 0.0 % | 45.0 | 4.2 | Completely brittle failure | Residual strength 55 %, no spalling |
| Steel fiber (Steel) | 1.0 ~ 2.0 % | 65.0 ~ 85.0 | 12.0 ~ 22.0 | Increased strain-softening toughness | Strength degradation due to oxidation |
| PVA fiber | 1.5 ~ 2.0 % | 40.0 ~ 55.0 | 8.5 ~ 14.5 | Strain hardening (ductility 3~5 %) | Fiber melting at $200 °C$ and above |
| Carbon fiber (Carbon) | 0.5 ~ 1.5 % | 50.0 ~ 68.0 | 10.0 ~ 16.0 | High-strength elastic behavior | Stable up to $600 °C$ |
