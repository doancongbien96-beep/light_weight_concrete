# Effects of Steel Fiber and Microfiber (Basalt, PVA, PE) Hybridization on the Direct Tensile Behavior of 150 MPa Ultra-High-Performance Concrete (UHPC)

## Summary

To overcome the limited tensile strain capacity ($\varepsilon_{tu} < 0.7\%$) and maximize fiber reinforcement efficiency in 150 MPa-grade ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC, W/B = 0.20, incorporating zirconia silica fume ZSF), Professor Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University and Professor Su-Tae Kang at Daegu University designed four hybrid formulations combining macro straight steel fibers ($d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}, L_f = 19.5\ \text{mm}$) with three distinct microfibers—**basalt ($d_f = 17\ \mu\text{m}$), polyvinyl alcohol (PVA, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$), and ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (PE, $d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$)** at $1.0\ \text{vol.\%}$ steel + $0.5\ \text{vol.\%}$ microfiber (total $1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$). The authors evaluated 28 d compressive strength, hardened density, JSCE dogbone uniaxial direct tension (displacement rate 0.1 mm/min, gauge length 80 mm), crack patterns, and the **Tensile Performance Efficiency Index**. Compared to the 1.5 vol.% mono-steel mix (S1.5), the **S1.0-PE0.5 mix maintained a compressive strength of 148.5 MPa while simultaneously increasing ultimate tensile strength by 13 % to 11.1 MPa and expanding ultimate tensile strain capacity by 39 % to 0.99 %**, demonstrating positive multiscale synergy with saturated multiple microcracking. In contrast, basalt fibers (S1.0-B0.5) suffered brittle rupture, dropping tensile strain capacity by 68 %, while PVA fibers experienced interfacial damage that reduced tensile strength by 17 %, establishing that only high-strength compliant PE microfibers yield effective multiscale bridging synergy in 150 MPa matrix environments.

## Key Contributions

1. **39 % increase in UHPC tensile strain capacity ($0.71\% \rightarrow 0.99\%$) via steel-PE microfiber hybridization**:
   - Combining 1.0 vol.% steel with 0.5 vol.% PE microfibers achieved 11.1 MPa tensile strength and ~1.0 % strain capacity, overcoming the ductility limits of mono-steel reinforcement.
2. **Mechanistic differentiation among basalt, PVA, and PE microfibers in hybrid UHPC**:
   - Demonstrated distinct failure modes: basalt (brittle rupture $\rightarrow$ 68 % ductility reduction), PVA (excessive chemical bonding $\rightarrow$ strength loss), and PE (slip-hardening pullout $\rightarrow$ multiple cracking with 39 % ductility gain).
3. **Formulation of compressive-strength-normalized Tensile Performance Efficiency Index**:
   - Introduced an efficiency index deducting compressive strength variations to mathematically prove that only steel-PE hybridization exhibits positive synergy across all tensile metrics.
4. **Establishment of a 2-stage multiscale bridging mechanism**:
   - PE microfibers dispersed initial microcracking stresses, while macro-steel fibers arrested macrocrack opening during subsequent localized deformation.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–4, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: Type I Portland cement (OPC) 1.0, zirconia silica fume (ZSF, specific surface area $8.0\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{SiO}_2 > 94\%$) 0.25.
  - Filler: Siliceous filler (mean particle size $2.2\ \mu\text{m}$, $\text{SiO}_2 > 99\%$) 0.30.
  - Fine aggregates: Silica sand ($d_{max} \le 0.5\ \text{mm}$) 1.10.
  - Water-to-binder ratio: W/B = 0.20 (polycarboxylate superplasticizer 1.6 wt.% by binder).
  - Fiber combinations (total $1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    1. S1.5 (Control): Straight steel fibers ($d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}, L_f = 19.5\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 2500 MPa) 1.5 vol.%.
    2. S1.0-B0.5: Steel 1.0 vol.% + basalt fibers ($d_f = 17\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 15\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 1950 MPa) 0.5 vol.%.
    3. S1.0-PVA0.5: Steel 1.0 vol.% + PVA fibers ($d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa) 0.5 vol.%.
    4. S1.0-PE0.5: Steel 1.0 vol.% + PE fibers ($d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 18\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 3000 MPa) 0.5 vol.%.
- **Testing protocols (Figure 1)**:
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes, 28 d water curing at $23\pm 3\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Direct tension: JSCE dogbone specimens ($330\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, 80 mm gauge length, 0.1 mm/min, dual LVDTs).
  - Crack analysis: Optical microscopy tracking of crack widths and counts.

### 2. Multiscale fracture mechanics and bridging synergy
- **Slip-hardening bridging of PE microfibers**: High-strength (3000 MPa), flexible PE microfibers pulled out progressively without rupture, sustaining bridging stress and promoting saturated multiple cracking.
- **Limitations of basalt microfibers**: While basalt fibers elevated first crack strength to 8.20 MPa, they ruptured abruptly upon crack opening due to localized shear stress concentrations, drastically degrading tensile ductility.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that steel-PE microfiber hybridization maximizes both tensile strength (11.1 MPa) and strain capacity (0.99 %) in 150 MPa UHPC through multiscale bridging synergy.
