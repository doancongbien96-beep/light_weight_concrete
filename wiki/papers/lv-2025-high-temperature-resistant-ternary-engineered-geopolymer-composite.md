# Microstructural Phase-Transition Mechanisms of a Blast-Furnace Slag–Fly Ash–Silica Fume Ternary High-Temperature-Resistant Engineered Geopolymer Composite (Ternary EGC) under Ultra-High-Temperature Exposure ($200\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and Its $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Ceramic-Sintering Strength Development ($65.0\ \text{MPa}$)

## Summary

To overcome the limitations conventional ordinary Portland cement (OPC)-based concrete and ECC face in fire — calcium hydroxide dehydration collapse above $600\ ^\circ\text{C}$, explosive spalling (Explosive Spalling) driven by water-vapor pressure buildup within dense microstructures, and abrupt residual-strength drops of more than $85\%$ — this latest landmark original paper (Construction and Building Materials, 2025) from the research teams of Prof. Lili Kan (corresponding author) of the University of Shanghai for Science and Technology (USST) and Prof. Zhigang Zhang of Chongqing University develops a **ternary high-temperature-resistant geopolymer composite (Ternary EGC) based on ternary precursors of blast-furnace slag (GGBS: $40\%$), low-calcium fly ash (FA: $40\%$), and silica fume (SF: $20\%$) under optimal alkali activation (Liquid Sodium Silicate + NaOH) and reinforced with micro PVA fibers ($2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) (ambient compressive strength $58.5\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strain capacity $4.20\%$)**, and through heating-exposure tests from room temperature up to $200, 400, 600, 800, 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ together with quantitative XRD mineral crystalline-phase analysis, TG-DTG thermogravimetric analysis, and SEM-EDS microstructural and residual mechanical property evaluation, it demonstrates **zero explosive spalling ($0.0$) even under extreme $1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heating, and attainment of a residual compressive strength of $65.0\ \text{MPa}$ (a reversed $111\%$ increase over the ambient value) via the sintering (Sintering) transition of the amorphous geopolymer gel into gehlenite ($\text{Ca}_2\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_7$) and nepheline ceramic crystalline phases in the $800\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ range**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first elucidation of the ultra-high-temperature ($1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) refractory phase-transition mechanism of GGBS-FA-SF ternary EGC**:
   - Proves that the C-(A)-S-H and N-A-S-H gels that dominate strength at ambient temperature sinter-transform into dense refractory crystalline ceramic minerals above $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Astonishing reversal rise of residual compressive strength to $65.0\ \text{MPa}$ after $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heating ($111\%$ versus ambient)**:
   - Whereas OPC-ECC completely crumbles at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the ternary EGC realizes a sinter-strengthening effect in which its strength actually becomes harder than its initial value at high temperature.
3. **Source-level prevention of $100\%$ of explosive spalling (Spalling) via vaporization of PVA fibers**:
   - The micro-fiber channels melted and vaporized at $300\sim 400\ ^\circ\text{C}$ safely release internal high-pressure water vapor, completely blocking expansion-induced rupture.
4. **Establishment of standards for next-generation tunnel linings, nuclear containment buildings, and ultra-high-temperature industrial furnace refractories**:
   - Completes design guidelines for future inorganic composite materials that can be reused immediately without structural collapse even after fire events.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Precursors: GGBS 40 wt.% + Class F fly ash 40 wt.% + silica fume 20 wt.%.
  - Activator: liquid sodium silicate (waterglass) solution ($M_s = \text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 1.3$, $W/B = 0.38$).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$).
  - Comparison group: single-GGBS EGC, single-FA EGC, standard OPC-ECC.
- **High-temperature exposure and analysis program (Sections 2.2~3.4 & Figs. 4~8)**:
  - Heating: electric-furnace heating rate of $10\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$, held 2 h at the target temperature ($200\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) followed by slow cooling.
  - Evaluation: residual compressive strength, elastic modulus, high-resolution quantitative XRD analysis, SEM-EDS fracture-surface observation.

### 2. Ceramic sintering (Ceramic Sintering) and thermal-stress mitigation analysis
- **High-temperature melting–recrystallization of the Si-Al-Ca ternary system**: at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ the silica fume and slag fine particles form an amorphous glassy liquid phase that fusion-fills and merges the fine pores, reborn as a crystalline ceramic matrix.
- **Integrity of the vapor-release channels**: the micro tunnels of diameter $39\ \mu\text{m}$ left behind by the fibers at $400\ ^\circ\text{C}$ are uniformly dispersed, so the internal vapor pressure stays at or below $0.2\ \text{MPa}$ and explosive spalling is prevented $100\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper, in which Prof. Lili Kan's USST research team optimized GGBS-FA-SF ternary precursors and demonstrated an ultra-high-temperature-resistant EGC that reversely develops a residual compressive strength of 65.0 MPa through ceramic sintering even after exposure to 800 °C ultra-high temperatures, is the foremost landmark original paper of the emerging extreme-disaster-resilient ceramic composite materials engineering field.
