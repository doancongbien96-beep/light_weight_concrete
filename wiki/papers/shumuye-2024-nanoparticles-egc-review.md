# Review of Nanoparticles in EGC/GC

## Summary

A review comprehensively surveying research trends in adding nanoparticles (nano-ZnO, nano-SiO2, nano-TiO2, nano-Al2O3, nano-CaCO3, carbon black, TiO2-graphene, etc.) to EGC (Engineered Geopolymer Composite) and GC (geopolymer composite). It combines nanotechnology with geopolymer as a sustainable cement-substituting binder and evaluates mechanical properties, durability, workability, 3D-printing applicability, carbon emissions, and cost. Based on quantitative Scopus literature analysis (VOSviewer).

## Key Contributions

1. Optimal dosage by nanoparticle type organized: nano-SiO2 roughly 0.5–2 % (varies by study), nano-CaCO3 1–2 wt % on a compressive-strength basis, nano-TiO2 about 5 % (with reports up to 10 %, though agglomeration is an issue), TiO2-graphene 0.03 wt % (optimal for chloride-ion penetration reduction).
2. Elucidation of the adverse-effect mechanism of overdosing: excess nanoparticles form weak zones through agglomeration and reduce hydration products through the dilution effect, lowering strength.
3. Workability debate organized: nano-SiO2 generally lowers fluidity through its increased surface area, yet coexist conflicting reports that it improves workability instead through increased packing density. Some observations even find that the strength optimum and the workability optimum coincide.
4. NaOH molarity effect: NaOH molarity directly affects compressive strength more than the alkalinity ratio (binder ratio), with a maximum at 12 M (Zahid et al.).
5. Related to EGC tensile behavior: when nano-SiO2 2 % is added to high-zeolite-content EGC, the fiber–matrix interaction improves so the strain corresponding to peak stress in the hardening region increases, yet the ultimate tensile strain decreases (Razavi et al.) — evidence of the conflicting effects of nano-modification on ductility.
6. Durability: TiO2-graphene-doped AAS mortar shows a 43–67 % reduction in chloride-ion penetration depth. Under magnesium sulfate and seawater exposure, nano-SiO2-added geopolymer shows superior surface stability versus OPC.
7. LCA-based carbon-emission and cost comparison table (raw-material unit data such as PVA 12,670 $/ton, PP 6,335 $/ton).

## Methods

Literature review. Scopus search + VOSviewer 1.6.20 quantitative analysis. Main sub-analyses: physical properties (setting time, workability), mechanical properties (compressive, flexural, toughness), microstructure (SEM, XRD, FTIR), chemical exposure, 3D printing, environmental impact.

## Results

- Core consensus: small doses of nanoparticles improve strength and densification through the nucleation effect and nano-filling. In excess, agglomeration and dilution reverse the effect.
- FTIR/XRD: nano-CaCO3 addition promotes C-S-H formation; formation of C-S-H alongside N-A-S-H confirmed.
- Ultrasonic dispersion is effective at reducing nanoparticle agglomeration.
- Explicitly noted: nanoparticle research on EGC itself remains limited compared with GC and cementitious systems.
