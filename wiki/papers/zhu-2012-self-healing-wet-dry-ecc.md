## Summary
A study precisely evaluating the self-healing behavior and the recovery rates of dynamic elastic modulus (resonant frequency nondestructive testing) and direct tensile mechanics of ductile composites (PVA-ECC) with prior tensile strain damage ($\epsilon = 1.0 \sim 3.0 \%$, crack widths $w < 60 \mu m$) under wet-dry cycling conditions simulating real environments. Atmospheric $CO_2$ uptake is maximized during drying stages while $Ca^{2+}$ leaching hydration reactions continue during wetting stages; more than 95 % of fine cracks of at most $50 \mu m$ were completely sealed by calcium carbonate and more than 90 % of the initial dynamic elastic modulus based on resonant frequency was restored.

## Key Contributions
- **Self-healing quantified through resonant frequency (RF) nondestructive testing**: RF recovery index established based on ASTM C215 ($RF_{rec} = (RF_t - RF_{dam})/(RF_0 - RF_{dam})$).
- **Autogenous healing kinetics under wet-dry cycles clarified**: 85 % of resonant frequency recovered within 14 d and 92 % within 28 d.
- **Retensile strain-hardening saturation verified**: complete healing confirmed by new cracks forming at fresh locations rather than at healed pre-existing cracks upon re-tensioning.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash replacement ratio 60 %).
- **Healing conditions**: wet-dry cycles ($20 °C$, $12 h$ water immersion + $12 h$ exposure at $50 \% RH$, up to 28 d).
- **Evaluation**: continuous resonant frequency measurement, optical microscopy crack closure rate measurement, uniaxial dogbone re-tension tests.

## Results
- After 28 d wet-dry healing: mean crack closure rate $97.2 \%$ and dynamic elastic modulus recovery rate $91.5 \%$.
- Re-tension tests exhibited a tensile strain capacity of $3.5 \%$ with perfect reproduction of secondary strain hardening.
