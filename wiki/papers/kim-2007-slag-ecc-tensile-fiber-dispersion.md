# Tensile Strain-Hardening and Fluorescence Image-Based Fiber Dispersion Performance of Slag-ECC Incorporating Ground Granulated Blast-Furnace Slag (GGBFS)

## Summary

To maximize tensile strain capacity while securing moderate compressive strength ($40\sim 55\ \text{MPa}$) in strain-hardening engineered cementitious composites (ECC), Prof. Jin-Keun Kim (KAIST), Prof. Yun Yong Kim (Chungnam National University), and Prof. Gi-Jol Ha (Kyungil University) developed **Slag-ECC** incorporating **25 % ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBFS, mean particle size $11 \pm 3\ \mu\text{m}$)** by cement mass. The authors systematically evaluated (1) chemical debonding energy ($G_d$) and frictional interfacial shear stress ($\tau_0$) via **single fiber pull-out tests**, (2) matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$, $J_{tip}$) via **three-point notched beam bending**, (3) strain-hardening behavior and tensile strain capacity via **direct tensile tests**, and (4) PVA fiber dispersion uniformity via **fluorescence microscopy image analysis**. The study micromechanically clarified that the glassy particle dispersion effect of GGBFS enhances paste rheology, promoting uniform spatial distribution of PVA fibers. **Slag-ECC (W/C = 48 %, 25 % slag)** more than doubled the tensile strain capacity from **$1.5\% \rightarrow 3.5\%$** compared to the reference ECC (W/C = 48 %, no slag), while the fiber dispersion coefficient ($\alpha$) increased by $29\%$ from **0.48 $\rightarrow$ 0.62**, quantitatively confirming that improved fiber dispersion is the driving mechanism for enhanced tensile ductility.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of optimal micromechanical mix design space for Slag-ECC ($W/C = 48\sim 60\%$, $S/C = 80\%$, 25 % slag)**:
   - Determined the optimal W/C window satisfying both the energy ratio criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip}$) and the stress ratio criterion ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{cr}$) through systematic 3-stage micromechanical testing.
2. **First standardized quantitative methodology for PVA fiber dispersion via fluorescence microscopy**:
   - Implemented GFP-filtered fluorescence imaging under mercury-lamp excitation, binarization, and statistical variation coefficient ($\phi(x)$) on $n \times n$ grids to define the dispersion coefficient ($\alpha = 1 - \phi(x)$).
3. **Clarification of the glassy particle dispersion mechanism of slag**:
   - Showed that GGBFS glassy particles adsorb superplasticizer molecules to form electrical double layers, enhancing paste fluidity and enabling uniform spatial distribution of PVA fibers.
4. **Decoupling of fiber dispersion benefits from matrix toughness changes in doubling tensile ductility ($1.5\% \rightarrow 3.5\%$)**:
   - Demonstrated that although slag increases matrix toughness ($J_{tip}$)—which theoretically reduces the margin for multiple cracking—the significant enhancement in fiber dispersion overrides this drawback to deliver net ductility gains.

## Methods

- **ECC mixture proportions (Table 1)**:
  - Binders: Type I OPC (mean particle size $11.7 \pm 4.8\ \mu\text{m}$), GGBFS (mean size $11 \pm 3\ \mu\text{m}$, 25 % cement replacement).
  - Sand: Silica sand (mean size $130 \pm 38\ \mu\text{m}$), S/C = 80 %.
  - Water-to-cement ratio: W/C = 28 %, 35 %, 48 %, 60 % (4 levels), W/B = W/(C + Slag).
  - PVA fibers: Kuraray REC15, diameter $39\ \mu\text{m}$, length $12\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength $1600\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 42.8\ \text{GPa}$, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$.
  - Chemical admixtures: High-range water reducer (HRW), HPMC viscosity agent.
- **Single fiber pull-out tests (Fig. 2 & Table 2)**:
  - Embedded length $l_e = 1.0\ \text{mm}$, pull-out speed $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$.
  - Calculated $G_d$ (chemical debonding energy) and $\tau_0$ (frictional bond strength).
- **Matrix fracture toughness tests (Fig. 3 & Table 3)**:
  - Three-point bending on single-edge notched beams (SENB) to determine $K_m$ and $J_{tip} = K_m^2/E_m$.
- **Direct tension tests (Fig. 4)**:
  - Dogbone specimens ($13\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, $80\ \text{mm}$ gauge length), displacement control at $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$.
- **Fluorescence fiber dispersion tests (Figs. 5, 12)**:
  - Olympus BX51 fluorescence microscope with GFP filter and CCD camera (4$\times$ magnification).
  - Cut and polished sections ($20\ \text{mm} \times 36\ \text{mm}$) adjacent to the primary crack.

### 2. Slag dispersion mechanics and micromechanical interpretation
- **Dual role of slag**: (1) Glassy slag particles adsorb superplasticizer to improve paste fluidity and disperse PVA fibers uniformly ($\alpha$ increased by 29 %), (2) pozzolanic hydration densifies the matrix, slightly elevating $J_{tip}$.
- **Dominance of fiber dispersion**: The substantial increase in crack-bridging efficiency resulting from enhanced fiber dispersion far outweighed the minor increase in matrix toughness, resulting in a net 2-fold increase in tensile strain capacity.
- **Workability threshold at low W/C**: At W/C = 28 % and 35 %, high paste viscosity hindered uniform fiber dispersion in standard mixers, sharply raising $J_{tip}$ and compromising strain-hardening.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that substituting 25 % GGBFS in ECC significantly improves PVA fiber dispersion uniformity and doubles tensile strain capacity to 3.5–4.0 %.
