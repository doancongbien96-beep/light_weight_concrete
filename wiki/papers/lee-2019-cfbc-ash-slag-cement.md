# High-Volume Slag Cement Using CFBC Bottom Ash as an Activator

## Summary

Paper by the user's group (co-authored by B.Y. Lee of Chonnam National University; corresponding author H.-K. Kim of Chosun University). This paper demonstrates that CFBC (circulating fluidized bed combustion) bottom ash, which contains free lime, portlandite, and anhydrite, can act as a secondary activator for slag hydration. Substituting it at 20 wt % into high-volume slag cement (HVSC) yields reduced autogenous shrinkage plus control of unwanted expansion, with strength variation held within ±15 % — practically acceptable.

## Key Contributions

1. **Secondary-activator function of CFBC ash demonstrated**: free lime, portlandite, and anhydrite dissolve in water, giving a slight increase in early heat evolution together with an enhanced second peak (accelerated secondary hydration of slag).
2. **Opposite expansion behavior in OPC vs HVSC**: OPC mixes undergo rapid expansion beyond 3000 µm/m at 91 days through ettringite formation under sufficient moisture with CFBC ash — HVSC mixes exhibit only minor swelling.
3. **20 wt % CFBC ash substitution in HVSC**: achieves autogenous-shrinkage reduction and expansion control simultaneously. Strength variation ±15 % (negligible given the standard deviation).
4. **Dual recycling of industrial by-products**: presents a circular-resource utilization model in which CFBC ash complements HVSC performance without any separate activator.

## Methods

- Materials: GGBS+OPC controls, with CFBC bottom ash (containing free lime, portlandite, anhydrite) used as the activator.
- Variables: CFBC ash dosage and moisture condition varied for the HVSC and OPC systems respectively.
- Analysis: heat of hydration (isothermal calorimetry), hydration products (XRD/TGA), microstructure (SEM/MIP), length change, compressive strength.
- Comparison: expansion behavior contrasted when the same CFBC ash is incorporated into OPC.

## Results

- HVSC: substituting 20 wt % CFBC ash reduces autogenous shrinkage and suppresses expansion, with strength variation within ±15 %.
- OPC: incorporating CFBC ash causes expansion exceeding 3000 µm/m at 91 days (excessive ettringite formation) — unsuitable.
- Early heat evolution: slightly increased with CFBC ash use (heat of dissolution); the second peak reflects accelerated slag hydration.
