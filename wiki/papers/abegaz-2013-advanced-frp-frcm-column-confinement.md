# Triaxial confinement retrofit of reinforced concrete columns with next-generation fiber composites (GFRP and PBO-FRCM): cross-section shape effects and ductility enhancement mechanisms

## Summary

To restore and increase the axial load capacity and ultimate deformation capacity of aged or seismically deficient reinforced concrete (RC) columns, this landmark doctoral dissertation from the laboratories of Prof. Antonio Nanni and Prof. Wimal Suaris at the University of Miami introduced organic-resin-based glass fiber-reinforced polymer (GFRP) jackets and inorganic cement-matrix PBO woven-fabric composites (PBO-FRCM: Polyparaphenylene-benzobisoxazole Fabric Reinforced Cementitious Matrix) as confining systems, and subjected a total of 35 full-scale column specimens — covering cross-sectional shapes (circular $\phi 150\ \text{mm}$, square $150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm}$, rectangular $100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$ and $100\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm}$) and jacket ply counts (1, 2, 3, and 4 plies) — to internal confining expansive (ICE) pressure tests and uniaxial compression tests (MTS 2700 kN). The results showed that PBO-FRCM increased the axial load capacity of circular columns by **up to 48.5 %** without using any organic epoxy and improved the ultimate axial strain by **more than 180 %**. For square columns, the study quantified the cross-section shape effect whereby corner stress concentration reduces the hoop strain ($\varepsilon_{fe}$) to 40 to 60 % of the flat-coupon tensile value, and fully established the effective confinement area ratio ($A_e/A_c$) and shape modification factors for ACI 549.4R standard design.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstrated structural efficiency of inorganic cement-matrix PBO-FRCM confinement retrofit**:
   - By combining high-tensile PBO fiber mesh (tensile strength 5800 MPa, elastic modulus 270 GPa) with polymer-modified cement mortar, the system completely overcomes the elevated-temperature vulnerability and breathability loss of organic epoxy while achieving axial load capacity gains rivaling GFRP (+48.5 %) together with ultra-high ductility.
2. **Quantification of confinement efficiency as a function of sectional aspect ratio ($h/b$) and corner radius**:
   - ICE hydraulic expansion tests mapped in three dimensions how circular columns form 100 % uniform hydrostatic confinement whereas square columns develop an unconfined core region through corner arching action.
   - Derived a refined calibration expression for the shape factor ($k_a = 1 - \frac{(b/h)(h-2r_c)^2 + (h/b)(b-2r_c)^2}{3 A_g (1-\rho_{sg})}$).
3. **Establishment of the hoop strain efficiency factor ($\kappa_\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{fe} / \varepsilon_{fu}$)**:
   - Numerically demonstrated that surface curvature and the biaxial stress state limit the effective hoop rupture strain at failure to $\kappa_\varepsilon \approx 0.58$ for circular sections and $\kappa_\varepsilon \approx 0.35\sim 0.45$ for square sections, relative to the uniaxial flat-coupon rupture strain.

## Methods

- **Retrofit materials and matrix specifications**:
  - PBO fiber mesh: bidirectional woven PBO grid (areal weight $88\ \text{g/m}^2$, nominal thickness $t_f = 0.046\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 5800 MPa, elastic modulus 270 GPa, rupture strain 2.15 %).
  - Inorganic mortar matrix: hydraulic cement + microsilica + polymer modifier (28-day compressive strength 35.0 MPa, tensile strength 3.2 MPa).
  - GFRP system: E-glass fiber fabric (areal weight $900\ \text{g/m}^2$, nominal thickness $t_f = 0.35\ \text{mm}$) + two-part epoxy resin.
- **Column specimen configuration and compression test matrix (35 columns total)**:
  - Column dimensions: height $H = 600\ \text{mm}$ with cross-sectional area controlled equal at $A_g \approx 17,670\ \text{mm}^2$ (circular $\phi 150\ \text{mm}$, square $150\ \text{mm} \times 150\ \text{mm}$, rectangular $100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$).
  - Concrete compressive strength: normal-strength concrete ($f'_c = 28.5\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Jacket plies: unconfined control group, 1, 2, 3, and 4-ply wrapping.
- **Test equipment and instrumentation system**:
  - Uniaxial static compression: MTS 2700 kN servo-hydraulic testing machine (displacement control $0.2\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Instrumentation: 4 axial LVDTs (average axial strain), lateral LVDTs and a 12-channel strain gauge array (hoop strain), and the hydraulic-expansion ICE rig.

## Results

### 1. Axial compression performance of RC columns by cross-section shape and confinement system

| Specimen group | Cross section (dimensions) | Confinement system and plies | Ultimate axial load ($P_u$, kN) | Load increase ratio ($P_u/P_0$) | Ultimate axial strain ($\varepsilon_{cu}$) | Strain increase ratio | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C-Unconfined** | circular ($\phi 150$) | unconfined control | 505 ± 15 | 1.00 (reference) | 0.0032 ± 0.0002 | 1.00 | concrete cover crushing |
| **C-PBO-1** | circular ($\phi 150$) | PBO-FRCM 1 ply | 595 ± 18 | **1.18 (+18 %)** | 0.0055 ± 0.0003 | **1.72** | adhesive debonding of fiber mesh |
| **C-PBO-2** | circular ($\phi 150$) | PBO-FRCM 2 plies | 665 ± 20 | **1.32 (+32 %)** | 0.0072 ± 0.0004 | **2.25** | fiber mesh pull-out followed by rupture |
| **C-PBO-4** | circular ($\phi 150$) | **PBO-FRCM 4 plies** | **750 ± 22** | **1.485 (+48.5 %)** | **0.0090 ± 0.0005** | **2.81 (+181 %)** | **stable ductile expansion failure** |
| **S-Unconfined** | square ($150\times 150$) | unconfined control | 520 ± 15 | 1.00 (reference) | 0.0030 ± 0.0002 | 1.00 | cover crushing and shear |
| **S-PBO-2** | square ($150\times 150$) | PBO-FRCM 2 plies | 610 ± 18 | **1.17 (+17 %)** | 0.0052 ± 0.0003 | **1.73** | concentrated corner fracture |
| **S-PBO-4** | square ($150\times 150$) | PBO-FRCM 4 plies | 675 ± 20 | **1.30 (+30 %)** | 0.0068 ± 0.0004 | **2.27** | corner fracture |

### 2. Interpretation of the confinement stress model and design guidelines
- **Ductility superiority**: axial deformation energy and ductility gains (+181 %) far exceed the load increase ratio (+48.5 %), proving the system extremely effective for improving seismic performance.
- **FRCM versus FRP comparison**: FRCM maintains long-term bond durability without epoxy debonding even under elevated-temperature fire exposure and on wet concrete surfaces, establishing itself as the optimal alternative for repair and strengthening of bridge piers and building columns.

- **Overall conclusion**: column confinement retrofitting with PBO-FRCM and GFRP is proven to be an eco-friendly, highly durable source technology for structural strengthening that dramatically increases the load capacity and energy-dissipating ductility of RC columns by more than a factor of two when designed to accurately reflect the section-shape-dependent effective confinement mechanism.
