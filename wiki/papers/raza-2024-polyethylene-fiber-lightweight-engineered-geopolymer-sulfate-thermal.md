# Material characterization and thermal-mechanical performance of PE fiber-reinforced lightweight engineered geopolymer composites (PE-LEGC) under sulfate attack ($\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4/\text{MgSO}_4$): ultra-low thermal conductivity $k = 0.32\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$, tensile strain capacity $\varepsilon_u = 4.60\%$, and integrity through 180 days of exposure

## Summary

To simultaneously resolve the expansion-induced fragmentation caused by sulfate chemical attack on building envelope structures exposed to harsh marine-coastal and underground environments, together with the energy inefficiency of heavy self-weight and seismic brittleness, this study developed **an eco-friendly lightweight engineered geopolymer composite (PE-LEGC: oven-dry density $1620\ \text{kg/m}^3$, 28-day compressive strength $41.5\ \text{MPa}$) that reinforces a cement-free alkali-activated matrix based on Class F fly ash and GGBS with lightweight expanded clay aggregate (LWA) and ultra-high-strength PE microfibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, exposed it to long-term immersion in $5.0\%\ \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ and $5.0\%\ \text{MgSO}_4$ solutions for 180 days, and rigorously evaluated uniaxial direct tension, compressive strength, thermal conductivity ($k$), XRD mineral phases, and SEM-EDS microstructure, demonstrating **a tensile strain capacity of $4.10\%$ and a compressive strength retention of $92.5\%$ even after 180 days of harsh sulfate immersion (conventional OPC-ECC loses $45\%$ of its strength to ettringite expansion), retained insulation performance at a dry thermal conductivity of $k = 0.32\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$, and a $75\%$ reduction in carbon emissions** — the latest 2024 landmark origin paper in Elsevier's Case Studies in Construction Materials by the research team of **Prof. Wensu Chen (Curtin University, Australia) and Prof. Ali Raza (UET Taxila, Pakistan, corresponding author)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First worldwide elucidation of the ultra-long-term thermal-mechanical durability of lightweight EGC under combined sulfate attack environments**:
   - Demonstrates the impermeable protective shell of a three-dimensional N-A-S-H / C-A-S-H composite gel free of calcium-based ettringite expansion under $\text{MgSO}_4$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ environments.
2. **Perfect preservation of ultra-lightweight ($1620\ \text{kg/m}^3$) and ultra-low thermal conductivity ($0.32\ \text{W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$)**:
   - The insulating pore structure of the microporous LWA is preserved even after sulfate immersion, continuously sustaining reductions in building heating and cooling energy demand.
3. **Uniaxial direct-tensile strain capacity of $\varepsilon_u = 4.60\%$ with high ductility of $4.10\%$ retained after 180 days of exposure**:
   - Hydrophobic PE fibers do not embrittle even under chemical attack and continue to exhibit stable bridging frictional slip.
4. **A standard proposed for smart zero-energy envelope panels across global marine-salinity and sulfate zones**:
   - Chemical-attack resistance, high thermal insulation, and seismic toughness completed within a single eco-friendly composite.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binder: Class F fly ash (60 wt.%) + GGBS ground granulated blast-furnace slag (40 wt.%).
  - Activator: grade-$D$ sodium silicate solution + $10\ \text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ combined solution ($W/B = 0.34$).
  - Aggregates: lightweight porous expanded clay (LWA, $d \le 2.0\ \text{mm}$) + fine silica sand.
  - Reinforcing fiber: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE microfiber ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Harsh-environment immersion and evaluation program (Sections 2.2~3.4 & Figs. 4~8)**:
  - Attack solutions: aqueous $5.0\%\ \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ and $5.0\%\ \text{MgSO}_4$ (28-, 90-, and 180-day immersions).
  - Evaluation: uniaxial direct tensile testing, compressive strength on 50 mm cubes, TCi thermal conductivity measurement, and high-resolution SEM-EDS.

### 2. Magnesium sulfate attack microstructure and fiber-interface analysis
- **M-S-H decalcification defense**: when magnesium ions penetrate, the low-calcium geopolymer matrix forms an ultrafine impermeable brucite (magnesium hydroxide) protective layer at its surface, blocking corrosion of the interior N-A-S-H gel.
- **PE fiber surface soundness**: PE polymer chains, inert to strong acids and sulfate ions, deliver intact tensile bridging capacity without surface damage even after 180 days of exposure.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the latest top landmark origin paper of 2024 in sustainable infrastructure and chemically durable composite materials engineering, in which the research teams of Prof. Wensu Chen (Curtin University) and Prof. Ali Raza combined a fly ash-slag-based lightweight geopolymer with PE fibers to demonstrate an ultra-durable lightweight insulating composite material preserving a tensile strain capacity of 4.10 % and a compressive strength retention of 92.5 % even after 180 days of harsh sulfate attack.
