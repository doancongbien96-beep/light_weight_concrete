# Evaluation of Governing Factors on Concrete Spalling Under ISO-834 Standard Fire Exposure: Water-to-Binder Ratio, Moisture, Air Content, Silica Fume, and Moisture Clog Mechanisms

## Summary

To systematically investigate the material mechanisms governing explosive spalling in high-strength and ultra-high-performance concrete exposed to high fire temperatures, Professor Dongyeop Han at Gyeongsang National University fabricated 14 concrete series ($100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$) varying water-to-binder ratio (W/B = 0.15, 0.25, 0.35), air content (3.0 %, 4.5 %, 6.0 %), internal moisture content (0 %, 3 %, 5 %), aggregate type (granite vs basalt dilatometry), maximum aggregate size ($d_{max} = 5, 10, 20\ \text{mm}$), and supplementary cementitious materials (silica fume SF, fly ash FA, ground granulated blast furnace slag GGBS). Specimens were subjected to **ISO-834 standard fire exposure (1 h duration, maximum temperature $945\ ^\circ\text{C}$)** to quantify mass loss, cracking, and failure morphology. Ultra-high-strength concrete with W/B = 0.15 experienced **complete explosive spalling with 99.0 % mass loss within 20 min of heating** due to dense capillary pore blockage preventing vapor escape. Conversely, increasing air content to 6.0 % or incorporating fly ash/slag provided interconnected pressure-relief pores, **reducing mass loss to 8.0 ~ 9.0 % and preventing explosive spalling completely (100 % prevention)**. Oven-dry concrete (0 % moisture) experienced 0.0 % spalling under identical fire heating, experimentally proving that "moisture clog" vapor pressure buildup is the primary governing mechanism of explosive spalling.

## Key Contributions

1. **Experimental verification of 99 % complete explosive spalling in UHSC (W/B = 0.15)**:
   - Confirmed that ultra-low capillary permeability raises internal vapor pressures above matrix tensile strength, triggering catastrophic disintegration.
2. **100 % spalling mitigation via air entrainment (6.0 % air) and SCM substitution**:
   - Demonstrated that entrained air voids ($10\sim 1000\ \mu\text{m}$) and blended slag/fly ash matrices relieve vapor pressures, limiting mass loss to 8 %.
3. **Identification of spalling risks from silica fume addition**:
   - Proved that 10 % silica fume densifies pore throats and isolates pores, increasing explosive spalling mass loss to 87 % at equivalent strength.
4. **Validation of Moisture Clog Theory via 0 % moisture testing**:
   - Verified that thermal stress alone cannot induce spalling in completely dry concrete, confirming pore water vaporization pressure as the root cause.

## Methods

- **Materials and concrete mixture design (Tables 1–2, by mass)**:
  - Binders: OPC Type I (specific gravity 3.15, fineness $3300\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), fly ash (FA: specific gravity 2.21, fineness $4100\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$), silica fume (SF: specific gravity 2.20, specific surface area $200000 cm^2/g$).
  - Aggregates: Crushed granite ($d_{max} = 5, 10, 20\ \text{mm}$) and crushed basalt.
  - Three W/B series: W/B = 0.15 (ultra-high strength), W/B = 0.25 (high strength), W/B = 0.35 (normal strength).
  - Air contents: 3.0 %, 4.5 %, and 6.0 % (controlled by AE agent).
  - Moisture contents: 0 % (oven dry), 3 %, and 5 % (by oven-dry mass).
- **Testing setup and fire exposure (Figures 1–3)**:
  - Heating furnace: Gas burner fire testing furnace (ISO-834 curve: $T = 20 + 345 \log_{10}(8t + 1)$, 60 min exposure, peak temperature $945\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Specimen dimensions: $100\ \text{mm} \times 100\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$ prisms (tested at 56 d).
  - Thermal expansion: NETZSCH DIL 402C dilatometer ($\phi 6\ \text{mm} \times 16\ \text{mm}$ cylinders, ambient to 1000 °C).

## Results

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Water-to-binder ratio** | **W/B = 0.15** | 3.0 % | 5.0 % | **99.0 ± 1.0 % (Total loss)** | **Catastrophic explosive spalling** | Complete disintegration in 20 min |
| **Water-to-binder ratio** | W/B = 0.25 | 3.0 % | 5.0 % | 33.0 ± 2.5 % | Severe spalling | Surface scaling of 20~35 mm |

### 2. Vapor pressure and thermodynamic mechanisms
- **Pore size vs vapor pressure buildup**: Capillary pores ($0.003\sim 10\ \mu\text{m}$) restrict saturated vapor escape at high temperatures, raising internal pressure to $5\sim 8\ \text{MPa}$, while entrained air voids ($10\sim 1000\ \mu\text{m}$) act as expansion chambers keeping internal pressure below 0.5 MPa.
- **The silica fume paradox**: While silica fume increases room-temperature strength, it isolates pore networks, increasing explosive spalling risk by over 10-fold under high fire temperatures.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work quantitatively established the relationships among W/B ratio, moisture content, pore structure, and silica fume in controlling concrete explosive spalling under ISO-834 fire conditions.
