## Summary
This study evaluated the physicochemical reactivity and composite mechanical behavior of porous coal gasification slag (CGS) discharged from integrated gasification combined cycle (IGCC) processes, for its reuse as a supplementary cementitious material (SCM) and fine-aggregate substitute. The pozzolanic reactivity arising from the glassy amorphous silica/alumina constituents of CGS was analyzed via alkali dissolution and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), and the compressive strength, micropore structure, and drying shrinkage characteristics of cement mortars incorporating ground CGS (specific surface area $450 m2/kg$) at 10 ~ 40 wt % replacement were characterized. With 20 wt % CGS replacement, a 28 d compressive strength of 55 MPa or above was achieved through reaction with cement hydration products.

## Key Contributions
- **Determination of the pozzolanic activity index of coal gasification slag**: based on the amorphous glassy content (> 80 %) formed by the high-temperature gasification quenching process, a 28 d pozzolanic activity of 92 % was secured.
- **Discovery of the internal curing effect of porous particles**: demonstrated that internally absorbed moisture in fine-aggregate-size CGS is progressively released during later stages of hydration, reducing autogenous shrinkage by 25 %.
- **Verification of heavy metal immobilization and environmental friendliness**: leaching tests confirmed that hazardous heavy metals are safely immobilized within the hydration gel of the cement matrix.

## Methods
- **Materials**: OPC, ground CGS (median particle size $d_{50} = 12.5 \mu m$), fine-aggregate CGS (fineness modulus 2.4).
- **Replacement ratios**: binder replacement of 10 %, 20 %, 30 %, 40 % and fine aggregate replacement of 25 %, 50 %.
- **Test items**: hydration heat measurement, TGA/DTG measurement of calcium hydroxide ($Ca(OH)_2$) consumption, MIP porosity analysis, compressive strength.

## Results
- 20 wt % CGS replacement mix: 28 d compressive strength $56.2 MPa$, 90 d compressive strength $64.8 MPa$ (a level equal to or exceeding OPC).
- The internal curing action of CGS suppressed early autogenous shrinkage to $180 \mu\epsilon$, improving drying-shrinkage cracking resistance.
