## Summary
Based on a UNEP (United Nations Environment Programme) report, this is the highest-authority review presenting an economically feasible technology roadmap for large-scale reduction of greenhouse gas ($CO_2$) emissions from the global cement and concrete industry. It analyzes clinker factor reduction strategies for cutting decarbonation and fuel combustion emissions of clinker production, and systematizes the reaction mechanisms, raw material availability, mechanical performance, and durability of Limestone Calcined Clay Cement ($LC^3$) technology. It demonstrated that $LC^3$ technology replaces up to 50 % of clinker while cutting $CO_2$ by 40 %, providing strength equal to or better than OPC along with excellent sulfate resistance.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the synergy mechanism of limestone calcined clay cement ($LC^3$)**: presents the principle whereby alumina from calcined kaolinite clay (metakaolin) reacts with limestone carbonate to form hemicarboaluminate/monocarboaluminate crystals that densify the pore structure extremely.
- **Analysis of global raw material supply potential**: analysis of worldwide clay and limestone reserves capable of replacing the limited supply of fly ash and slag.
- **Establishment of four core decarbonization strategies for the cement industry**: (1) energy efficiency of clinker production, (2) alternative fuel utilization, (3) maximization of clinker substitutes (SCMs and $LC^3$), (4) optimization of concrete mix and structural design.

## Methods
- Integrated analysis of global cement production data, $CO_2$ emission inventories, and thermodynamic hydration phase equilibria.
- Compilation of property and durability data for $LC^3$-50 (clinker 50 %, calcined clay 30 %, limestone 15 %, gypsum 5 %).

## Results
- $LC^3$ systems: 7 d and 28 d compressive strengths equal to or better than OPC, with chloride diffusion coefficients reduced to 1/4 ~ 1/10 of OPC.
- Certified as the most realistic near-term alternative, immediately cutting $CO_2$ emissions per tonne of clinker produced from $850 kg$ to below $500 kg$.
