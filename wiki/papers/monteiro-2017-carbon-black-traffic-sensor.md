# Pressure-Sensitive Carbon Black Cement Composite Sensor for Traffic Monitoring

## Summary

Study that endowed a cement composite with piezoresistive sensing functionality by incorporating carbon black (CB) at 6.5 wt % (by binder mass; 1.91 vol %). Evaluation used large 600 cm³ slab-type specimens under repeated compressive loading at automobile traffic load levels and across a 15~45 °C temperature range. Linear and reversible sensing performance with a gauge factor of 40~60 was secured, demonstrating applicability as traffic-volume and axle-weight measurement sensors embedded in road pavements.

## Key Contributions

1. Full-scale verification: unlike the inadequate small specimens of prior literature, evaluation on large slabs (100 × 100 × 60 mm) that can actually be installed in pavements, under real traffic stress levels (up to 2 MPa).
2. Quantified temperature dependence: raising the temperature from 15 to 45 °C reduces piezoresistive sensitivity by about 30 % nonlinearly while linearity and repeatability are maintained. Resistance rises steeply below 25 °C and decreases gently above it.
3. Electrical measurement protocol: owing to the dielectric and polarization characteristics of CB/cement composites, a polarization time of about 180 s is required to reach stable resistance. Constant-current driving at 2 mA is the stable region (the asymmetric region lies above 0.5 mA). Resistance is computed from the voltage signal alone via a voltage-divider arrangement.
4. Hysteresis countermeasures organized: comparison of three methods — DC post-correction, AC voltage excitation, and biphasic DC supply (Downey et al.).
5. Practicality of CB alone demonstrated: sensitivity is 1–2 orders of magnitude lower than CNT or CF (GF 55 vs 220~445), yet CB is superior in cost, workability, and signal stability. Equivalent performance with 4× less CB than the literature baseline.

## Methods

CEM II 32.5R + CB N330 + high-range water reducer 4 % (doubling as dispersant), W/C 0.6, cement:aggregate volumetric ratio 0.5. Ultrasonication for CB dispersion (24 kHz, 200 W, 15 min). Galvanized mesh electrodes at 4 cm spacing, with epoxy moisture-proof coating. Loading: LT1 (20 kN constant amplitude, 10 cycles), LT2 (1~20 kN stepwise increase, 6 cycles × 5 sets), at 1 kN/s. Experiments conducted at four temperatures (15/25/35/45 °C), 24 runs total.

## Results

- Mechanical: compressive strength 18.82 ± 0.46 MPa, tensile strength 4.37 ± 0.37 MPa, elastic modulus 14.88 ± 0.98 GPa (28 days).
- Sensing: FCR–strain response linear (R² ≈ 0.997), mean GF 60 (15 °C) → 40 (45 °C). Stress sensitivity at 25 °C improved 32 % over preceding studies.
- Specimen-to-specimen scatter: resistance ±12 %, sensing parameters ±20 % — aggregate inhomogeneity, rather than CB dispersion, is estimated as the primary cause.
