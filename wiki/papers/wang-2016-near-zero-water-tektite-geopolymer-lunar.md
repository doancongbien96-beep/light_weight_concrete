## Summary
This research paper from the Chinese Academy of Sciences developed a near-zero water consumption lunar geopolymer composite in order to realize infrastructure construction under the extreme water-constrained environment of the lunar surface. Tektite (a natural impact-derived amorphous aluminosilicate glass, $SiO_2 73 \%$, $Al_2O_3 13 \%$) was used as a lunar soil simulant precursor, combined with a closed-loop water recycling system that captures and reuses 100 % of the water released during the curing process following the high-alkali activator reaction. It was demonstrated that the tektite-based geopolymer achieved a water recovery rate of **95.5 %** during low-temperature dehydration at $80 °C$ after room-temperature curing, while exhibiting a compressive strength of **48.5 MPa** and a flexural strength of **7.2 MPa**.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a closed-loop water capture-recycling geopolymerization reaction cycle**: reduced the net water consumption to less than $5 kg$ for construction of a $1 m^3$ structure.
- **Elucidation of the room-temperature alkaline dissolution reactivity of lunar-simulant tektite amorphous glass**: rapid formation of a three-dimensional Si-O-Al geopolymer network skeleton under $NaOH + Na_2SiO_3$ activation.
- **Demonstration of high-vacuum durability after complete dehydration**: retained 92 % of compressive strength inside a vacuum chamber ($10^{-5} Pa$) without outgassing.
- **Establishment of the feasibility of in-situ construction of lunar landing pads and habitat blocks**: cut the hydrogen/water transport mass from Earth by 95 %.

## Methods
- Precursor: natural tektite powder ($d_{50} = 8.5 \mu m$, amorphous fraction 92 %).
- Activator: $NaOH 10 M$ + liquid sodium silicate ($Ms = 1.5$), initial $W/B = 0.30$.
- Water recovery process: molding in a sealed curing chamber $\rightarrow$ vacuum condensation capture (condensation trap) at $80 °C$ $\rightarrow$ 100 % recycling of the mixing water.
- Testing: ASTM C109 uniaxial compressive strength (20 mm cubes), XRD crystalline phase analysis, FTIR gelation degree, TGA thermogravimetric moisture analysis.
