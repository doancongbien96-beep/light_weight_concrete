## Summary
A study experimentally clarifying the initial crack impact count ($N_1$), final failure impact count ($N_2$), impact fracture energy, and biaxial flexural ductile behavior of metakaolin ferrocement (MK-Ferrocement) panels ($500 \times 500 \times 25 mm$) reinforced with woven wire mesh (2 to 6 layers stacked) in high-density cement mortar replacing a highly reactive metakaolin (MK, cement replacement 0, 5, 10, 15 wt %) as pozzolanic admixture under drop-weight impact loading (4.5 kg steel ball). The mix with 10 wt % metakaolin and 6 mesh layers increased the failure impact count by a factor of **28** over unreinforced concrete and absorbed an impact energy of **4850 J**.

## Key Contributions
- **Mesh bond enhancement through ITZ densification by metakaolin clarified**: consumes calcium hydroxide ($Ca(OH)_2$) to form C-A-S-H gel, improving steel wire bond strength by 35 %.
- **Impact energy absorption rate formulated as a function of mesh layer count (2 to 6 layers)**: as layers increase, local punching failure transitions to an in-plane biaxial tensile membrane mode.
- **Impact ductility index ($I_d = N_2/N_1$) improved by a factor of 4.2**: long-duration shape retention without fragment spalling even after initial cracking.
- **Mix optimization for protective thin plates and impact-resistant tank structural materials**: design standard for lightweight thin-plate protective members established.

## Methods
- Binder: OPC + highly reactive metakaolin (0, 5, 10, 15 wt %), $W/B = 0.40$, Sand/Binder = 2.0.
- Mesh reinforcement: galvanized woven steel wire mesh (wire diameter 1.0 mm, mesh opening 10 mm, 2, 4, and 6 layers).
- Impact testing: ACI 544 standard drop-weight impact machine (hammer mass 4.5 kg, drop height 457 mm, $500 \times 500 \times 25 mm$ panels).
