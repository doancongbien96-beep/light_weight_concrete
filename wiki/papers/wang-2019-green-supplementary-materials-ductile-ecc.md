## Summary
A study in which Professor Victor Li's research team at the University of Michigan elucidated the micromechanics-based mix design, the carboaluminate hydrate reaction, and the uniaxial direct tensile behavior of an eco-friendly low-carbon composite (LC3-ECC) that achieved high-ductility tensile strain-hardening performance while reducing cement clinker usage by 50 % through a limestone-calcined clay blended binder (Limestone Calcined Clay Cement, LC3: limestone powder 15 % + metakaolin 30 % + cement 50 %). Based on the low fracture toughness of the LC3 matrix ($K_m = 0.42 MPa\sqrt{m}$), the pull-out bridging capacity of PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$) was maximized, achieving a 28 d compressive strength of **48.5 MPa**, direct tensile strength of **5.4 MPa**, tensile strain capacity of **5.2 %**, and a **48 %** reduction in embodied carbon emissions.

## Key Contributions
- **Completion of a micromechanics-based green ECC mix framework based on the LC3 binder**: realization of high-ductility ECC anywhere in the world using locally deposited clays and limestone, even in regions with limited industrial byproduct supply.
- **Elucidation of ITZ densification through hemi/monocarboaluminate hydrate formation**: optimization of fiber interfacial frictional stress at $\tau_0 = 1.65 MPa$.
- **Attainment of a PSH energy index of $J_b'/J_{tip} = 6.2$**: realization of a 28 d direct tensile strain capacity of 5.2 % and an average crack width of $30 \mu m$ or less.
- **Presentation of a standard sustainable low-carbon infrastructure mix**: a 50 % cut in clinker intensity and substantial savings across the full life-cycle carbon footprint.

## Methods
- Binder: cement 50 % + calcined illite/kaolin clay 30 % + limestone powder 15 % + gypsum 5 %, $W/B = 0.28$, Sand/Binder = 0.36.
- Fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, length 12 mm).
- Tests: dog-bone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, LVDT), 28 d compressive strength, XRD crystalline phase analysis, MIP porosity.
