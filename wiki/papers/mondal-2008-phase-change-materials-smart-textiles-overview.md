# Dynamic Thermal Regulation Technology of Phase Change Materials (PCMs) for Smart Textiles and Porous Matrices: A Comprehensive Review of Microencapsulation, Coating Processes, and Latent Heat Release Mechanisms

## Summary

To realize smart textiles, functional membranes, and architectural membrane structures that keep body and space temperature constant by responding in real time to external ambient temperature changes, this highest-authority landmark review by **Dr. Subrata Mondal of Colorado State University (USA)**, published in Applied Thermal Engineering in 2008, consolidates the **physical principles of phase-transition latent heat absorption/release of phase change materials (PCMs: straight-chain paraffinic hydrocarbons, fatty acids, polyethylene glycol PEG, inorganic salt hydrates), polymer-shell microencapsulation processes (Microencapsulation: interfacial polymerization, in-situ polymerization, complex coacervation), and matrix coating/spinning technologies**, and establishes **design guidelines for smart thermoregulating composite materials that secure an isothermal thermal buffering (Dynamic Thermal Buffer) effect via phase-change latent heat ($100\sim 250\ \text{J/g}$) under abrupt external temperature swings, together with long-term washing/thermal-cycling durability**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Systematization of PCM theory and dynamic insulation for smart textile/membrane structures**:
   - Moving beyond conventional static thermal-conductivity-based insulation, established an active thermoregulation mechanism that reversibly absorbs and releases heat across the phase-transition temperature band.
2. **Consolidation of the four key PCM microencapsulation manufacturing processes**:
   - Organized the reaction chemistry and process variables of 1) interfacial polycondensation, 2) in-situ polymerization (melamine-formaldehyde shell), 3) complex coacervation (gelatin–gum arabic), and 4) sol-gel inorganic encapsulation.
3. **Establishment of PCM integration technologies within fibers and porous substrates (four application routes)**:
   - Derived the technical advantages and drawbacks of microcapsule surface coating, hollow-fiber internal filling, direct incorporation during wet/melt spinning, and penetration impregnation.
4. **Application roadmap for aerospace, medical thermal insulation, and smart building envelope membranes**:
   - Completed the technological foundation for extending PCM fiber technology, which began with NASA spacesuit development, to large tent membrane structures, cold-protective clothing, and smart building facades.

## Methods

- **Reviewed literature scope and material categories (Sections 1~6 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Synthesis of roughly 100 core worldwide primary sources on PCMs, polymer encapsulation, and smart fibers.
  - PCMs covered: $n$-octadecane, $n$-eicosane, paraffin mixtures, fatty acids, PEG.
  - Capsule shell materials: melamine-formaldehyde (MF), polyurea, polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA), gelatin.
- **Performance evaluation metrics**:
  - DSC phase-change temperature and enthalpy ($\Delta H_m$), capsule shell burst strength, thermogravimetric (TGA) thermal stability, air permeability, and thermal resistance.

### 2. Interpretation of microcapsule mechanical sealing and dynamic thermal buffering mechanisms
- **Leakage completely blocked by the polymer shell**: dense polymer crosslinked shells of $1\sim 10\ \mu\text{m}$ size fully confine the liquefied paraffin core, excluding oil bleeding even under washing and repeated thermal expansion.
- **Body-temperature-responsive thermal buffering**: when the wearer's skin temperature rises, the PCM absorbs heat to prevent overheating; when the temperature falls, it releases heat — automatic homeostasis that delays a rapid drop in body temperature.

- **Overall conclusion**: this is the highest-authority landmark review paper in the field of smart thermal-control functional materials, in which Dr. Subrata Mondal fully systematized both the chemical processes for microencapsulating and integrating PCMs into smart textiles and porous materials and the physical mechanisms of dynamic thermoregulation.
