# Zero-Leakage Mechanism of a Cement-Coated Scoria Lightweight-Aggregate-Based PEG-PCM Composite and $4.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Reduction of the Indoor Peak Temperature of Concrete

## Summary

To substantially reduce the cooling energy load of buildings in hot-weather-climate regions and to solve the chronic problems of liquid-state leakage and strength degradation of organic phase change materials (PCMs), **an innovative shape-stabilized lightweight composite PCM (SCPC) was developed by vacuum-impregnating polyethylene glycol (PEG-1000: melting point $37.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $152.5\ \text{J/g}$) into natural porous volcanic scoria (Scoria, SC) and forming an inorganic barrier coating of cement paste on the surface**, and the 28-day compressive strength, thermal conductivity, DSC melting/freezing latent heat, and real-time solar-driven thermal-delay behavior of a model building chamber were precisely evaluated for structural lightweight concretes as functions of the SCPC replacement ratio (0 %, 20 %, 40 %, 60 %, 80 %, 100 %) — **the 2025 Journal of Building Engineering landmark original paper by the research team of Mohammed Maslehuddin, University Distinguished Professor, and Dr. Khaled Mohaisen of King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), Saudi Arabia, in which the 60% SCPC replacement mix secures a compressive strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ while lowering the indoor peak temperature by $4.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and delaying the time to reach the peak by 125 min**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of a completely zero-leakage PCM using natural volcanic scoria and an inorganic cement coating**:
   - Instead of expensive epoxy or carbon-based supports, a natural scoria aggregate loaded with $45\ \text{wt.}\%$ PEG together with a thin cement-paste shell coating is applied, achieving even at $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ a liquid leakage rate of $0.0\%$.
2. **Establishment of the optimal balance between structural lightweight-concrete strength ($>28\ \text{MPa}$) and high heat-storage performance**:
   - At 60 % SCPC replacement, a 28-day compressive strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ is maintained while a volumetric latent-heat capacity of $45.2\ \text{J/g}$ is imparted to the concrete, realizing a single material that integrates structural, insulating, and heat-storage functions.
3. **Demonstration of a $38.5\%$ reduction in concrete thermal conductivity and a $4.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ mitigation of the indoor peak temperature**:
   - The thermal conductivity is lowered to $1.15 \rightarrow 0.71\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$, the indoor-space peak temperature is dropped by $4.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ under daytime high-temperature solar load, and the thermal delay time is extended by 125 min.
4. **Design of a phase-change temperature band ($37.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$) optimized for extremely hot climates**:
   - The thermodynamic phase-change point is precisely controlled so that the latent-heat absorption of the PCM activates just before the daytime maximum temperature is reached, tailored to Middle Eastern environments where outdoor maxima reach $45\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## Methods

- **Materials and SCPC composite synthesis (Sections 2.1~2.2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Support: natural scoria lightweight aggregate (SC, particle size $4.75\sim 9.5\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 1.65, water absorption 12.5%).
  - Phase change material: PEG-1000 (polyethylene glycol, molecular weight 1000, melting point $37.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $152.5\ \text{J/g}$).
  - Fabrication process: 2 h vacuum impregnation in an $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ vacuum chamber (0.08 MPa) $\rightarrow$ removal of surface residue $\rightarrow$ tumbling coating with cement paste ($\text{W/C} = 0.30$) $\rightarrow$ room-temperature curing.
- **Concrete mixes and thermal/mechanical evaluation (Sections 2.3~2.4 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Concrete mix: OPC 42.5 + silica sand + SC/SCPC coarse aggregate (W/C = 0.40).
  - Six replacement levels: `MSC` (0%), `M20SCPC` (20%), `M40SCPC` (40%), `M60SCPC` (60%), `M80SCPC` (80%), `M100SCPC` (100%).
  - Compressive strength: $100\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (ASTM C39).
  - Thermal properties: ISOMET 2114 thermal-conductivity measurement, Rigaku XRD, FE-SEM/EDS.
  - Thermal-performance chamber test: a $300 \times 300 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ model box equipped with $50\ \text{mm}$-thick concrete panels, with heating-cooling cycles measured for 10 h under a $250\ \text{W}$ infrared lamp.

### 2. Interpretation of the mutual-confinement and thermal-buffering mechanism of the cement barrier coating
- **Interfacial sealing power of the inorganic cement shell**: the $50\sim 100\ \mu\text{m}$ cement hydration layer coated on the porous scoria surface physically and completely blocks the outward exudation of PEG molecules, preventing contamination of the cement matrix during mixing and curing.
- **Daytime solar latent-heat absorption and nocturnal gradual heat release**: PEG-1000, which operates at $37.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, absorbs the conductive heat flowing into the indoor space as latent heat and lowers the daytime peak temperature by $4.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$, then releases it slowly at night, cutting the peak cooling-electricity demand by more than $40\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark original paper in the field of smart energy-saving concrete for extremely hot climates; by developing a zero-leakage SCPC composite based on natural volcanic scoria and a cement-shell coating, it secures a structural strength of 28.5 MPa while lowering the indoor peak temperature by 4.8 ℃ and achieving a thermal delay of 125 min.
