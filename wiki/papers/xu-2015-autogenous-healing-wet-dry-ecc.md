## Summary
A study that applied prior tensile damage (strains of $1.0 \%, 2.0 \%$, mean crack widths $30 \sim 50 \mu m$) to high-ductility PVA-ECC and then evaluated autogenous healing behavior, watertightness recovery rate, and recovery of re-tensile mechanical properties under repeated wet-dry cycles simulating a real atmospheric environment (12 h water immersion + 12 h air drying). The combination of atmospheric $CO_2$ supply during the drying stage and moisture supply during immersion caused calcite ($CaCO_3$) crystals to precipitate rapidly inside cracks; after 28 d of wet-dry cycling, 98 % of fine cracks below $50 \mu m$ were completely sealed and 85 % of the initial elastic modulus was recovered.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the acceleration mechanism of calcium carbonate precipitation under repeated wet-dry conditions**: XRD and SEM-EDS demonstrated that $CaCO_3$ precipitation proceeded 1.5 times faster under wet-dry cycling than under continuous water immersion.
- **Quantification of the watertightness recovery rate through a hydraulic-head water permeability test**: a permeability coefficient recovery rate $\eta_k > 95 \%$ was achieved after 14 d of healing.
- **Complete restoration of re-tensile strain-hardening performance**: re-tensile tests after self-healing confirmed strength restoration, with cracking occurring at new locations rather than at the healed existing cracks.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash 55 wt %).
- **Healing condition**: repeated wet-dry cycles ($20 °C$, $12 h$ immersion + $12 h$ drying at $60 \% RH$, up to 28 d).
- **Test items**: specimen water permeability test, optical-image crack closure measurement, uniaxial dogbone re-tensile test.

## Results
- After 28 d of wet-dry healing, mean crack closure rate $96.8 \%$ and watertightness recovery rate $99.2 \%$.
- A re-tensile strain capacity of $3.8 \%$ was exhibited, maintaining secondary strain-hardening capability.
