## Summary
To improve the tensile deformation capacity and ductility of the existing K-UHPC (compressive strength 160 ~ 200 MPa), this paper by the research team of Dr. Kyung-taek Koh (Korea Institute of Civil Engineering and Building Technology), Dr. Jeong-il Choi and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University) investigated the uniaxial direct tensile behavior, initial cracking strength, maximum tensile strength, ultimate tensile strain, and crack patterns of UHPC mixes in which 33 % (0.5 vol %) of the steel fibers was replaced with high-strength organic/inorganic microfibers (PE: diameter 12 µm, length 18 mm, $f_t$ = 2700 MPa; PVA: diameter 40 µm, length 12 mm, $f_t$ = 1100 MPa; basalt: diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, $f_t$ = 2100 MPa), starting from a reference combination of 0.2 mm-diameter straight steel fibers (length 19.5 mm at 1.0 % + length 16.5 mm at 0.5 %, H-S mix) at a total fiber volume fraction of 1.5 vol %. The paper demonstrated that in the replacement mix of steel fiber 1.0 % + PE fiber 0.5 % (H-S-PE), the maximum tensile strength increased by **13 %, from 14.3 MPa to 16.2 MPa**, and the ultimate tensile deformation capacity improved dramatically **by 39 %, from 0.71 % to 0.99 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **First clarification of the effect of steel-fiber/microfiber hybrid combinations in an ultra-high-strength UHPC matrix**: combined incorporation of steel and PE fibers delays macro-crack opening and increases ductility.
- **Quantified comparison of tensile performance by fiber type**: PE fiber simultaneously improved tensile strength (+13 %) and tensile deformation capacity (+39 %), whereas PVA fiber reduced tensile strength (-17 %) and deformation capacity (-18 %).
- **Demonstration of the chemical bond between basalt fiber and the matrix**: basalt replacement increased first-cracking strength (+18 %) and maximum tensile strength (+3 %) through strong adhesion to the matrix, but the response became brittle and deformation capacity decreased by 68 %.
- **Crack-pattern and residual crack-width analysis**: the H-S reference specimen showed a fine single crack of 23 µm width, while H-S-PE developed multiple cracks resisting up to a crack width of 98 µm.

## Methods
- Matrix mix: Type 1 Portland cement 1.0, zirconium silica 0.25, filler (> 99 % $SiO_2$, mean particle size 2.2 µm) 0.3, silica sand (≤ 0.5 mm) 1.1, expansive agent 0.075, shrinkage-reducing agent 0.01, $W/B$ = 0.20.
- Fiber combinations (total volume fraction fixed at $V_f$ = 1.5 vol %):
  - **H-S (reference)**: steel fiber (19.5 mm) 1.0 % + steel fiber (16.5 mm) 0.5 %
  - **H-S-PE**: steel fiber (19.5 mm) 0.67 % + steel fiber (16.5 mm) 0.33 % + PE fiber (18 mm) 0.5 %
  - **H-S-PVA**: steel fiber (19.5 mm) 0.67 % + steel fiber (16.5 mm) 0.33 % + PVA fiber (12 mm) 0.5 %
  - **H-S-B**: steel fiber (19.5 mm) 0.67 % + steel fiber (16.5 mm) 0.33 % + basalt fiber (12 mm) 0.5 %
- Tests: dumbbell-type uniaxial direct tensile test (0.1 mm/min), optical-microscope crack-width measurement, and hardened density measurement.
