# Room-Temperature Carbon Nanofiber (CNF) Surface Coating on Polyethylene (PE) Fibers: 66.5 % Interfacial Bond Amplification and 155 MPa Ultra-High-Performance Strain-Hardening Concrete (SHUHPC)

## Summary

To overcome the weak chemical bonding and low interfacial friction of hydrophobic ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (PE) fibers in dense ultra-high-performance cementitious matrices, Professor En-Hua Yang and co-workers at Nanyang Technological University (NTU, Singapore) developed a room-temperature aqueous dip-coating method that self-assembles carbon nanofibers (CNFs) onto PE fiber surfaces via hydrophobic-hydrophobic interactions without thermal degradation. Investigating single-fiber pullout mechanics, uniaxial direct tensile behavior, matrix compressive strength (150~155 MPa), and FESEM interface nanostructures, the authors demonstrated that CNF surface modification increased the interfacial frictional bond strength ($\tau_0$) by **66.5 % ($1.82 \rightarrow 3.03\ \text{MPa}$)** and slip-hardening coefficient ($\beta$) by **108 %**. Incorporating only 1.5 vol.% CNF-coated PE fibers into a 155 MPa matrix produced a strain-hardening ultra-high-performance concrete (SHUHPC) achieving an extraordinary tensile strength of **12.9 MPa**, a tensile strain capacity of **4.6 %**, and tightly controlled saturated multiple microcracking with crack widths below **$35\ \mu\text{m}$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Room-temperature CNF coating on PE fibers without thermal degradation**:
   - Developed an aqueous dip-coating method bypassing high-temperature chemical vapor deposition (CVD) to assemble dense CNF layers on low-melting-point ($130\ ^\circ\text{C}$) PE fibers.
2. **66.5 % amplification in single-fiber interfacial friction ($\tau_0 = 1.82 \rightarrow 3.03\ \text{MPa}$)**:
   - Hydration products (C-S-H) interlocked within CNF nanostructures, inducing nano-interlocking that maximized slip-hardening resistance during pullout.
3. **Concurrent 12.9 MPa tensile strength and 4.6 % tensile ductility in 150 MPa SHUHPC**:
   - Satisfied the micromechanical strain-hardening energy complementary condition ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 2.5$) at only 1.5 vol.% fiber loading, resolving the trade-off between ultra-high matrix strength and high tensile ductility.
4. **Saturated multiple microcrack control ($35\ \mu\text{m}$ crack width)**:
   - Reduced average crack spacing from $5.8\ \text{mm} \rightarrow 3.4\ \text{mm}$ and restricted crack widths below $35\ \mu\text{m}$, blocking aggressive ion ingress for extreme durability.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and SHUHPC mix matrix (Table 1, mass ratio)**:
  - Binders: OPC 1.0, silica fume 0.25 (W/B = 0.16, water 0.20).
  - Micro-fillers: Silica flour ($d_{50} = 10\ \mu\text{m}$) 0.25, fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$) 0.30.
  - Superplasticizer: Polycarboxylate SP 0.024 (30 % solid content).
  - Reinforcing fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    - Spectra-900 high-strength PE fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 38\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 2600 MPa, modulus 73 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
    - CNF coating: Pyrograf-III CNFs ($d = 100\ \text{nm}, L = 20\sim 200\ \mu\text{m}$, specific surface area $40\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$), immersed in 0.5 wt.% aqueous CNF dispersion for 1 h and dried at 60 °C.
- **Testing methods and setup**:
  - Single fiber pullout: Micro-servo system (embedment length $L_e = 1.0\sim 3.0\ \text{mm}$, displacement rate $0.1\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Direct tension: JSCE dogbone specimens ($150\ \text{mm} \times 35\ \text{mm} \times 15\ \text{mm}$, 150 mm gauge length, MTS 250 kN, $0.2\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 (50 mm cubes, 28 d water curing, $100\ \text{kN/min}$).
  - Microstructure: JEOL JSM-7600F FESEM imaging of fiber-matrix interfaces.

### 2. Micromechanical criteria and strain-hardening mechanisms
- **Energy performance index ($J_b'/J_{tip}$)**: CNF coating raised the complementary bridging energy $J_b'$ from $45.2\ \text{J/m}^2$ to $78.5\ \text{J/m}^2$ (+73.7 %), satisfying the strain-hardening margin condition ($J_b'/J_{tip} = 3.14 > 1.0$) in high-fracture-toughness matrices ($J_{tip} \approx 25\ \text{J/m}^2$).
- **Nano-interlocking mechanism**: FESEM micrographs verified that CNFs anchored on fiber tunnels sustained shear resistance against matrix particles, preventing frictional softening of smooth PE filaments.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that room-temperature CNF surface coating enhances PE fiber interfacial bond strength by 66.5 %, achieving 12.9 MPa tensile strength and 4.6 % tensile ductility in a 155 MPa ultra-high-performance cementitious matrix.
