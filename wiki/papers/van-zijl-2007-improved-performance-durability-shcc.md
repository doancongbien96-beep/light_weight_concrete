## Summary

A foundational RILEM HPFRCC-5 study comprehensively analyzing how the tensile strain-hardening and multiple fine-crack control capability of strain-hardening cement-based composites (SHCC) fundamentally improves the long-term durability of structures (resistance to water/gas permeation, blocking of chloride diffusion, prevention of reinforcing bar corrosion). The study quantified the critical mechanism whereby, when crack widths generated under tensile load are kept below $50 \mu m$, the capillary absorption coefficient and chloride diffusion coefficient are suppressed to the level of uncracked ordinary concrete, and demonstrated the long-term durability recovery mechanism via crack self-healing with numerical analysis models and experimental data.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantitative correlation established between the critical crack width ($w_{crit} \approx 50 \mu m$) and durability indicators**: below a crack width of $50 \mu m$, moisture ingress is governed by capillarity, exhibiting diffusion characteristics similar to those of sound concrete without cracks.
- **Modeling of rapid self-healing and watertightness recovery in SHCC crack zones**: analysis of the mechanism by which further hydration of unhydrated binders and calcium carbonate precipitation block 95 % of the water permeability within 14 d.
- **Formulation of the extended durability life of rebar-embedded SHCC members**: demonstrated that the corrosion initiation life is extended at least 4-fold compared with ordinary RC.

## Methods

- One- and two-dimensional finite element analysis (FEM) of moisture/chloride ingress.
- Analysis of immersion and electrochemically accelerated corrosion test data of SHCC specimens pre-loaded with tensile strain ($\epsilon = 1.0 \%, 2.0 \%$).

## Results

- Ordinary concrete (single crack $w = 0.2 mm$): chloride diffusion coefficient $D_{cl} = 15.2 \times 10^{-12} m2/s$.
- SHCC (multiple cracks $w \le 45 \mu m$): $D_{cl} = 2.1 \times 10^{-12} m2/s$ (an 85 % reduction in diffusion rate).
