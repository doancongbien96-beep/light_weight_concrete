# Mechanical Properties of Ultra-High Ductility Cementitious Composites (UHDCC) Across Normal to Ultra-High Strength (40 to 120 MPa): Compression, Direct Tension, and Shear

## Summary

To surpass the ductility limits of conventional PVA fiber composites (ECC/SHCC with tensile strain capacities of 2~4 %) and achieve metal-like ultimate tensile elongations (8~11 %) across the entire strength range from normal strength (40 MPa) to ultra-high strength (120 MPa), Professors Ke-Quan Yu of Tongji University and Shi-Lang Xu of Zhejiang University developed Ultra-High Ductility Cementitious Composites (UHDCC) by optimizing ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (PE) short fibers ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 3000 MPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) and multicomponent binders (cement + slag + limestone powder + silica fume, W/B 0.14~0.32). The authors systematically characterized the mechanical behavior through cylinder uniaxial compression tests ($\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$), dogbone uniaxial direct tension tests (JSCE), Iosipescu shear tests, and digital image correlation (DIC) deformation mapping. The experimental findings demonstrated that controlling the W/B ratio achieved 28-day compressive strengths scaled from **43 MPa to 115 MPa**, while maintaining uniaxial tensile strengths of **6.2 MPa to 16.5 MPa**, tensile strain capacities of **8.0 % ~ 11.1 %**, and strain energy absorption of **$450\sim 1036\ \text{kJ/m}^3$**. Under pure shear, UHDCC exhibited enhanced shear ductility with shear strengths of **6.8 ~ 18.5 MPa** and shear strains of **3.5 ~ 6.5 %**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Realization of ultra-high ductility (8~11 %) across the entire spectrum from normal strength (40 MPa) to ultra-high strength (120 MPa)**:
   - Despite increasing matrix fracture toughness with matrix strength, optimizing the high strength (3000 MPa) and high aspect ratio (500) of PE fibers alongside slag-silica fume binders enabled an **ultimate tensile strain capacity of 8.0 %** simultaneously with a **tensile strength of 16.5 MPa** at an ultra-high compressive strength of 115 MPa.
2. **Characterization of pure shear ductility and constitutive relations via Iosipescu shear testing**:
   - V-notched Iosipescu specimens completely prevented brittle shear failure, quantitatively documenting shear strain-hardening behavior with shear strain capacities of **3.5 ~ 6.5 %** accompanied by multiple fine shear cracking.
3. **Establishment of unified empirical relationships linking compressive, tensile, and shear strengths**:
   - Established design formulas for structural applications relating compressive strength ($f'_c$) to ultimate tensile strength ($\sigma_{tu} = 0.14 f'_c$) and shear strength ($\tau_u = 0.16 f'_c$).

## Methods

- **Raw materials and UHDCC mix proportions (Tables 1 & 2)**:
  - Binders: Ordinary Portland cement (CEM I 52.5 R) + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) + limestone powder (LP) + silica fume (SF).
  - Fine aggregates: Micro-silica sand (mean diameter $100\sim 150\ \mu\text{m}$, sand-to-binder ratio 0.20~0.36).
  - Mix proportions (four strength grades):
    - Grade 40 (W/B = 0.32, $f'_c \approx 43\ \text{MPa}$)
    - Grade 60 (W/B = 0.24, $f'_c \approx 64\ \text{MPa}$)
    - Grade 80 (W/B = 0.18, $f'_c \approx 85\ \text{MPa}$)
    - Grade 120 (W/B = 0.14, $f'_c \approx 115\ \text{MPa}$)
  - Reinforcing fiber: Toyobo ultra-high molecular weight PE fiber (diameter $24\ \mu\text{m}$, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
- **Testing apparatus and experimental methods**:
  - Compression test: $\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 200\ \text{mm}$ cylinders (MTS 4600 kN, displacement control at $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$, axial and lateral LVDTs).
  - Direct tension test: JSCE standard dogbone ($13\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 80\ \text{mm}$ gage section, $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$, two LVDTs and DIC).
  - Shear test: Iosipescu shear specimens ($150\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm} \times 20\ \text{mm}$, central $90^\circ$ notch depth 11 mm, diagonal 45° LVDTs).

### 2. Multiple cracking and failure mode analysis
- **Compressive failure**: In contrast to explosive brittle crushing in conventional high-strength concrete, microcracking was suppressed along a 70° shear plane, showing ductile compressive behavior.
- **Tensile multiple cracking**: Crack numbers reached **80 ~ 120** per specimen, with crack spacings of $0.8\sim 1.2\ \text{mm}$, maintaining crack widths tightly at **30 ~ 65 µm** even at a 16.5 MPa tensile strength.
- **Ultra-high energy dissipation**: Strain energy density reached $1036\ \text{kJ/m}^3$, providing energy dissipation for seismic and impact protective structures.

- **Overall conclusion**: UHDCC combined 115 MPa ultra-high strength with 8~11 % tensile ductility and 18.5 MPa shear resistance, establishing a structural high-performance composite that overcomes the brittleness of traditional concrete and conventional ECC.
