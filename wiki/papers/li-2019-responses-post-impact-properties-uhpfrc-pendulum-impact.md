# Dynamic Damage Behavior and Post-Impact Residual Mechanical Property Evaluation of Ultra-High Performance Fibre Reinforced Concrete (UHPFRC) under Pendulum Impact: Damage Index ($D$), Multi-Impact Resistance, and Flexural Capacity Retention Mechanism

## Summary

To quantitatively evaluate the failure mechanisms of ultra-high performance concrete (UHPFRC: compressive strength $155.0\ \text{MPa}$) and its post-impact structural residual capacity under the repeated low-velocity impacts (Low-Velocity Repeated Impact) experienced by marine bridge piers subjected to ship collision, rockfall protection barriers, and breakwater structures, this Composite Structures 2019 landmark original paper by the research team of **Dr. P. P. Li and Prof. Q. L. Yu (corresponding author) at Eindhoven University of Technology (TU Eindhoven), the Netherlands** developed **a pendulum impact test setup (Pendulum Impact Setup: impact velocity $1.5\sim 4.5\ \text{m/s}$, impact energy $15\sim 60\ \text{J}$, repeated blows $N = 1\sim 100$) capable of perfectly controlling reproducibility and the accuracy of each single impact blow**, and performed crack propagation tracking via high-speed video camera, dynamic impact force measurement with a piezoelectric load cell, ultrasonic non-destructive damage index ($D$) determination, and post-impact residual flexural/compressive tests, thereby demonstrating **a dramatic increase in impact resistance count by more than 15 times with increasing steel fiber volume fraction ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$), perfect retention of residual ultimate flexural strength at $78.5\%$ even after 20 consecutive impacts, and a quantitative prediction model based on the ultrasonic damage index ($D \le 0.25$)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of a UHPFRC-dedicated high-precision pendulum-type repeated impact test method and dynamic load measurement system**:
   - Completely eliminates the secondary rebound impact interference, the chronic defect of conventional drop weight tests, and precisely controls the pure input energy per single blow.
2. **Establishment of a nonlinear damage mechanics model relating repeated impact count to the UHPFRC damage index ($D$)**:
   - Combines the ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) degradation ratio with the dynamic crack opening displacement to quantify the internal micro-fracture progression of the material on a $0\sim 1$ scale.
3. **Demonstration of post-impact (Post-Impact) residual flexural performance retention of $78.5\%$**:
   - While ordinary high-strength concrete (HSC) shatters completely after only 2~3 impacts, UHPFRC maintains an excellent residual flexural strength of $22.5\ \text{MPa}$ even after 20 high-energy impacts.
4. **Elucidation of the multi-stage dissipation mechanism of micro steel fibers under repeated impact energy**:
   - Proves that the interfacial frictional slip and plastic deformation of fibers during impact wave passage completely suppress rear-face tensile spalling caused by stress wave reflection.

## Methods

- **Materials and UHPFRC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binder: Type 1 cement + silica fume + ground quartz sand ($W/B = 0.18$, 28-day compressive strength $155.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Reinforcing fibers: brass-coated straight steel fibers ($d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}, L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, V_f = 0.0, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5\ \text{vol.}\%$).
- **Pendulum impact test and evaluation program (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Setup: pendulum arm of 1.5 m radius + 15 kg steel impactor head (built-in dynamic load cell).
  - Specimens: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ prismatic beam specimens.
  - Evaluation: dynamic impact force–time history, ultrasonic transmission velocity (UPV), post-impact static four-point flexural failure tests.

### 2. Dynamic impact energy absorption and crack-bridging analysis
- **Extension of the impact duration**: as steel fibers suppress crack opening through elastic-plastic action, the dynamic impact action duration ($\Delta t$) lengthens from $2.5\ \text{ms} \rightarrow 8.5\ \text{ms}$, mitigating peak stress concentration.
- **Retention of residual ductility after repeated impacts**: fiber bonding remains preserved even after 20 consecutive blows, still exhibiting deflection hardening and enormous plastic deformation energy upon flexural failure.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-tier landmark original paper in the field of ultra-high performance concrete dynamics and impact protection engineering, in which the TU Eindhoven research team quantified the dynamic damage index and post-impact residual mechanical performance of UHPFRC through pendulum-type repeated impact testing and elucidated the mechanism of 78.9% flexural capacity retention via steel fiber bridging.
