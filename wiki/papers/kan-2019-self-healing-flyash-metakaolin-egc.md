# Autonomous Self-Healing Mechanisms and Tensile Property Recovery of Fly Ash/Metakaolin Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC) after Tensile Pre-Damaging (1~3 %) under Air Exposure and Wet-Dry Cycles

## Summary

To establish the durability and microcrack self-healing behavior of zero-cement, ductile **Engineered Geopolymer Composites (MFA-EGC)**, Professor Li-li Kan at USST and Professor Min Wu at Aarhus University synthesized an alkali-activated matrix from fly ash (HFA/LFA) and metakaolin (MK) activated by $\text{NaOH}$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$, reinforced with oil-coated PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$). The authors introduced tensile pre-strains ($\varepsilon_{pre} = 1.0\%, 2.0\%, 3.0\%$) at 3, 7, and 28 d, followed by 20 d of exposure to two self-healing environments: ambient air exposure versus wet-dry cycling. Saturated multiple cracking constrained **over 84 % of residual crack widths below $10\ \mu\text{m}$**, providing optimal conditions for self-healing. Unlike traditional cement-based ECC where wet-dry cycles are required, **ambient air exposure yielded superior self-healing performance in EGC**. Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ reacted with excess $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ in pore solutions to precipitate natrite ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$), calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$), and secondary N-A-S-H polycondensation products, achieving **100~115 % tensile strength recovery and 90~105 % tensile strain capacity recovery** during reloading.

## Key Contributions

1. **First demonstration of autonomous self-healing in cementless EGC following tensile strain-hardening**:
   - Closed over 90 % of residual cracks within 20 d across pre-damaged ($1.0\sim 3.0\%$) 100 % cementless MFA-EGC specimens.
2. **Identification of ambient air exposure superiority in EGC self-healing**:
   - Demonstrated that air exposure accelerates carbonation-induced sodium/calcium carbonate precipitation and secondary geopolymerization without requiring water immersion.
3. **Over 110 % tensile strength recovery and 95 % strain capacity restoration**:
   - Reloaded specimens exhibited self-strengthening, initiating new microcracks at uncracked matrix locations rather than reopening healed cracks.
4. **Tight microcrack control ($w < 10\ \mu\text{m}$ in 84 % of cracks) enabling rapid healing**:
   - Tightly bridged microcracks under $10\ \mu\text{m}$ shortened capillary diffusion distances, accelerating mineral closure.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture design (Tables 1–3, mass ratios)**:
  - Precursors: High-calcium fly ash (HFA, $\text{CaO}$ 14.8 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 48.5 %), low-calcium fly ash (LFA), metakaolin (MK, calcined at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Activator: $\text{NaOH}$ pellets + liquid $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ ($\text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 3.2$ modulus).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray Kuralon-II REC-15 PVA fibers (diameter $40\ \mu\text{m}$, length 12 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, modulus 41 GPa, 1.2 wt.% oil coating, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - Mixture proportions: Binder (FA:MK = 8:2) : silica sand : activator solution = 1.0 : 0.3 : 0.45.
- **Testing protocols (Section 2.3 & Figs. 3–4)**:
  - Pre-damaging: Dogbone specimens (80 mm gauge length, 0.5 mm/min) loaded to 1.0 %, 2.0 %, 3.0 % strain at 3, 7, 28 d and unloaded.
  - Healing regimes: (1) Laboratory air exposure ($20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, RH 60 %), (2) 10 wet-dry cycles (24 h water immersion + 24 h air drying, total 20 d).
  - Reloading: Direct tension tested after 20 d healing to failure.
  - Analytical characterization: Phenom ProX SEM/EDS, Bruker D8 Advance XRD, Bruker Tensor FT-IR.

### 2. Microstructural and chemical self-healing mechanisms
- **Multi-phase carbonate and N-A-S-H polycondensation**: Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ reacted with $\text{Na}^+$ to form thermonatrite and calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$), while unreacted fly ash particles underwent secondary geopolymerization into N-A-S-H gels, rigidly bonding crack flanks.
- **Self-strengthening mechanism**: Healed crack interfaces developed higher bond strength than the original matrix, forcing new cracks to propagate through virgin matrix and producing tensile strength recovery rates exceeding 100 % (105~112 %).

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper proved that cementless EGC achieves complete self-healing under ambient air exposure via carbonation and secondary geopolymerization, recovering over 100 % of its initial tensile strength.
