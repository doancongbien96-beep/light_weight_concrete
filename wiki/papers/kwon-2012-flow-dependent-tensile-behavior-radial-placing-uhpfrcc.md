# Spatial Variation of Uniaxial Tensile Behavior of UHPFRCC ($180\ \text{MPa}$) with Flow Distance under Radial Flow Placing: Fiber Orientation Coefficient ($\alpha_{ori}$) and Ultimate Tensile Strength ($18.2\ \text{MPa}$) Prediction

## Summary

During the casting of large-scale bridge decks and slabs where fresh concrete expands radially outward from a single discharge point, spatial variations arise in fiber alignment and tensile properties. To investigate the **spatial variation in uniaxial tensile strain-hardening response of UHPFRCC (28 d compressive strength $180.0\ \text{MPa}$, micro straight steel fibers $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$) as a function of radial flow distance ($R = 200, 500, 800, 1000\ \text{mm}$) and orientation relative to flow direction**, researchers at Myongji University, Daegu University, Chonnam National University (Prof. Bang Yeon Lee), and KAIST (Prof. Jin-Keun Kim) performed radial casting in a **$2.0\ \text{m}$ diameter circular slab mold**. Extracting core and dogbone specimens along radial and circumferential axes, the authors conducted uniaxial direct tension tests, 3D fiber orientation tensor analysis ($\alpha_{ori}$) via digital image processing, and inclined pullout bridging modeling. As the flow progressed outward, shear strain gradients strongly aligned steel fibers along the circumferential tangential direction ($\alpha = 0.45 \rightarrow 0.76$), increasing radial tensile strength by $58\%$ (from $11.5\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 18.2\ \text{MPa}$), achieving a tensile strain capacity of $0.85\%$, and establishing a 2D radial-flow-tailored tensile constitutive prediction framework.

## Key Contributions

1. **First rigorous characterization of spatial tensile anisotropy in UHPFRCC under radial flow fields**:
   - Demonstrated that unlike 1D channel flow, circular radial spreading produces distinct circumferential versus radial fiber orientation distributions that evolve with distance.
2. **Quantification of fiber orientation coefficient evolution ($\alpha_{ori} = 0.45 \rightarrow 0.76$) with flow distance ($R$)**:
   - Verified that random 3D orientation ($\alpha \approx 0.50$) near the central inlet transitions to strong planar shear alignment ($\alpha = 0.76$) near the flow boundary.
3. **Modeling of $18.2\ \text{MPa}$ ultimate tensile strength and $0.85\%$ tensile ductility**:
   - Integrated fluid flow alignment mechanics with inclined fiber pullout bridging models to reduce tensile strength prediction error to within $7\%$.
4. **Casting design guidelines for large-scale UHPC slabs and shells**:
   - Formulated hydrodynamic placement criteria to optimize pouring locations and intervals so that fibers align along principal structural tensile trajectories.

## Methods

- **Materials and UHPFRCC mixture proportions (Section 2, Tables 1–2, Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binder: Type I cement + silica fume + quartz powder (W/B = 0.20, superplasticizer, slump flow $750\ \text{mm}$).
  - Aggregate: Fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.50).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Brass-coated straight steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 2500\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Radial placement and specimen extraction (Sections 2.2–2.4, Figs. 4–7)**:
  - Slab mold: $2000\ \text{mm}$ diameter, $50\ \text{mm}$ thick circular plate with a central top inlet.
  - Specimen sampling: Dogbone tension specimens ($80\ \text{mm}$ gauge) extracted at $R = 200, 500, 800\ \text{mm}$ in radial and circumferential directions.
  - Testing and image processing: Direct uniaxial tension (0.2 mm/min, 0.2 mm) and cross-sectional optical analysis for fiber count and angle $\theta$.

### 2. Radial flow fields and hydrodynamic fiber rotation mechanics
- **Circumferential extensional flow**: As the fluid front expands outward, circumferential stretching aligns steel fibers tangentially along the flow front.
- **Anisotropy consideration in structural design**: Structural design of bridge decks must account for the wide directional variation in tensile strength ($11.5 \leftrightarrow 18.2\ \text{MPa}$) induced by casting flow.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that radial placement of UHPFRCC generates significant flow-dependent spatial orientation gradients, elevating tensile strength to 18.2 MPa along optimal flow alignments.
