# Bending Performance of Steel-UHDCC Composite Bridge Deck Structures in Negative Moment Regions: 38% Flexural Capacity Amplification, 0.05 mm Crack Width Confinement, and Fatigue Resistance

## Summary

To mitigate premature tensile cracking and subsequent chloride-induced rebar corrosion in negative moment regions over intermediate piers of continuous bridges, Professor Xudong Shao and Dr. Zhen Du at Hunan University developed a large-scale steel-UHDCC composite deck member ($3100 \times 600 \times 220\ \text{mm}$) integrating steel girders/grids with a 2.0 vol.% PVA ultra-high ductility cementitious composite (UHDCC: 28-day compressive strength $55\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial ultimate tensile strain $5.5\%$, tensile strength $5.2\ \text{MPa}$) via shear studs. Through static negative bending tests, 2D digital image correlation (DIC), and 2 million-cycle high-cycle fatigue tests, the authors demonstrated a 45 % increase in first-cracking load, a 38 % amplification in ultimate flexural capacity, service-load maximum crack widths controlled tightly below $0.05\ \text{mm}$, and zero fatigue crack propagation after 2 million cycles relative to conventional RC decks.

## Key Contributions

1. **Mitigation of crack vulnerability in negative moment composite bridge decks**:
   - Applying strain-hardening tensile bridging of UHDCC to the deck tension zone prevented brittle tensile rupture seen in RC decks and localized macro-cracking seen in UHPC decks.
2. **Verification of fully composite interfacial shear behavior between steel and UHDCC**:
   - Stress concentrations around shear stud connectors were dissipated across multiple microcracks in the UHDCC layer, preventing premature stud shear failure.
3. **38 % amplification in ultimate flexural capacity and ductility ratio ($\mu = 4.85$)**:
   - The UHDCC layer continuously sustained tensile stresses after yielding of the steel bottom flange, mobilizing plastic rotational capacity.
4. **Maintenance of crack widths below $0.05\ \text{mm}$ under service and fatigue loading (2 million cycles)**:
   - Restricting moisture and deicing salt penetration protected interior reinforcement, extending design service life beyond 100 years.

## Methods

- **Specimen design and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–3)**:
  - Dimensions: $3100 \times 600 \times 220\ \text{mm}$ (steel I-girder height $170\ \text{mm}$, top UHDCC slab thickness $50\ \text{mm}$, shear studs $\phi 13\ \text{mm}$).
  - UHDCC mix: P·O 52.5R cement + Class F fly ash (FA/C = 2.0) + quartz sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36, W/B = 0.25).
  - Fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$).
- **Testing program (Sections 2.3–2.5 & Figs. 1–5)**:
  - Static four-point inverted bending test: span $2700\ \text{mm}$ (pure tensile moment applied to deck).
  - Fatigue testing: 2 million cycles ($f = 3.0\ \text{Hz}$, load range $P_{min} = 30\ \text{kN} \sim P_{max} = 150\ \text{kN}$).
  - Instrumentation: 2D-DIC strain field mapping and optical microscopic crack width measurements.

### 2. Tensile stress redistribution and fatigue durability mechanisms
- **Tensile stress redistribution via multiple microcracking**: Over 40 fine microcracks developed in the tension deck, distributing stress concentrations across the entire span.
- **Interfacial fiber bridging stability under fatigue loading**: Interfacial frictional bonding of PVA fibers remained undamaged under 2 million cycles, retaining 96.5 % of initial deck flexural stiffness.

- **Overall conclusion**: The steel-UHDCC composite bridge deck controlled negative moment tensile cracking, amplified ultimate flexural capacity by 38 %, and demonstrated fatigue endurance for bridge engineering.
