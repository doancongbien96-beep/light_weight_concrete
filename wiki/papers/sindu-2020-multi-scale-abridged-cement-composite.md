# Multi-Scale Hybrid Cement Composite (MS-ECC)

## Summary

A multi-scale ECC that sequentially optimizes and integrates reinforcements across three length scales — nano (CNT), micro (PVA fibers), macro (continuous carbon fibers). Single-scale experiments located the optima of CNT 0.1 wt % (by cement mass) and PVA 2 vol %; after hybridizing these and laminating two layers of continuous carbon fiber sheet, the composite achieved a tensile strength of 23 MPa plus a strain capacity above 8000 με. This is a technical paper from ACI Materials Journal, not a specification document (the filename ACI 19-322 derives from manuscript number M-2019-322).

## Key Contributions

1. Identification of single-scale optima: CNT peaks at 0.1 wt % with tensile strength 4.65 MPa (~2.7x the 1.72 MPa reference) and elastic modulus 23.28 GPa (~1.8x the 13 GPa reference); at 0.12 wt % strength collapses to 1.82 MPa — an adverse agglomeration effect. PVA 2 vol % delivers tensile strength 3.90 MPa (+127 %) and strain capacity 25.65×10⁻³ (~160 times the reference) while leaving the elastic modulus unchanged (13 GPa).
2. Quantified synergy of the nano+micro hybrid (vs. the plain matrix): MSGA12F2 (0.12 % CNT + 2 % PVA) reaches flexural strength 14.41 MPa (~7x), energy dissipation 2.902×10⁻³ J (~20x), and fracture energy 2799.14 J/m² (~70x). Relative to PVA alone: flexure +52 %, dissipated energy +109 %, fracture energy +25 %. The fracture process zone (FPZ) enlarges by +15 % in length and +80 % in width.
3. Separation of scale-specific contributions via three-region interpretation of the load-deflection curve: Region I stiffness gain (CNT), Region II multi-peak strain-hardening (PVA multiple cracking), Region III monotonic rise with large deflection (continuous carbon fibers). Each length-scale reinforcement's behavioral signature appears sequentially along a single curve.
4. DIC-based quantification of crack processing: after validating virtual-gauge strains against strain gauges, strain contours document decreasing crack width, an increasing number of crack paths, and longer pre-fracture crack propagation as PVA content increases.
5. Application direction: thin plates (10 mm) can be fabricated, making the material suitable for structural repair/strengthening and seismic upgrade.

## Methods

Single-scale and NM hybrid mixes used cement:silica sand (100–250 μm) = 1:0.5 at w/c 0.4. CNTs (length 10–20 μm, outer diameter 50–80 nm, E 1–1.8 TPa) were mixed in after dispersion in a 0.02 g/mL gum arabic surfactant solution with 1 h ultrasonication. PVA fibers (length 8 mm, diameter 40 μm, E 41 GPa, Kuraray). Water curing for 28 days. Direct tension on dog-bones (330×60×12 mm, gauge width 30 mm, 0.1 mm/min); three-point flexure/fracture prisms (40×40×160 mm, CMOD clip gauge); fracture energy per JCI-S-001-2003. DIC used two synchronized high-speed cameras plus a speckle pattern. The MS-ECC matrix was cement:silica fume:sand (250 μm) = 1:0.7:0.7, w/c 0.25, HRWRA 2 %, PVA 2 vol %, CNT 0.1 %. Into a 500×100×10 mm mold: 3 mm paste → continuous carbon fiber layer (15 mm spacing) → 3 mm paste → second fiber layer (15–20 mm spacing) → remaining fill. Evaluated in four-point bending.

## Results

CNT alone (Table 5): MSGA06/08/1/12 tensile strength 3.18/4.24/4.65/1.82 MPa, strain capacity 0.29/0.22/0.20/0.09×10⁻³, flexural strength 3.06/2.78/2.91/2.47 MPa, fracture energy 41.65/62.54/41.81/35.02 J/m². PVA alone: MSF0.5–2 tensile strength 1.72→3.90 MPa, strain capacity 13.80→25.65×10⁻³, flexural strength 6.45→9.49 MPa, fracture energy 383.51→2230.59 J/m² (all maximal at 2 vol %). NM hybrid (Table 7): MSGA1F2 flexure 14.66 MPa, dissipation 2.460×10⁻³ J, fracture energy 1822.69 J/m²; MSGA12F2 respectively 14.41 MPa, 2.902×10⁻³ J, 2799.14 J/m². Completed MS-ECC: tensile strength 23 MPa, more than 8000 microstrain, bending resistance up to a deflection of 27 mm (1/16 of the span).
