# Effect of Sand Content (S/B Ratio) on Bond Performance of EGC Repair Materials

## Summary

This experimental study investigated the effect of sand-to-binder ratio (S/B = 0.3~1.0) on the physical and mechanical properties of Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC) and their bond strength to concrete substrates and embedded reinforcing bars for reinforced concrete repair applications. The binder comprised fly ash and GGBS at a 1:1 mass ratio activated with anhydrous sodium silicate powder, reinforced with a hybrid fiber system of 18 mm PE fibers (1.5 %) and 13 mm steel fibers (0.5 %). A key achievement is the retention of tensile strain-hardening at high S/B ratios.

## Key Contributions

1. **Strain-hardening at high S/B ratios**: Maintained a tensile strength of 6.12 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 4.16 % even at S/B = 1.0, exceeding conventional recommendations (S/B ≤ 0.3). The critical mechanism was utilizing 18 mm PE fibers (aspect ratio 1059); since critical fiber volume fraction ($V_{f,crit}$, V_f,crit) is inversely proportional to fiber aspect ratio, strain-hardening was preserved in high-S/B matrices without increasing fiber dosage.
2. **Effects of increasing S/B ratio**: Elevated first crack strength (3.55→5.61 MPa), decreased stress performance margin ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc}$, 1.54→1.08, σ_0/σ_fc), decreased tensile strain capacity (7.28→4.16 %), and reduced multiple cracking density (verified via DIC).
3. **Concrete bond strength**: Increased linearly from 1.15→1.61 MPa with increasing S/B ratio, strongly correlating with matrix elastic modulus; repair matrix first crack strength served as the governing factor for bond. Meeting BS EN 1504-3 Class R3 criteria (1.5 MPa) required S/B 0.8 or higher.
4. **Rebar bond performance**: S/B ratio had minimal impact on rebar pullout (all 7 d pullout bond strengths exceeded 14 MPa), correlating directly with compressive strength. Failure occurred via frictional pullout rather than concrete splitting, as fibers bridged radial tensile cracks induced by rebar rib slip.
5. **BSE microstructural analysis**: Increasing S/B reduced interfacial microcracking by mitigating geopolymer autogenous/drying shrinkage, directly improving substrate bond strength.

## Methods

- 4 mixtures with S/B 0.3/0.6/0.8/1.0, fixed W/B 0.42, micro-silica sand (maximum size 400 μm, median size D50 233 μm). Cured sealed at 25 °C and RH >80 %.
- Tensile testing: JSCE dog-bone specimens tested at 7 d with DIC crack characterization.
- Bond testing: BS EN 1542 pull-off tests (surface roughness 1.2 mm sand patch).
- Rebar bond: RILEM RC6 pull-out tests (10 mm deformed rebar, embedment length 50 mm).

## Results

- Compressive strength: Exceeded 65 MPa at 7 d, reaching 75.3~81.9 MPa at 28 d (peaking at S/B 0.6).
- Fresh workability: Slump flow decreased from 162→116 mm with increasing S/B. In inverted slump tests, mixtures with S/B ≥ 0.6 retained self-weight without slumping (suitable for overhead repairs).
- Optimal mixture: S/B 0.8 provided the best compromise across mechanical properties, workability, and substrate/rebar bond.
