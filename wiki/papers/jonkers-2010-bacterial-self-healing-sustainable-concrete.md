# Bacterial Self-Healing Concrete via Alkaliphilic *Bacillus* Spores and Calcium Lactate Biomineralization

## Summary

To extend the service life of concrete structures and reduce global $\text{CO}_2$ emissions from cement manufacturing, Professor Henk M. Jonkers and Erik Schlangen at TU Delft developed a bacterial self-healing concrete. The system incorporates **alkaliphilic endospore-forming bacteria (*Bacillus pseudofirmus*, *Bacillus cohnii*, $6\times 10^8\ \text{spores/cm}^3$)** capable of surviving in high-alkali concrete ($\text{pH} \approx 11\sim 13$) in a dormant state alongside an organic carbon/calcium source, **calcium lactate ($\text{CaC}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_6$)**. Upon crack formation, ingressing water and oxygen activate the bacterial spores, precipitating **$20\sim 80\ \mu\text{m}$ calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystals that autonomously seal the crack**. The authors established an ammonia-free metabolic pathway where 1 mole of calcium lactate produces **a total of 6 moles of $\text{CaCO}_3$** via secondary reaction between metabolic $\text{CO}_2$ and matrix portlandite ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$). Furthermore, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) demonstrated that matrix pore refinement ($0.1\sim 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$) during hydration restricts direct-mixed spore longevity, highlighting the need for protective encapsulation.

## Key Contributions

1. **First demonstration of bacterial self-healing via alkaliphilic spores in concrete**:
   - Successfully induced biomineralization in high-alkali ($\text{pH} > 11.5$) cement matrices using *B. pseudofirmus* and *B. cohnii* endospores.
2. **Formulation of a 2-step amplified calcium lactate metabolic cycle**:
   - Established an eco-friendly reaction yielding 6 moles of calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) per 1 mole of calcium lactate by consuming endogenous $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ without ammonia release.
3. **Microscopic observation (ESEM) of $20\sim 80\ \mu\text{m}$ calcite precipitation**:
   - Confirmed dense rhombohedral calcite crystals bridging and sealing microcrack gaps.
4. **Correlation between pore refinement (MIP) and spore viability (MPN)**:
   - Revealed that direct spore viability drops after 4 months (135 d) as pores shrink below $0.8\sim 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$, establishing the scientific rationale for carrier encapsulation.

## Methods

- **Bacterial strains and sporulation (Sections 2.1–2.3)**:
  - Strains: Alkaliphilic *Bacillus pseudofirmus* (DSM 8715), *Bacillus cohnii* (DSM 6307) (optimum pH 9.5~10.5, alkali limit pH 11.5).
  - Sporulation: Cultured at $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ $\rightarrow$ heat-shocked ($80\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 20 min) $\rightarrow$ spore suspension prepared ($6\times 10^8\ \text{spores/cm}^3$).
- **Specimens and mixture designs (Section 2.4 & Table 1)**:
  - Cement: CEM I 42.5 N, W/C = 0.40 and 0.50 ($40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm}$ cubes).
  - Healing agents: Spores ($6\times 10^8\ \text{spores/cm}^3$) + organic substrates (yeast extract 0.1 %, peptone 0.1 %, calcium acetate 0.5 %, calcium lactate 0.5 wt.%).
- **Analytical methods (Sections 2.4–2.5)**:
  - Viability: Most Probable Number (MPN) counts at 3, 7, 28, 135 d.
  - Pore structure: MIP (AutoPore IV, freeze-dried).
  - Microstructure: Environmental SEM (Philips XL30).
  - Compressive strength: Tested at 3, 7, 28 d.

### 2. Biomineralization reaction pathways and pore mechanics
- **Two-step calcite precipitation mechanism**:
  $$\text{Step 1: } \text{CaC}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_6 + 5\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow + 5\text{CO}_2 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  $$\text{Step 2: } 5\text{CO}_2 + 5\text{Ca(OH)}_2 \rightarrow 5\text{CaCO}_3\downarrow + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  Consuming calcium hydroxide fills cracks with dense calcite without neutralizing matrix pH.
- **Pore confinement on spore viability**: Beyond 28 d, C-S-H growth reduced $0.8\sim 1.0\ \mu\text{m}$ pores, physically crushing $1\ \mu\text{m}$ spores and reducing viable cell counts after 135 d.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper established the global foundational principles of bacterial self-healing concrete using alkaliphilic spore biomineralization.
