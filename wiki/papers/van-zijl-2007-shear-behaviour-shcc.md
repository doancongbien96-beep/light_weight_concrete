## Summary

A study elucidating the two-dimensional plane shear behavior and shear transfer mechanism of strain-hardening cement-based composites (SHCC) through the Ohmori panel shear test and the Iosipescu direct shear test. Unlike conventional concrete, which develops diagonal tension cracks early under shear load and fails in brittle shear, SHCC was proven to form multiple fine inclined cracks along the principal tensile stress direction, exhibiting pseudo shear yielding and ultra-high-ductility shear strain hardening ($\gamma_u > 3.0 \%$). A bridging stress constitutive model under biaxial stress states was established to present a seismic design theory for shear walls.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of the pure shear strain-hardening ($\tau-\gamma$) behavior of SHCC**: demonstration of ultra-high-ductility shear deformation capacity, with shear strain reaching $3.5 \sim 5.0 \%$ from post-yield shear up to failure.
- **Elucidation of the inclined multiple fine-crack shear bridging mechanism**: a dense network of inclined microcracks orthogonal to the principal tensile stress axis forms, maintaining shear transfer through fiber bridging alone without aggregate interlock.
- **Feasibility of reduced shear reinforcement in shear wall design secured**: a high nominal shear strength at the level of $V_n > 0.4 \sqrt{f_c'}$ is secured even without shear reinforcement bars.

## Methods

- **Materials**: PVA-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.26$).
- **Testing technique**: Ohmori panel shear testing machine ($250 \times 250 \times 30 mm$ panels), displacement-controlled pure shear loading.
- **Instrumentation**: shear strain ($\gamma$) and principal stress analysis via rosette strain gauges and biaxial LVDTs.

## Results

- Pure shear strength $\tau_u = 5.8 MPa$ achieved — about $1.2\times$ the uniaxial tensile strength of $4.8 MPa$.
- Stable shear energy dissipation without diagonal-tension brittle failure confirmed up to an ultimate shear strain of $\gamma_u = 4.2 \%$.
