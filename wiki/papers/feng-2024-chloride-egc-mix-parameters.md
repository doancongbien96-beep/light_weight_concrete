# Chloride Erosion Resistance and Mixture Parameters of Fly Ash/Slag-Based EGC

## Summary

This study investigated the enhancement of chloride erosion resistance in fly ash-based EGC through GGBS incorporation. The effects of GGBS content (40~100 %), water-to-binder ratio W/B (0.32~0.38), alkali activator dosage (4~7 %), and PE fiber content (1.0~2.5 vol %) on chloride diffusion coefficients, erosion depth, and strength loss were evaluated through 120 d immersion tests. The optimized EGC simultaneously achieved an apparent chloride diffusion coefficient of 0.0661×10⁻¹² m²/s, compressive strength of 70~80 MPa, tensile strength of 5~6 MPa, and tensile strain capacity of 8 %.

## Key Contributions

1. **GGBS content effect**: Increasing GGBS stimulated C-A-S-H formation, reducing porosity (24.13 $\rightarrow$ 13.41 %) and critical pore diameter (5.16 $\rightarrow$ 3.29 nm), thereby enhancing chloride resistance. Hydrotalcite binding of $\text{Cl}^-$ without structural breakdown served as an additional immobilization mechanism. However, tensile strain capacity decreased when GGBS exceeded 60 % (8.15 $\rightarrow$ 3.89 %) because fine fly ash particles no longer filled the fiber-matrix interface to facilitate smooth pullout.
2. **W/B effect**: Lowering W/B improved chloride resistance (diffusion coefficient growth from 30 to 120 d was 96 % vs 282 %). At W/B = 0.38, tensile strength dropped abruptly by 33.68 %, establishing an upper bound for mixture design.
3. **Optimum alkali activator dosage at 6 %**: Promoted Si and Al dissolution to maximize C-A-S-H and minimize porosity (10.06 %); however, 7 % activator caused premature precipitation of aluminosilicate gels and skinning effects.
4. **Optimum PE fiber volume fraction at 2.0 %**: Crack bridging restrained crack propagation and maximized chloride impermeability. Dosages of 2.5 % increased void contents due to poor dispersion. Post-erosion tensile strain capacity increased by up to 59 % due to interfacial relaxation facilitating fiber pullout.
5. **Preservation of strain-hardening**: All mixtures retained strain-hardening characteristics following chloride erosion.

## Methods

- Anhydrous sodium metasilicate activator, PE fibers (diameter 19~43 μm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa).
- ASTM C1543 3 % NaCl ponding/immersion for 30, 60, 90, and 120 days.
- Depth-dependent powder sampling (2~35 mm) with Fick's second law fitting in MATLAB to determine apparent chloride diffusion coefficients. MIP, XRD, and SEM microstructural analysis.

## Results

- Compressive strength loss was driven by the formation of expansive oxychloride complex salts ($\text{CaCl}_2\cdot\text{Ca(OH)}_2\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
- Friedel's salt crystallized primarily after 90 days, contributing minimally to early-age chloride binding.
- Eroded PE-2.0 specimens exhibited 1.5 times the strength and 3 times the strain capacity of unexposed standard ECC reported in literature.
