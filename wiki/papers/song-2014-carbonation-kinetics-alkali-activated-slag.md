# Accelerated Carbonation Kinetics, C-S-H Decalcification, and Microstructural Transition of Alkali-Activated Blast-Furnace Slag (AAS) Mortar as a Function of Alkali Activator Content ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ 3~7 wt.%)

## Summary

A pioneering landmark AAS carbonation kinetics source paper by the research team of Professors Bang Yeon Lee and Jin-Kyu Song at Chonnam National University, which — targeting durability verification for real-structure application of alkali-activated slag (AAS: Alkali-Activated Slag) mortar (W/B = 0.485, S/B = 2.75), a replacement for the greenhouse-gas-intensive ordinary Portland cement (OPC) — integratedly performed the initial flow of the mortar and its compressive strength development as functions of the dosage of the powdered sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$) alkali activator (3 wt.%, 5 wt.%, and 7 wt.% of slag by mass, as $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ equivalent), together with the carbonation penetration depth ($d_c$) under an **accelerated carbonation chamber (temperature $20\pm 0.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, relative humidity $70\pm 2\%$, $\text{CO}_2$ concentration 5.0 %, continuous exposure for 7/14/21 days)**, the residual compressive strengths before and after carbonation, and **XRD, FT-NIR (Fourier-transform near-infrared spectroscopy), DTG (derivative thermogravimetric analysis), and ESEM (environmental scanning electron microscopy)** analysis. The results elucidated the fundamental reaction mechanism: in contrast to OPC, whose hydration product $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ carbonates to precipitate calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) within the pores and thereby exhibits densification behavior with post-carbonation compressive strength **increasing by 40.5 % from $32.1 \rightarrow 45.1\ \text{MPa}$**, AAS contains no free calcium hydroxide, so its principal hydration product, low-basicity C-S-H(I) gel, is itself carbonated, loses calcium, and transforms into porous amorphous silica gel ($\text{SiO}_2\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O}$), reducing the post-carbonation compressive strength **by 32.0 % from $42.5 \rightarrow 28.9\ \text{MPa}$**. However, when the activator content was raised to 7 wt.% (G7), the initial C-S-H matrix became densified and the 21-day carbonation depth dropped drastically by 64.7 %, from **$25.2\ \text{mm} \rightarrow 8.9\ \text{mm}$**, and a precise exponential correlation between the compressive strength of AAS mortar and its carbonation depth, **$d_c = 56.28 \exp(-0.074 f_c)$ ($R^2 = 0.9986$)**, was established.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the fundamental differences between OPC and AAS in carbonation reaction pathway and strength change mechanism**:
   - ESEM and FT-NIR fully demonstrate that OPC gains strength by 40.5 % through the $\text{Ca(OH)}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3$ reaction, whereas AAS collapses into amorphous silica gel through C-S-H decalcification, losing 32 % of its strength.
2. **Quantification of the 2.8-fold increase in carbonation resistance with activator dosage ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ 3% $\rightarrow$ 7%)**:
   - Demonstrates that as the alkali activator content increases, the hydration reaction rate and matrix densification improve sharply, reducing the 21-day carbonation depth by 64.7 % from $25.2\ \text{mm} \rightarrow 8.9\ \text{mm}$.
3. **Establishment of an ultra-high-precision exponential prediction model ($R^2 = 0.9986$) relating AAS carbonation depth to compressive strength**:
   - Through the equation $d_c = 56.28 e^{-0.074 f_c}$, completes a durability design model that predicts the carbonation depth of AAS members from non-destructive strength measurements with 99.8 % reliability.
4. **Establishment of a four-way microstructural transition analysis system based on FT-NIR, DTG, and ESEM**:
   - Visualizes the formation of the $1420\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ carbonate peak, quantifies the DTG calcite decarbonation peak at $500\sim 800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and captures the enlargement of fine pores on fracture surfaces.

## Methods

- **Material specifications and mix design (Tables 1~2, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: ordinary Portland cement (OPC Type I, specific surface area $301\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$) vs. ground granulated blast-furnace slag powder (GGBS, specific surface area $430\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$, $\text{CaO}$ 42.09 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 33.58 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 12.70 %).
  - Powder-type alkali activator: anhydrous sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$, purity 99 %).
  - Mix proportions: water-to-binder ratio W/B = 0.485 and silica-sand-to-binder ratio S/B = 2.75, fixed.
  - Four mix groups:
    1. OPC (control group): OPC 100 %.
    2. G3: GGBS 94 wt.% + Activator 6 wt.% ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ equivalent 3.0 wt.%).
    3. G5: GGBS 90 wt.% + Activator 10 wt.% ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ equivalent 5.0 wt.%).
    4. G7: GGBS 86 wt.% + Activator 14 wt.% ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ equivalent 7.0 wt.%).
- **Experimental equipment and accelerated carbonation test (Figures 1~3)**:
  - Accelerated carbonation chamber: temperature $20\pm 0.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, humidity $70\pm 2\%$, $\text{CO}_2$ 5.0 %, exposure for 7, 14, and 21 days.
  - Carbonation depth: measured after spraying 1.0 % phenolphthalein on the split surface of $50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ cubes.
  - Microstructure: PANalytical XRD, PerkinElmer Spectrum 400 FT-NIR ($4000\sim 400\ \text{cm}^{-1}$), Setaram TG92 DTG ($25\sim 1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $10\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$), Quanta 200 FEG ESEM/EDX.

### 2. Interpretation of decalcification chemical reactions and microstructure
- **Carbonation kinetics equation model**: $d_c = 56.276 \exp(-0.074 f_c)$ ($R^2 = 0.9986$); as the compressive strength of AAS mortar increases 2.85-fold from 14.9 MPa (G3) to 42.5 MPa (G7), the carbonation penetration depth decreases exponentially.
- **DTG and FT-NIR analysis**: owing to its high-density C-S-H structure, the G7 mix retains a substantial fraction of the residual C-S-H bonding peak ($960\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) even after carbonation, preserving 28.9 MPa of structural residual strength.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper by Professor Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University is a representative landmark source paper that elucidated the carbonation kinetics and decalcification mechanism of cement-free alkali-activated slag mortar through world-class physicochemical analysis, establishing both activator-content control and an exponential prediction model.
