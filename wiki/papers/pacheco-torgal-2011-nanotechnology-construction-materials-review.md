# Comprehensive Review of Nanotechnology in Construction and Building Materials (Nano-Silica/CNT/Nano-$\text{TiO}_2$): Performance Enhancement, Photocatalytic Self-Cleaning, and Nanotoxicity Mechanisms

## Summary

A highest-authority landmark review paper published in Construction and Building Materials in 2011 by **Professor Fernando Pacheco-Torgal and Professor Said Jalali of the University of Minho, Portugal**, which provides a comprehensive and balanced overview of the **core nanomaterials applied to cement concrete and building envelope materials (nano-silica nano-$\text{SiO}_2$, carbon nanotubes CNT / carbon nanofibers CNF, photocatalytic nano-$\text{TiO}_2$, nano-$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, nanoclay)** — covering their hydration acceleration, nanopore filling, mechanical strength enhancement ($+50\sim 100\%$), ultra-high durability, self-cleaning and air-purification photocatalytic action, nanoindentation-based C-S-H gel elastic modulus analysis, and the **environmental nanotoxicity (Nanotoxicity) and occupational health and safety issues arising during manufacturing and construction** — with the aim of fundamentally innovating the performance limits of construction materials at the molecular and nanoscale and realizing smart sustainable construction.

## Key Contributions

1. **Full-scale compilation of the mechanisms of the five core nanomaterials inside cement composites**:
   - Perfectly organizes the working principles of 1) Nano-$\text{SiO}_2$ (ultra-rapid pozzolanic reaction and nano C-S-H nucleation), 2) CNT/CNF (nanoscale crack bridging and piezoresistive sensing), 3) Nano-$\text{TiO}_2$ (UV photocatalytic $\text{NO}_x$ degradation and superhydrophilic self-cleaning), 4) Nano-$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ (electromagnetic shielding), and 5) Nanoclay (rheology control and shrinkage suppression).
2. **Quantification of early-age strength and durability amplification through nano-silica incorporation**:
   - Establishes that with $1\sim 3\ \text{wt.}\%$ nano-silica addition, the 3-day early compressive strength increases by $50\%$ and the chloride diffusion coefficient plummets by $70\%$.
3. **Elucidation of the photocatalytic air-purification and self-cleaning mechanism of nano-$\text{TiO}_2$**:
   - Systematizes the process by which surface-coated $\text{TiO}_2$ particles absorb solar ultraviolet radiation to generate hydroxyl radicals ($\cdot\text{OH}$), neutralizing atmospheric nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_x$) and organic pollutants into water and nitrates.
4. **First public articulation of the inhalation toxicity and environmental risks (Drawbacks) of nanomaterials**:
   - Points out the asbestos-like dust inhalation hazard of CNTs and the potential leaching of nanoparticles into groundwater, emphasizing the need for safe handling guidelines.

## Methods

- **Scope of the reviewed literature and topics analyzed (Sections 2~5 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Analysis of a total of 88 primary sources at the cutting edge of nanomaterials/construction science.
  - Core nanotechnologies covered:

### 2. Interpretation of nanoscale C-S-H gel modification and photocatalytic reaction mechanisms
- **Acceleration of C-S-H seeding by nano-silica**: nano-$\text{SiO}_2$ particles rapidly consume calcium hydroxide ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) crystals, converting them into a high-density, high-strength C-S-H gel and completely sealing off the nanopores.
- **Photocatalytic self-cleaning surfaces**: $\text{TiO}_2$-coated surfaces exposed to sunlight develop a superhydrophilic contact angle approaching $0^\circ$, so that rainwater penetrates beneath the contaminants and washes the surface dust away on its own.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the highest-authority landmark review in which Professor Fernando Pacheco-Torgal, for the first time, provided a balanced and comprehensive overview of both the mechanical and environmental innovations of nanotechnology in the field of construction materials and its potential nanotoxicity risks, thereby supplying essential guidance for nano-construction-material research and the establishment of safety standards.
