## Summary
To elucidate the tensile creep mechanism that mitigates the high autogenous shrinkage and drying shrinkage stresses undergone by ambient-cured eco-friendly geopolymer composites (PVA-EGC) at early ages (1 ~ 7 days), the research team of Dr. H. Zhu identified in 2024 the tensile creep strain under sustained tensile stress ratios ($\sigma/f_t = 0.30 \sim 0.60$), the stress relaxation coefficient, and the restrained ring shrinkage cracking resistance per ASTM C1581. It was demonstrated that the early-age tensile creep of PVA-EGC relieved the restrained tensile stress by more than **72 %** through relaxation driven by its highly viscoelastic N-A-S-H gel flow, blocking the occurrence of early-age cracking at the source.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of an early-age tensile creep model dedicated to ambient-cured geopolymers**: formalization of the viscoelastic relaxation coefficient of aluminosilicate gel.
- **Elucidation of the mechanism relieving restrained shrinkage stress by 72 %**: the pronounced spontaneous creep characteristics of the geopolymer fully offset the shrinkage cracking risk.
- **Verification of crack-free stability through the ASTM C1581 ring test**: zero through-cracks achieved even after more than 90 days under restrained shrinkage.
- **Establishment of design criteria for jointless eco-friendly road pavements and tunnel linings**: securing the reliability of early-age crack control.

## Methods
- Mix: PVA-EGC (GGBS 50 % + fly ash 50 %, liquid sodium silicate, Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber $V_f = 2.0 \%$).
- Tensile creep test: temperature $23 ± 1 °C, RH 60 \%$, sustained stress ratios 0.3 and 0.5 $f_t$, loading ages of 1, 3, and 7 days, displacement measured for 90 days.
- Restrained ring test: continuous measurement with strain gauges on the inner circumferential surface of a standard steel ring per ASTM C1581.
