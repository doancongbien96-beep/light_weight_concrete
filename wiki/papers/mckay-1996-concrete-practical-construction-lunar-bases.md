## Summary

The original ASCE Space 96 paper in which Dr. David S. McKay's research team at NASA Johnson Space Center (JSC) comprehensively evaluated field production and practical construction of lunar concrete, combining the technology for producing water ($H_2O$) on site through hydrogen reduction of ilmenite ($FeTiO_3$) in lunar soil (regolith) with a process for manufacturing sintered cement from crushed local rock, toward constructing the large shielding domes, launch pads, and habitation facilities of a permanent crewed base on the lunar surface. The paper demonstrated that the mass of hydrogen ($H_2$) that must be transported from Earth to produce 1 ton of concrete amounts to only **0.4 %** of the total weight of the concrete, establishing lunar concrete as the most economical space construction material, cutting transport costs by more than 95 % relative to metal or composite structures.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of an ISRU-based concrete mixing-water production cycle via hydrogen reduction**: mixing water $100 \%$ procured locally through the reaction $FeTiO_3 + H_2 \rightarrow Fe + TiO_2 + H_2O$.
- **Presentation of a process for synthesizing an aluminosilicate cement binder by high-temperature sintering of lunar regolith**: extraction of inorganic binder through concentrated-solar-furnace heating at $1200 °C$.
- **Establishment of mixing/placing protocols for ultra-low gravity (1/6 g) and vacuum**: design of a sealed rotating-drum mixer and a vapor-pressure-controlled curing chamber.
- **Sizing of space radiation (GCR, SPE) and micrometeorite shielding thickness**: an annual allowable dose below $50 mSv$ achieved with $0.5 m$ of concrete plus $1.5 m$ of regolith cover.

## Methods

- Chemical composition analysis of Apollo 15 and 17 regolith and property evaluation of the JSC-1 lunar simulant.
- Mass balance and energy-efficiency computational analysis of the hydrogen-reduction chemical reactor.
