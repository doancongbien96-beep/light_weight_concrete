# Mechanical and Crack-Healing Comparison of PE-PVA Hybrid SHCC under Natural and Underwater Conditions

## Summary

An experimental paper that directly compares the mechanical and crack-healing performance of an identical-mix PE-PVA hybrid fiber SHCC placed under two conditions, underwater curing (M-W) and outdoor natural exposure (M-O) (Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University co-author; Yoon Yong Kim of Chungnam National University corresponding author). The underwater condition excelled in both mechanical and healing performance; the crack-width threshold of underwater healing was about 60 μm, whereas healing in the natural environment was practically limited.

## Key Contributions

1. Direct-comparison design holding the mix fully identical with environmental condition as the sole variable — natural exposure (N-C) came out -10 % in compressive strength, -4 % in tensile strength, and -3 % in tensile strain capacity relative to underwater (U-C).
2. Definition and application of a healing rate γr = (1 - Wc-h/Wc-i) × 100 %: after 28 days of healing, the underwater γr was 38.6 times the natural-environment value. The underwater healing crack-width threshold was ≈ 60 μm (consistent with literature-reported values), while the residual crack width in the natural condition remained essentially unchanged.
3. Stiffness recovery (resonant-frequency RF method): normalized RF dropped to roughly one fifth after pre-cracking → after 28 days of healing, recovery stood at +27 %p for M-W and +12 %p for M-O relative to the undamaged initial value. The natural condition still showed relative stiffness recovery but lacked crack closure — the authors suggest healing products may have been washed out by rainfall.
4. Post-healing (reloaded) tensile behavior: both conditions kept strain-hardening; tensile strength was essentially unchanged (7.93→7.72 / 7.60→7.54 MPa) while strain capacity fell by about one quarter (8.00→5.85 % / 7.74→5.79 %) — attributed to increased fiber-matrix interfacial bond from healing products → the logic of reduced crack width and reduced crack number.
5. Crack-pattern differences: the underwater condition showed +61 % in crack number (67.1 vs 41.6 cracks), -37 % in average crack width (95.6 vs 151.2 μm), and -39 % in crack spacing (1.20 vs 1.96 mm) — ωc < 0.15 mm, superior against the ACI 318 exterior-use limit of 0.30 mm.

## Methods

Type I cement with 50 wt% GGBS replacement, w/b 0.4, crumb-rubber powder (40 mesh) 0.1 + silica sand 0.5 by mass ratio, expansive agent 0.05, PE 12 mm × 16 μm (2700 MPa, E 88 GPa) 1.25 vol% + PVA 8 mm × 40 μm (1560 MPa, E 41 GPa) 0.10 vol% (1.35 % in total). Demolded after 2 days at 23 °C ± 3 °C and cured under each condition up to 28 days. At an age of 28 days the specimens were pre-cracked in tension at 3 % strain, then given 28 further days of healing. Natural exposure record: days 3–28 brought 8 rain days · 143 mm · average 28.5 °C · RH 75.2 %; days 29–56 brought 18 rain days · 274 mm · average 25.5 °C · RH 84.3 %. Measurements: density (mass in air/water), compression (ASTM C109/109M, 50 mm cubes), tension (JSCE recommendation, 80 mm gauge dumbbells), crack width (digital microscope at 84× magnification), RF recovery, and reloaded tension testing at 56 days.

## Results

| Item | M-W (underwater) | M-O (natural) |
|---|---|---|
| fcr (MPa) | 3.74 ± 0.19 | 3.13 ± 0.43 |
| fts (MPa) | 7.93 ± 0.62 | 7.60 ± 0.31 |
| εc (%) | 8.00 ± 0.69 | 7.74 ± 0.63 |
| Et (MPa m/m) | 0.46 | 0.41 |
| Crack number ηc | 67.1 ± 6.0 | 41.6 ± 6.6 |
| Mean crack width ωc (μm) | 95.6 ± 5.6 | 151.2 ± 17.6 |
| Crack spacing lc (mm) | 1.20 ± 0.11 | 1.96 ± 0.26 |
| Post-healing fts-h (MPa) | 7.72 ± 0.18 | 7.54 ± 0.30 |
| Post-healing εc-h (%) | 5.85 ± 0.13 | 5.79 ± 0.05 |

- Density: theory-experiment difference of 0 % for M-W vs 2.0 % for M-O (higher porosity in the M-O matrix).
- fcr/fc of 8.3 %/7.7 % (ordinary-concrete level) and fts/fc ≈ 20 % (room left for structural-performance improvement).
- Underwater healing: small cracks (Wc ≤ 50 μm) and large cracks (Wc > 50 μm) alike were sealed with crystalline products, with residual crack width near zero at the final stage. Natural healing: over an initial Wc range of 30~210 μm the residual crack width stayed essentially unchanged.
- The natural condition, with rainfall and solar radiation included, acted through insufficient moisture supply or a washing-out effect on healing products — concluding suitability for underwater infrastructure.
