## Summary
This study elucidated the flexural behavior, interfacial debonding resistance, and crack-control capability of reinforced concrete (RC) beam members whose tensile-side cover concrete had deteriorated and suffered crack damage, when repaired by section patch repair with ductile fiber reinforced cementitious composite (DFRCC / ECC), through full-scale flexural loading tests and fracture mechanics analysis. Whereas specimens repaired with ordinary polymer repair mortar suffered premature shear debonding at the repair-material interface driven by stress concentration at the tips of existing cracks, the DFRCC repair layer suppressed interfacial debonding through high tensile bridging stress and formed multiple fine cracks, greatly improving the ultimate flexural capacity and energy dissipation capacity of the beam member.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the repair-to-substrate interface stress-relief mechanism**: the tensile strain capacity of the DFRCC layer ($> 3.0 \%$) disperses the stress concentration at the tips of substrate RC cracks into numerous fine bridging zones, cutting off interfacial debonding.
- **Quantification of flexural strength and ductility recovery ratios**: compared with an undamaged reference RC beam, the DFRCC-repaired beam showed a 15 % higher yield load, a 28 % higher ultimate load, and 2.1 times greater fracture energy dissipation.
- **Optimal section design guide according to repair thickness**: demonstrated that maximum strengthening efficiency is achieved once an effective repair thickness ($h_{rep}/h \approx 0.15 \sim 0.25$) is secured.

## Methods
- **Material mix**: PVA-DFRCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.28$, compressive strength $48 MPa$, tensile strength $4.8 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $3.8 \%$).
- **Specimens**: $150 \times 200 \times 1800 mm$ RC beams (tensile reinforcement ratio 0.78 %).
- **Repair method**: after chipping away 30 mm and 50 mm of the tensile-side cover concrete, the DFRCC was wet-cast as the repair material. Four-point flexural loading test (span 1500 mm).

## Results
- Ordinary mortar-repaired beam: interfacial debonding cracks initiated at a load of $35 kN$, and after reaching a peak load of $52.4 kN$ the beam failed in sudden brittle fashion.
- DFRCC-repaired beam: achieved a peak load of $72.8 kN$, with more than 35 fine cracks ($w < 0.05 mm$) distributed across the lower DFRCC repair layer, securing stable flexural yielding behavior without failure.
