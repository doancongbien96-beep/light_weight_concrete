## Summary
A research paper from Tongji University and The Hong Kong Polytechnic University that developed a micromechanics mix tailoring technique for low-fiber UHP-ECC achieving a compressive strength of 120 MPa, a direct tensile strength of 12 MPa, and a tensile strain capacity of **6.0 %** while cutting the dosage of expensive ultra-high molecular weight PE fibers from the conventional 2.0 vol % to **1.0 ~ 1.5 vol %**, in order to maximize the practical applicability and economy of ultra-high performance strain-hardening composites. Slag-silica fume fine particle packing homogenized matrix flaws and induced a fiber surface friction coefficient ($\beta = 0.80$), reducing fiber cost by **40 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Micromechanics PSH design criteria for low-fiber ($V_f = 1.2 \%$) UHP-ECC established**: energy index $J_b'/J_{tip} = 5.5$ secured.
- **Compressive strength of 120 MPa and tensile strain capacity of 6.0 % demonstrated**: ultra-high strength-ultra-high ductility preserved even under reduced fiber content.
- **Material cost cut by 38 %**: commercialization economics secured for large bridge decks and expansion joints.
- **Tensile energy absorption density of $420 kJ/m^3$ achieved**: protective and seismic structural material performance requirements satisfied.

## Methods
- Matrix: cement + silica fume (20 %) + ground slag powder (30 %), $W/B = 0.18$, micro silica sand.
- Fiber: UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa).
- Tests: dogbone direct tension test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), 28 d compressive strength, single fiber pullout test.
