# Tensile creep of SHCC

## Summary

Pioneering study of the time-dependent behavior of SHCC by Boshoff–van Zijl of Stellenbosch
University (conference presentation, July 2007). To elucidate the mechanism of tensile creep of
SHCC, they carried out a four-stage experimental program: (1) uncracked tensile creep plus
drying shrinkage, (2) creep of specimens pre-cracked to 1 % strain under various load levels,
(3) creep of single-fiber pull-out, and (4) creep of the fiber itself. Conclusion: tensile creep
of SHCC consists of three components — matrix creep, time-dependent fiber pull-out, and
(additional) formation of new multiple cracks — and they isolated the finding that the creep
increase of a cracked cross-section comes not from creep of the fiber itself but from
time-dependent pull-out. Creep of the cracked zone increases exponentially rather than linearly
with load level.

## Key Contributions

- Decomposition of creep sources into three elements (matrix creep + time-dependent fiber
  pull-out + additional multiple-crack formation): the classification that became the standard
  framework for subsequent SHCC long-term behavior research.
- Quantified evidence that cracking governs creep: at 50 % load the creep strain of a pre-cracked
  (1 % strain) specimen was about 4x that of an uncracked specimen. When one specimen cracked
  about 4 hours into an uncracked test, its creep rate surged — direct demonstration that the
  number of cracks is itself a creep variable.
- Nonlinearity of load-level dependence: 8-month creep compliance Cc rose exponentially from
  0.49 at 30 %, 2.27 at 50 %, and 3.94 at 70 % to 13.39 mm/m/MPa at 80 % — underscoring the
  importance of managing service-load levels.
- Experimental proof excluding fiber creep: even holding a PVA monofilament at 800 MPa (half its
  capacity) for 60 hours produced no significant elongation (below the 0.5 %/60 h baseline). By
  contrast, fibers embedded in the matrix all pulled out completely within 80 hours under a creep
  load of 50 % of the static pull-out resistance — time-dependent pull-out is the governing
  mechanism.
- Debonded state accelerates creep: a fiber group fully debonded before creep loading showed a
  higher creep rate than the group left bonded — residual bond governs the time-dependent
  component of pull-out resistance.
- Drying shrinkage of uncracked SHCC measured at about 0.23 % over 8 months — interpreted as
  absorbable against a tensile ductility of 5 %, with the caveat that the unsealed test condition
  means this includes drying creep.

## Methods

- Mix: W/B 0.4, aggregate-to-binder 0.5, CEM I 42.5 : PozzFill fly ash : Corex slag = 45 : 50 : 5
  by mass, fine aggregate, PVA fiber (length 12 mm, diameter 40 μm) at 2 vol %,
  superplasticizer + viscosity modifier. Tested after 14-day water curing.
- Tensile creep: gauge section 16 × 30 mm, gauge length 88 mm with two LVDTs fixed on aluminum
  frames. Lever-arm-and-weight loading of two specimens in series. Climate room at 23 ± 1 °C and
  RH 65 ± 5 %, unsealed condition. Loaded to 50 % (~1.39 MPa) of the static ultimate stress of
  2.79 MPa.
- Pre-cracked creep: loaded under Zwick machine displacement control up to 1 % tensile strain and
  unloaded; then 30/50/70/80 % load levels × 2 specimens each, 8 total. Crack widths photographed
  over time.
- Fiber pull-out creep: embedded lengths 1.3~1.5 mm, 10 N load cell; creep load of 1.2 MPa set as
  50 % of the average interfacial shear resistance τ0 = 2.4 MPa (established in rate-dependent
  pull-out tests on the same mix). Bonded group of 3 + pre-debonded group of 3.
- Fiber creep: microscope tracking of two points along the fiber, exploiting 40 μm scale marks.

## Results

- Uncracked: shrinkage dominated the response, with the creep envelope valid only after shrinkage
  subtraction. The specimen that cracked during the test was excluded from envelope derivation.
- Cracked zone: repeated sudden strain jumps within the first several hours — interpreted as the
  signal of new crack initiation. Photos of the 80 % specimen confirmed crack widths widening
  over time.
- Pull-out creep: all 6 fibers pulled out completely within 80 hours. Time to complete pull-out
  varied widely between individuals.
- Implication: predicting long-term deflection and deformation of SHCC in its multiple-cracked
  state requires reflecting both crack count and load level together, and a creep safety-margin
  concept relative to static strength is needed.
