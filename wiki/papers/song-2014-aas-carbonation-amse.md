# Carbonation Characteristics of AAS Mortar (English Extended Version)

## Summary

User-group paper (Chonnam National University, Bang Yeon Lee co-author). The English extended version (international publication of the 2012 Korean-language paper song-2012-aas-carbonation-characteristics) in which AAS mortars activated with sodium silicate powder (G3·G5·G7, Na₂O 3~7 %) and OPC mortar were subjected to accelerated carbonation at CO₂ 5 %, and the changes in hydration products were identified through XRD, FT-NIR, DTG, and ESEM. Whereas OPC, thanks to its portlandite buffer, actually gains strength under carbonation, the paper demonstrated at the product level that AAS loses strength as CSH collapses into amorphous silica gel.

## Key Contributions

- Precise confirmation of the simplicity of AAS hydration products: CSH plus minor alumina compounds (albite, sodium aluminium silica). The absence of portlandite is the direct cause of rapid carbonation.
- ESEM evidence of post-carbonation microstructural collapse: AAS shows a CSH→amorphous silica gel transition plus complete disappearance of alumina compounds with fabric loosening, whereas OPC shows needle-shaped products filling the pores.
- DTG tracking of the chemically bound water dehydration peak of silica gel (300~800 °C) — proposes a thermal-analysis index of the degree of carbonation.
- Activator dosage effect: Na₂O 7 % (G7) increases CSH per unit volume and densifies the fabric, improving carbonation resistance, with a 21-day depth comparable to OPC.

## Methods

- Mix: GGBS (Blaine 430 m²/kg, mean particle size 8.5 µm, basicity 1.81), W/B 0.485, S/B 2.75. Sodium silicate powder activator at 6/10/14 % by mass of binder (Na₂O 3/5/7 %).
- Compressive strength and carbonation depth measured after accelerated carbonation; paste specimens analyzed by XRD, FT-NIR, DTG, and ESEM (×2,000).

## Results

- The carbonation depth of AAS advances faster than OPC, but G7 is at a level similar to OPC at 21 days.
- Strength: OPC tends to increase after carbonation, while AAS (G3) decreases — mitigated at G7.
