## Summary
This is the original paper in which Professors Seong-Tae Shin, Bang Yeon Lee, Jin-Wook Bang, and Yun-Yong Kim elucidated, through four-point flexural testing of full-scale beams and nonlinear sectional analysis, the strengthening mechanism whereby casting a high-ductility fiber reinforced cementitious composite (PVA-DFRCC) layer as an overlay on the lower tensile side of reinforced concrete (RC) beam members degraded in service or deficient in seismic/flexural capacity restores flexural capacity, crack dispersion, and deflection ductility. It demonstrated that the ultimate flexural capacity increases by up to **42 %** depending on the DFRCC strengthening thickness ($t = 20, 40 mm$) and the interface treatment applied to the substrate concrete (chipping, epoxy primer), and that the main crack opening width is controlled perfectly to $0.05 mm$ or less.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the flexural capacity and ductility increase delivered by DFRCC tensile overlay strengthening**: the tensile bridging membrane of the DFRCC absorbs the crack-tip stress of the brittle substrate, improving flexural moment capacity by 42 %.
- **Elucidation of the mechanism suppressing shear debonding at the substrate–DFRCC interface**: securing surface chipping (Chipping) roughness mobilizes an interfacial shear strength of 2.8 MPa or higher, maintaining fully composite section behavior.
- **Demonstration of crack control and durability restoration**: tensile cracks at the bottom of the substrate are converted into more than 60 finely dispersed cracks inside the DFRCC layer.
- **Presentation of a practical design formula for flexural strengthening of aging structures**: establishment of a strengthening-thickness determination formula through a segmented sectional nonlinear model.

## Methods
- 8 full-scale RC beams ($150 \times 250 \times 2000 mm$, span 1700 mm, tension reinforcement 2-D13, $f_y = 420 MPa$).
- Strengthening material: PVA-DFRCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $f_c = 46.5 MPa$, direct tensile strength $4.8 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $3.2 \%$).
- Strengthening thickness: $t = 0, 20, 40 mm$ (cast on the beam tensile side).
- Testing: four-point flexural loading test, LVDT deflection measurement, and full-field DIC analysis of cracking and interfacial slip.
