# Fiber-optic sensor smart bridges — EFPI instrumentation and two field applications

## Summary

An overview article on fiber-optic smart structures published in the June 2003 issue of IEEE Instrumentation & Measurement Magazine (not a journal research paper in the conventional form). It organizes the principles of fiber-optic strain sensing, particularly extrinsic Fabry-Perot interferometric (EFPI) sensors, and introduces two real bridge applications: the all-FRP composite bridge at the University of Missouri-Rolla (UMR) campus and an I-44 overpass pier-cap repair site. The framework of the argument is that permanent instrumentation, once proven reliable, lowers the risk of introducing new materials such as FRP.

## Key Contributions

1. **EFPI sensor system organized**: multiple-reflection interference occurs in the air-gap space between two fiber end faces aligned by a capillary tube; gauge length is set by the capillary length (typically under 1 cm), suiting point measurement of local strain. A single-ended configuration based on the reflected signal is insensitive to environmental noise, and a multi-wavelength demodulation scheme that yields absolute strain using a broadband source plus a wavelength demodulator is explained with figures.
2. **UMR smart composite bridge**: Missouri's first all-FRP bridge (shared pedestrian and highway load rating). A 9 m span I-beam was built by stacking 76 mm square pultruded carbon/glass tubes in seven cross-ply layers, balancing stiffness and cost through a balanced arrangement of carbon tubes (high stiffness, high cost) and glass tubes (low cost). EFPI sensors were embedded in surface grooves of the primary load-bearing layers (upper and lower carbon-FRPC tube layers).
3. **Field verification values**: the 4-layer specimen survived the entire failure test; the I-beam specimen correlated well with resistive strain gauges at identical locations; in the real bridge load test, errors versus finite element predictions were within 4 % at worst. Under passage of a pickup truck applying 8.4 % of the design rating of 142.4 kN (32,000 lb), elastic behavior without residual strain after loading-unloading was confirmed within a compressive strain range of -10 μɛ or less, and load-strain linearity was confirmed in a heavy truck test.
4. **I-44 overpass repair instrumentation**: a site where two RC piers and a pier cap damaged by vehicle impact were rebuilt and strengthened with carbon-FRP sheets. Crack-propagation sensors were attached at the base of three existing cracks in the pier cap; intentional debonding bubbles of 12 cm diameter were created in the tension-side FRP sheet to monitor propagation via debonding sensors; structural integrity was judged from signature strains of five sensors throughout the load tests.

## Methods

- Sensor installation protocol: surface cleaning (acetone) then epoxy bonding; groove embedding for assembly impact protection (surface grooves in concrete at I-44); lead wires routed along tube interfaces or inside transverse grooves and gathered into sealed patch boxes. Data acquisition used permanent sensor networks with equipment brought out only during testing, performing initial baselines and periodic remeasurement.
- Case 1 (composite bridge): laboratory loading of a 4-layer pre-failure specimen and a full-scale 7-layer I-beam (with LVDTs and resistive gauges alongside), field application of dump-truck-class loads, student experiments, and remote-sensing testbed operation.
- Case 2 (I-44): sensor placement per three monitoring targets (cracks, debonding, signature strain), environmental protection including caulking finishes.
- Support: NSF CRCD EEC-9872546, MODOT, UMR Center for Infrastructure Engineering Studies.

## Results

- Embedded fiber-optic sensors operated during and after destructive loading of reinforced concrete structures (confirmed indoors and in the field) — post-catastrophe survivability is their differentiator against electrical sensors.
- Composite bridge: sensor-to-FEM error below 4 %, agreement with resistive gauges, and small-load sensitivity confirmed. In moving-load responses, peak compressive strain at eccentric positions occurred at midspan crossing.
- I-44: successful baseline measurement of all sensors — subsequently functioning as a long-term deterioration monitoring system.
