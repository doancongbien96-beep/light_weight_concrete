## Summary

This is an early study that elucidated the extrusion rheology, two-dimensional in-plane fiber orientation, and flexural deflection-hardening performance of PVA short-fiber-reinforced cement sheet composites using a vacuum extrusion forming process. By incorporating a high-viscosity methylcellulose (HPMC) thickener together with fly ash and silica fume and imposing high shear deformation as the mixture passed through the extrusion die, the fibers were highly aligned along the direction of extrusion advance. Thereby, a flexural modulus of rupture (MOR) of $22 \sim 28 MPa$ and very high flexural toughness were achieved even at a low fiber content of $V_f = 1.0 \sim 2.0 \%$.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of the two-dimensional fiber orientation mechanism through shear flow in the extrusion die**: upon nozzle passage, the short fibers were aligned in the extrusion direction ($0°$) by the shear stress gradient, improving the effective fiber efficiency coefficient ($\eta_L$) to 0.75 or higher.
- **Rheological mix design of the extrudable paste**: through high-viscoelastic binder design, shape retention immediately after extrusion (green strength) was secured and a continuous production process without demolding established.
- **Enhanced matrix interfacial adhesion of the PVA fibers**: the extrusion vacuum degassing process removed interfacial micro-voids, increasing the frictional shear bond strength ($\tau_0$) by more than 30 %.

## Methods

- **Mix and materials**: cement + fly ash (30 %), silica fume (10 %), PVA fibers (lengths 6 mm, 12 mm, diameter 39 µm, $V_f = 1.0 \sim 2.0 \%$), HPMC (1.0 wt %).
- **Extrusion equipment**: two-axis screw vacuum extruder, slit die (thickness 8 mm, width 50 mm).
- **Test items**: 3-point and 4-point flexural tests, statistical analysis of fiber orientation angles by electron microscopy.

## Results

- Extruded panel with a fiber volume fraction of $V_f = 2.0 \%$: flexural strength $26.5 MPa$ and a flexural deflection ductility index of 12.5 developed (flexural strength 1.8× higher than cast placement).
- Fiber orientation analysis confirmed that the fraction of fibers aligned within $\pm 15°$ of the extrusion direction was 65 % or more.
