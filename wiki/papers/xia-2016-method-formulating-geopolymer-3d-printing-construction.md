# Four-Stage Mix Design Methodology for a Cement-Free Geopolymer Ink for 3D Concrete Printing (3DCP): Extrudability, Shape Retention, 20-Layer Formwork-Free Buildability, and Rheology Control Mechanisms

## Summary

To realize fully automated digital layered manufacturing without formwork (3D Construction Printing) and to suppress carbon emissions from cement production, a four-part printability evaluation framework for optimizing a fly ash and ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) based alkali-activated geopolymer binder as a dedicated 3D printing ink was established for the first time (Printability: 1. Extrudability, 2. Flow & Shape Retention, 3. Open Time, 4. Buildability). By precisely controlling the concentrations of the sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$) and sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$) activators, a nanoclay thixotropy-modifying admixture, and the fine aggregate gradation, the study achieved zero nozzle blockage during continuous extrusion through a $15\ \text{mm}$ nozzle, zero buckling collapse of lower layers during continuous stacking over 20 layers, dimensional accuracy within $2\%$, and a 28-day compressive strength of $35.0\ \text{MPa}$. This is the landmark 2016 Materials & Design source paper by the research group of Professor Jay Sanjayan and Dr. Ming Xia at Swinburne University of Technology, Australia.

## Key Contributions

1. **First formalization of a four-stage standard mix design methodology for geopolymer 3D printing in construction**:
   - Established a protocol that quantitatively judges, stage by stage, the essential engineering conditions of a printable geopolymer (extrudability $\rightarrow$ flowability $\rightarrow$ open time $\rightarrow$ buildability).
2. **Elucidation of geopolymer thixotropy and the rate of structural rebuilding (structuration rate)**:
   - Characterized the dynamic rheological behavior in which strong shear thinning during passage through the nozzle enables low-viscosity extrusion, followed by immediate recovery of shear stress immediately after deposition to retain shape.
3. **Demonstration of formwork-free self-supporting buildability over 20 layers**:
   - Derived the critical static yield stress condition ($\tau_{y0} \ge 1.5\ \text{kPa}$) under which lower layers neither compress nor buckle under the loads applied by subsequently deposited layers.
4. **Realization of a cement-free environmentally friendly digital construction system**:
   - Reduced carbon emissions by $80\%$ relative to ordinary Portland cement while securing a 28-day compressive strength of $35.0\ \text{MPa}$ and a flexural strength of $5.5\ \text{MPa}$ for 3D-printed structural members.

## Methods

- **Materials and geopolymer ink formulation (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Raw materials were Class F fly ash ($60\sim 80\ \text{wt.}\%$) blended with ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) ($20\sim 40\ \text{wt.}\%$).
  - The alkaline activator consisted of a $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ solution ($\text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 2.0$) combined with an $8\ \text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ solution (activator/binder ratio = 0.35~0.42).
  - The aggregate was fine silica sand ($d_{max} = 1.18\ \text{mm}$, S/B = 1.0~1.5).
- **Printability evaluation system (Sections 2.2~2.5 & Figs. 5~8)**:
  - A custom three-axis gantry-type extrusion 3D printer was used (nozzle diameter $15\ \text{mm}$, extrusion speed $50\sim 150\ \text{mm/s}$).
  - Quantitative measurements:
    1. Slump flow and shape retention factor.
    2. Open time measured as penetration resistance over time.
    3. Filament dimensional deformation and the maximum number of stackable layers.

## Results

### 1. Comparison of extrudability, open time, maximum formwork-free stacked layers, and hardened compressive strength across geopolymer mix variables (Section 3 & Tables 4~6 & Figs. 9~14)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **GP-Slag-30 (optimal mix)** | **30.0 wt.%** | **$0.36$** | **$30 \pm 3\ \text{min}$ (optimal)** | **fully continuous extrusion** | **$>20$ layers stacked (self-supporting)** | **$<2.0\%$ (ultra-high precision)** | **$35.0 \pm 1.8\ \text{MPa}$** |

### 2. Interpretation of sialate-siloxo gelation and static yield stress evolution in the alkali-activated geopolymer
- **Rapid early strength promotion by GGBS**: $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions leached from GGBS formed C-A-S-H gel early within the aluminosilicate network, raising the static yield stress sharply to $\tau_{y0} = 1.8\ \text{kPa}$ within 5 min after deposition.
- **Retention of interlayer bond strength**: when the printing gap time was kept within 5 min, the shear strength of the stacking interface remained at $90\%$ or more of the matrix strength.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the highest-level landmark source in digital construction and environmentally friendly layered manufacturing, in which the research group of Professor Jay Sanjayan established a standard design methodology that precisely controls the extrudability, shape retention, open time, and 20-layer self-supporting buildability of a cement-free geopolymer for 3D concrete printing.
