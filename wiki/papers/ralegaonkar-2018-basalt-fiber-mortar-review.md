# Review of Chopped Basalt Fiber-Reinforced Mortar

## Summary

A review consolidating the manufacturing process, physical and chemical properties, mortar reinforcement effects, mix-design challenges, test standards, and civil-engineering applications of chopped basalt fiber (CBF). Basalt is a natural material made by melting volcanic rock (at about 1400 °C); compared with glass fiber or carbon fiber it consumes less energy and is non-carcinogenic.

## Key Contributions

1. Basic property table of CBF: density 2.65 g/cm³, tensile strength 3000~4500 MPa, elastic modulus 95~115 GPa, elongation at break 2.4~3.0 %. Used chopped to diameters of 10~20 µm and lengths of 3~130 mm.
2. The alkali-resistance problem and remedies: severe degradation in a pH 12.5 environment (accelerated in the presence of sulfate ions). ZrO₂ coating is more effective than TiO₂, and CBF containing 5.7 wt % ZrO₂ attains alkali resistance comparable to AR-glass (ZrO₂ 18.8 %) (Lipatov et al.).
3. Synthesis of optimal dosage: most studies fall in the 1~3 % range. 1 wt % (lime mortars, brick masonry); 12 mm at 1 % (compressive strength +47 %, flexural +55 %); 3 % under dynamic conditions (beyond which fracture stress decreases).
4. Interfacial property comparison data (Choi & Lee): in single-fiber pullout tests the frictional bonding and slip-hardening coefficients of CBF exceed those of PVA by 3 % and 24.4 % respectively, but its strength-reduction factor with increasing orientation angle is 3× and 9× those of PE and PVA — a brittle fracture character.
5. Dynamic performance: at high strain rate (100/s) the dynamic amplification factor reaches up to 5, with dynamic fracture energy increasing alongside (Asprone et al.). Glass fiber outperforms CBF at high strain rates (Fenu et al.).

## Methods

Literature review. Main subtopics: CBF manufacture (platinum-rhodium bushing spinning), thermal stability (about 40 °C higher than glass fiber), chemical resistance (SEM, weight loss), fresh, mechanical, durability, and dynamic properties by mortar mix, and repair, electromagnetic-wave-absorbing, and 3D printing applications.

## Results

- Alongside strength and toughness gains, numerous reports of reduced drying shrinkage and improved abrasion resistance, freeze-thaw resistance, and water-permeation resistance.
- With nanosilica addition (NFRM), large early-strength gains (up to a 168 % increase in 3-day compressive strength) and improved salt-frost scaling resistance (Ghazy et al.).
- Limitations: excessive dosage reduces flowability (spatial network formation), increases air entrainment, and there are cases of reversed 28-day strength (Dong et al.).
- In alkali-activated binders, the NaOH concentration must be controlled at 5.5 mol/L or below (Funke et al.).
