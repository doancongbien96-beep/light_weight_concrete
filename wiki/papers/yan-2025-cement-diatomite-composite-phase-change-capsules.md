## Summary
To fundamentally block phase change material leakage and strength degradation in building insulation and thermal-energy-storage mortars, this most recent 2025 study established the latent heat storage characteristics, thermal conductivity, and mechanical compressive strength when applied to mortar of a composite phase change capsule (Cement-Diatomite ss-PCM) in which paraffin (phase change temperature $24 °C$, latent heat $\Delta H = 160 J/g$) was vacuum-adsorbed into natural porous diatomite (specific surface area $45 m^2/g$, porosity 75 %) and then double-coated on the surface with a dense cement-based inorganic nanoshell. It demonstrated release of an effective latent heat of **32.8 J/g** together with achievement of a leakage rate of **0.0 %** after 500 thermal cycles and mitigation of the indoor temperature fluctuation range by **4.2 °C**.

## Key Contributions
- **Development of a new diatomite-cement inorganic double-shell encapsulation technology**: complete assurance of fire safety (non-combustible class A1) and cement matrix adhesion compared with polymer organic shells.
- **Zero leakage achieved at high paraffin loading (65 wt %)**: dual shielding by capillary tension in diatomite nanopores and the outer cement hydration membrane.
- **Thermal-storage mortar attained a 28 d compressive strength of 35.8 MPa**: fully satisfied (100 %) the strength required of structural finishing materials.
- **Demonstrated a 34 % reduction in annual building heating and cooling energy**: an optimal solution for thermal-storage envelopes of zero-energy buildings.

## Methods
- Capsule preparation: molten paraffin adsorption in a vacuum impregnation apparatus $\rightarrow$ spray coating with a fine cement paste slurry and hydration hardening.
- Mortar mixing: capsules substituted by volume in OPC mortar (10, 20, 30 vol %).
- Testing: DSC latent heat analysis, Hot Disk thermal conductivity meter, 28 d compressive strength, and temperature monitoring of a $500 \times 500 mm$ full-scale thermal-storage wall.
