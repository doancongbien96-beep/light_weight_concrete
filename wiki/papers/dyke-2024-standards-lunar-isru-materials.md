# Standardized Testing Protocols, Extreme Space Environment Design Criteria, and Landing Pad Infrastructure Engineering Guidelines for Lunar ISRU Structural Materials

## Summary

To support the construction of sustainable lunar bases, launch and landing pads, blast shields, habitats, and roadways under NASA's Artemis and Moon-to-Mars exploration initiatives while minimizing reliance on Earth-supplied payloads, in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) of lunar regolith composites has become central to space civil engineering. Addressing the absence of global testing standards and building codes for lunar structural materials, Professor Shirley J. Dyke and co-workers at Purdue University analyzed the extreme environmental parameters of the Moon (hard vacuum $10^{-10}\ \text{Torr}$, thermal swings from $-180\ ^\circ\text{C} \sim +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$, unshielded cosmic radiation, $1/6 g$ gravity, hypervelocity micrometeoroid impacts, and $2000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ rocket plume thermal shock) and adapted terrestrial building codes (ASTM C39, ACI 318, ASME, ISRM) for space environments. The authors established standardized testing protocols and reliability-based design frameworks to evaluate uniaxial compression, direct tension, shear, fracture toughness, fatigue endurance, and automated additive construction (AAC) quality. Using lunar landing pads as a primary benchmark, the study formulated structural criteria to resist rocket plume thermal-mechanical erosion, establishing a load and resistance factor design (LRFD) framework for sintered regolith, sulfur concrete, and geopolymer composites with target failure probabilities of $10^{-4}\sim 10^{-6}$.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a standardized testing and certification framework for lunar ISRU structural materials**:
   - Identified limitations of terrestrial standards (ASTM C31/C39) and formulated remote non-destructive and destructive testing protocols under vacuum and $-180\ ^\circ\text{C} \sim +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$ cryogenic-to-high thermal cycles.
2. **Formulation of structural design criteria for lunar launch and landing pads**:
   - Quantified thermo-mechanical requirements to prevent regolith erosion from rocket exhaust plumes (velocity $> 2000\ \text{m/s}$, temperature $> 2000\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
3. **Establishment of quality control standards for automated additive construction (AAC) on the Moon**:
   - Defined non-destructive ultrasonic and CT inspection criteria for interlaminar shear strength and void control during laser/microwave sintering and binder jetting.
4. **Development of performance-based Load and Resistance Factor Design (LRFD) for space infrastructure**:
   - Derived safety factors and target failure probabilities ($10^{-4}\sim 10^{-6}$) based on structural criticality, accounting for the spatial variability of lunar regolith (Highlands vs Mare).

## Methods

- **Analysis of extreme lunar environmental load conditions (Section 2 & Table 1)**:
  - Thermal loads: Surface temperature swings over 28-day diurnal cycles (day $+120\ ^\circ\text{C}$, night $-180\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $\Delta T = 300\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Atmosphere and radiation: Pressure $10^{-10}\ \text{Torr}$ (hard vacuum), galactic cosmic rays (GCR), and solar particle events (SPE).
  - Gravity and impact: $1/6 g$ gravitational acceleration, micrometeoroid impacts at $10\sim 70\ \text{km/s}$.
  - Landing loads: Dynamic pressure and $2000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ thermal shock from vertical descent rocket engines.
- **Comparison of ISRU candidate materials and processing routes (Sections 3–4)**:
  - Sintered regolith (microwave, concentrated solar, and laser melting).
  - Sulfur concrete (waterless sulfur binder + regolith aggregate).
  - Geopolymer composites (alkali-activated regolith geopolymers).
  - Polymer-infused regolith composites.
- **Standardized testing protocol development (Sections 5–7)**:
  - Vacuum chamber uniaxial compression tests (ASTM C39 space adaptation), three-point bend fracture toughness tests (ASTM C293), and interlaminar direct shear tests.

| **Polymer composite** | Thermoset resin infusion | 60 ~ 90 MPa | 12.0 ~ 20.0 MPa | Weak (thermal decomposition occurs) | 100 % polymer transported from Earth | UV/radiation aging and VOC outgassing |

### 2. Landing pad and structural safety mechanisms
- **Plume erosion suppression for landing pads**: Regolith particles ejected at high speed ($>1000\ \text{m/s}$) by rocket plumes threaten orbiting assets and base infrastructure, requiring interlocking sintered ceramic blocks capable of enduring $>1500\ ^\circ\text{C}$ with verified shear bond standards.
- **Vacuum-thermal fatigue testing standards**: In the absence of atmospheric moisture, drying shrinkage is eliminated, but thermal expansion mismatches from 300-degree diurnal cycles dominate; a standard $10^4$-cycle thermal fatigue protocol was proposed.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established testing protocols, material performance benchmarks, and design frameworks for In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) structural materials for human lunar habitats and landing infrastructure.
