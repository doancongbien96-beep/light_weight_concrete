# Fiber Orientation Evaluation in Short-Fiber Composites I: Contrast Enhancement for Image Analysis

## Summary

A methodology paper that solved the problem of insufficient glass fiber–matrix contrast in reflected-light optical micrographs of polished cross sections through oxygen ion plasma etching plus platinum sputtering (Leoben University, Austria). SEM-level black-and-white contrast images were obtained on an optical microscope across thermoplastic matrices in general — PA66, PA46, PEEK, and PES among others — making low-cost automated statistical analysis over many specimens × many fibers feasible. A polymer-matrix origin paper driven by the same problem consciousness as ECC fiber-orientation image analysis (the kim-2008 fluorescence technique, etc.).

## Key Contributions

- Established a standard specimen preparation procedure: low-pressure polishing (25~50 N) followed by finishing with 1 µm diamond suspension (30 min total). Over-polishing introduces edge rounding and relief topography, while under-polishing causes fiber pullout and protrusion — the dominant error sources.
- Oxygen ion etching (1 kV vacuum, about 1 h) excavates only the matrix surface uniformly and leaves fibers standing proud, generating edge-scattered light; subsequent platinum sputtering maximizes the reflectance difference. Fibers render white and matrix black — complete separation.
- Confirmed superior reproducibility over heat-decomposition and mechanical roughening treatments — a universal protocol applicable irrespective of matrix type.

## Methods

- Materials: glass short-fiber reinforced thermoplastics such as PA66-GF30 (injection molded).
- Equipment: reflected-light microscope (Zeiss Axsioskop) + CCD digitization; comparative SEM imaging (carbon coating).
- Analysis: the out-of-plane orientation angle was estimated from the major/minor axis ratio of fiber-intersection ellipses on the polished section (Bay–Tucker stereology).
