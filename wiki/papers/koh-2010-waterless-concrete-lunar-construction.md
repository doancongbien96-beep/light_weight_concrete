# Thermal-Pressurized Molding Process and Mechanical Properties of Polymer-Glass Fiber Waterless Concrete for Lunar Habitat Construction

## Summary

To address extreme water scarcity and vacuum desiccation of conventional hydraulic cements during lunar outpost construction, researchers at Hanyang University's e-Construction Laboratory (Prof. Tae Sup Lee's group) developed a 100 % waterless concrete system combining thermoplastic polymer powder, short glass fibers, and lunar regolith simulant. The authors established a 4-phase evolutionary thermal-pressuring molding system (Phase 1: continuous screw extrusion $\rightarrow$ Phase 2: dual-stage heated compression mold $\rightarrow$ Phase 3: insulation-controlled heating $\rightarrow$ Phase 4: Teflon-release precision temperature-controlled molding) to fabricate $50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ cubic specimens for compressive and tensile testing. Integrating 6 embedded cartridge heaters with a two-part split mold and Teflon (PTFE) release liners prevented localized polymer burning and mold adhesion damage. The molded waterless concrete exhibited compressive strengths of **$10.3\sim 34.5\ \text{MPa}$** and ductile fiber-bridging tensile resistance, demonstrating the engineering feasibility of waterless concrete manufacturing for lunar precast protective structures.

## Key Contributions

1. **First development of 100 % waterless polymer concrete for lunar base construction**:
   - Eliminated water consumption (0.0 %) by combining thermal-melt polymer powder with lunar regolith simulant into a rapid-curing structural material.
2. **Evolution of 4-stage controlled heating and pressuring molding systems**:
   - Addressed slurry heterogeneity in Phase 1 and silicone adhesion damage in Phases 2~3 by introducing PTFE release liners and multi-channel PID temperature control in Phase 4.
3. **Suppression of tensile brittleness via glass fiber hybridization**:
   - Incorporated alkali-resistant glass fibers to bridge microcracks and provide structural integrity under lunar diurnal thermal cycling.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mixture proportions (Sections 3–4)**:
  - Lunar regolith simulant: KLS-1 / JSC-1 basaltic simulant ($d_{50} \approx 50\ \mu\text{m}$, over 50 % passing No. 200 sieve).
  - Binder: Thermoplastic/thermosetting engineering polymer powder ($15\sim 25\ \text{wt.\%}$ binder content).
  - Reinforcement: Alkali-resistant short glass fibers (length $6\sim 12\ \text{mm}$).
- **4-phase molding process architecture (Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Phase 1 (Extrusion): Direct slurry extrusion using a heated screw extruder.
  - Phase 2 (Heating Formwork): Dual-stage sliding mold with 6 internal cartridge heaters under top compression.
  - Phase 3 (Controlled Insulation): External insulation jacket with silicone release agents.
  - Phase 4 (Teflon Separation System): PTFE-coated liners with $180\sim 220\ ^\circ\text{C}$ PID heating and cooling control.
- **Mechanical characterization**:
  - Uniaxial compression: $50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ cubes tested per ASTM C109.
  - Tensile/flexural testing: Direct tension and splitting tensile evaluations.

## Results

### 1. Physical and mechanical performance across molding phases (Table & Experimental Phases)

| Molding Phase & Process | Release Layer & Temperature Control | Bulk Density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | Compressive Strength ($\sigma_c$, MPa) | Tensile / Flexural Response | Specimen Surface & Quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 (Screw extrusion)** | Direct metal mold discharge | 1.75 ± 0.06 | 10.3 ± 0.8 MPa | 71.3 kPa (Initial direct tension) | High porosity, fragile corners |
| **Phase 2 (Dual heating mold)** | Silicone film (Non-uniform heat) | 1.88 ± 0.05 | 18.5 ± 1.2 MPa | Microcracking | Adhesion damage during demolding |
| **Phase 3 (Insulation heating)** | Insulation + silicone film | 1.95 ± 0.04 | 24.2 ± 1.5 MPa | Crack control | Localized thermal stress cracks |
| **Phase 4 (Teflon precision)** | **PTFE liner + PID control** | **2.08 ± 0.03** | **34.5 ± 1.8 MPa** | **Ductile fiber pullout** | **Smooth defect-free surfaces** |

### 2. Space civil engineering implications
- **Efficacy of thermal-pressurized molding**: Simultaneous bidirectional compression and uniform heat transfer evacuated air voids, elevating compressive strength by **3.3-fold from 10.3 MPa to 34.5 MPa**.
- **Foundational baseline for lunar infrastructure**: Established an independent engineering framework for waterless composite mix design and high-temperature pressurized molding.

- **Overall conclusion**: This study demonstrated the successful fabrication of 34.5 MPa structural waterless concrete through a 4-stage thermal-pressuring molding process for future human lunar base construction.
