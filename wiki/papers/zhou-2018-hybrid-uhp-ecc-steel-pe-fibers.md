## Summary
In a cement matrix at the 135 MPa compressive strength class, this study by the research team of Professor Christopher Leung (Hong Kong University of Science and Technology) elucidated the uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening envelope, the four-point flexural modulus of rupture (MOR), and the crack opening control behavior of a hybrid UHP-ECC that combines at multiple scales the high stiffness and early crack confinement of micro straight steel fiber (Steel Fiber, $L_f = 13 mm$) with the superductile pull-out bridging capacity of ultrahigh-molecular-weight PE fiber ($L_f = 12 mm$) at a total $V_f = 2.0 \%$ with Steel 0.5 ~ 1.5 vol %. The optimal hybrid mix (Steel 1.0 % + PE 1.0 %) achieved outstanding mechanical performance: 28 d compressive strength **138.5 MPa**, direct tensile strength **15.2 MPa**, direct tensile strain capacity **6.8 %**, and flexural strength **28.5 MPa**.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the multiscale bridging synergy of ultra-high-strength hybrid UHP-ECC**: steel fiber raised the first-cracking strength to 7.8 MPa, and PE fiber led secondary strain-hardening up to 15.2 MPa.
- **Attainment of a tensile fracture energy density of $850 kJ/m^3$**: energy absorption capacity increased by 65 % relative to the single-fiber mixes.
- **Confinement across the entire load-crack width range**: individual crack widths held below $25 \mu m$ until the peak tensile load was reached.
- **Standardization for ultra-high-performance bridge expansion joints and seismic retrofit materials**: realization of slim joint construction without steel reinforcement.

## Methods
- Matrix: low-heat cement + silica fume (20 %) + fine slag (25 %), $W/B = 0.16$.
- Fiber combinations: Plain, steel only (2.0 %), PE only (2.0 %), hybrid (Steel 1.5 % + PE 0.5 %, Steel 1.0 % + PE 1.0 %, Steel 0.5 % + PE 1.5 %).
- Tests: dogbone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), four-point flexural test ($100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$), 28 d compressive strength (100 mm cube).
