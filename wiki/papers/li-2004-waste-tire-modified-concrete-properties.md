# Mechanical Properties and Energy-Absorption Toughening Mechanisms of Concrete with Waste-Tire Rubber Chip and Fibrous Strip (Tire Fiber) Substitution: Demonstration of a Threefold Fracture Energy Increase and Crack-Bridging Behavior

## Summary

To solve the waste tire disposal problem and maximize the impact resistance and vibration-damping performance of road pavements and buffer structures, a joint research team of Prof. Guoqiang Li (Louisiana State University) and Baoshan Huang (University of Tennessee) fabricated nine concrete mixes in which 15 vol.% of the coarse aggregate was replaced with waste tire chips and length-graded tire fibers (Tire Fibers: 25.4 mm, 50.8 mm, 76.2 mm, with and without steel belt wire, plus a PP fiber hybrid), systematically evaluating slump, air content, 28-day compressive strength, split tensile strength, elastic modulus ($E$), Poisson's ratio ($\nu$), and split-tensile load-displacement fracture energy ($\int P d\delta$) — a Cement and Concrete Research (CCR) 2004 landmark origin paper. Rubber substitution moderately reduced compressive strength ($37.5 \rightarrow 23.5\sim 28.2\ \text{MPa}$), whereas the inclusion of long-fiber-type tires containing steel belt wires (Batch 9) raised the fracture energy absorption capacity by more than $300\%$ relative to the control concrete, demonstrating a ductile bridging failure mechanism in which specimens do not separate even after cracking.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the difference in failure mechanism by waste tire rubber shape (chip vs. fibrous strip)**:
   - First comparative analysis showing that plain rubber chips act as stress-concentrating flaws inside the matrix and degrade strength, while tire fibers of high aspect ratio provide bridging (Bridging) toughness at the crack tip.
2. **Demonstration of post-crack bridging toughening by steel-belt-wire-embedded tire fibers**:
   - The high-strength steel belt wires residual from tire processing act synergistically with the elastic rubber, constraining crack opening during the split-tensile test and increasing the fracture energy by more than $300\%$.
3. **Proportional improvement in tensile toughness with increasing tire fiber length (25.4 mm $ \rightarrow $ 76.2 mm)**:
   - As the fiber length increases, a longer mechanical bond length with the matrix is secured, implementing gradual softening behavior after the load drop.
4. **Analysis of the limits of physical anchorage (Physical Anchorage) via drilled tire chips**:
   - Drilling a 5 mm hole at the chip center to form a cement-paste-column anchor yielded only marginal strength improvement owing to poor paste filling during casting, proving that continuous fiberization is the superior solution.

## Methods

- **Materials and design of the nine mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Control concrete: W/C = 0.45, coarse aggregate (crushed stone of 19 mm maximum size) + natural sand.
  - Rubber substitution: uniform replacement of 15 % of the coarse aggregate volume.
  - Nine mix groups:
    1. `Batch 1`: control concrete (Control, no substitution).
    2. `Batch 2`: plain tire chips (cross-section $5 \times 5\ \text{mm}$, length 15~20 mm).
    3. `Batch 3`: car tire chips alone.
    4. `Batch 4`: drilled anchor chips ($\phi 5\ \text{mm}$ hole).
    5. `Batch 5`: tire fibers ($L = 25.4\ \text{mm}$).
    6. `Batch 6`: tire fibers ($L = 50.8\ \text{mm}$).
    7. `Batch 7`: tire fibers ($L = 76.2\ \text{mm}$).
    8. `Batch 8`: tire fiber + PP fiber ($V_f = 0.1\%$) hybrid.
    9. `Batch 9`: steel-belt-wire-embedded tire fibers ($L = 50.8\ \text{mm}$).
- **Test methods (Section 2.3 & Fig. 3)**:
  - Fresh concrete: ASTM C143 slump, ASTM C231 air content.
  - Hardened concrete (28 days): compressive strength (ASTM C39, $\phi 100 \times 200\ \text{mm}$), split tensile strength (ASTM C496, lateral deformation measured with an LVDT), static elastic modulus and Poisson's ratio (ASTM C469).

### 2. Interpretation of the fracture behavior and energy absorption mechanisms
- **Non-separating ductile failure of the split-tensile specimens**: the control concrete fractures into two pieces with a loud report immediately upon reaching peak load, whereas Batch 9 (steel-belt tire fibers) develops such a large residual plastic tensile capacity after the peak — with the steel-rubber fibers firmly bridging the two faces of the crack — that separation requires hammer blows.
- **Poisson's ratio rise and the impact-buffering mechanism**: rubber particles of very low elastic modulus flexibly accommodate the lateral expansion of the matrix, raising Poisson's ratio from $0.18 \rightarrow 0.22$ and acting as a damper that absorbs volumetric deformation energy under dynamic impact loading.

- **Overall conclusion**: this is a CCR landmark origin paper that raised the fracture energy of concrete more than 3.5-fold by fibering waste tire rubber and exploiting the combined bridging effect with steel belt wires, thereby establishing a high-ductility rubberized concrete design technology applicable to road pavements, crash barriers, and impact-resistant structures.
