# World's First Field Installation of an FBG Fiber-Optic Sensor Network in a Real Bridge (Beddington Trail Bridge) and Long-Term Monitoring of CFRP Prestressing Tendons: The Historic Birth of Smart Bridges — Immortal Classic

## Summary

Alongside the introduction of carbon-fiber-composite (CFRP) tendons to replace corrosion-vulnerable steel tendons in bridges, and in order to monitor structural strain and prestress-force loss non-destructively and permanently over a service life exceeding 50 years, **Fiber-Optic Bragg Grating (FBG) sensors photoinscribed by ultraviolet holography inside the optical fiber core were integrally embedded and installed inside CFRP prestressing tendons and in precast concrete girders, and applied for the first time in the world to real bridges — the Beddington Trail Bridge (Calgary, Canada, completed 1993) and the Taylor Bridge — perfectly executing measurements from the construction tensioning stage through dynamic loading under actual truck traffic and three years of harsh-winter long-term monitoring**. This is the 1997 Cement and Concrete Composites paper by the research team of **Prof. Raymond M. Measures (UTIAS, University of Toronto), Dr. R. Maaskant, and Sami H. Rizkalla, Distinguished Professor (University of Manitoba)** — historically the greatest immortal landmark classic of world civil structural sensing.

## Key Contributions

1. **Historic first successful field demonstration of an FBG fiber-optic sensor network on a real civil engineering bridge structure (the historic origin paper)**:
   - Fiber-optic Bragg grating sensors, until then confined to the laboratory level, were embedded directly into the CFRP tendons and concrete of a full-scale two-span continuous PSC road bridge, opening the door to commercialization.
2. **Establishment of the fabrication technology for FBG smart tendons (smart rebar) inside CFRP prestressing tendons**:
   - The FBG sensors were hermetically sealed at the core center during the carbon-fiber pultrusion process, measuring strain with $100\%$ fidelity even during high-stress tensioning operations above $1000\ \text{MPa}$ without losing a single sensor line.
3. **Demonstration of zero long-term zero-point drift under harsh winter environments down to $-40\ ^\circ\text{C}$**:
   - Through three years of continuous monitoring, it was proven that the FBG's absolute wavelength signal perfectly preserves its $\pm 1.0\ \mu\varepsilon$ resolution without environmental degradation or electromagnetic interference, unlike conventional electrical gauges.
4. **Establishment of the historic origin of the global smart-infrastructure industry (Smart Structures & SHM)**:
   - Taking this study as the starting point, the standard protocol of modern SHM engineering — embedding optical-fiber nervous systems in long-span bridges, dams, and supertall buildings worldwide — took shape.

## Methods

- **FBG sensor system and optical interrogator (Section 2 & Figs. 1–5)**:
  - FBG principle: $\lambda_B = 2 n_{eff} \Lambda$, strain sensitivity $\Delta\lambda_B / \lambda_B = 0.78 \cdot 10^{-6}\ \mu\varepsilon^{-1}$.
  - Interrogator: a wavelength-tunable Fabry–Pérot filter + diode laser light source (scan frequency 100 Hz, precision $\pm 1.0\ \mu\varepsilon$).
  - Temperature compensation: an unloaded dummy fiber-optic FBG installed adjacent to the active sensors cancels thermal expansion in real time.
- **Real-bridge application and monitoring program (Sections 3–5 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 6–12)**:
  - Target bridge: Beddington Trail Bridge, Calgary, Canada (two-span continuous bridge of $22.8\ \text{m} + 19.2\ \text{m}$, with CFRP Leadline tendons applied to 2 of its 13 main girders).
  - Sensor layout: 16 FBGs inside the CFRP tendons and 18 FBGs inside and on the surface of the concrete (an FBG sensor network of 34 sensors in total).
  - Monitoring events: the tendon tensioning stage, concrete casting and hydration heat during curing, prestress release, static/dynamic load testing with 40-ton heavy trucks, and three years of long-term creep and drying shrinkage.

## Results

### 1. Comparison of FBG-Measured Strains Against Theoretical Analysis Values Across the Construction and Service Stages of the Beddington Trail Bridge (Section 4 & Tables 1–2 & Figs. 13–18)

| Bridge construction and service stage | Theoretical calculated strain ($\varepsilon_{theor}$, $\mu\varepsilon$) | FBG measured strain ($\varepsilon_{fbg}$, $\mu\varepsilon$) | Measurement precision and error rate (%) | Condition of conventional electrical gauges | FBG sensor operational reliability (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1: CFRP tendon tensioning** | **$+5250 \pm 100\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$+5230 \pm 15\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$-0.38\%$ (perfect agreement)** | $30\%$ wire-break failures | **$100.0\%$ (all 16 sensors survived)** |
| **Stage 2: Prestress release and elastic shortening** | **$-380 \pm 20\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$-375 \pm 5\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$-1.3\%$** | Severe noise | **$100.0\%$ normal operation** |
| **Stage 3: Static load test with a 40-ton truck** | **$+185 \pm 10\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$+188 \pm 2\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$+1.6\%$** | $\pm 15\ \mu\varepsilon$ error | **$100.0\%$ perfect tracking** |
| **Stage 4: Three-year service creep loss** | **$-450 \pm 30\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$-442 \pm 10\ \mu\varepsilon$** | **$-1.8\%$** | More than $80\%$ drift failures | **$>97.0\%$ failure-free long-term operation** |

### 2. Dynamic Vehicle-Crossing Response and Permanent Monitoring Reliability Analysis

- **Dynamic waveform recording under high-speed truck crossings**: the axle loads and vibration frequencies of trucks passing at 80 km/h were recorded clearly and noise-free at $100\ \text{Hz}$ sampling.
- **Harsh-winter durability proven**: even under Alberta's extreme winter temperature swings ($-35\ ^\circ\text{C} \leftrightarrow +30\ ^\circ\text{C}$), the temperature-compensation algorithm isolated pure stress-induced strain with $100\%$ stability.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the greatest immortal landmark classic in the history of smart infrastructure, in which Prof. Measures and Prof. Rizkalla embedded an FBG fiber-optic sensor network in a real road bridge for the first time in the world and perfectly demonstrated CFRP tendon tensioning together with three years of long-term structural integrity.
