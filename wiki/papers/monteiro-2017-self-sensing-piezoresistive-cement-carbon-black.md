# Piezoresistive Response ($\text{FCR} = 35\%$) of Carbon Black Nano-Particle Loaded Self-Sensing Cement Composite and Its Real-Time Structural Health Monitoring Mechanism

## Summary

To overcome the durability degradation and high cost limits of externally attached sensors and to let concrete structures themselves sense load and damage, this study developed a **self-sensing cement composite with uniformly dispersed high-specific-surface-area carbon black (CB: particle size $30\ \text{nm}$, at $0, 2, 5, 7, 10\ \text{wt.}\%$ of binder)** and precisely evaluated **the electrical conductivity percolation threshold ($4.5\ \text{wt.}\%$), the fractional change in resistivity (FCR) under repeated uniaxial compression, stress sensitivity, capillary absorption, and 28-day compressive strength**, demonstrating **at CB $7.0\ \text{wt.}\%$ a 28-day compressive strength of $48.5\ \text{MPa}$, a stress sensitivity of $0.22\ \text{MPa}^{-1}$ (FCR up to $35\%$, perfectly reversible linear response), and a $30\%$ reduction in capillary absorption**. This is the landmark origin paper by Dr. André O. Monteiro and Prof. Paulo B. Cachim (University of Aveiro, Portugal) with Prof. Pedro M. F. J. Costa (KAUST), Cement and Concrete Composites, 2017.

## Key Contributions

1. **Completion of a low-cost carbon black based smart self-sensing cement composite**:
   - Using commodity carbon black instead of expensive carbon nanotubes achieved equal or better piezoresistive sensing sensitivity at one tenth of the cost.
2. **Precise identification of the electrical conductivity percolation threshold ($4.5\ \text{wt.}\%$)**:
   - As CB content rose from $2 \rightarrow 5\ \text{wt.}\%$, electrical resistivity plunged four orders of magnitude from $10^6 \rightarrow 10^2\ \Omega\cdot\text{cm}$, elucidating the conductive-pathway formation mechanism.
3. **Demonstration of reversible linear piezoresistive response (FCR up to $35\%$)**:
   - Under repeated compression, the load history matched the resistivity change (FCR) exactly, tracking real-time stress state and overloads within $3\%$ error.
4. **Simultaneous durability improvement through nano-filler effects**:
   - Fine carbon black particles filled cement nano-pores, cutting capillary absorption by $30\%$ and securing smart sensing together with high durability.

## Methods

- **Materials and CB cement composite mixes (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Binder: CEM I 42.5R ordinary Portland cement.
  - Conductive nano-filler: Printex XE2-B conductive carbon black (mean primary particle size $30\ \text{nm}$, specific surface area $1000\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, at 0, 2, 5, 7, 10 wt.%).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 200\ \mu\text{m}$, S/C = 1.5, W/C = 0.45, superplasticizer dispersed by ultrasonication).
- **Test and instrumentation program (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Specimens: $40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prisms with four embedded stainless mesh electrodes (four-probe AC/DC measurement).
  - Repeated uniaxial compression: monotonic and five-cycle loading between $0\sim 15\ \text{MPa}$ (load control at 0.2 MPa/s).
  - Instrumentation: Keysight 34461A 6½-digit digital multimeter → $\text{FCR} = (\rho(t) - \rho_0)/\rho_0$ and $\text{Sensitivity} = \text{FCR}/\Delta\sigma$.

### 2. Interpretation of tunneling effects and the piezoresistive mechanism
- **Quantum tunneling conduction**: near the percolation threshold (7 wt.%), compression narrows the nano-gaps (< $2\ \text{nm}$) between CB particles, exponentially raising electron tunneling probability and sharply reducing resistivity.
- **Full reversibility through elastic recovery**: on unloading, the elastic restoring force of the cement matrix returns the CB particle spacing to its original state, producing stable repeated sensing signals without residual resistivity drift.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is the landmark origin paper of smart construction materials, fully demonstrating a high piezoresistive change rate of 35 % and real-time structural health monitoring capability while maintaining a compressive strength of 48.5 MPa through low-cost carbon black nanoparticles.
