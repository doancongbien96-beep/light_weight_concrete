# Casting-Flow-Induced Fiber Orientation Mechanisms and Deflection-Hardening Optimization in Self-Compacting Fiber-Reinforced Cementitious Composites (FR-SCC)

## Summary

To achieve deflection-hardening flexural behavior in 30 mm ultra-thin unreinforced precast roof elements spanning 2.5 m while minimizing steel fiber content to an economical $1.27\ \text{vol.\%}$ ($100\ \text{kg/m}^3$), Professors Liberato Ferrara and Marco di Prisco at Politecnico di Milano investigated the shear-induced alignment of micro-steel fibers ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.16\ \text{mm}$) along casting flow paths (unidirectional longitudinal channel flow vs central radial flow) in self-compacting fiber-reinforced concrete (FR-SCC / DHFRCC). From 30 mm thick slabs, beam specimens ($150\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm}$) sawn parallel (longitudinal) and perpendicular (transverse) to the flow were evaluated through four-point bending, optical image analysis of fiber orientation factors ($\alpha$), and mechanical anisotropy. Specimens cast parallel to the principal tensile stress achieved orientation factors of **$\alpha = 0.78 \sim 0.85$**, yielding an ultimate flexural strength of **16.5 MPa** with distinct deflection-hardening multiple cracking, whereas transverse specimens dropped to $\alpha = 0.38$, showing brittle softening with a flexural strength of **7.8 MPa** (**2.1-fold flexural capacity disparity**). This demonstrated that flow tailoring is a primary engineering parameter governing structural performance in high-performance composites.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of casting-flow-induced fiber orientation mechanisms**:
   - Elucidated the rheological mechanism where shear velocity gradients ($\dot{\gamma}$) during mold filling align micro-steel fibers along slurry streamlines.
2. **2.1-fold amplification in flexural strength and deflection hardening via flow direction control**:
   - At identical fiber volume fractions ($V_f = 1.27\ \%$), parallel-flow casting increased flexural strength from **7.8 MPa to 16.5 MPa (+112 %)** and expanded post-cracking deflection capacity over 3 times without load drops.
3. **Quantification of cross-sectional fiber orientation factor ($\alpha$) via image analysis**:
   - Ellipse fitting on fracture surfaces confirmed that $\alpha \ge 0.80$ in parallel-flow sections (quasi-1D alignment), agreeing quantitatively with fib Model Code and RILEM formulations.
4. **Design of unreinforced 30 mm ultra-thin precast roof elements**:
   - Validated precast fabrication of thin roof panels supporting self-weight and snow loads across a 2.5 m span without conventional rebar.

## Methods

- **FR-SCC / DHFRCC mixture proportions and properties (Tables 1–3)**:
  - Binders: CEM I 52.5 R cement $600\ \text{kg/m}^3$ + Class F fly ash $150\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (W/B = 0.32).
  - Aggregate: Silica sand ($d_{max} = 2.0\ \text{mm}$) $980\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
  - Superplasticizer: Polycarboxylate SP at 1.8 wt.% (Marsh cone flow time 22~28 s, mini-slump flow 280 mm).
  - Reinforcing fibers: High-tensile straight micro-steel fibers ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.16\ \text{mm}$, aspect ratio 81, tensile strength $> 2600\ \text{MPa}$, dosage $100\ \text{kg/m}^3$, $V_f = 1.27\ \text{vol.\%}$).
  - 28 d compressive strength: 85 ~ 95 MPa.
- **Slab casting and specimen preparation (Figure 2)**:
  - Full-scale slab dimensions: $1000\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm}$.
  - Casting configurations:
    1. Slab A (Unidirectional channel flow): Injected from one end along the 1.0 m length.
    2. Slab B (Central radial flow): Injected at the center, inducing radial expansion.
- **Testing program**:
  - Four-point bending: $150\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm}$ beams (span 450 mm, pure bending zone 150 mm, displacement rate $0.1\ \text{mm/min}$, dual LVDTs).
  - Optical microscopy fiber counting and ellipse orientation analysis ($\alpha$).

## Results

### 1. Four-point flexural performance across flow directions and slab locations (Table 4 & Figures)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Slab A - L1 (Longitudinal)** | Fully parallel ($0^\circ$) | **0.82 ± 0.03** | 7.5 ± 0.4 | **16.5 ± 0.8** | **2.20 (Deflection-hardening)** | Multiple microcracking + pullout |
| **Slab A - L2 (Longitudinal)** | Fully parallel ($0^\circ$) | **0.79 ± 0.03** | 7.2 ± 0.3 | **15.8 ± 0.7** | **2.19 (Deflection-hardening)** | Multiple microcracking + pullout |
| **Slab A - T1 (Transverse)** | Fully orthogonal ($90^\circ$) | **0.38 ± 0.02** | 6.8 ± 0.3 | **7.8 ± 0.4** | **1.15 (Brittle softening)** | Single-crack localization |

### 2. Rheological fiber alignment and structural design implications
- **Anisotropy ratio**: Flexural capacity parallel to the flow was **2.1 times** that in the transverse direction, indicating that precast injection gates must be aligned with primary tensile stress axes.
- **Economic high toughness**: Flow optimization at $V_f = 1.27\ \%$ matched or exceeded flexural ductility achieved in random composites at $V_f = 2.0\sim 2.5\ \%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that controlling self-compacting casting flow paths to align fibers with principal tensile stresses enables deflection-hardening in ultra-thin structural elements with minimal fiber dosage.
