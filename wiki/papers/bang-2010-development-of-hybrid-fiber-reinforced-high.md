# Development of Hybrid Fiber-reinforced High-strength Lightweight Cementitious Composite (HFSLCC)

## Summary

An ECC-family paper co-authored by Bang Yeon Lee during his time at Michigan, with Prof. Yun Yong Kim of Chungnam National University as corresponding author. The body text confirmed that 'Bang' in the filename is first author Jin-Wook Bang, a different person from Bang Yeon Lee (Lee is the third author). A high-strength lightweight cementitious composite (HFSLCC) that achieves, simultaneously, lightness at a unit mass of roughly 1660 kg/m³ and strain-hardening behavior via a lightweight filler (glass bubble) and PVA+PE hybrid fibers was designed with Li's micromechanics and steady-state cracking theory and verified by direct tension testing.

## Key Contributions

- **Lightweight application of ECC design theory**: the mixes were pre-designed by substituting matrix fracture toughness test data and fiber pull-out test values into the complementary energy condition (J'tip ≤ J') and the strength condition (σfc ≤ σ0), and the minimum toughness ratio (toughness ratio = J'/J'tip) criterion of 3.0 was secured for every mix already at the prediction stage — a design-verification consistency case subsequently proven when direct tension tests realized the predictions.
- **Lightweight filler optimization**: quantified the paradoxical effect whereby increasing the content of glass bubble (hollow spherical particles, density 0.6 g/cm³, mean particle size 30 μm) lowers the unit mass while also reducing fracture toughness K_IC (range 0.31→0.16 MPa√m), which from the toughness-ratio standpoint actually becomes favorable for multiple cracking. However, constrained by dispersion and mixing performance limits, W/C 50 % plus glass bubble at 25 % of cement mass was set as the practical optimum.
- **Hybrid fiber role-partitioning design**: hydrophilic PVA (12 mm, 40 μm) induces numerous fine cracks, while hydrophobic PE (15 mm, 12 μm) takes charge of controlling the major cracks with large crack widths. As the PE content grows, J' and σ0 on the bridging curve increase, but once hydrophobic PE exceeds 25 % of the total fiber volume it clumps and experimental performance drops instead — an optimal PE upper bound of 0.5 % (out of a 2 % total) is proposed.
- **Low-fiber-content high-efficiency mix**: PVA 1.0 % + PE 0.5 % (1.5 % combined) was derived as the most efficient mix, delivering tensile performance on par with the 2.0 %-fiber mixes — an improvement in constructability and economy.
- **Performance metrics**: tensile strain capacity minimum 2.0 %, maximum about 3.0 %; maximum tensile strength 4.2~4.5 MPa; compressive strength 57~59 MPa; unit mass 1660~1710 kg/m³.

## Methods

- **Micromechanics design**: adopted Li's PVA-ECC steady-state cracking theory and Lin's interface characterization methodology. Interfacial variables obtained from fiber pull-out tests (PVA: frictional bond 1.83 MPa · chemical bond energy 1.85 J/m²; PE: 0.63 MPa · 0 J/m²) were substituted into the bridging stress σ(δ) integral expression to compute the fiber bridging curves.
- **Matrix fracture properties**: fracture toughness tests (clip gauge CMOD) and unit mass tests (KS F 2409) over the W/C 45/55 % × glass bubble 15/25/35 % series, plus rotational-viscometer rheology testing to determine the GGBS content (minimum yield stress at 25 %).
- **Mix proportions**: mass ratios on a cement basis: water 0.5, silica sand 0.8 (mean particle size 130 μm, honoring Lepech-Li's 200 μm upper limit), slag 0.25, glass bubble 0.25, HPMC 0.004, PCSP 0.010~0.015. Four fiber mixes: I(1.0+0.5), II(1.0+1.0), III(1.5+0.5), IV(1.75+0.25) vol.%.
- **Verification tests**: dogbone-shaped direct tension specimens (3 per mix, 2 LVDTs, 10 kN under displacement control), Ø100×200 mm compression cylinders water-cured for 28 days (KS F 2403).

## Results

- **Toughness ratio prediction**: J' peaked at Mix 2 with 392.1 J/m² (PVA-only 2 %: 248.4 J/m²); with J'tip spanning 1.76~5.29 J/m², every mix exceeded a toughness ratio of 3.0 — confirming in advance that both the energy condition and the strength condition are satisfied.
- **Direct tension**: all four mixes exhibited strain-hardening and multiple cracking. First-cracking strength 2.6~3.0 MPa, maximum tensile strength 4.2~4.5 MPa, strain capacity Ⅰ 3.0 % / II 2.0 % / III 2.5 % / IV 2.7 % — more than 200× the 0.01 % strain of ordinary concrete.
- **Prediction-experiment comparison**: measured σ0 (4.2~4.5 MPa) > measured σfc (2.6~3.0 MPa) experimentally reconfirmed the strength condition. However, Mix II, which had the largest J', showed the lowest strain capacity — interpreted as fiber clumping caused by excessive hydrophobic PE addition, raising the need for follow-up quantitative dispersion assessment based on fluorescence image processing.
- **Physical properties**: unit mass Ⅰ 1660 / II 1680 / III 1690 / IV 1710 kg/m³; compressive strengths 57.03 / 56.54 / 59.06 / 58.96 MPa, respectively.
