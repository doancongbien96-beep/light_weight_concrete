## Summary
To prevent progressive collapse of building frames upon middle column removal, this 2025 ASCE Journal of Structural Engineering study precisely elucidated the load redistribution mechanism, flexural-to-catenary action transition, and collapse resistance capacity of full-scale two-span frames ($6000 \times 2500 mm$), in which steel-FRP composite bars (SFCB) possessing post-yield secondary elastic tensile stiffness and high-ductility UHPC-ECC composite were hybridly applied to beam-column plastic hinge regions, through precise experiments and nonlinear large-deformation finite element analysis. It was demonstrated that the fusion of SFCB and UHPC-ECC increased the progressive collapse resistance load by **210 %** relative to an ordinary RC frame and extended the ultimate vertical displacement deflection **2.8-fold**.

## Key Contributions
- **First elucidation of the SFCB/UHPC-ECC fusion progressive collapse prevention mechanism**: the continuous tension of SFCB and the multiple fine crack tensile membrane of UHPC-ECC fully supported the catenary tension.
- **Complete prevention of concrete crushing and rebar fracture in the plastic hinge region**: stable load redistribution without joint failure even under large deformation ($\Delta = 450 mm$).
- **3.1-fold improvement of the progressive collapse ultimate resistance load**: a large margin in progressive collapse safety secured compared with the ordinary RC frame.
- **Proposal of design standard formulas for special seismic/blast-resistant frames**: adoption of the DoD UFC 4-023-03 progressive collapse prevention design criteria.

## Methods
- Four full-scale two-span single-story frame specimens ($6000 \times 2500 mm$, column section $300 \times 300 mm$, beam section $200 \times 350 mm$).
- Reinforcement variables: ordinary steel rebar versus SFCB (steel core + basalt fiber cover); beam joint material: ordinary concrete versus UHPC-ECC ($f_c = 120 MPa, \epsilon_u = 6.5 \%$).
- Test: quasi-static vertical compression test on the central column (MTS 1000 kN actuator, displacement control at $1.0 mm/min$).
