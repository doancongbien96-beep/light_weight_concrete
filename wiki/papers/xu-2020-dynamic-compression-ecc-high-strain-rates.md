## Summary
A study that evaluated the dynamic uniaxial compressive behavior of high-ductility composites (PVA-ECC) under impact and blast loading conditions using a split Hopkinson pressure bar (SHPB) over an ultra-high strain rate band ($\dot{\epsilon} = 30 \sim 200 s^{-1}$). The dynamic compressive strength increase factor (DIF), peak strain, dynamic elastic modulus, and energy absorption capacity were precisely measured as functions of increasing strain rate. It was demonstrated that under high-speed impact compression the bridging action of PVA fibers strongly constrains lateral expansion of axial fine cracks, so that, unlike ordinary concrete, a high residual deformation absorption capacity ($\epsilon_p > 2.5 \%$) is exhibited without fragmentation collapse.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a DIF formula for PVA-ECC dynamic compression at ultra-high strain rates**: $DIF = 1 + 0.085 \ln(\dot{\epsilon}/\dot{\epsilon}_0)$ ($10^{-5} \le \dot{\epsilon} \le 200 s^{-1}$).
- **Clarification of the fragmentation suppression and plastic energy absorption mechanism under impact**: at $\dot{\epsilon} = 180 s^{-1}$ the dynamic energy absorption density increased by a factor of 4.2 relative to static loading.
- **Fracture fragment size analysis**: high-speed video demonstrated that specimens after impact remained in solid block form instead of being completely pulverized.

## Methods
- **Material mix**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, REC15, $W/B = 0.25$, static compressive strength $52 MPa$).
- **Test equipment**: 75 mm diameter aluminum SHPB apparatus, semiconductor strain gauges, and a high-speed camera.
- **Specimens**: disc-type specimens with a diameter of 70 mm and a thickness of 35 mm.

## Results
- From the static compressive strength $52.5 MPa \rightarrow \dot{\epsilon} = 180 s^{-1}$, the dynamic compressive strength reached $88.5 MPa$ ($DIF = 1.68$).
- The peak compressive strain increased more than fourfold from the static value of $0.45 \%$ to the dynamic value of $1.85 \%$, demonstrating excellent impact ductility.
