# Autogenous Healing of ECC at Early Age

## Summary

An original paper from Michigan that evaluated self-healing by imposing artificial damage on 3-day-old ECC through direct-tension pre-straining (0.3 to 3 %), then exposing it to four environments: water/air cycling, water/hot-air cycling, 90 %RH/air cycling, and underwater immersion. Healing degree was quantified by resonant frequency (RF) and uniaxial tensile stiffness recovery ratio, demonstrating that ECC with crack width controlled at or below 60 µm can recover mechanical performance even at early age (stiffness recovery beyond mere watertightness recovery).

## Key Contributions

1. **Moisture contact is the foremost environmental factor for healing**: continuous or intermittent contact with external water is the most important factor triggering autogenous healing of early-age ECC. RF data show rapid recovery during the first few cycles, deceleration after 4 to 5 cycles, and a plateau beyond 10 cycles.
2. **Inverse correlation between initial damage level and recovery ratio**: RF and stiffness recovery ratios were highest at 0.3 to 0.5 % pre-straining (few cracks). Even with pre-straining up to 3 %, healing occurred, though incomplete, provided the mean crack width remained below 50 µm.
3. **Demonstration of true mechanical recovery**: unlike prior literature reporting only recovery of watertightness (transport property), this study quantified mechanical performance recovery in the form of stiffness and RF recovery, reaching 100 % complete recovery under some conditions (0.3 % pre-straining under CR1 and CR2).
4. **Clarification of the difference between early age and mature age**: mature ECC aged over 90 days maintained ultra-fine ~15 µm cracks even at 2 % strain, whereas 3-day-old specimens showed widening crack widths with increasing pre-strain, a mechanism explained by greater matrix stiffness and superior crack-width control capacity at higher age.

## Methods

- **Mix proportion**: Type I OPC + silica sand (mean 110 µm) + Class F fly ash (ASTM C618, Rockdale TX) + PVA fibers (Kuralon-II REC-15) at 2 vol % + PC-based high-range water-reducing admixture (ADVA Cast 530). PVA fiber: diameter 39 µm × length 12 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, density 1300 kg/m³, oil coating 1.2 wt % (to reduce interfacial chemical bonding per ECC micromechanics design). Strain capacity 3 %, steady-state crack width ~60 µm.
- **Specimens**: coupon specimens 300 × 76 × 12.5 mm, mixed in a Hobart mixer (20 L), covered with plastic sheeting and demolded at 24 h, then air cured for 2 days at 20 ± 1 °C and RH 50 ± 5 %.
- **Pre-damage**: direct tension at 3 days imposed strains of 0.3, 0.5, 1, 2, and 3 % (0.3 % being roughly 30 times the failure strain of ordinary concrete).
- **Healing environments**: CR1 water/air, CR2 water/hot air, CR3 90%RH/air, CR4 underwater immersion.
- **Measurement**: nondestructive resonant frequency (RF) measurement plus uniaxial tensile retesting to determine the stiffness recovery ratio.

## Results

- RF recovery: rapid rise followed by a plateau in moisture-contact environments. Cases of 100 % recovery existed.
- Stiffness recovery: greatest at 0.3 % pre-strain; recovery confirmed even at 3 %, though incomplete.
- Early-age specimens recovered less efficiently than mature-age ones (difference in crack-width control capability).
