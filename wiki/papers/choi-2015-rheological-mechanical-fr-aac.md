# Rheology and Micromechanical Mechanisms of a Fiber-Reinforced Alkali-Activated Slag Composite (FR-AAC) Achieving Ultra-Low Viscosity ($\mu_p < 1\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) and Strain-Hardening Ductility ($\varepsilon_{tu} = 2.38\%$) Simultaneously

## Summary

A leading rheology–micromechanics landmark origin paper by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University, which developed an **ultra-low-viscosity highly ductile fiber-reinforced alkali-activated composite (FR-AAC: Fiber-Reinforced Alkali-Activated Composite)** by combining a ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, specific surface $4280\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$) binder that excludes ordinary Portland cement 100 % with a combined calcium hydroxide ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) and sodium carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$) alkali activator plus hydrophilic PVA fiber ($V_f = 1.0, 1.3\ \text{vol.\%}$, W/B = 0.40, 0.45), in order to satisfy the ultra-low-viscosity flowability required by structural crack-repair grouting and oil-well/narrow-space pumping injection processes (plastic viscosity $\mu_p < 1.0\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, yield stress $\tau_y < 25\ \text{Pa}$) while overcoming the loss of tensile strain capacity that conventional low-viscosity cementitious composites suffered through fiber sedimentation and non-uniform dispersion; the team systematically performed rheological correlation modeling via V-funnel efflux time and mini-slump flow diameter together with uniaxial tension, compressive strength, **single-fiber pullout tests ($\tau_0, G_d, \beta$) and matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) micromechanical analysis**. The results showed that the proposed composite fully realized ultra-low viscosity ($\mu_p \approx 0.85\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, about 1/10 the level of ordinary concrete), with a V-funnel efflux time within 18 s and a flow diameter of 240 mm or more. Even in this ultra-low-viscosity state the fibers dispersed uniformly without clumping, and mix M40-1.3 exhibited outstanding pseudo strain-hardening with multiple fine cracks ($w < 50\ \mu\text{m}$): 28-day compressive strength **$28.2\ \text{MPa}$**, uniaxial tensile strength **$4.15\ \text{MPa}$**, and tensile strain capacity **$2.38\ \%$**, while fully satisfying the micromechanical energy criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip} = 2.35 > 1.0$).

## Key Contributions

1. **Simultaneous achievement of ultra-low viscosity ($\mu_p < 1.0\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, 1/10 the level of ordinary concrete) and 2.38 % tensile strain capacity**:
   - Broke through the limit of conventional UHPFRCC that lost strain-hardening capability at low viscosity, establishing a composite design technique that reconciles pumping injectability with 2.38 % super-high ductility.
2. **Establishment of a quantitative correlation model of rheological properties based on V-funnel efflux time and slump flow**:
   - Derived linear correlation functions between rheometer-measured viscosity and yield stress and simple field tests (V-funnel $T \le 18\ \text{s}$, Slump Flow $D \ge 230\ \text{mm}$).
3. **Establishment of AAS micromechanics parameters through single-fiber pullout and fracture toughness tests**:
   - Precisely measured the fracture toughness of the alkali-activated slag matrix ($K_m = 0.38\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{0.5}$), the chemical bond strength of PVA fiber ($G_d = 1.45\ \text{J/m}^2$), and the frictional shear stress ($\tau_0 = 2.10\ \text{MPa}$), theoretically verifying the steady-state crack-propagation condition.
4. **Securing 28.2 MPa structural-grade compressive strength as grout and repair injection material**:
   - Applied a cement-free eco-friendly inorganic binder to cut $\text{CO}_2$ emissions by 75 % while exhibiting strength exceeding the specifications for ground improvement and crack-injection materials.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and FR-AAC mix proportions (Table 1~4, by mass)**:
  - Binder: ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS, specific gravity 2.90, specific surface $4280\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, $\text{CaO}$ 42.1 %, $\text{SiO}_2$ 34.5 %, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ 15.2 %).
  - Combined alkali activator: $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (6.0 wt.% of slag) + $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (4.0 wt.% of slag).
  - Admixture: polycarboxylate superplasticizer (HRWRA, $0.8\sim 1.2\ \text{wt.\%}$ of binder mass).
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray hydrophilic PVA fiber ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elastic modulus 41 GPa).
  - Four mix groups: M40-1.0 (W/B 0.40, $V_f$ 1.0 %), M40-1.3 (W/B 0.40, $V_f$ 1.3 %), M45-1.0 (W/B 0.45, $V_f$ 1.0 %), M45-1.3 (W/B 0.45, $V_f$ 1.3 %).
- **Test equipment and methods (Figure 1~3)**:
  - Rheology tests: rotational rheometer (parallel-plate measuring system, shear rate $0.1\sim 100\ \text{s}^{-1}$), V-funnel (bottom nozzle $15\ \text{mm} \times 15\ \text{mm}$, 1.0 L), mini-slump cone ($\phi 70\times \phi 100\times 50\ \text{mm}$).
  - Uniaxial tension test: JSCE dogbone specimen ($330\ \text{mm} \times 60\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, servo testing machine $0.2\ \text{mm/min}$, gauge length 80 mm).
  - Single-fiber pullout and wedge-splitting fracture toughness tests ($0.2\ \text{mm/min}$, $0.001\ \text{mm/s}$).

### 2. Rheology–micromechanics correlation mechanism interpretation
- **Correlation between W/B ratio and the fiber interface**: increasing the W/B ratio from 0.40 to 0.45 further lowers plastic viscosity, but the reduced matrix densification lowers the interfacial frictional shear strength ($\tau_0$) of the fiber, dropping ultimate tensile strength and bridging energy. W/B = 0.40 was thereby identified as the optimal mix point achieving ultra-low viscosity and 2.38 % strain capacity simultaneously.
- **Multiple-crack dispersion**: mix M40-1.3 formed more than 25 fine cracks evenly throughout the entire tensile deformation range, completely preventing localization of failure.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University is a world-class rheology–micromechanics fusion landmark origin paper that simultaneously achieved sub-$1\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ ultra-low viscosity and 2.38 % super-ductile strain-hardening using a cement-free alkali-activated slag binder.
