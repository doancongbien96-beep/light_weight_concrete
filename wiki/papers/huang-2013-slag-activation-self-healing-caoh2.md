# Self-Healing of Cracks in Slag Cementitious Materials via Calcium Hydroxide Activation of Unhydrated Slag: Gas Permeability Reduction and Ultrasonic Wave Recovery

## Summary

To drastically curtail carbon emissions associated with cement clinker production by utilizing blast furnace slag cements with 66~80 wt.% slag replacement (CEM III/B 42.5R, W/C = 0.30), Dr. Haoliang Huang and Professor Guang Ye at Delft University of Technology (TU Delft Microlab) developed a **slag-activated self-healing system**. Instead of using strong alkalis like $\text{NaOH}$ or sodium silicate that risk alkali-silica reaction (ASR), the system injects a **saturated calcium hydroxide solution ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$)** with complete chemical compatibility into cracks to alkali-activate unhydrated slag exposed along crack faces. Investigating mineral phases (FTIR, TGA/DTG, ESEM-EDS), air permeability reduction (Cembureau cell), and ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) recovery in RC beams containing embedded hollow glass tubes, the authors showed that saturated $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ dissolves $\text{Si-O-Si}$ and $\text{Al-O-Si}$ glassy bonds to precipitate **dense C-S-H gel ($\text{Ca/Si} = 1.2, \text{Al/Si} = 0.21$), ettringite, and monocarboaluminate**. In $10\ \mu\text{m}$ microcracks, air permeability dropped from $1.8 \times 10^{-16} \rightarrow 2.5 \times 10^{-17}\ \text{m}^2$ within 250 h (10 d), achieving a **self-healing efficiency of 86.1 %** (64.6 % higher than water immersion at 52.3 %) and **recovering ultrasonic pulse velocity to 95 % of uncracked baseline levels**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Alkali-activation self-healing mechanism using saturated $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ solution**:
   - Bypassed ASR risks associated with caustic alkalis by utilizing saturated $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ to activate latent hydraulic slag grains along crack walls.
2. **Micro-mineralogical characterization of healing products (FTIR/TGA/ESEM-EDS)**:
   - Identified that healing precipitates consist of low-$\text{Ca/Si}$ ($1.2$) C-S-H gel, ettringite, and monocarboaluminate, providing superior structural adhesion compared to calcite alone.
3. **86.1 % self-healing efficiency via Cembureau gas permeability testing**:
   - Quantified crack sealing kinetics over 250 h via Hagen-Poiseuille relations, demonstrating healing rates more than 2-fold faster than pure water immersion.
4. **95 % UPV recovery with embedded brittle glass tube release system**:
   - Validated autonomous fluid release in RC beams using embedded $\phi 5\ \text{mm}$ hollow glass tubes, fully restoring elastic wave transmission across healed crack planes.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and specimen preparation (Table 1 & Figure 1)**:
  - Cement: Blast furnace slag cement CEM III/B 42.5R (~70 wt.% slag, W/C = 0.30).
  - Aggregates: River sand (0~4 mm, S/C = 1.75), silica fume 1.0 wt.%, polycarboxylate SP 1.5 wt.%.
  - Three specimen series:
    1. Series I (Mineral analysis): $40\ \text{mm} \times 40\ \text{mm} \times 5\ \text{mm}$ plates.
    2. Series II (Gas permeability): $\phi 100\ \text{mm} \times 15\ \text{mm}$ disks (with 1.0 wt.% PVA fibers, splitting tension inducing $w = 10 \pm 3\ \mu\text{m}$ cracks).
    3. Series III (UPV beams): $125\ \text{mm} \times 125\ \text{mm} \times 1000\ \text{mm}$ RC beams with embedded $\phi 5\ \text{mm}$ (1 mm wall) hollow glass tubes.
- **Testing protocols**:
  - Gas permeability: Cembureau cell ($0.05\sim 0.3\ \text{MPa}$ nitrogen differential pressure, 2 h vacuum pre-drying).
  - Ultrasonic pulse velocity: Pundit Lab transducers (54 kHz direct transmission).
  - Microstructural analysis: ESEM (Philips XL30), EDS ($\text{Ca/Si}, \text{Al/Si}, \text{Mg/Si}$), FTIR ($4000\sim 400\ \text{cm}^{-1}$), TGA/DTG ($20\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$).

### 2. Slag alkali activation and microstructure densification mechanics
- **Supplying $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{OH}^-$ ions**: Saturated $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ elevates crack solution pH above 12.5, breaking glassy slag networks to trigger voluminous secondary C-S-H gel formation.
- **Watertightness and wave propagation**: Precipitated C-S-H blocked 86 % of air flow channels and restored acoustic continuity across crack boundaries.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established a chemical self-healing route for slag cementitious materials using saturated calcium hydroxide to activate unhydrated slag, achieving 86 % permeability reduction and 95 % UPV recovery.
