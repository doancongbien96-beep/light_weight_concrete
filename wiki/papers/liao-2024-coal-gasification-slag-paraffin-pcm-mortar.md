# Thermal and Mechanical Performance of Cement Mortars Incorporating Coal Gasification Slag (CGS)-Based Paraffin Shape-Stabilized PCM (CGS-P): $3.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Indoor Peak-Temperature Reduction and a 135-Minute Extension of Thermal Lag

## Summary

To reduce the heating and cooling loads that account for more than $40\%$ of building energy consumption, and to enable high-value resource recycling of coal gasification slag (CGS), an industrial by-product, a research team led by **Prof. Kun Ma of Kunming University of Science and Technology** synthesized a **shape-stabilized phase change material (CGS-P, paraffin loading 30 wt.%)** that exploits the fine porous skeleton of CGS as a capillary-adsorption support for organic paraffin, via vacuum impregnation, and rigorously evaluated the microstructure, 28-day compressive and flexural strengths, latent heat capacity ($\Delta H_m = 52.8\ \text{J/g}$), thermal conductivity, and the indoor solar-heating model-box temperature-control performance of **composite energy storage cement mortars (CESCM)** in which CGS-P replaced natural sand at 0 %, 15 %, and 30 %. This is a landmark 2024 original paper in the Journal of Building Engineering. The results show that the CESCM30 mix achieves a **thermal conductivity reduction from $1.35 \rightarrow 0.48\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ ($64.4\%$ lower), a $3.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ drop in indoor peak temperature, and a 135-minute thermal lag effect while retaining structural strength of 28-day compressive strength $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ and flexural strength $5.8\ \text{MPa}$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of a coal gasification slag (CGS)-based paraffin shape-stabilized composite PCM (CGS-P)**:
   - Exploiting the intrinsic specific surface area of CGS ($56.3\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$) and the capillary forces of its fine mesopores, a highly stable CGS-P (melting latent heat $52.8\ \text{J/g}$, phase-change temperature $28.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$) was synthesized exhibiting zero paraffin leakage rate (0.0 %) even at $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Simultaneous attainment of structural strength and insulation performance in the composite energy storage mortar (CESCM30)**:
   - Even at 30 % sand substitution, 28-day compressive strength $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ and flexural strength $5.8\ \text{MPa}$ were secured while the insulating thermal conductivity was sharply lowered to $0.48\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$.
3. **Demonstration of building thermal regulation through a 24-hour indoor model-box solar heating test**:
   - Relative to ordinary mortar, the indoor maximum temperature was lowered by $3.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and the minimum raised by $1.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, easing the diurnal temperature swing and delaying the indoor peak-arrival time by 135 minutes.
4. **Elucidation of the cement hydration gel (C-S-H) microstructure and compatibility upon CGS-P incorporation**:
   - BET, XRD, DSC, and SEM-EDS elemental mapping established that the paraffin is physically confined perfectly within the CGS porous body alone, without hindering the cement hydration reaction.

## Methods

- **Synthesis of the CGS-P composite PCM (Section 2.2 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Porous support: coal gasification slag powder ($d_{50} = 45\ \mu\text{m}$, BET specific surface area $56.3\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Phase change material: paraffin wax (pure latent heat $178.5\ \text{J/g}$, melting point $28.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Fabrication process: liquid paraffin was vacuum-adsorbed at a 30 wt.% ratio in an $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ vacuum oven under 0.08 MPa reduced pressure.
- **Mortar mix design (Section 2.3 & Table 2)**:
  - `CESCM`: ordinary cement mortar control (cement:sand:water = 1:3:0.5).
  - `CESCM15`: 15 % of the sand volume replaced with CGS-P (polycarboxylate SP added).
  - `CESCM30`: 30 % of the sand volume replaced with CGS-P.
- **Property measurement and thermal performance testing (Section 3 & Figs. 2~7)**:
  - Thermal characterization: TA Instruments DSC-2500 differential scanning calorimeter, Hot Disk thermal conductivity analyzer.
  - Solar thermal regulation: a $20\ \text{mm}$ thick panel mounted on top of an insulated $300 \times 300 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ test chamber, measured over 24-hour heating/cooling cycles under a 500W infrared lamp.

### 2. Interpretation of the Thermal Energy Storage and Phase-Change Lag Mechanism
- **Suppression of indoor temperature rise through latent-heat absorption**: when daytime solar heat reaches the building envelope, the paraffin confined inside the CGS porous body undergoes a solid $\rightarrow$ liquid phase change at $28.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$, absorbing substantial heat of $\Delta H_m = 15.6\ \text{J/g}$ and blocking heat transfer into the interior for 135 minutes.
- **Reduction of heating load through nocturnal latent-heat release**: as the nighttime temperature falls, the liquid paraffin solidifies again and slowly releases the stored heat indoors, raising the indoor minimum temperature by more than $1.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and maximizing thermal comfort.

- **Overall conclusion**: this landmark original paper establishes smart zero-energy-building composite mortar technology that upcycles coal gasification slag waste into the carrier of a high-functionality shape-stabilized PCM, maintaining structural strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$ while lowering indoor peak temperature by $3.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and cutting thermal conductivity by $64\%$.
