## Summary
A 2024 study that characterized the thermal properties, thermal conductivity, latent heat capacity, and mechanical performance in cement mortar application of a novel form-stable phase change material (CA-PA/EG ss-PCM) prepared by vacuum-impregnating a bio-based fatty acid eutectic of caprylic acid (CA) and palmitic acid (PA) (CA-PA, phase change temperature $22.5 °C$, latent heat $\Delta H = 152 J/g$) into porous expanded graphite (EG, specific surface area $35 m^2/g$), developed to produce a thermal energy storage building mortar optimized for the indoor heating and cooling comfort temperature band of buildings ($20 \sim 26 °C$). It demonstrated that the highly thermally conductive skeleton of expanded graphite enhances the thermal conductivity fourfold to **1.95 W/(m·K)** while delivering an effective latent heat of **38.5 J/g**.

## Key Contributions
- **Synthesis of an indoor thermal comfort tailored fatty acid eutectic (CA-PA $T_m = 22.5 °C$) ss-PCM**: realization of the optimal phase change temperature.
- **Fourfold maximization of thermal conductivity through the three-dimensional porous skeleton of expanded graphite ($0.48 \rightarrow 1.95 W/(m\cdot K)$)**: 65 % reduction in phase change heat charging time.
- **Securement of a mortar 28 d compressive strength of 32.5 MPa**: finish panel strength requirements satisfied at the 20 wt % incorporation condition.
- **Mitigation of indoor peak temperature fluctuation by 4.8 °C**: 38 % reduction in the heating and cooling loads of zero-energy buildings.

## Methods
- ss-PCM preparation: molten CA-PA eutectic liquid (mass ratio 82:18) was adsorbed onto expanded graphite in a vacuum impregnation apparatus (loading ratio 85 wt %).
- Mortar mixes: ss-PCM was substituted by volume into OPC mortar (10, 20, 30 vol %).
- Testing: Hot Disk TPS thermal conductivity analyzer, DSC latent heat analysis, 28 d compressive/flexural strength, and full-scale exterior wall insulation chamber thermal performance tests.
