# Overview of ECC Materials and Applications (Li 2003 Invited Review)

## Summary

V.C. Li's invited review summarizing the first decade since the invention of ECC (inaugural volume of JACT). It organizes the micromechanics-based design philosophy (the σ-δ curve as the link connecting material design and structural design), the multiple-cracking conditions of strength criterion·energy criterion, the development process of interface tailoring for REC15 PVA fibers, five major characteristics of R/ECC structural behavior, three application domains, and ten future R&D tasks. A hub note linking the wiki's theory axis and application axis.

## Key Contributions

- Formalization of the σ-δ bridging curve as a 'nonlinear spring law': when complementary energy is large, flat steady-state cracks form (δ_ss<δ_p) with multiple cracking; when small, a Griffith crack forms with softening. This figure system became the standard diagram of all subsequent ECC literature.
- Codification of the two conditions for multiple cracking: (1) strength criterion σ_fc≤σ_cu, (2) energy criterion J′b>Jtip. Presentation of the ISMD concept integrating PDDA (performance-driven design) and PBDC (performance-based structural design).
- Quantitative case of interface tailoring: with PVA surface coating of 0.8~1.2 wt %, frictional bond τ0 drops to 1~2 MPa, critical fiber volume fraction is minimized to about 2 %, and strain capacity rises from 1 %→5 %. The REC15 fiber is the product of this design.
- Five major characteristics of R/ECC: shear reinforcement can be omitted (Ohno shear beam), large imposed deformation absorption (link slab at 1.3 % strain with uncracked concrete), deformation compatibility with steel reinforcement (minimized interfacial shear stress), synergy with FRP strengthening (prevention of premature FRP rupture), damage tolerance (notch insensitivity).
- Reinterpretation of strength vs ductility: concrete panel with compressive strength 50 MPa (structural capacity 38 kN) vs ECC panel with compressive strength 41 MPa (56 kN) — paradoxical data showing that material ductility raises structural strength.

## Methods

- Review paper (based on the keynote lecture at the 2002 DFRCC workshop). Synthesis of micromechanics theory (steady-state crack analysis), interface tailoring experiments (single-fiber pullout + direct tension), and R/ECC structural testing·FEM literature.

## Results

- Crack width: in ECC with 2 % REC15 (coating 0.8 %), widths stabilize at about 80 μm after 1 % strain — remaining below the 100 μm water-permeability threshold (Tsukamoto 1990 et al.) is the core basis for durability.
- Three application domains: seismic/heavy-load safety infrastructure (beams·columns·hybrid joints·self-centering frames·strengthening walls), durability infrastructure in harsh environments (dams·bridge deck undersides·sewer pipes·tunnel lining repair), construction productivity (omission of shear reinforcement, spraying·extrusion·self-compacting methods).
- Ten future tasks: standard mix designs, premix, raw-material supply, material performance specifications (strain capacity·tensile strength·multiple cracking), standard test methods, numerical analysis tools, integration of application knowledge, sustainability (fly ash·recycled fibers), structural innovation (self-adaptive frames), ISMD.
