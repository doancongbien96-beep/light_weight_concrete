## Summary
In this latest study, serpentine powder, a magnesium-silicate hydrate mineral, was incorporated as a partial-replacement admixture into a high-strength cement matrix (HS-ECC), suppressing the chemical fracture toughness of the matrix while simultaneously optimizing the frictional shear stress ($\tau_0$) at the fiber-matrix interface, thereby synergistically enhancing tensile strength and tensile strain capacity at the same time. The layered nanostructure and the magnesium leaching action of serpentine formed an M-S-H / C-S-H hybrid gel, realizing extraordinary high-strength, ultra-high-ductility performance reaching a direct tensile strength of 14.8 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 8.2 % at a compressive strength level of 110 MPa.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the mechanism by which serpentine simultaneously improves the PSH index**: serpentine particles relaxed the matrix fracture toughness $K_m$ to $0.58 MPa\sqrt{m}$ while increasing the fiber pull-out slip frictional resistance by 30 %.
- **New record of the simultaneous attainment of ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility**: a compressive strength of $112 MPa$, a tensile strength of $14.8 MPa$, and a ductility of $8.2 \%$ achieved.
- **Microstructure densification through M-S-H hybrid phase formation**: the serpentine hydration mechanism elucidated through XRD, TEM, and 29Si NMR analyses.

## Methods
- **Material mix**: cement + serpentine powder (10 ~ 30 wt % replacement) + silica fume, $W/B = 0.18$, UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$).
- **Test items**: uniaxial dogbone direct tensile test, single-fiber pull-out test, 28 d compressive strength, TEM-EDS, and 29Si MAS-NMR.

## Results
- 20 wt % serpentine replacement mix: 28 d compressive strength of $112.5 MPa$, ultimate tensile strength of $14.8 MPa$, tensile strain capacity of $8.25 \%$, and average crack width of $20 \mu m$.
- Demonstrated that the tensile energy absorption capacity improved by 45 % compared with the conventional silica sand mix.
