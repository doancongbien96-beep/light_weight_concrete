# Waste tyre rubberized concrete: properties at fresh and hardened state

## Summary

An experimental study (University of Salento, Italy) on rubcrete in which shredded waste-
tyre rubber replaces coarse or fine aggregate at 25–75 % by weight on an equal-gradations
basis, testing workability, density, compressive and flexural strength, and post-cracking
behavior. The headline findings: coarse-aggregate substitution degrades strength far more
than fine-aggregate substitution, whereas coarse rubber chips confer fiber-reinforced-
concrete-class energy absorption in the post-cracking regime. The study is distinguished by
opening with material characterization including the rubber's own tensile properties
(9.04 MPa, elongation at break 256 %).

## Key Contributions

- Upfront characterization of tyre shreds: sieve analysis (maximum residue 31.1 wt % on the
  20 mm mesh), density 1.089 ± 0.036 g/cm3 (COV 3.28 %), and ASTM D638 tension giving tensile
  strength 9.04 MPa, ultimate strain 256 %, E0 3.37 MPa, E50 2.39 MPa, E100 2.78 MPa. The
  scatter of literature densities (0.43–1.15 g/cm3) across tyre types (passenger vs truck)
  is attributed to differences in rubber origin.
- Quantified advantage of fine- over coarse-aggregate substitution: compressive strength
  drops −54.4 %/−61.9 % for coarse substitution at 50 %/75 % but only −28.3 %/−37.1 % for
  fine substitution. Flexural strength shows a wider gap: −28.2 % for both 50 % and 75 %
  coarse substitution versus −5.8 %/−7.3 % for fine.
- Post-cracking benefit: only the coarse-substitution mixes (RB1, RC1) showed appreciable
  residual tensile stress; ASTM C1018 toughness indices for RC1 were I5=4.96, I10=9.92,
  I20=17.8 (within accepted fiber-concrete ranges 6, 12, 25), with energy absorption of
  196 kN·mm (RB1 113 kN·mm). Coarse rubber chips prevent sudden secondary separation of
  the beam.
- Workability actually improved: all mixes with untreated rubber rose from slump class S4
  (180 mm, control) to S5-class 215–225 mm — demonstrating practicality with existing plant.
- Application proposals: impact/blast-resistant structures (bunkers, low-profile Jersey
  barriers), vibration-damping foundations (railway stations), noise barriers; together with
  the open verification tasks of long-term behavior and leaching (zinc, cadmium, lead,
  polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons).

## Methods

- Mixes: cement 335 kg/m3 throughout. Stage 1 (coarse substitution): 12.5–20 mm coarse
  aggregate replaced by rubber at 25/50/75 % volume (RA1/RB1/RC1). Stage 2 (fine
  substitution): 10–12.5 mm aggregate replaced at 15/30/50/75 % (RA2–RD2). Rubber max size
  20 mm (stage 1) and 10–15 mm (stage 2); hand-fed without admixture treatment;
  fluidifier 3.35 L/m3.
- Rubber tension tests: electromechanical LR30K machine, displacement control, 1 kN load
  cell, fiberglass tab anchorage, 5 specimens.
- Fresh concrete: UNI EN 12350 slump and unit weight. Hardened concrete: compression per
  UNI EN 12390 (five 150 mm cubes, curing 20 °C / RH 75 %, 28 days).
- Flexure: stage 1, 250×250×900 mm beams under four-point (third-point) loading; stage 2,
  100×100×350 mm beams three-point loading; central deflection measured with two LVDTs,
  tested at 28 days.
- Toughness: ASTM C1018-97 indices I5, I10, I20 and residual strength factors R5,10 and
  R10,20.

## Results

- Density: decreases proportionally to rubber content — coarse substitution −5.8 % at 50 %
  (2234 kg/m3) and −8.8 % at 75 %; fine substitution −6.0 % at 50 %, −8.3 % at 75 %. Scope
  for lightweight building elements.
- Compressive strength: controls C1 45.80 MPa (coarse series) and C2 27.11 MPa (fine
  series). Coarse substitution RA1 −47.8 %, RB1 −54.4 %, RC1 −61.9 %; fine substitution
  RA2 −11.6 %, RB2 −24.7 %, RC2 −28.3 %, RD2 −37.1 %.
- Flexural strength: baselines C1 3.51 MPa and C3 5.34 MPa. Coarse substitution −16.6 % at
  25 %, −28.2 % at both 50 % and 75 %; fine substitution −4.5 to −7.3 % across 15–75 %,
  marginal.
- Load-deflection curves: fine substitution at 25 and 50 % behaved like plain brittle
  concrete; at 75 % post-peak residual stress nearly vanished. Coarse substitution at 50
  and 75 % showed gentle softening branches with high energy absorption.
- Literature comparison (Topcu 1995, Eldin & Senouci 1993, Toutanji 1996): agreement that
  coarse chips reduce strength far more than fine substitution; consistent with Toutanji's
  −57 to −70 % at 50–75 % total-aggregate substitution.
