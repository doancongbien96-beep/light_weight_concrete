# Flexural Cracking Resistance and Impact Energy Absorption ($16.5\ \text{kJ/m}^2$) Mechanism of Hybrid-Fiber (PVA/PE/Steel) Reinforced Cement Composites

## Summary

To effectively suppress brittle crack propagation in cement composites across multiple scales and secure structural safety under dynamic impact loads, cement composites were designed by incorporating **micro organic fibers (hydrophilic PVA, high-strength PE) and macro hooked steel fibers (SF) in single and hybrid combinations ($V_f = 1.5\sim 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, and four-point bending notched-beam fracture tests, drop-weight impact tests, and high-resolution image-based crack propagation analysis were performed, thereby elucidating **the flexural strength of $14.5\ \text{MPa}$, the static fracture energy of $16.5\ \text{kJ/m}^2$, and the peak-stress dispersion and rear-face fragment-scatter suppression mechanisms under impact in the steel-fiber/PE-fiber hybrid mixture**. This is a 2015 landmark origin paper by the joint research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University, corresponding author), researcher Wan-Shin Park, Prof. Du-Yeol Ryu, and Prof. Gyuyong Kim (Chungnam National University)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the multi-scale hybrid synergy between micro synthetic fibers (PVA/PE) and macro steel fibers**:
   - Demonstrated a multi-stage bridging mechanism in which fine micro fibers suppress initial crack formation and micro-crack opening, while the large steel fibers restrain the opening of the main crack in the large-deformation regime.
2. **Demonstration of a 110-fold amplification of the static flexural fracture energy ($G_F$) ($0.15 \rightarrow 16.5\ \text{kJ/m}^2$)**:
   - Quantified, through RILEM standard 3-point/4-point bend fracture tests, the post-peak ductile tensile-zone formation behavior of hybrid-fiber composites compared with single-fiber composites.
3. **Elucidation of crack-propagation delay and impact absorption capacity under impact loading**:
   - Proved that, under dynamic impact, the high shear resistance of the steel fibers together with the high-elongation frictional pull-out action of the PE fibers dissipates impact-wave energy throughout the matrix.
4. **Comparative analysis of interfacial bond behavior by synthetic fiber type (hydrophilic PVA vs. hydrophobic PE)**:
   - Precisely analyzed the differences in how the fiber-rupture mode governed by the high interfacial bonding of PVA fibers and the frictional pull-out mode of PE fibers affect composite toughness.

## Methods

- **Materials and composite mixtures (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ordinary Portland cement (OPC) + fly ash (FA 20 wt.%).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 120\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40, W/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fiber combinations:
    1. `SF`: hooked steel fiber ($L_f = 30\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 0.50\ \text{mm}$, $V_f = 1.5\%$).
    2. `PVA`: Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $V_f = 1.5\%$).
    3. `PE`: Spectra PE fiber ($L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, $V_f = 1.5\%$).
    4. `Hyb-SF/PE`: steel fiber 1.0% + PE fiber 0.75%.
- **Mechanical test program (Section 2.2 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Notched-beam flexural test: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ notched beam (span 300 mm, notch depth 20 mm).
  - Impact test: drop-weight impact testing machine ($50\ \text{kg}$ hammer drop).

### 2. Analysis of the multi-scale fiber bridging and impact cushioning mechanisms

- **Scale-bridging effect**: The micro PE fibers suppress microcracks at the $10\sim 50\ \mu\text{m}$ level, doubling the first-cracking strength, while the macro steel fibers firmly grip macro crack openings of $0.5\sim 2\ \text{mm}$, raising the fracture energy to $16.5\ \text{kJ/m}^2$.
- **Impact-wave dispersion and fragment-scatter prevention**: When a dynamic impact wave is applied, the hybrid bridging network converts the impact energy into shear and flexural deformation energy, blocking rear-face fragment scattering completely.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark origin paper on structural fiber-reinforced materials in which the research team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee at Chonnam National University amplified the fracture energy 110-fold through multi-scale hybridization of steel and PE fibers and maximized impact-load cushioning performance.
