## Summary
A representative CBM review paper by the research team of Professor Caijun Shi of Hunan University that comprehensively reviewed, on the basis of some 200 research papers worldwide, the fatal explosive spalling, the degradation of residual compressive/tensile/flexural strength, the thermal decomposition of nano C-S-H gel, and the fine pore structure evolution mechanisms that occur when ultra-high performance concrete (UHPC) is exposed to fire and elevated temperatures ($200 \sim 1000 °C$), and that systematized hybrid fiber design guidelines for spalling prevention. Through a hybrid combination of steel fiber and low-melting-point polypropylene (PP) fiber ($V_f = 0.2 \% PP + 2.0 \% Steel$), it established fire-resistant design criteria that block spalling 100 % completely even under an $800 °C$ ultra-high-temperature fire while securing a residual compressive strength ratio of **55 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Quantification of the three principal triggering factors of elevated-temperature explosive spalling in UHPC**: accumulation of ultra-high vapor pressure caused by extremely low porosity, thermal stress gradients, and thermal expansion mismatch.
- **Systematization of the pressure-relief network design principle based on PP fiber melting ($165 °C$)**: identification of the optimal ranges of fiber diameter $18 \sim 32 \mu m$ and incorporation rate $0.15 \sim 0.30 vol \%$.
- **Establishment of master curves of residual mechanical property degradation by temperature ($200 \sim 1000 °C$)**: proposal of revision guidance for the Eurocode 4 and ACI 216 standards.
- **Presentation of non-destructive diagnosis techniques for post-fire residual load-bearing capacity of structures**: establishment of evaluation protocols based on ultrasonic velocity and resonant frequency.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of elevated-temperature exposure test data from some 200 global UHPC studies.
- Multidisciplinary integration of degradation mechanisms using TGA/DSC, XRD, SEM-EDS, and mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP).

## Results
- Envelope data of standard UHPC residual mechanical properties by heating temperature:

| $400 °C$ | 95 % spalling fragmentation | **No spalling (safe)** | 85 % | 65 % | Onset of calcium hydroxide decomposition |
| $600 °C$ | 100 % spalling collapse | **No spalling (safe)** | 55 % | 32 % | Decomposition and dehydration of C-S-H gel |
