# Crack-Control Performance in the Hogging-Moment Regions of Natural-Curing Steel–UHTCC/ECC and Steel–UHPC Continuous Composite Beams: 3–4× Improvement in Crack-Initiation Load and Crack Widths Constrained Below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ at Ultimate Load

## Summary

A 2022 landmark original paper in the ASCE Journal of Bridge Engineering by the joint research team of **Zhejiang University and Fuzhou University (Guannan Wang, Jian-ping Lin)**, which — to fundamentally solve the problems of premature cracking caused by the enormous tensile stresses acting on the deck-slab concrete and the resulting rebar-corrosion durability degradation in the intermediate-support hogging-moment regions of continuous steel–concrete composite bridges — conducted hogging-moment flexural loading tests on 2.5 m span continuous composite beams incorporating **normal concrete (Steel-NSC), ultra-high performance concrete (Steel-UHPC), and ultra-high toughness cementitious composite (Steel-UHTCC/ECC)** each under **natural curing conditions without costly steam curing**, and precisely compared and evaluated the crack-initiation loads, multiple-crack dispersion patterns, load-step-wise crack width evolution ($w-P$), and ultimate rotation ductility. The study demonstrated that the **Steel-UHTCC composite beam improved its initial cracking load threefold over NSC ($40 \rightarrow 120\ \text{kN}$)** and that up to reaching the ultimate failure load (600 kN) the maximum crack width remained **perfectly constrained below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ ($0.05\ \text{mm}$) across the entire tension slab** — a saturated multiple fine-cracking behavior.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of complete control of hogging-moment tension-slab crack widths below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ through natural-curing UHTCC/ECC substitution**:
   - While the normal reinforced-concrete slab (Steel-NSC) exceeded the early cracking limit at $w > 0.3\ \text{mm}$, UHTCC physically constrained the crack width to $0.05\ \text{mm}$ or less even at the ultimate load stage (600 kN), achieving semi-permanent waterproofing and corrosion protection.
2. **Drastic increase of the crack-initiation load in the hogging-moment region**:
   - Compared with normal concrete (NSC: 40 kN), Steel-UHTCC delayed cracking to 120 kN (3×) and Steel-UHPC to 160 kN (4×), dramatically expanding the serviceability limit state (SLS) margin.
3. **Securing on-site natural-casting constructibility for prefab construction with steam curing eliminated**:
   - Without high-temperature steam curing at $90\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the mechanical performance of 120 MPa-class UHPC and UHTCC with tensile strain capacity of $3.0\%$ was fully realized in the composite structure under natural ambient curing alone.
4. **Delay of local buckling of the steel girder and maximization of rotation ductility**:
   - The neutral axis was stabilized by the sustained tensile bridging stress transfer of the UHTCC slab, preventing premature buckling of the compression-side steel flange and increasing the plastic-hinge rotation capacity by more than $40\%$.

## Methods

- **Material properties (Section 2 & Table 1)**:
  - `NSC`: compressive strength $42.5\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strength $2.8\ \text{MPa}$.
  - `UHPC (natural curing)`: compressive strength $125.0\ \text{MPa}$, first-cracking tensile strength $7.5\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strength $9.2\ \text{MPa}$ (steel fibers $V_f = 2.0\%$).
  - `UHTCC (natural curing)`: compressive strength $55.0\ \text{MPa}$, first-cracking tensile strength $3.8\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strength $5.2\ \text{MPa}$, tensile strain capacity $3.2\%$ (PVA fibers $V_f = 2.0\%$).
- **Continuous composite beam specimens and loading conditions (Section 3 & Figs. 6~8)**:
  - Specimen geometry: span $L = 2.5\ \text{m}$, I-shaped steel beam ($360 \times 138 \times 12 \times 16\ \text{mm}$, Q345B steel).
  - Slab geometry: width $B = 600\ \text{mm}$, thickness $H = 80\ \text{mm}$, longitudinal reinforcement ratio $\rho_s = 2.0\%$.
  - Precise flexural loading of the cantilever/intermediate-support hogging-moment region (load control $\rightarrow$ displacement control at $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$).
  - Instrumentation: optical crack microscope ($0.01\ \text{mm}$ precision) and a strain-gauge network.

### 2. Analysis of the hogging-moment crack dispersion and stress redistribution mechanisms
- **Strain hardening and stress dispersion of UHTCC**: upon tensile crack formation, the PVA fibers across the crack faces continuously redistribute the tensile stress into the surrounding slab, fundamentally blocking concentration into a single large crack and forming an ultra-fine crack network spaced at $10\sim 20\ \text{mm}$ over the entire deck slab.
- **High-strength crack-delay mechanism of UHPC**: because UHPC maintains a completely crack-free state up to 160 kN owing to its high matrix tensile strength, it is optimal for regions requiring the prevention of cracking itself due to high water-tightness demands, while UHTCC is shown to be optimal for post-cracking large-deformation ductile confinement.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is an ASCE landmark original reference that resolved the chronic hogging-moment tensile cracking problem of continuous steel–concrete composite bridges through substitution with natural-curing UHTCC/ECC and UHPC slabs, constraining crack widths below $50\ \mu\text{m}$ over the entire range from service loads to ultimate loads, thereby establishing a fundamental structural design technology securing a hundred-year service life for bridge decks.
