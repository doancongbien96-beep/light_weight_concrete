# Microstructural phase conversion and fire-resistant behavior of calcium aluminate cement (CAC)-based ultra-high performance concrete (UHPC) under ultra-high-temperature ($200\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) exposure: the mechanism behind $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ ceramic sintering and the emergence of a residual compressive strength of $105\ \text{MPa}$

## Summary

To fundamentally overcome the defects that ordinary Portland cement (OPC)-based UHPC suffers in fire and ultra-high-temperature thermal shock ($>600\ ^\circ\text{C}$) environments — dehydration collapse of calcium hydroxide/C-S-H, destructive explosive spalling caused by water-vapor-pressure buildup within the dense microstructure, and loss of more than $80\%$ of residual strength — this study developed **fire-resistant ultra-high performance concrete in which OPC was replaced at the $100\%$ level by calcium aluminate cement (CAC), combined with silica fume and micro steel fibers ($2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) (CAC-UHPC: room-temperature compressive strength $152.0\ \text{MPa}$)**, and through electric-furnace high-temperature heating exposure tests from room temperature up to $200, 400, 600, 800, 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ together with quantitative XRD/TGA/DSC crystalline-phase analysis, MIP pore-structure evaluation, and SEM-EDS microstructural examination, demonstrated **zero explosive spalling ($0.0$) even under extreme fire exposure at $1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$, crystalline gehlenite ($\text{C}_2\text{AS}$) and anorthite ceramic phase conversion driven by high-temperature sintering at $800\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and a residual compressive strength of $105.0\ \text{MPa}$ ($69.1\%$ retention relative to room temperature)** — the highest-authority landmark origin paper of Cement and Concrete Research 2017 by the team of **Dr. N. K. Lee of the National University of Singapore (NUS) and Drs. Koh Kyung-taek and Ryu Geum-seong (corresponding author) of the Korea Institute of Civil Engineering and Building Technology (KICT)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first complete elucidation of the ultra-high-temperature ($1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) fire-resistant microstructural mechanism of cement-free CAC-based UHPC**:
   - Demonstrated that, unlike OPC, which collapses above $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, CAC hydrates react with silica fume at high temperature and convert into a dense, robust ceramic phase ($\text{Ca}_2\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_7$).
2. **Prevention of explosive spalling at the $100\%$ level even without polypropylene (PP) fibers**:
   - During the crystal transition of CAC hydrates ($\text{CAH}_{10} \rightarrow \text{C}_3\text{AH}_6$), pores coarsen slightly (pore coarsening) and self-form escape channels for water vapor, cutting off internal vapor-pressure rupture at its source.
3. **Demonstrated remarkable retention of a residual compressive strength of $105.0\ \text{MPa}$ after heating to $1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$**:
   - While conventional OPC-UHPC collapses to below $15\ \text{MPa}$ at $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, CAC-UHPC perfectly preserves about $70\%$ of its room-temperature strength.
4. **Establishment of a material design standard dedicated to nuclear power plant containment structures and fire-resistant tunnel linings**:
   - Mix design guidelines were established for next-generation extreme-disaster-prevention concrete combining rapid strength development with ultra-high-temperature fire resistance.

## Methods

- **Materials and CAC-UHPC mix proportioning (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binders: calcium aluminate cement (CAC: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 52.5\%, \text{CaO} = 37.8\%$) + amorphous silica fume (SF/CAC = 0.25, W/B = 0.22, polycarboxylate superplasticizer).
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40).
  - Reinforcing fibers: brass-coated straight steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 2500\ \text{MPa}$).
- **High-temperature exposure and analysis program (Sections 2.2~2.5 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Heating: heating rate $5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$, held at the target temperature ($200, 400, 600, 800, 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$) for 2 hours, then slow cooling.
  - Evaluation: residual compressive strength of 50 mm cubes, TGA/DTG thermogravimetric analysis, high-resolution quantitative XRD analysis, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP), SEM-EDS.

### 2. Interpretation of the ceramic sintering and self-prevention mechanisms against explosive spalling
- **High-temperature ceramification above $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$**: calcium aluminate and amorphous silica fume react at high temperature and recrystallize into the minerals gehlenite ($\text{Ca}_2\text{Al}_2\text{SiO}_7$) and anorthite ($\text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8$), reversing strength upward to $105.0\ \text{MPa}$.
- **Self-relief of vapor pressure**: during initial heating to $200\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the volume shrinkage exceeding $50\%$ that accompanies the $\text{CAH}_{10} \rightarrow \text{C}_3\text{AH}_6$ hydrate phase transition opens fine escape pores, preventing explosive spalling at the $100\%$ level.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the world-leading landmark origin study in the field of ultra-high-temperature refractory ceramic composites, in which Dr. N. K. Lee of the National University of Singapore and Drs. Ryu Geum-seong and Koh Kyung-taek of KICT elucidated the ceramic sintering mechanism that maintains a residual compressive strength of 105.0 MPa without explosive spalling even under extreme fire exposure at 1000 °C.
