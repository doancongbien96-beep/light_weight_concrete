# Static Mechanical Properties and Drop-Weight Impact Resistance of Amorphous Metallic and Standard Steel Fiber-Reinforced Concrete: Bond Pullout and $4.5$-Fold Impact Energy Absorption Mechanisms

## Summary

To overcome corrosion susceptibility and interfacial friction limitations in conventional round steel fibers and prevent tensile cracking and scabbing under impact loading, Prof. Gyu-Yong Kim (Chungnam National University), Prof. Nenad Gucunski (Rutgers University), and Dr. Jeong-Soo Nam investigated concrete and mortar reinforced with **ultra-thin ribbon-shaped corrosion-resistant amorphous metallic fibers (thickness $29\ \mu\text{m}$, width $1.6\ \text{mm}$, $L_f = 30\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1400\ \text{MPa}$, volume fractions $0.5\sim 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$) and hooked-end steel fibers**. Single-fiber pullout tests, ASTM C1609 flexural toughness evaluations, and $100\ \text{kg}$ drop-weight impact tests demonstrated that the high specific surface area of amorphous ribbons amplified chemical bond by $1.8$-fold, enhanced ASTM C1609 flexural toughness ($T_{150}^{150} = 138.0\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$) by $4.5$-fold, increased drop-weight impact resistance blows by 10-fold, and provided complete corrosion resistance for long-term durability.

## Key Contributions

1. **First characterization of pullout behavior for ultra-thin ribbon-shaped amorphous metallic fibers**:
   - The ribbon geometry provides $2.5$-fold higher specific surface area than circular fibers, elevating interfacial chemical bond by $1.8$-fold and maximizing sliding frictional dissipation.
2. **4.5-fold enhancement in flexural toughness ($T_{150}^{150}$) and residual flexural strength**:
   - Incorporating $1.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ amorphous metallic fibers suppressed post-cracking load drops, sustaining stable plastic deflection hardening.
3. **Elimination of fragment scattering and rear-face scabbing under 100 kg drop-weight impact**:
   - Thousands of amorphous ribbons formed a 3D confining mesh that bridged tensile bursting cracks and prevented spalling ($0.0$).
4. **Permanent corrosion resistance in chloride and marine environments**:
   - The non-crystalline amorphous atomic structure prevents fiber corrosion and bridging degradation across cracked sections.

## Methods

- **Materials and fiber specifications (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Matrix: Type I Portland cement + silica fume (W/B = 0.35, coarse aggregate $d_{max} = 13\ \text{mm}$).
  - Reinforcing fibers ($V_f = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$):
    1. Amorphous metallic fibers: $t = 29\ \mu\text{m}, w = 1.6\ \text{mm}, L_f = 30\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1400\ \text{MPa}$.
    2. Hooked steel fibers: $d_f = 0.5\ \text{mm}, L_f = 30\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1100\ \text{MPa}$.
- **Mechanical and impact testing (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 5–8)**:
  - Single-fiber pullout: Micro-pullout apparatus at 0.5 mm/min.
  - Flexural toughness: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ prisms (ASTM C1609 4-point bending).
  - Drop-weight impact: 100 kg hammer dropped from 1.0 m (980 J impact energy).

### 2. Ribbon geometry and impact energy dissipation mechanics
- **Enhanced interfacial bond via surface maximization**: The flat ribbon shape increased frictional contact area by $250\%$, accelerating energy absorption during fiber pullout.
- **Microcrack dispersion**: Stress waves were dispersed into dozens of microcracks rather than a single catastrophic fracture, preventing explosive failure.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that ribbon-shaped amorphous metallic fibers amplify the flexural toughness and drop-weight impact resistance of high-strength concrete by over 4.5-fold while ensuring permanent corrosion protection.
