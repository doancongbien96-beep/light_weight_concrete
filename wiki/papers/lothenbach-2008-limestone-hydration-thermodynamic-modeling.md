# Hydrate Assemblage Evolution and GEMS Thermodynamic Modeling of Portland Cement Hydration upon Limestone Filler ($\text{CaCO}_3$) Incorporation: Mechanisms of Monocarbonate Formation and Ettringite Stabilization

## Summary

To elucidate the chemical interactions of cement hydration reactions and the evolution of the phase assemblage (Phase Assemblage) that occur when limestone powder (Limestone Powder, $\text{CaCO}_3$) is substituted into Portland cement, this study systematically performed **Gibbs free-energy-minimization-based GEMS thermodynamic modeling (Thermodynamic Modeling)** together with isothermal hydration calorimetry, quantitative XRD analysis, TGA thermogravimetric analysis, and pore-solution-expression ICP-OES/IC ion concentration analysis from 1 day up to 400 days of age, becoming the first in the world to fully formalize the **chemical mechanism whereby, upon limestone addition, monocarbonate and hemicarbonate preferentially precipitate instead of monosulfate (Monosulfate) and ettringite (Ettringite) is stabilized over the long term, increasing the total hydrate solid volume and reducing porosity** — the **enduring, world's most highly cited landmark source paper of 2008 in Cement and Concrete Research (CCR) by the research team of Dr. Barbara Lothenbach of the Swiss Federal Laboratories for Materials Science and Technology (Empa) and Professor Karen Scrivener of the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the chemistry of monocarbonate formation and ettringite stabilization in limestone-blended cement hydration**:
   - It demonstrated that the carbonate ions ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) of limestone combine with the $\text{C}_3\text{A}$ aluminate phase to stabilize monocarbonate ($3\text{CaO}\cdot\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\cdot\text{CaCO}_3\cdot 11\text{H}_2\text{O}$), so that sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) is permanently preserved as ettringite rather than being converted into monosulfate.
2. **Demonstration of perfect agreement between GEMS thermodynamic modeling and measured quantitative data (XRD/TGA/pore solution)**:
   - Hydrate volumes, bound water contents, portlandite ($\text{CH}$), ettringite, and monocarbonate crystal quantities over 1 day to 400 days, as well as changes in the $\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{SO}_4^{2-}, \text{Al}^{3+}, \text{OH}^-$ concentrations of the pore solution, were precisely predicted by thermodynamic equilibrium calculations.
3. **Quantification of the hydrate volume expansion and pore-structure densification mechanism**:
   - It clarified the filling effect (Chemical Filler Effect) whereby the formation of ettringite and monocarbonate, whose molecular volumes exceed that of monosulfate, produces a net increase in the solid volume of the hardened body and reduces capillary porosity.
4. **Completion of the theoretical cornerstone for the design of modern low-carbon limestone calcined clay cements ($\text{LC}^3$)**:
   - It provides a thermodynamic framework from which the optimal combination ratio between alumina-rich ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) supplementary cementitious materials and limestone can be derived.

## Methods

- **Materials and specimen preparation (Section 2.1~2.3 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Reference cement: ordinary Portland cement (PC: OPC CEM I, $\text{CO}_2 < 0.4\%$).
  - Limestone-blended cement: PC + high-purity ground limestone substituted at 4.3 wt.% (PC4).
  - Hydrated pastes: W/C = 0.4, stored in sealed plastic containers, cured isothermally at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Thermodynamic modeling and analytical techniques (Sections 2.4 & 3)**:
  - GEMS-PSI thermodynamic package: utilizing the CEMDATA07 thermodynamic database and applying ideal solid-solution models.
  - Isothermal calorimetry: heat of hydration measured with a TAM Air 8-channel calorimeter.
  - Pore solution analysis: pore solution extracted with high-pressure expression equipment $\rightarrow$ ICP-OES (cations) and ion chromatography (anions).
  - Phase analysis: XRD Rietveld analysis and TGA-DTG($30\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$).

### 2. Interpretation of the thermodynamic phase equilibria and ion-reaction mechanisms
- **Blocking of ettringite consumption**: in the reference cement, once gypsum is exhausted after 28 days the residual aluminates decompose the existing ettringite and convert it into monosulfate, but when limestone is present the carbonate ions react preferentially and form monocarbonate, so the decomposition of ettringite is perfectly blocked.
- **Net increase in solid volume and compensation of chemical shrinkage**: the total volume of monocarbonate ($\approx 262\ \text{cm}^3/\text{mol}$) and preserved ettringite ($\approx 707\ \text{cm}^3/\text{mol}$) forms about $15\%$ greater solid volume than the monosulfate ($\approx 312\ \text{cm}^3/\text{mol}$) formation pathway, perfectly filling the pores of the hardened paste.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the supreme enduring landmark source paper of the cement chemistry and materials science fields, in which Barbara Lothenbach and Karen Scrivener completely proved through thermodynamic modeling and experiments that limestone powder is not a mere physical filler but a decisive chemical participant in cement hydration processes, and established the mechanisms of ettringite stabilization and monocarbonate formation.
