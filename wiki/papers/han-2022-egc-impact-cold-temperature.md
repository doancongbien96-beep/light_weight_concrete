# Impact Resistance of EGC in Cold Temperatures

## Summary

This study developed ambient-cured engineered geopolymer composites (EGC) incorporating slag, metakaolin, and 2 vol % domestic PVA fibers, evaluating their dynamic impact behavior at −10 °C, −20 °C, and −50 °C using a drop-weight impact machine equipped with a liquid nitrogen environmental chamber. Conventional ECC and cement mortar were tested as control benchmarks. The investigation clarified the influence of alkali activator concentrations (6~14 mol/L) on compressive, tensile, and low-temperature impact properties.

## Key Contributions

1. **Alkali concentration-mechanics relationships**: Increasing alkali concentration from 6 to 14 mol/L increased compressive strength from 10.6 to 37.2 MPa (saturating beyond 12 mol/L upon sufficient dissolution of Al and Si species) and tensile strength from 1.2 to 7.5 MPa, whereas tensile strain capacity decreased from 4.7 to 1.9 %. Mechanistically, higher alkali concentrations increased matrix fracture toughness ($J_{tip}$), diminishing the pseudo-strain-hardening energy margin ($J_b'/J_{tip}$).
2. **Material-dependent temperature effects**: Cooling from −10 to −50 °C increased peak impact loads and reduced maximum displacements across all materials due to low-temperature embrittlement. However, while the energy dissipation coefficient of ECC decreased (peaking at 52.8 % at −10 °C), that of EGC increased—with EGC-14 reaching 95.2 % at −50 °C (2.5-fold higher than ECC).
3. **Superior matrix impact toughness of geopolymer over cement**: Attributed to the homogenous 3D tetrahedral aluminosilicate network. Maximum displacement of GC-14 was 0.5 mm vs 0.25 mm in cement mortar at −50 °C.
4. **Enhanced PVA-geopolymer interfacial bonding**: Microscopic analysis revealed crushed geopolymer fragments adhering to PVA pullout channels, confirming high interfacial shear friction and load transfer efficiency.
5. **Failure mechanisms**: ECC and EGC exhibited post-peak load oscillations and progressive multi-layered crushing via PVA fiber pullout and rupture, whereas plain matrices suffered single through-body brittle fracture.

## Methods

- Mix proportions: Metakaolin : slag : silica sand = 0.5 : 0.5 : 0.4 (mass ratio), activator modulus $\text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 1.25$, PVA fibers (diameter 26 μm, length 12 mm, elastic modulus 36.3 GPa, tensile strength 1560 MPa) at 2 % (2 vol %).
- Specimens: 70.7 mm cubes for compression, JSCE dogbone specimens for uniaxial tension, and 50×50×13 mm plates for impact testing.
- Impact loading: 2.41 kg drop weight from heights of 100/300/500 mm after 1 h isothermal holding at target cold temperatures.

## Results

- Compressive strength of EGC was equal to or slightly higher than unreinforced geopolymer matrix due to robust PVA-geopolymer bonding (opposite to the trend observed in cementitious ECC vs mortar).
- Peak impact load: EGC-14 reached 8200 N under 100 mm drop height at −50 °C, exceeding ECC under identical conditions.
- Energy dissipation coefficient: High-concentration matrices (GC12, GC14) showed declining dissipation with falling temperature, while lower-concentration matrices (GC6~10) showed increasing dissipation due to unreacted aluminosilicate particles acting as defect initiators.
- Under a 500 mm drop height, EGC-14 maintained an energy dissipation ratio of 76.7 % at −50 °C.
