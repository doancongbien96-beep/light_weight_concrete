# Objective and Subjective Evaluation of Floor Impact Sound in Apartment Buildings

## Summary

This foundational study couples physical acoustic measurements using standard impact sources (tapping machine and bang machine tire) with subjective jury listening evaluations to assess floor impact sound isolation in multi-family residential buildings. The authors analyzed low-frequency vibration transmission characteristics across wall-type and frame-type slabs of varying thicknesses, demonstrating that resonance sound pressure in the $63 \sim 125\ \text{Hz}$ octave bands generated by the bang machine tire impact source represents the primary cause of resident annoyance. Enhancing resilient interlayer structures and slab damping performance was identified as the key to mitigating floor impact noise.

## Key Contributions

- **Mapping of physical acoustic parameters to subjective auditory responses**: Analyzed linear and non-linear correlations between physical acoustic measurements and subjective human annoyance across lightweight (tapping machine) and heavyweight (bang machine) impact sources.
- **Evaluation of sound isolation across slab thicknesses and structural typologies**: Quantified the reduction limits ($3 \sim 5\ \text{dB}$) in low-frequency impact sound levels achieved by increasing slab thickness (120 mm, 150 mm, 180 mm).
- **Applicability of single-number rating index ($L_{i,Fmax,AW}$)**: Proposed a heavyweight impact sound classification framework utilizing inverse A-weighted rating curves.

## Methods

- **Field acoustic testing**: Conducted floor impact noise measurements in full-scale residential test buildings in accordance with KS F 2810.
- **Receiving room measurements**: Measured precise sound pressure levels ($L_p$) across $1/1$ and $1/3$ octave bands with background noise corrections.
- **Subjective jury tests**: Played back impact sounds recorded in an anechoic chamber to 30 subjects, evaluating psychoacoustic annoyance using the Semantic Differential Scale.

## Results

- For bang machine impacts, peak sound pressure levels of $78 \sim 85\ \text{dB}$ were recorded in the $63\ \text{Hz}$ octave band, confirming that increasing slab thickness alone provides limited low-frequency vibration suppression.
- Subjective jury evaluations revealed that residents exhibited more than two-fold higher negative responses ('heaviness', 'startle', 'annoyance') toward low-frequency heavyweight impact sounds than toward high-frequency lightweight tapping sounds.
