# 50g centrifuge-model seismic behavior of a geotechnical seismic isolation (GSI) system based on high-damping polyurethane (HDPU, damping ratio $\zeta \ge 15\%$): superstructure peak acceleration reduction of $60\%$ and the soil-structure interaction (SSI) seismic protection mechanism

## Summary

To overcome the constructional and economic limits of the conventional practice of installing expensive isolation bearings (Lead-Rubber Bearings) on individual superstructure columns, and to protect an entire structure together with its underground foundation from seismic shock waves in a single measure, this study designed a **geotechnical seismic isolation system (Geotechnical Seismic Isolation: GSI) that embeds high-damping viscoelastic polyurethane pads (High-Damping Polyurethane: HDPU, shear modulus $G = 1.2\ \text{MPa}$, modal damping ratio $\zeta = 15.5\%$, a commercial Sorbothane® layer) in the ground immediately beneath the foundation slab**, performed large-scale $50\text{g}$ dynamic geotechnical centrifuge shake-table tests, multi-axis earthquake excitation measurement (El Centro and Kobe records), and 3D nonlinear soil-structure interaction (Soil-Structure Interaction: SSI) finite-element analysis, and demonstrated **permanent seismic safety featuring a drastic $60\%$ reduction of superstructure peak horizontal acceleration versus the fixed base ($0.48\text{g} \rightarrow 0.19\text{g}$), a $55\%$ cut in inter-story drift ratio, minimization of permanent ground settlement to within $0.5\ \text{mm}$, and zero ($0.0$) isolation-device maintenance**, published as the Bulletin of Earthquake Engineering 2024 highest-authority landmark original paper by the research team of **Prof. Hing-Ho Tsang of Swinburne University of Technology, Australia, and Prof. Wen-Yi Hung of National Central University (NCU), Taiwan**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first centrifuge demonstration of HDPU-based ground seismic isolation (GSI)**:
   - Under a $50\text{g}$ centrifuge acceleration environment, the full-scale soil stress state was reproduced completely, proving the dynamic energy dissipation mechanism of the HDPU isolation layer.
2. **Confirmed dramatic attenuation of earthquake-wave horizontal acceleration by $60\%$ and shear force by $58\%$**:
   - The viscoelastic polyurethane layer absorbs the high-frequency seismic impact energy transmitted from the lower ground through shear hysteresis loops, cutting off its transmission to the superstructure.
3. **Zero differential settlement of the ground and prevention of excessive rocking**:
   - The excessive settlement and rotational tilting that are drawbacks of the soft rubber-sand mixed soil (RSM) approach are perfectly suppressed by the elastic restoring force of the HDPU pads.
4. **Establishment of a low-cost, maintenance-free ground-integrated seismic design standard**:
   - Provides guidelines that dramatically upgrade the seismic safety grade of an entire building simply by laying the pads during foundation excavation, without the replacement burden of expensive mechanical isolation devices.

## Methods

- **GSI system configuration and HDPU material properties (Sections 2~3 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Isolation material: highly viscoelastic polyurethane (Sorbothane®, density $1350\ \text{kg/m}^3$, Shore hardness 50 Duro, shear modulus $G = 1.20\ \text{MPa}$, damping ratio $\zeta = 15.5\%$, Poisson's ratio $\nu = 0.49$).
  - Soil model: dry silica sand deposit (relative density $D_r = 75\%$).
  - Structural model: scaled 4-story RC moment-resisting frame (aluminum equivalent stiffness, natural frequency $f_n = 2.45\ \text{Hz}$).
- **Centrifuge shake-table testing (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 6~10)**:
  - National Central University (NCU) large geotechnical centrifuge ($50\text{g}$ acceleration).
  - Earthquake inputs: El Centro 1940 (far-field event) and Kobe 1995 (near-fault directivity event), PGA = 0.1g ~ 0.5g.
  - Instrumentation: piezoelectric accelerometers at 12 locations, LVDT dynamic settlement gauges, and pore-pressure and earth-pressure transducers.

### 2. Analysis of viscoelastic shear-deformation energy dissipation and rocking suppression
- **Shear-deformation filtering by the HDPU isolation layer**: as horizontal earthquake waves pass through the isolation layer, the $15.5\%$ high-damping viscoelastic hysteretic behavior of the HDPU absorbs high-frequency acceleration as heat energy, blocking structural excitation at the source.
- **Incompressible bulk stability from Poisson's ratio 0.49**: while permitting isolation shear flexibly in the horizontal direction, the material barely contracts in the vertical direction, perfectly suppressing differential foundation settlement and rocking rotation to within $0.1^\circ$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the highest-level landmark original work in modern earthquake engineering and integrated ground-structure seismic protection, in which Prof. Hing-Ho Tsang fully demonstrated 50g centrifuge-model seismic testing of a geotechnical seismic isolation system (GSI) using high-damping polyurethane (HDPU), proving a 60 % reduction of superstructure seismic acceleration together with ground-settlement integrity.
