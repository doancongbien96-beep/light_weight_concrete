# Uniaxial Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\varepsilon_{tu} = 4.5\%$) and Multiple Microcracking of Slag-Based Cementless Composites Influenced by Polyethylene (PE) Fiber Aspect Ratio ($L_f/d_f$) and Tensile Strength

## Summary

To eliminate greenhouse gas emissions from cement clinker and maximize recycled resource utilization, Professor Bang Yeon Lee and co-workers at Chonnam National University formulated a 100 % cementless alkali-activated binder comprising ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS 90 wt.%) and calcium hydroxide ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ 10 wt.%). The authors investigated the effects of two high-aspect-ratio ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (PE) fiber types—**PE1: diameter $12\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, aspect ratio 1250, tensile strength 2700 MPa vs PE2: diameter $13\ \mu\text{m}$, length 15 mm, aspect ratio 1154, tensile strength 3030 MPa, fixed dosage $V_f = 1.75\ \text{vol.\%}$**—and water-to-binder ratios (W/B = 0.25, 0.30) on hardened density, 28 d compressive strength, JSCE dumbbell uniaxial direct tensile stress-strain behavior, multiple crack density, tensile strain capacity ($\varepsilon_{tu}$), and energy absorption toughness. At W/B = 0.25, mixture M1 incorporating PE1 fibers (8.3 % higher aspect ratio) attained a **28 d compressive strength of $44.8\ \text{MPa}$, first crack strength of $4.85\ \text{MPa}$, ultimate tensile strength of $7.55\ \text{MPa}$, and an ultimate tensile strain capacity of $4.50\pm 0.35\%$**. This represents an 18.4 % improvement in strain capacity over mix M2 using higher-strength PE2 fibers (3.80 % strain capacity), demonstrating that higher aspect ratio ($L_f/d_f = 1250$) enhances interfacial contact area and slip-hardening bridging efficiency. Furthermore, this study established a cost-effective formulation reducing fiber dosage by 12.5 % from 2.0 vol.% to $1.75\ \text{vol.\%}$ while securing 4.5 % tensile ductility.

## Key Contributions

1. **4.5 % tensile strain-hardening in 100 % cementless slag composites**:
   - Dispersed $1.75\ \text{vol.\%}$ PE fibers in an eco-friendly alkali-activated slag matrix, simultaneously achieving 44.8 MPa compressive strength, 7.55 MPa tensile strength, and 4.5 % elongation.
2. **Experimental validation that fiber aspect ratio ($L_f/d_f = 1250$ vs 1154) governs bridging performance**:
   - Proved that 2700 MPa fibers with an aspect ratio of 1250 outperformed 3030 MPa fibers by expanding interfacial frictional contact area, boosting strain capacity by 18.4 % ($3.8\% \rightarrow 4.5\%$).
3. **Economic optimization via reduced fiber dosage ($1.75\ \text{vol.\%}$)**:
   - Reduced costly PE fiber dosage by 12.5 % while significantly exceeding the high-ductility SHCC standard threshold (>3.0 % elongation).
4. **Elimination of entrapped air voids (0 %) via viscosity and defoamer regulation**:
   - Maintained theoretical-to-measured density error within 1.1 %, preventing premature matrix failure caused by pore defects.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3, mass ratios)**:
  - Binder: GGBS (Blaine $4320\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$, density $2.92\ \text{g/cm}^3$) 90 wt.% + $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (95 % purity) 10 wt.%.
  - Fine aggregates: Micro-silica sand ($d \le 100\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40).
  - Reinforcing fibers (2 types, fixed $V_f = 1.75\ \text{vol.\%}$):
    1. PE1: Diameter $d_f = 12\ \mu\text{m}$, length $L_f = 15\ \text{mm}$, aspect ratio 1250, tensile strength 2700 MPa, modulus 88 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
    2. PE2: Diameter $d_f = 13\ \mu\text{m}$, length $L_f = 15\ \text{mm}$, aspect ratio 1154, tensile strength 3030 MPa, modulus 100 GPa, density $0.97\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
  - Chemical admixtures: Polycarboxylate SP, HPMC thickener, silicone-based defoamer.
  - 3 test series:
    - M1: W/B = 0.25, PE1 fibers ($L_f/d_f = 1250$).
    - M2: W/B = 0.25, PE2 fibers ($L_f/d_f = 1154$).
    - M3: W/B = 0.30, PE2 fibers ($L_f/d_f = 1154$, higher water content).
- **Testing protocols (Figure 1 & Section 2.3)**:
  - Hardened density: ASTM C642 Archimedes immersion ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes).
  - Compressive strength: KS L 5105 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes, 28 d wet cured at $20\pm 2\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Direct tension: JSCE dogbone specimens ($330\ \text{mm} \times 30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm, $0.1\ \text{mm/min}$, dual 25 mm LVDTs).

### 2. Bridging mechanics and multiple microcracking
- **Slip-hardening mechanics of aspect ratio 1250**: M1 specimens developed 25~30 saturated microcracks ($w \approx 45\ \mu\text{m}$); high-aspect-ratio PE1 fibers pulled out progressively under increasing crack opening displacements, sustaining strain hardening up to 4.5 % elongation.
- **W/B ratio influence**: Increasing W/B from 0.25 (M2) to 0.30 (M3) decreased interfacial frictional bond strength, lowering tensile strength and toughness by 21.2 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that PE fiber aspect ratio governs tensile strain-hardening in 100 % cementless slag composites, securing 4.5 % tensile ductility and 44.8 MPa compressive strength at a reduced fiber content of $1.75\ \text{vol.\%}$.
