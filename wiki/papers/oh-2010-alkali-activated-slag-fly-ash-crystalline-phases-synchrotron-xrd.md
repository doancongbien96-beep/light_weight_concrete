# Synchrotron XRD Analysis of Alkali-Activated Slag and Fly Ash Geopolymers: Compressive Strength of $112.5\ \text{MPa}$ and the Evolution Mechanism of Hydrotalcite/Zeolite Nano Crystalline Phases

## Summary

To elucidate at the nanoscale the mechanism of compressive strength development and crystalline phase formation in alkali-activated binders (AAMs) and geopolymers that replace Portland cement, this study applied two alkali activators ($10\text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ and sodium silicate Waterglass) to **ground granulated blast furnace slag (SLAG), Class C fly ash (FAC), and Class F fly ash (FAF)**, and precisely evaluated diffraction analysis with **the ultra-high-resolution synchrotron XRD (Synchrotron Radiation X-ray Diffraction) at Beamline 12.2.2 of the Advanced Light Source (ALS), Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL)** together with 28-day compressive strength — thereby achieving a compressive strength of $112.5\ \text{MPa}$ in the sodium-silicate-activated slag and identifying the strength-contribution mechanism of the hydrotalcite ($\text{Mg}_6\text{Al}_2\text{CO}_3\text{(OH)}_{16}\cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $d = 7.73\ \text{Å}$) and hydroxycancrinite (Hydroxycancrinite, $d = 6.38\ \text{Å}$) nano crystalline phases alongside the amorphous gels, in an **immortal landmark origin paper of Cement and Concrete Research (CCR) 2010** by the research team of **Prof. Paulo J. M. Monteiro of UC Berkeley and Prof. Jae-Eun Oh (now UNIST)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First identification of nano crystalline phases through ultra-high-resolution synchrotron (ALS Synchrotron) XRD**:
   - Minor amounts of hydrotalcite ($d = 7.73, 3.85\ \text{Å}$) and hydroxycancrinite ($d = 6.38\ \text{Å}$) crystalline phases, indistinguishable by conventional laboratory tube XRD, were perfectly separated and identified with a monochromatic synchrotron beam ($\lambda = 0.6975\ \text{Å}$).
2. **Demonstration of universal hydrotalcite formation across slag and Class F/C fly ashes**:
   - It was established that hydrotalcite forms extensively as the key aluminum-absorbing phase not only in high-calcium slag but also in alkali-activated Class F/C fly ash systems containing trace magnesium.
3. **Correlation established between activator-type compressive strength ($112.5\ \text{MPa}$) and nano gel structure**:
   - It was demonstrated that the sodium silicate activator abundantly supplies dissolved monomeric $\text{Si}$ to form a dense amorphous C-A-S-H / N-A-S-H gel network, thereby expressing the ultra-high strength of $112.5\ \text{MPa}$.
4. **Separation of the mechanical strength contributions of crystalline phases (zeolite phases) versus the amorphous gel**:
   - It was crystallographically established that the dense three-dimensional polycondensation of the amorphous gel, rather than the zeolite crystalline phases themselves, is the dominant factor in compressive strength development.

## Methods

- **Materials and precursor chemical composition (Section 2 & Table 1)**:
  - `SLAG`: Australian ground granulated blast furnace slag ($\text{CaO} = 43.8\%$, $\text{SiO}_2 = 34.5\%$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 13.8\%$, $\text{MgO} = 5.8\%$).
  - `FAC`: Bridger Class C fly ash ($\text{CaO} = 14.5\%$, $\text{SiO}_2 = 55.2\%$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 16.8\%$).
  - `FAF`: Bridger Class F fly ash ($\text{CaO} = 6.8\%$, $\text{SiO}_2 = 62.5\%$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 18.2\%$).
  - Alkali activators:
    1. $10\text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ aqueous solution.
    2. PQ Corp N-38 sodium silicate ($\text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 3.22$) + $\text{NaOH}$ adjusting solution ($\text{Ms} = 0.4, 1.0, 1.4$).
- **Curing and analytical techniques (Sections 2–3 & Figs. 1–4)**:
  - Curing: $23\ ^\circ\text{C}$ ambient and $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heat curing (1, 7, and 28 days).
  - Compressive strength: $25.4\ \text{mm}$ cylindrical and cubic specimens (ASTM C109).
  - Synchrotron XRD: LBNL ALS Beamline 12.2.2 (wavelength $\lambda = 0.6975\ \text{Å}$, CCD Area Detector, $2\theta$ resolution $\pm 0.005^\circ$).

### 2. Interpretation of hydrotalcite phase stability and the nano structural mechanism
- **Buffering action of the Mg-Al layered crystal**: $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions leached from the slag and fly ash react with six-coordinated $\text{Al}^{3+}$ to form hydrotalcite ($\text{Mg}_6\text{Al}_2\text{CO}_3\text{(OH)}_{16}\cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$), which acts as a buffer that stabilizes the $\text{Al/Si}$ chemical composition of the surrounding C-A-S-H/N-A-S-H gels.
- **Precision of synchrotron XRD**: peaks finely overlapped in $2\theta$ angle were separated, clearly proving the formation of the three-dimensional aluminosilicate cages of hydroxycancrinite.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is an immortal top-authority landmark origin paper in cement and geo-materials science, in which UC Berkeley and the LBNL national laboratory applied synchrotron XRD to alkali-activated materials for the first time in the world, elucidating the formation behavior of hydrotalcite and zeolite nano crystalline phases and the gel mechanism behind the development of 112.5 MPa compressive strength.
