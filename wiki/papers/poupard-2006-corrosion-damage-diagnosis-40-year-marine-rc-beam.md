# Non-Destructive Electrochemical Diagnosis (OCP / $R_p$) of an RC Beam After 40 Years of Natural Marine Exposure and Its 1:1 Mapping to Rebar Cross-Section Loss ($25\sim 40\%$)

## Summary

To accurately assess the residual life and safety of infrastructure reinforced concrete (RC) structures in long-term marine service, this study systematically compared — through 1:1 positional mapping — **non-destructive electrochemical diagnosis (open-circuit potential mapping OCP, linear polarization resistance $R_p$, concrete electrical resistivity) with destructive post-verification (dimensional measurement of fully exposed rebars, mass loss, chloride diffusion profiles, carbonation depth)** on a **reinforced concrete beam member ($2.45 \times 0.25 \times 0.15\ \text{m}$) exposed naturally for 40 years in the marine atmospheric environment of the French Atlantic coast**, demonstrating for the first time worldwide that **the zone with potentials below $-350\ \text{mV}$ (vs. CSE) and corrosion current density $I_{corr} > 1.0\ \mu\text{A/cm}^2$ matched perfectly with actual main-rebar cross-section loss of $25\sim 40\%$ and longitudinal cover cracking**. This is the highest-authority landmark classic paper by Dr. V. L'Hostis and Dr. O. Poupard of the French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission (CEA Saclay), Cement and Concrete Research, 2006.

## Key Contributions

1. **First exhaustive investigation worldwide of the corrosion mechanism of a real RC beam after 40-year natural marine exposure**:
   - Complete dissection of the actual evolution of chloride ingress, pitting, and general corrosion over 40 years of natural exposure rather than accelerated testing.
2. **Complete verification of the quantitative reliability of non-destructive electrochemical diagnosis (OCP / $R_p$)**:
   - Proved by 1:1 measurement that ASTM C876 potential criteria and the Stern-Geary polarization relation accurately predict actual local cross-section reduction and mass loss of the rebars.
3. **Demonstration of the critical chloride threshold ($C_{crit} \approx 0.4\ \text{wt.}\%$ of cement)**:
   - Chemical analysis confirmed that total chloride content at the 25 mm cover depth reached $0.8\sim 1.2\%$ of cement weight, fully destroying the rebar passive film.
4. **Identification of the critical loss ratio for longitudinal cover cracking by corrosion expansion pressure**:
   - Established that when main rebar radius reduction reaches $50\sim 80\ \mu\text{m}$ ($1.0\sim 1.5\%$ radius loss), longitudinal corrosion cracks of $0.1\sim 0.5\ \text{mm}$ penetrate the cover surface.

## Methods

- **Specimen and 40-year exposure history (Sections 2~3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Beam dimensions: $2450 \times 250 \times 150\ \text{mm}$ (four $\phi 14\ \text{mm}$ main bars, $\phi 6\ \text{mm}$ stirrups, design cover 25 mm).
  - Concrete mix: OPC Type I (cement content $350\ \text{kg/m}^3$, W/C = 0.52, 28-day compressive strength $35\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Exposure environment: 40 years outdoor atmospheric exposure at the Le Havre coastline, France (splash/seawind chlorides).
- **Non-destructive and destructive precision diagnosis (Sections 3.4~3.6 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Open-circuit potential mapping (OCP): full-surface mapping on a $50 \times 50\ \text{mm}$ grid with a copper sulfate electrode (CSE).
  - Polarization resistance ($R_p$): electrochemical polarization test with guard-ring sensor → $I_{corr} = B / (R_p \cdot A)$ with $B = 26\ \text{mV}$.
  - Concrete electrical resistivity: four-probe Wenner array measurement.
  - Destructive verification: cover breakout → acid cleaning of rebars → laser 3D micrometer section-area measurement at $10\ \text{mm}$ intervals.

### 2. Interpretation of macro-cell formation and section reduction mechanisms
- **Oxygen concentration cells and galvanic corrosion**: the cracked lower rebars act as anodes while the sound upper rebars act as an extended cathode, concentrating corrosion current and accelerating local pitting rates fivefold.
- **Volume expansion pressure of rebar corrosion products**: corrosion products ($\text{Fe(OH)}_3$, $\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$) expand to $2.5\sim 4.0$ times the parent metal volume, generating internal pressures above $15\ \text{MPa}$ that exceed the tensile strength of the cover concrete and induce cracks up to $1.5\ \text{mm}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This is the highest-authority landmark classic paper in which the CEA Saclay team mapped non-destructive electrochemical diagnosis 1:1 against actual rebar section loss on a real RC beam after 40 years of marine exposure, proving the accuracy of NDT diagnosis and establishing the textbook standard for marine durability design and repair timing.
