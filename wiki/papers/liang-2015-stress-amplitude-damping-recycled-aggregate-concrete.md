# Loss-Factor Enhancement up to 2.5× in Recycled Aggregate Concrete (RAC) via the Stress-Amplitude-Dependent Lazan Damping Model ($D_e = J\sigma_a^n$) and the Double-ITZ Friction Dissipation Mechanism

## Summary

To predict the seismic response reduction and vibration control performance of building structures, the research team of **Prof. Tiejun Liu of Harbin Institute of Technology (HIT) and Chair Prof. Jianzhuang Xiao of Tongji University** theoretically derived the nonlinear energy dissipation behavior of **recycled coarse aggregate (RCA: 0 %, 30 %, 70 %) and steel-fiber/polymer/rubber-powder modified recycled aggregate concrete (RAC)** under dynamic stress amplitude using the Lazan material damping model ($D_e = J \sigma_a^n$) and the Theodorsen-Garrick complex modulus model, and through cantilever vibration testing driven by an electromagnetic exciter established **the nonlinear evolution laws of the dynamic loss factor ($\eta$) and damping energy coefficient ($J$) with increasing stress amplitude ($F = 50\sim 200\ \text{N}$) and RCA replacement** — the landmark Materials 2015 original paper. The study demonstrated that **RAC with 70% RCA replacement and rubber-steel-fiber hybrid RAC achieve a loss factor up to 2.53 times that of ordinary concrete ($\eta = 0.0242$), reaching $\eta = 0.0612$, and that as vibration stress increases, frictional slip across the double ITZ is activated so that damping energy grows exponentially**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of an integrated theoretical model for the stress-amplitude-dependent member damping loss factor ($\eta_s$)**:
   - Combining Lazan's specific damping energy equation ($D_e = J \sigma_a^n$) with the member cross-section coefficient ($\psi_c$) and the longitudinal stress distribution coefficient ($\psi_l$) completes the closed-form analytical solution $\eta_s = \frac{J E}{\pi} \sigma_{am}^{n-2} \psi_c \psi_l$.
2. **Demonstration of a 2.53-fold loss-factor increase through recycled aggregate substitution and hybrid modification**:
   - Solely replacing 70% RCA increased the loss factor by $60\%$ ($0.0242 \rightarrow 0.0385$), while RAC with combined scrap-tire rubber powder and steel fiber recorded a loss factor of $0.0612$, exhibiting maximized vibration damping capability of more than 2.5 times that of ordinary concrete.
3. **Identification of nonlinear damping activation with increasing vibration load amplitude**:
   - Instrumented evidence that as the excitation force amplitude rises from $50\ \text{N} \rightarrow 200\ \text{N}$, frictional motion between internal microcracks accelerates, further raising the loss factor by $6\sim 15\%$.
4. **Quantification of the coupled viscoelastic-friction mechanism of a multiphase heterogeneous composite (the double ITZ)**:
   - Theoretically established that the porous interfaces between the old adhered mortar and the new cement paste (Old ITZ & New ITZ) act as sliding-friction damping dampers under dynamic shear deformation.

## Methods

- **Materials and RAC mix design (Section 3.1 & Tables 1~5)**:
  - Binder: OPC 42.5 (28-day compressive strength 50.4 MPa).
  - Aggregates: natural sharp sand (NFA) + natural crushed stone (NCA), plus recycled coarse aggregate (RCA).
  - Mix groups:
    1. `RCA-0`: RCA 0% (reference concrete).
    2. `RCA-30`: RCA 30% replacement.
    3. `RCA-70`: RCA 70% replacement.
    4. `RCA-MSF+PF`: RCA + micro steel fiber (1.0%) + polypropylene fiber (0.1%).
    5. `RCA-RP+SF`: RCA + scrap rubber powder (RP 5%) + steel fiber (1.0%).
    6. `RCA-FA+US`: RCA + fly ash (20%) + ultrasonically cleaned RCA.
- **Cantilever dynamic damping test (Section 3.2~3.3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Specimen: $100 \times 100 \times 1200\ \text{mm}$ prismatic beam (cantilever with a 1000 mm span).
  - Electromagnetic exciter loading: force amplitudes of $50\ \text{N}, 100\ \text{N}, 150\ \text{N}, 200\ \text{N}$.
  - Loss factor $\eta$ evaluated via the resonant-peak half-power bandwidth method and phase-difference analysis.

### 2. Interpretation of double-ITZ frictional slip and the nonlinear damping mechanism
- **Frictional energy dissipation at the old adhered mortar interface**: Under vibration loading, the double interface (Double ITZ) between the old adhered mortar retained on the recycled aggregate surface and the new paste induces microscopic Coulomb friction slip, rapidly dissipating vibration energy.
- **Nonlinear exponential behavior of damping energy with increasing stress amplitude**: The Lazan damping exponent shows $n > 2.0$ ($2.08\sim 2.38$), so loss energy grows far more steeply than linear proportionality as stress amplitude increases, exerting stronger vibration control under large-deformation seismic loading.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the Materials landmark original study that first formulated the stress-amplitude-dependent damping behavior of recycled aggregate concrete (RAC) through the Lazan energy model and amplified the loss factor 2.53-fold via scrap-rubber and fiber hybrid modification, completing a precise mechanical foundation for the damping design of structures.
