# Cyclic Behavior of the Footing–ICP Pile Connection

## Summary

User-group paper (co-authored by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University;
corresponding author Prof. Yun Yong Kim, Chungnam National University). To evaluate the
cyclic loading behavior of the connection between an internally infilled, laterally
reinforced ICP pile and its footing, one PHC control specimen and two ICP specimens were
fabricated and tested under cyclic loading. The ICP connection outperformed the conventional
PHC connection in every respect: ductility ratio, suppression of stiffness degradation, and
energy dissipation capacity. Lateral reinforcement was confirmed to strengthen the composite
action between the hollow pile body and the infilled concrete.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantified improvement in cyclic behavior of the ICP connection**: increased load-carrying
   capacity + improved ductility ratio + greater energy dissipation capacity + reduced stiffness
   degradation — superior to conventional PHC in every aspect of connection performance under
   cyclic loading.
2. **Crack-control mechanism of lateral reinforcement**: lateral reinforcement does not affect
   the cracking strength itself but reduces crack width, crack spacing, and the inclination and
   length of diagonal cracks — distributed crack widths secure the ductility of the connection.
3. **Indirect verification of composite action through strain distributions**: analysis of the
   lateral-reinforcement strain distributions confirmed that the pile body and the infilled
   concrete are effectively bonded — demonstrating the interfacial bond behind ICP composite
   action.

## Methods

- Specimens: one PHC control + two ICP specimens (lateral reinforcement arrangement +
  internal concrete infill).
- Loading: cyclic loading test; horizontal load applied at the connection region.
- Measurements: load–displacement hysteresis curves, ductility ratio, stiffness degradation
  rate, dissipated energy.

## Results

- ICP specimens achieved stable hysteretic response with improved load-carrying capacity,
  ductility, and energy dissipation capacity.
- Cracking: lateral reinforcement reduced crack width, spacing, and diagonal-crack length.
- Strain distributions confirmed composite action between the pile body and the infilled
  concrete.
