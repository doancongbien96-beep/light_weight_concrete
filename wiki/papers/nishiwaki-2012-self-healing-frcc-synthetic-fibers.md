# Mechanism of Microcrack Self-Healing Crystal Nucleation and Water-Tightness Recovery Rate ($98.5\%$) in FRCC Depending on the Chemical Polarity of Synthetic Fibers (Hydrophilic PVA vs. Hydrophobic PE/PP)

## Summary

To spontaneously restore the long-term durability and water-tightness of cracked fiber-reinforced cementitious composites (FRCC), **to elucidate the effect of the chemical surface polarity (Chemical Polarity) and hydrophilic/hydrophobic characteristics of the reinforcing fibers on calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) crystal precipitation inside crack gaps, tensile pre-cracks ($w = 50\sim 150\ \mu\text{m}$) were applied to FRCC specimens reinforced with four types of synthetic micro fibers (hydrophilic polar PVA fiber, chemically modified Vinylon, nonpolar hydrophobic PE fiber, nonpolar PP fiber, $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, and **air permeability testing (Air Permeability), constant-head water permeability testing, scanning electron microscopy (SEM-EDS), and X-ray diffraction (XRD) crystalline-phase analysis were performed under wet-dry cyclic curing (Wet-Dry Cycles, $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$, 24 h in water / 24 h in air, for 28 days)**, demonstrating **that PVA fibers carrying hydrophilic hydroxyl groups ($-\text{OH}$) strongly induce heterogeneous nucleation of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions on the fiber surface, achieving a self-healing water-tightness recovery rate of $98.5\%$ at crack widths of $100\ \mu\text{m}$ or less and precipitating calcite crystals more than twice as dense as with nonpolar PE/PP fibers** — a 2012 landmark original paper in the Journal of Advanced Concrete Technology by the research team of **Prof. Tomoya Nishiwaki and Endowed Chair Professor Hirozo Mihashi of Tohoku University, Japan**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first demonstration of the correlation between the chemical surface polarity of fibers and self-healing efficiency**:
   - Beyond the fiber's simple mechanical role of constraining crack width, demonstrated that the chemical functional groups on the fiber surface act as catalytic seeds for self-healing crystal precipitation.
2. **Elucidation of the heterogeneous nucleation mechanism of hydrophilic PVA fibers**:
   - The polar $-\text{OH}$ functional groups of PVA fibers electrostatically adsorb calcium ions ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) in the pore solution, so that during carbonation reactions calcite crystals grow rapidly in three dimensions along the fiber surface.
3. **Verification of complete water-tightness recovery ($98.5\%$) at crack widths of $100\ \mu\text{m}$ or less**:
   - Measured that after 28 days of wet-dry cycling the water permeability coefficient of PVA-FRCC recovered to the level of intact specimens, $1.5 \times 10^{-10} \rightarrow 2.2 \times 10^{-12}\ \text{m/s}$.
4. **Identification of the self-healing limits of nonpolar PE/PP fibers and the need for surface modification**:
   - Demonstrated that hydrophobic PE and PP fibers, while excellent in crack-constraining ability, show insufficient crystal attachment on the fiber surface, leaving their healing rate at only $50\%$ of that of PVA.

## Methods

- **Materials and FRCC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ordinary Portland cement + ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS 30 wt.%).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.40, W/B = 0.35).
  - Four types of reinforcing fibers ($V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$):
    1. `PVA fiber`: Kuraray REC15 ($d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, hydrophilic polar functional groups).
    2. `Vinylon fiber`: chemically modified vinylon ($d_f = 35\ \mu\text{m}$, intermediate polarity).
    3. `PE fiber`: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE ($d_f = 31\ \mu\text{m}$, nonpolar hydrophobic).
    4. `PP fiber`: polypropylene ($d_f = 40\ \mu\text{m}$, nonpolar hydrophobic).
- **Self-healing and property measurements (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Tensile pre-cracking: uniaxial tensile loading ($w = 50, 100, 150\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Wet-dry cyclic curing: 28-day cycles (24 h in water $\leftrightarrow$ 24 h in air, $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Measurements: Torrent air permeability tester, constant-head water permeability tester, SEM-EDS, and XRD.

### 2. Interpretation of electrostatic ion adsorption on the fiber surface and calcite crystal growth
- **Template effect of the PVA fiber surface**: The polar $-\text{OH}$ groups attract the $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions leached during hydration reactions at high concentration, exceeding the solubility product and thereby greatly lowering the nucleation energy of $\text{CaCO}_3$, which combines with atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$.
- **Inter-fiber crystal bridging**: Thousands of PVA fibers crossing between the crack faces serve as a physical scaffold, allowing crystals to rapidly grow bridges across the entire crack gap.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the most authoritative landmark original paper in the field of concrete self-healing, in which Prof. Hirozo Mihashi and Prof. Tomoya Nishiwaki elucidated that the chemical polarity of the reinforcing fibers is the key catalyst of self-healing crystal precipitation and proved the $98.5\%$ water-tightness recovery mechanism of hydrophilic PVA fibers.
