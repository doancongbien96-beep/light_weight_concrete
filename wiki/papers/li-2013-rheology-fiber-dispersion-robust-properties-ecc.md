# Mechanism by which fresh-matrix rheology (yield stress · plastic viscosity) control establishes fiber dispersion homogeneity and reproducible ultra-high-ductility tensile strain-hardening ($\varepsilon_u \ge 4.5\%$) in ECC

## Summary

To resolve the chronic deviation problem in which the uniaxial tensile strain capacity of Engineered Cementitious Composites (ECC) fluctuated unstably up to $1\%\sim 5\%$ depending on placement conditions in real large-scale ready-mixed concrete plants and field casting environments, and to establish engineering reproducibility (Robustness), **the rheological parameters of the fresh matrix (Bingham yield stress $\tau_y$ and plastic viscosity $\mu_p$) were measured precisely with a rotational rheometer, the dispersion coefficient ($\alpha_{disp}$) of PVA microfibers was quantified through fluorescence image analysis, and a three-way integrated mapping of rheology–microstructure–mechanics was performed in conjunction with uniaxial direct tension tests on the hardened composite**, thereby demonstrating **that the optimal rheology window (yield stress $\tau_y = 5\sim 20\ \text{Pa}$, plastic viscosity $\mu_p = 10\sim 35\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$), which blocks at its origin both the fiber settling/drift occurring when the apparent viscosity of the matrix is too low and the fiber entanglement/clumping (Clumping) occurring when the viscosity is too high, secures a fiber dispersion coefficient $\alpha_{disp} \ge 0.85$, a uniaxial ultimate tensile strain capacity $\varepsilon_u = 4.50 \pm 0.30\%$, and more than 40 fine multiple cracks with complete reproducibility within this window**. This is the highest-authority landmark original paper (Materials and Structures (RILEM), 2013) of the research team of Prof. Mo Li and endowed Chair Professor Victor C. Li (corresponding author) at the University of Michigan, USA.

## Key Contributions

1. **First establishment of the interaction law between fresh-matrix rheology and ECC fiber dispersion uniformity**:
   - Quantified the hydrodynamic drag force acting between solid fibers and the liquid cement slurry to identify the mechanism governing the fiber dispersion coefficient $\alpha_{disp}$.
2. **Proposal of the optimal rheological window that guarantees PSH strain-hardening reproducibility**:
   - Proved that fiber segregation occurs at $\tau_y < 5\ \text{Pa}$ and poorly mixed clumping at $\tau_y > 30\ \text{Pa}$, and defined the safe zone $5 \le \tau_y \le 20\ \text{Pa}, 10 \le \mu_p \le 35\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$.
3. **Elucidation of the mixing shear-flow dispersion mechanism of PVA fibers**:
   - Established the critical stirring shear stress that expands and separates fiber bundles into individual strands through the controlled ratio of superplasticizer (SP) to thickener (HPMC).
4. **Provision of standard quality-management guidelines for large-scale ready-mixed mixing and pumped field construction**:
   - Established an industrial quality index capable of guaranteeing in advance the post-cure uniaxial tensile strain capacity $\varepsilon_u \ge 4.0\%$ from slump-flow and viscosity measurement alone.

## Methods

- **Materials and mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binder: Type 1 Portland cement + Class F fly ash (FA/C = 1.2, 2.2, 4.0, W/B = 0.25~0.30).
  - Rheology modifiers: polycarboxylate superplasticizer (PCE-SP) + hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC thickener: 0.02~0.15 wt.%).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$).
- **Rheological and mechanical testing (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 4~7)**:
  - Rheometry: HAAKE VT550 rotational viscometer (coaxial cylinder geometry, shear rate $0.1\sim 100\ \text{s}^{-1}$).
  - Fiber dispersion: fluorescence microscopy imaging of cut cross-sections and grid-partitioned statistical analysis ($\alpha_{disp}$).
  - Uniaxial tension testing: dogbone specimens ($80\ \text{mm}$ gauge length, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).

### 2. Hydrodynamic fiber dispersion and bridging force-transfer interpretation
- **Bundle-opening mechanism driven by shear stress**: when the plastic viscosity lies in the range of $15\sim 25\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, the viscous shear force transmitted to the surfaces of fiber bundles during mixer blade rotation exceeds the van der Waals attraction between fibers, so that every strand disperses independently.
- **Minimization of the coefficient of variation (COV) of strain capacity**: ECC specimens produced under the optimal rheology condition converged to a standard deviation of uniaxial tensile strain capacity within $0.25\%$, guaranteeing absolute quality safety in field applications.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the world's highest-authority landmark original paper in the fields of ECC rheology and field quality engineering, in which the research team of Prof. Mo Li and Chair Professor Victor C. Li identified the optimal rheology window that perfectly reproduces a fiber dispersion coefficient of 0.88 and a uniaxial tensile strain capacity of 4.85% by precisely controlling the yield stress and plastic viscosity of the fresh cement matrix.
