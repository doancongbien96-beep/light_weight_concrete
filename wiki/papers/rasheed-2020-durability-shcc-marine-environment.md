## Summary
A study that carried out an 18-month long-term evaluation of the resistance to chloride ion penetration, the rebar corrosion suppression performance, and the retention of tensile mechanical performance of strain hardening cementitious composites (SHCC) exposed to a marine environment (saltwater immersion and repeated wet-dry cycles). Tensile pre-strains of $1.0 \%$ and $2.0 \%$ were applied to form multiple fine cracks ($w < 50 \mu m$) in SHCC specimens, which were then subjected to $3.5 \% NaCl$ solution wet-dry cycles, followed by half-cell potential, linear polarization resistance (LPR), and chloride profile analyses. Owing to the self-healing action of the microcracks and the dense hydrated matrix structure, it demonstrated that the onset of rebar corrosion is delayed by more than 5 times compared with ordinary concrete.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the chloride diffusion blocking mechanism through microcrack width control**: demonstrated that SHCC with crack widths $w \le 50 \mu m$ maintains chloride penetration resistance at the level of uncracked concrete thanks to self-healing through calcium carbonate precipitation inside the cracks.
- **Confirmation of long-term preservation of tensile ductility under marine wet-dry cycles**: a tensile strain capacity of $3.5 \%$ or higher was retained even after 18 months of marine exposure, without fiber degradation.
- **Demonstration of an 85 % reduction in rebar corrosion rate**: polarization resistance measurements on rebar-embedded SHCC beams showed that the corrosion current density remained at $I_{corr} < 0.1 \mu A/cm2$ (a negligible corrosion state).

## Methods
- **Material mix**: PVA-SHCC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, $W/B = 0.25$, fly ash 55 wt %).
- **Exposure environment**: $3.5 \% NaCl$ wet-dry cycles (2 d immersion + 5 d air drying, up to 18 months).
- **Test items**: uniaxial tensile test, potentiostatic corrosion measurement, chloride penetration depth measurement with silver nitrate ($AgNO_3$).

## Results
- Ordinary reinforced concrete (RC, crack width $0.2 mm$): reached a corrosion potential of $-450 mV$ (high corrosion probability) within only 3 months, with rebar cross-section loss occurring.
- SHCC (pre-crack width $40 \mu m$): maintained a corrosion potential of $-180 mV$ (no corrosion initiated) even after 18 months, with a chloride penetration depth of only 12 mm.
