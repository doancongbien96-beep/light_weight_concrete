## Summary

This study evaluated thermal-storage concrete mortars incorporating various cement binders (ordinary Portland cement OPC, alumina cement, slag), thermally conductive aggregates (graphite, silica sand), and steel fibers, as solid thermal energy storage media for storing and releasing high-temperature heat above $450 °C$ in solar thermal power generation systems, characterizing residual compressive strength, tensile strength, thermal conductivity, and specific heat before and after thermal cycling (repeated heating-cooling at $450 °C$). The mix combining alumina cement with graphite retained a high specific heat ($1.25 kJ/(kg\cdot K)$) and excellent residual compressive strength (above 35 MPa) even after high-temperature thermal cycling, proving it the optimal material for large-capacity high-temperature thermal storage systems.

## Key Contributions

- **Elucidation of the thermo-mechanical degradation mechanism of cement composites under $450 °C$ high-temperature thermal cycling**: identification of strength loss driven by dehydration decomposition of $Ca(OH)_2$ in the OPC matrix, and confirmation of the superior heat resistance and stability of alumina cement.
- **Enhancement of thermal storage capacity through combined graphite and alumina cement addition**: specific heat improved by 25 % and thermal conductivity increased 2.4×, maximizing the heat storage/release rate.
- **Control of thermal-stress cracking through steel fiber incorporation**: suppression of matrix cracking under high-temperature restrained thermal stress and retention of residual tensile strength.

## Methods

- **Material mix design**: binders (OPC, alumina cement, GGBS), thermally conductive aggregates (natural silica sand, graphite powder), steel fibers ($V_f = 0.5 \%$).
- **Thermal cycling test**: heating in an electric furnace up to $450 °C$, 4 h isothermal holding, and cooling to room temperature; 30 cycles repeated.
- **Measurements**: specific heat measurement (DSC), transient hot-wire thermal conductivity measurement, and compressive and splitting tensile strength before and after thermal cycling.

## Results

- Alumina cement + graphite mix: thermal conductivity $2.85 W/(m\cdot K)$, specific heat $1.28 kJ/(kg\cdot K)$, residual compressive strength $38.2 MPa$ after 30 thermal cycles (72 % of initial strength retained).
- Design feasibility secured for economical solid thermal energy storage structures for solar high-temperature power generation systems.
