## Summary
To reduce material cost and optimize the high-strength-ultra-high-ductility performance of strain-hardening cementitious composites (SHCC), this study investigated the direct tensile behavior, crack distribution characteristics, and micromechanical bridging synergy of SHCC hybridized with hydrophilic PVA fibers and hydrophobic high-strength PE fibers at various ratios (PE:PVA = 100:0, 75:25, 50:50, 25:75, 0:100, total $V_f = 2.0 \%$). Through a complementary mechanism in which the high chemical bond of the PVA fibers promotes early multiple microcrack formation while the high-strength pull-out slip resistance of the PE fibers drives late-stage strain-hardening and ultimate tensile strength, the study demonstrated that the 50:50 hybrid mix economically achieved a tensile strength of 8.2 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 6.5 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Formulation of the complementary bridging energy ($J_b'$) synergy of PVA-PE hybrid fibers**: derivation of hybrid $\sigma-\delta$ curves combining single-fiber bridging models.
- **Derivation of the optimal balance between crack width control and ductility**: the PE 50 % + PVA 50 % combination secured an average crack width of $30 \mu m$ and ductility of $6.5 \%$.
- **Presentation of an economical high-performance SHCC mix guideline**: manufacturing cost was reduced by 40 % relative to pure PE-ECC while strength and ductility were improved by 50 % and 45 %, respectively, over PVA-ECC.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA fibers (REC15, strength 1600 MPa) and UHMWPE fibers (diameter 12 µm, strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 100 GPa).
- **Matrix**: $W/B = 0.22$, fly ash 50 wt %, silica fume 10 wt %.
- **Test items**: uniaxial dog-bone direct tensile test, single-fiber pull-out test, and DIC strain analysis.

## Results
- Pure PE (2.0 %): tensile strength $10.2 MPa$, strain capacity $8.1 \%$.
- Hybrid (PE 1.0 % + PVA 1.0 %): tensile strength $8.25 MPa$, strain capacity $6.52 \%$, number of cracks 65.
- Pure PVA (2.0 %): tensile strength $5.40 MPa$, strain capacity $4.20 \%$.
