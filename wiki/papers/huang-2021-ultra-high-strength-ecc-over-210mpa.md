# Development of Ultra-High-Strength ECC (UHS-ECC) with Compressive Strength Exceeding $210\ \text{MPa}$ and Simultaneous Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 4.20\%$, Strength $16.5\ \text{MPa}$)

## Summary

To overcome explosive brittle failures in ultra-high-strength concrete (compressive strength $>200\ \text{MPa}$) and achieve high compressive strength alongside ductile tensile strain-hardening, Professor Jian-Guo Dai and Bo-Tao Huang at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, along with Professor Victor C. Li at the University of Michigan, designed an ultra-high-strength engineered cementitious composite (UHS-ECC). The material combines a dense particle-packed matrix designed via modified Andreassen & Andersen packing (W/B = 0.14, fine quartz powder, silica fume, GGBS, and nano-silica) with high-strength surface-modified PE micro-fibers ($d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}, L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$ at $V_f = 1.5, 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$). Direct tension dogbone tests with full-field 2D-DIC, cylinder compressive tests, and pseudo-strain-hardening (PSH) micromechanical verifications ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1.85$) proved that UHS-ECC achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **$211.0\ \text{MPa}$**, an ultimate tensile strength of **$16.5\ \text{MPa}$**, a tensile strain capacity of **$4.20\%$**, and over 50 saturated microcracks ($w \le 35\ \mu\text{m}$).

## Key Contributions

1. **Simultaneous achievement of $210\ \text{MPa}$ compressive strength and $4.20\%$ tensile ductility**:
   - Overturned the traditional trade-off between ultra-high strength and brittleness, demonstrating robust strain-hardening exceeding 4 % ductility at a compressive strength of $211.0\ \text{MPa}$.
2. **Micromechanical PSH criteria verification in ultra-high-strength matrices**:
   - Optimized fiber-matrix frictional bond ($\tau_0 = 3.5\ \text{MPa}$) and complementary bridging energy despite elevated matrix fracture toughness ($K_m \approx 1.25\ \text{MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$), satisfying $J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1.85$.
3. **Multi-scale particle packing and complete elimination of ITZ defects**:
   - Packed inter-particle spaces using $7.5\ \mu\text{m}$ quartz powder and nano-silica, reducing capillary porosity below 1.5 % and eliminating weak interfacial transition zones (ITZ).
4. **Design basis for ultra-slender protective infrastructure**:
   - Established material standards for lightweight containment structures in nuclear power plants and explosion-resistant defense barriers.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture design (Section 2 & Tables 1–2 & Figs. 1–2)**:
  - Binders: CEM I 52.5N OPC, undensified silica fume, GGBS, fine quartz powder (QP: median size $7.5\ \mu\text{m}$, W/B = 0.14, polycarboxylate superplasticizer).
  - Aggregates: Refined quartz sand ($d_{max} = 180\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.30).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Ultra-high-molecular-weight PE micro-fibers ($V_f = 1.5, 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 100\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Testing and DIC instrumentation (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 3–5)**:
  - Uniaxial tension: Dogbone specimens (80 mm gauge length, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Instrumentation: 2D-DIC full-field strain mapping, crack spacing and opening analysis, LVDTs.
  - Compressive strength: $\phi 75 \times 150\ \text{mm}$ cylinders (ASTM C39/C469).

### 2. Fiber pullout and multiple cracking mechanics
- **Frictional pullout without fiber rupture**: Low chemical adhesion and hydrophobic properties of PE fibers prevented premature fiber breakage in the ultra-high-strength matrix, facilitating stable frictional pullout slip.
- **Over 50 saturated microcracks**: Distributed microcracks developed at tight 1.6 mm spacing throughout the gauge length with widths under $30\ \mu\text{m}$, sustaining 4.20 % tensile strain capacity.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that coupling dense packing micromechanics with high-strength PE fibers produces an ultra-high-strength ECC achieving 211.0 MPa compressive strength, 16.5 MPa tensile strength, and 4.20 % tensile strain capacity.
