# Flexural behavior of beams with bottom-overlay strengthening by GFRP reinforcing bars and high-performance fiber composites (UHPC/PVA-ECC): $68\%$ amplification of flexural capacity, crack-width confinement to $0.08\ \text{mm}$, and the interfacial debonding prevention mechanism

## Summary

To dramatically upgrade the flexural performance of aging reinforced concrete (RC) beams and permanently shut out the rebar corrosion problem, full-scale beam specimens ($2400 \times 200 \times 150\ \text{mm}$) were fabricated by hybrid monolithic casting of **non-corroding high-tensile GFRP (glass fiber-reinforced polymer) reinforcing bars ($\sigma_u = 1100\ \text{MPa}$, $E = 50\ \text{GPa}$) together with two high-performance fiber composites (steel-fiber UHPC overlay layer vs. PVA-ECC overlay layer, thickness $40\ \text{mm}$)** onto the tension side of existing RC beams, and four-point bending tests, 2D digital image correlation (DIC), and interfacial shear stress analysis were performed, with **the GFRP + PVA-ECC strengthened beams achieving an ultimate flexural capacity amplified by $68\%$ over the unstrengthened control beam, a deflection ductility ratio of $\mu = 4.85$, ultra-tight confinement of bottom tensile crack widths at or below $0.08\ \text{mm}$, and fully monolithic behavior without interfacial debonding** — a landmark Engineering Structures 2024 origin paper by **the joint research team of Prof. Xudong Shao of Hunan University, China and Yonsei University**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Complete compensation for the low elastic modulus and brittle tensile behavior of GFRP bars**:
   - The excessive crack widths and premature brittle fracture that occur under GFRP-rib-only strengthening are suppressed by the strain-hardening tensile bridging of PVA-ECC, slashing crack width to the $1/5$ level.
2. **One-to-one comparison of the flexural strengthening mechanisms of UHPC vs. ECC overlay layers**:
   - The steel-fiber UHPC strengthening layer favors higher stiffness and an elevated first-cracking load ($+85\%$), while the PVA-ECC strengthening layer proves outstanding at large-deformation deflection ductility ($\mu = 4.85$) and relief of interfacial stress concentration.
3. **Complete cutoff of premature interfacial debonding at the strengthening interface**:
   - Even without shear studs or anchors at the interface between substrate concrete and the strengthening layer, chipping surface treatment alone maintains a fully monolithic section thanks to the high tensile bond strength of ECC.
4. **Establishment of a standard for repair and strengthening of corrosion-exposed structures in marine and chemical plant environments**:
   - By fusing non-metallic GFRP, entirely free of steel-corrosion risk, with highly durable composites, a technology guaranteeing a post-repair service life of 50 years or longer was completed.

## Methods

- **Specimen configuration and strengthening mixes (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Existing RC beam dimensions: $2400 \times 200 \times 150\ \text{mm}$ (longitudinal bars 2-D16, stirrups D10@100 mm, concrete 28-day compressive strength $35.0\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Bottom strengthening layer (thickness $40\ \text{mm}$, embedded GFRP ribs 2-$\phi 12\ \text{mm}$):
    1. `Series-UHPC`: straight steel-fiber UHPC at 2.0 vol.% (compressive strength $145.0\ \text{MPa}$).
    2. `Series-ECC`: PVA-ECC at 2.0 vol.% (compressive strength $48.5\ \text{MPa}$, tensile ductility $4.0\%$).
- **Flexural test program (Section 2.3 & Figs. 2~5)**:
  - Simply supported four-point bending loading: span $2000\ \text{mm}$, pure bending zone $600\ \text{mm}$ (0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Instrumentation: DIC cross-sectional strain measurement, LVDT deflectometers, GFRP surface strain gauges.

### 2. Interpretation of the GFRP–ECC interaction and crack-dispersion mechanism

- **Synergy between the high tensile force of GFRP and fine-crack dispersion by ECC**: the GFRP ribs share the high elastic tensile load while the bottom cover ECC forms more than 30 multiple fine cracks, preventing local interfacial debonding on the GFRP surface.
- **Flexible relaxation of interfacial shear stress**: the low elastic modulus of ECC ($18\ \text{GPa}$) eases the stiffness mismatch with the substrate concrete, fully absorbing the shear stress concentration at the interface ends.

- **Overall conclusion**: Through the GFRP bar + PVA-ECC bottom-overlay strengthening method, this paper demonstrates a 68% amplification of flexural capacity, a 54% improvement in deflection ductility, and confinement of crack width to 0.08 mm or below, establishing the design standard for permanent strengthening of structures aged in corrosive environments — a landmark origin paper.
