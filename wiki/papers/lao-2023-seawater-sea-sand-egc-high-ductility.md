# High-Strength ($78.6\ \text{MPa}$) and Ultra-High-Ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 8.4\%$) Seawater Sea-Sand Engineered Geopolymer Composites (SS-EGC): Development and Nanoindentation Microstructural Mechanics

## Summary

To resolve severe shortages of freshwater and natural river sand while mitigating cement carbon emissions in coastal and island infrastructure, Prof. Jian-Guo Dai (Hong Kong PolyU) and Prof. Bo-Tao Huang (Zhejiang Univ.) developed seawater sea-sand engineered geopolymer composites (SS-EGC) using $100\%$ seawater and sea-sand. The composite integrates a fly ash (FA)-ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS) precursor matrix with high-strength PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, $L_f = 18\ \text{mm}$). Evaluating FA/GGBS precursor ratios (2:8, 4:6, 6:4), the authors characterized compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile strain-hardening, nanoindentation phase deconvolution, TGA/DTG hydration products, and 5-dimensional performance radar diagrams. The authors elucidated how seawater and sea-sand enhance strength ($78.6\ \text{MPa}$) and tensile strain capacity ($8.4\%$) beyond freshwater-silica sand EGC counterparts under ambient curing ($23\ ^\circ\text{C}$).

## Key Contributions

1. **Simultaneous achievement of $78.6\ \text{MPa}$ compressive strength and $8.4\%$ tensile strain capacity with 100 % seawater and sea-sand**:
   - Demonstrated that high strength and ultra-high ductility ($\varepsilon_u > 8.0\%$) can be stably achieved at ambient curing ($23\ ^\circ\text{C}$) without any freshwater or river sand.
2. **Identification of geopolymerization acceleration by seawater ions ($\text{Cl}^-, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{SO}_4^{2-}$)**:
   - Seawater chloride and magnesium ions accelerated alkaline dissolution, promoting dense C-(N)-A-S-H gel and Cl-hydrotalcite formation, elevating 28 d compressive strength by $10\sim 15\%$ over freshwater baselines.
3. **Statistical nanoindentation phase deconvolution (Gaussian Mixture Model)**:
   - Quantitatively isolated Low-Density C-(N)-A-S-H ($E \approx 18\ \text{GPa}$), High-Density C-(N)-A-S-H ($E \approx 32\ \text{GPa}$), and Ultra-High-Density phases, confirming increased HD phase volume fractions with seawater mixing.
4. **5-dimensional performance evaluation for strain-hardening stability**:
   - Standardized SS-EGC durability and mechanical robustness across 5 axes: compressive strength, tensile strength, tensile strain capacity, inverse crack width, and inverse crack width standard deviation.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture design (Tables 1–2)**:
  - Precursors: Class F fly ash (FA) + GGBS (Blaine fineness $420\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$).
  - Mixture ratios: F2S8 (FA:GGBS = 2:8), F4S6 (4:6), F6S4 (6:4).
  - Mixing water: Artificial seawater (ASTM D1141 chloride concentration) vs freshwater control.
  - Fine aggregates: Natural sea-sand ($d_{max} = 2.0\ \text{mm}$, chloride content $0.08\%$) vs river sand.
  - PE fibers: Diameter $24\ \mu\text{m}$, length $18\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength $3000\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $100\ \text{GPa}$, $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$.
  - Activator: Liquid sodium silicate + solid NaOH ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 6.0\%$, Ms = 1.2), borax retarder.
- **Testing protocols (Section 2.3, Figs. 1–5)**:
  - Compression: ASTM C109 $50\ \text{mm}$ cubes cured at $23\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 28 d.
  - Direct tension: JSCE dumbbell specimens ($30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$ cross-section, $80\ \text{mm}$ gauge length) at $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$.
  - Nanoindentation: Agilent G200 nanoindenter, peak load $2000\ \mu\text{N}$, $10 \times 10$ indentation grid.
  - Microstructure: TGA/DTG (ambient to 800 $^\circ\text{C}$), XRD, SEM-EDS.

### 2. Seawater geopolymer hydration and interfacial bridging mechanics
- **Accelerated high-density gel formation**: $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ in seawater accelerate alkaline dissolution, forming dense HD C-(N)-A-S-H around unreacted GGBS and increasing HD phase volume fraction by $12\%$.
- **Optimized fiber-matrix frictional bond**: The densified matrix provides optimal frictional confinement to hydrophobic PE fibers, preventing premature fiber rupture and sustaining an ultra-high tensile strain capacity of $8.4\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper proved that using 100 % seawater and sea-sand achieves $78.6\ \text{MPa}$ compressive strength and $8.4\%$ tensile ductility, establishing a sustainable materials foundation for marine infrastructure.
