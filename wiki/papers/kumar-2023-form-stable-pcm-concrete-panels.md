# Comparative Analysis of Form-Stable Phase Change Material (FSPCM) Concrete Panels Using 5 Porous Carriers (Aerogel, Expanded Perlite, Expanded Glass, Nanoclay, Silica Fume) for Building Envelopes

## Summary

To reduce building envelope heating/cooling losses and enhance indoor thermal comfort, researchers at Swinburne University of Technology (Prof. Jay Sanjayan's group) and RMIT University synthesized **form-stable phase change material (FSPCM)** composites. Paraffin PCM (RT27, melting range $26\sim 28\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat capacity $180\ \text{J/g}$) was vacuum-impregnated into 5 representative porous carriers: **silica aerogel granules (SAG), hydrophobic expanded perlite (HEP), recycled expanded glass (REG), nanoclay (NC), and silica fume (SF)**. The FSPCMs replaced 60 vol.% fine aggregates in Thermal Energy Storage Panels (TESP, $300\ \text{mm} \times 300\ \text{mm} \times 20\ \text{mm}$). Testing under identical protocols evaluated **high-temperature ($70\ ^\circ\text{C}$) exudation stability, DSC latent heat of fusion/solidification, thermal conductivity ($k$), 28 d compressive strength, indoor peak temperature reduction ($\Delta T_{peak}$), and thermal time lag**. **Hydrophobic expanded perlite (HEP-FSPCM)** achieved zero leakage ($0.0\%$), a **PCM loading capacity of 55.0 wt.%, panel compressive strength of $18.5\ \text{MPa}$, peak indoor temperature damping of $4.2\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and a 120-minute thermal delay**, realizing the optimal balance between structural strength and thermal energy storage. Silica aerogel (SAG) provided ultra-low thermal conductivity ($0.18\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) but dropped strength to 4.2 MPa, while recycled expanded glass (REG) delivered highest strength (22.5 MPa) with lower latent heat storage.

## Key Contributions

1. **Comprehensive 1:1 benchmark database of FSPCM concrete panels across 5 porous carriers**:
   - Standardized thermal and mechanical properties across SAG, HEP, REG, NC, and SF using identical PCM (RT27) and replacement dosage (60 vol.%).
2. **Identification of hydrophobic expanded perlite (HEP) as the optimal carrier**:
   - Retained zero exudation ($0\%$) at $70\ ^\circ\text{C}$ while achieving 18.5 MPa compressive strength and a $4.2\ ^\circ\text{C}$ indoor temperature reduction.
3. **Quantification of effective panel latent heat storage ($35\sim 65\ \text{J/g}$) via DSC**:
   - Verified paraffin molecular confinement within carrier micropores and stabilized phase change temperature ranges ($26.5\sim 27.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
4. **Demonstration of dynamic thermal damping (decrement factor $f = 0.58$) and 120-min time lag**:
   - Validated over 30 % reductions in peak cooling loads during simulated diurnal solar heating cycles.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and FSPCM formulations (Tables 1–2 & Figs. 1–2)**:
  - Phase change material: Rubitherm RT27 paraffin (phase transition range $26\sim 28\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $180\ \text{kJ/kg}$, thermal conductivity $0.2\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$).
  - 5 porous carriers:
    1. SAG (Silica Aerogel Granules): Particle size 2~4 mm, specific surface area $750\ \text{m}^2/\text{g}$, bulk density $0.09\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
    2. HEP (Hydrophobic Expanded Perlite): Particle size 1~3 mm, bulk density $0.12\ \text{g/cm}^3$ (silicone coated).
    3. REG (Recycled Expanded Glass): Particle size 1~2 mm, bulk density $0.32\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
    4. NC (Nano-Clay): Montmorillonite nanopowder.
    5. SF (Silica Fume): Amorphous microsilica.
  - Panel mixture: ASTM C150 Portland cement + silica sand (S/C = 2.75) + FSPCM (60 vol.% sand replacement).
- **Testing procedures (Sections 2.3–2.4 & Figs. 3–4)**:
  - Exudation stability: 55 mm filter paper absorption test at $70\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 30 min.
  - Thermal properties: DSC (Mettler Toledo, $-10\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $2\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$), ISOMET 2114 thermal conductivity analyzer.
  - Compressive strength: ASTM C109 ($50\ \text{mm}$ cubes, 28 d water curing).
  - Thermal box test: $300\ \text{mm}$ cubic insulated test chamber with front-mounted TESP heated under halogen cyclic irradiance.

### 2. Pore topology and capillary retention mechanisms
- **Zero-leakage mechanism in hydrophobic expanded perlite (HEP)**: Micro-scale internal pores ($1\sim 10\ \mu\text{m}$) combined with hydrophobic surface coating generate capillary surface tension that traps molten non-polar paraffin even at $70\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Strength reduction mechanism in silica aerogel (SAG)**: The delicate, highly porous skeleton of aerogel collapses during mixing and compressive loading, compromising matrix continuity.

- **Overall conclusion**: This 2023 comparative study verified the mechanical and thermal energy storage superiority of hydrophobic expanded perlite (HEP) as a form-stable PCM carrier for building envelope applications.
