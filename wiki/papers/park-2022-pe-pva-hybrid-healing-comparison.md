# PE-PVA Hybrid SHCC: Self-Healing Comparison between Natural and Underwater Conditions

## Summary

A paper from the user's group (Chonnam National University; Bang Yeon Lee co-author, Yoon Yong Kim corresponding author). A PE 1.25 % + PVA 0.10 % hybrid SHCC (cement:GGBS 1:1, W/B 0.40, waste-tire crumb rubber 10 %, expansive agent) was exposed to laboratory underwater immersion and to an outdoor natural environment for a direct comparison of mechanical and self-healing characteristics. The underwater condition achieved complete healing up to a crack width of 60 µm (the healing threshold), while the natural environment showed only limited healing, and its stiffness recovery was 15 %p lower than underwater.

## Key Contributions

- Direct-comparison design holding the mix identical across two curing environments: unlike prior literature centered on underwater and wet-dry cycling, outdoor natural exposure and underwater immersion were evaluated side by side — a basis for setting expectations in field applications.
- Demonstrated role division within the PE-PVA hybrid: PVA (hydrophilic) supplies the healing potential while PE (hydrophobic, high strength of 2700 MPa) carries the tensile ductility. A strain capacity of about 8 % was achieved at a total fiber volume of 1.35 vol %.
- Quantified environment-specific performance: the natural environment gave -10 % compressive strength, -4 % tensile strength, and -3 % strain capacity relative to underwater. Healing-threshold crack width: 60 µm underwater vs very limited in the natural environment.
- Post-healing reloading behavior: both mixes reproduced clear strain-hardening upon reloading; tensile strength was retained after healing while strain capacity decreased.

## Methods

- Mix (identical for M-W/M-O): cement 0.5 + GGBS 0.5 (binder fractions), W/B 0.40, crumb rubber (40 mesh) 0.1, silica sand 0.5, EA 0.05, SP 0.002, VMA 0.001, DF 0.001, PE (∅16 µm, length 12 mm) 1.25 vol % + PVA (∅40 µm, 8 mm) 0.10 vol %.
- Curing: 23 ± 3 °C for 2 days, demolding, then underwater (U-C) or outdoor natural (N-C) exposure up to 28 days.
- Tests: density, compression (50 mm cubes), and dumbbell direct tension on 8 specimens (3 of them used for healing evaluation). Healing metrics: crack-width reduction, stiffness recovery, and post-reload tensile performance.

## Results

- 28-day strain capacity of about 8 % (ultra-high ductility for both M-W and M-O). M-O came out 4 % lower in tensile strength and 3 % lower in strain capacity than M-W.
- Healing: after the 28-day healing period, M-W achieved complete sealing of cracks of ~60 µm or smaller, while M-O showed only marginal sealing.
- Stiffness recovery: M-O was 15 %p lower than M-W — the underwater condition is the superior healing-promoting environment.
