# Comprehensive Review of the Surface Modification, Interfacial Bond Mechanics, and Strain-Hardening Composite Design Mechanisms of Synthetic Fibers (PVA/PE/PP/PET/Nylon/Aramid) for Cementitious Composites

## Summary

A highest-authority landmark review paper published in Construction and Building Materials in 2019 by **Professor Hamid Reza Pakravan and Professor Masoud Latifi of Amirkabir University of Technology, Iran**, which comprehensively compiles — with the aim of overcoming the corrosion vulnerability and brittle-cracking limits of steel fibers and endowing cement-based composites with semi-permanent durability and ultra-high ductility — the molecular chemical structures, physico-mechanical specifications, hydrophilic/hydrophobic surface modification technologies (oiling coating, plasma treatment, nano-silica coating), the single-fiber pullout interfacial friction mechanics against the cement matrix ($\tau_0, G_d, \beta$), and the **micromechanical PSH energy/strength criteria of strain-hardening cementitious composites (ECC/SHCC)** of the **representative synthetic polymer fibers used in modern civil engineering and architecture (PVA, PE/UHMWPE, general-purpose PP, high-toughness HTPP, PET, nylon Nylon 66, PAN, aramid Kevlar)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Compilation of the chemical structures and mechanical property spectrum of the eight major synthetic fiber families**:
   - Perfectly organizes the tensile strength ($400\sim 3500\ \text{MPa}$), elastic modulus ($3.5\sim 130\ \text{GPa}$), elongation at break ($2\sim 25\%$), alkali resistance, and density of PVA, UHMWPE, PP, HTPP, PET, Nylon, PAN, and Aramid fibers.
2. **Establishment of a quantitative comparison table of single fiber–cement interfacial bond characteristics ($\tau_0, G_d, \beta$)**:
   - Formalizes the behavior of hydrophilic PVA fibers, with their high chemical bonding ($G_d = 2.0\sim 4.5\ \text{J/m}^2$) and slip-hardening ($\beta > 0$), and of hydrophobic PE/PP fibers, with their purely frictional bonding ($\tau_0 = 1.0\sim 2.5\ \text{MPa}$).
3. **Analysis of fiber surface modification (Surface Modification) technologies**:
   - Systematizes the latest advances in 1.2% oiling coatings, which mitigate the excessive chemical bonding of PVA fibers, and in plasma discharge/oxidizer etching technologies, which enhance the bond strength of hydrophobic PE/PP fibers.
4. **Presentation of micromechanics-based design guidelines for high-ductility ECC/SHCC**:
   - Establishes a framework for determining the critical fiber volume fraction ($V_{fc}$) required to satisfy the PSH energy index ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1.0$) and the strength index ($\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} \ge 1.0$).

## Methods

- **Analyzed fiber families and scope of the literature (Sections 2~5 & Tables 1~6)**:
  - Analysis of a total of 215 key primary sources.
  - Fiber types:
    1. `PVA (polyvinyl alcohol)`: Hydrophilic high-strength fibers such as Kuraray K-II REC15.
    2. `PE / UHMWPE (ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene)`: Ultra-high-strength/high-modulus fibers such as Spectra and Dyneema.
    3. `PP / HTPP (polypropylene / high-toughness PP)`: Low-cost alkali-resistant fibers.
    4. `Nylon, PET, PAN, Aramid (Kevlar)`: Specialty ballistic/impact-resistant fibers.
- **Interfacial and composite performance evaluation parameters (Section 6 & Figs. 1~12)**:
  - Single-fiber pullout test: chemical debonding energy ($G_d$), frictional shear strength ($\tau_0$), slip-hardening coefficient ($\beta$).
  - Uniaxial tension: first-cracking strength ($\sigma_{fc}$), ultimate tensile strength ($\sigma_{tu}$), tensile strain capacity ($\varepsilon_u$).

### 2. Interpretation of the damage and interfacial bridging mechanisms of polymer fibers
- **Oiling control of PVA fibers**: To prevent premature fiber rupture (Fiber Rupture) caused by the strong hydrogen bonds on the PVA surface, a 1.2% silicone/oil coating is applied to lower $G_d$ and induce the pullout mode.
- **Slip-hardening of PE fibers**: During pullout, the surface of hydrophobic PE fibers expands/delaminates slightly under frictional heat and matrix asperities, exhibiting slip-hardening resistance with $\beta > 0$ and thereby realizing enormous tensile ductility.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is an immortal highest-authority landmark review that completely compiled the physicochemical properties and interfacial micromechanics of every synthetic polymer fiber applied to modern cementitious composites, providing the compass for the design of ECC/SHCC and next-generation high-ductility construction composites.
