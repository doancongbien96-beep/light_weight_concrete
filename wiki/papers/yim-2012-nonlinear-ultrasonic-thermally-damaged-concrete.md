## Summary
A representative CCR landmark paper by Professors Yim Hong-Jae, Park Sung-Jin, Kim Jae-Hong, and Kwak Hyo-Gyeong of KAIST that developed and demonstrated a nonlinear ultrasonic nondestructive testing technique measuring the acoustic nonlinearity parameter ($\beta$, via higher harmonic generation at the second harmonic) for early nondestructive precise diagnosis of nano/micro fine cracks and C-S-H hydrate thermal decomposition damage occurring inside concrete members exposed to fire and high-temperature heating ($100 \sim 600 °C$). It was demonstrated that the nonlinear parameter $\beta$ exhibits extremely high sensitivity, more than **8 times** that of conventional linear ultrasonic velocity, against high-temperature thermal damage.

## Key Contributions
- **Second-harmonic measurement system of nonlinear ultrasound for diagnosing fine thermal damage in concrete established**: amplitude ratio ($A_2/A_1^2$) at twice the fundamental frequency $2f_0 (1.0 MHz)$ relative to $f_0 (500 kHz)$ extracted precisely.
- **Nonlinear parameter ($\beta/\beta_0$) formulated quantitatively as a function of heating temperature ($100 \sim 600 °C$)**: at $300 °C$ heating, linear velocity fell by only 15 % whereas $\beta$ surged 350 %.
- **Fine crack opening contact acoustic nonlinearity (CAN) mechanism clarified**: nonlinear elastic response of fine crack faces after heating-cooling analyzed.
- **Standard technology presented for precise post-fire safety diagnosis of tunnels and plants**: identification of 100 % of early latent damage with no visible cracking.

## Methods
- Specimens: $100 \times 100 \times 100 mm$ concrete specimens ($f_c = 40 MPa$), electric furnace heating ($100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600 °C$, held 2 hours then slow cooled).
- Nonlinear ultrasonic system: high-voltage pulser/receiver (RITEC RAM-5000), broadband PZT transducers, precision FFT harmonic spectrum analysis.
- Verification tests: ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), uniaxial compressive strength, elastic modulus, mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP).
