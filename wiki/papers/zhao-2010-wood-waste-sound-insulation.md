# Sound insulation characteristics of wood waste and waste-tire rubber composite panels

## Summary

A paper evaluating the transmission loss (TL) of wood-waste tire rubber composite panels (WRCP), which combine wood particles with crumbled waste-tire rubber through UF and PMDI resins, by the four-microphone standing wave tube method. The sound insulation performance of WRCP was superior to commercial particleboard and composite flooring, and TL improved as rubber content, crumble diameter, and PMDI dosage increased. Although a functional-composite paper outside the ECC focus of this wiki, its carbon black network structure and viscoelastic energy dissipation mechanisms intersect with the damping family of notes.

## Key Contributions

1. Demonstration of WRCP superiority: among four test panels (commercial particleboard 650 kg/m^3; composite flooring 820 kg/m^3; WRCP W/R 60:40 at 980 kg/m^3; W/R 50:50 at 985 kg/m^3), the TL ranking was No.4 (W/R 50:50) > No.3 (W/R 60:40) > No.2 > No.1 — raising recycled rubber content from 40 % to 50 % further improved TL.
2. Design factors quantified: within the rubber crumble diameter range of 1~5 mm, larger diameters improved low- and mid-frequency TL. Increasing PMDI dosage also raised TL, but its effect diminished above 1000 Hz.
3. Microstructural interpretation of the insulation mechanism: (a) delayed deformation of viscoelastic rubber molecular chains dissipates acoustic energy as heat; (b) about 29.5 % carbon black in waste-tire rubber forms a networked crosslinked structure inside the rubber, causing diffraction and dispersion of sound waves that lengthen transmission paths; (c) per mass law, rubber density of 1210 kg/m^3 exceeding that of wood suppresses transmission; (d) chemical bonding between the R-NCO groups of PMDI and the R-COOH/R-OH groups of wood forms a continuous interface that increases reflection/refraction attenuation of sound waves.
4. SEM observation: under hot pressing (170 °C, 300 s, 4 MPa), wood cell lumina compressed from circular to elliptical with reduced porosity, rubber crumbles deformed without cracking, increasing contact area, and a thickness-direction density gradient formed a sandwich structure — a continuous interface arises only when sufficient resin is present, improving both sound insulation and mechanical performance together.

## Methods

Four panel types of 300 x 200 x 12 mm (each in triplicate). Siberian larch (newly felled) particles with UF resin at 10 % (of solids), and rubber crumbles (diameter 1~5 mm, ground after steel fiber removal) with PMDI at 6 %. After pre-pressing at 1.5 MPa for 30 s, hot pressing at 4 MPa secured a target density of 1000 kg/m^3. The four-microphone standing wave tube covered incident frequencies of 250~1600 Hz (reliable range limited to 250~1250 Hz by tube diameter), at room temperature of 23 ± 2 °C. TL was computed from the sound pressure transmission coefficient tp of Eqs. (1)-(2). Interface and microstructure were observed by conventional SEM (not BSE).

## Results

- In all panels, TL increased with frequency up to a maximum near 1250 Hz and then decreased owing to measurement limits such as insufficient tube length, yet WRCP retained higher TL than commercial boards even beyond.
- Service performance perspective: WRCP combines the absorption/resonance characteristics of porous wood with the viscoelastic damping of rubber, achieving stable sound insulation over a broad frequency band versus single materials.
- The condition for securing mechanics and insulation simultaneously is a 'continuous interface via sufficient UF+PMDI'; resin shortage creates interfacial voids, degrading both bonding and insulation.
