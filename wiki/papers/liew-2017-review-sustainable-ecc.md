## Summary

To overcome the environmental burden and cost problems arising from the high unit cement content of high-ductility cement composites (ECC) and their use of expensive synthetic fibers, this paper comprehensively reviews research trends in eco-friendly sustainable ECC (Green/Sustainable ECC) that utilize industrial by-products (fly ash, slag, silica fume, biomass ash, geopolymers) and recycled/natural fibers. It analyzes the micromechanical advantage whereby high-volume fly ash substitution (FA/C = 2.0 ~ 4.0) lowers matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) and thereby rather maximizes PSH strain-hardening performance, and systematizes the achievements in reduced heat of hydration, suppressed drying shrinkage, and carbon emission reductions of 50 % or more.

## Key Contributions
- **Organization of the micromechanical mechanism of sustainable ECC**: elucidates the principle whereby high-volume supplementary cementitious material substitution lowers interfacial chemical bonding and $K_m$ to an ideal level instead of merely degrading matrix strength, improving tensile ductility beyond $3 \sim 5 \%$.
- **Construction of a mechanical/durability database by by-product substitution ratio**: presents the substitution limits and optimal mix conditions for fly ash, slag, bottom ash, rice husk ash (RHA), limestone powder, and so forth.
- **Life-cycle assessment (LCA) and economic analysis**: quantitative comparison of carbon emissions per unit volume and life-cycle maintenance cost savings relative to ordinary concrete.

## Methods
- Meta-analysis of some 150 recent studies on sustainable ECC.
- Synthesis of mix variables, static/dynamic tensile performance, compressive strength, crack width, self-healing behavior, and durability evaluation data.

## Results
- High-volume fly ash ECC (HVFA-ECC, FA/C = 3.0 ~ 4.0): a 70 % reduction in cement usage versus OPC-based ECC, 28 d tensile strain capacity of $4.0 \sim 6.0 \%$, and crack widths maintained at or below $40 \mu m$.
- Identification of the advantages and drawbacks of recycled aggregates and natural basalt/recycled PET fibers, along with remaining challenges for long-term durability improvement.
