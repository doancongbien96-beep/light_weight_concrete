## Summary

This production-engineering paper by Professors Bang Yeon Lee, Jin Wook Bang, and Yun Yong Kim precisely compared, through experiments and cross-sectional image analysis, the effects of two manufacturing processes — extrusion molding and casting — on the three-dimensional spatial fiber orientation angle distribution, flexural strain-hardening envelope, modulus of rupture (MOR), and deflection ductility of thin high-ductility fiber-reinforced cement composite (PVA-FRC) panels ($t = 15 mm$). During extrusion, shear flow through the nozzle highly aligned fibers along the panel length direction ($\alpha_\theta = 0.85$), and the extruded panels demonstrated a flexural strength 1.8 times higher and a flexural toughness 2.4 times higher than cast specimens.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantitative comparison of mechanical performance of extruded versus cast FRC panels**: fiber 2D plane alignment in extruded panels achieved a flexural strength of 24.5 MPa.
- **Elucidation of the fiber alignment mechanism induced by nozzle shear flow**: cross-sectional elliptical image analysis confirmed that the fraction of fibers oriented with $\theta < 20°$ relative to the extrusion direction reached 82 %.
- **Demonstration of dense microstructure formation**: vacuum de-airing and high-pressure compaction during extrusion reduced capillary porosity by 45 %.
- **Optimization of the manufacturing process for high-performance precast facade panels**: shorter demolding times and high-quality thin-panel production.

## Methods

- Mix: cement + silica fume + silica flour, viscosity modifier (HPMC), PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 39 µm, lengths 6 and 12 mm).
- Forming processes: vacuum extruder (die nozzle $15 \times 100 mm$, extrusion speed $1.5 m/min$) versus steel-mold vibration casting.
- Tests: four-point flexure (span 300 mm, $0.5 mm/min$), optical microscopy cross-sectional fiber orientation analysis, 28 d compressive strength.
