# Micromechanics-Based Design of a High-Damping Strain-Hardening Cementitious Composite (EA-ECC) Incorporating Emulsified Asphalt and the Mechanism of Its 3× Improvement in Dynamic Loss Factor ($\tan\delta$)

## Summary

To radically improve both the cushion-layer damage problem of high-speed rail slab track (CRTS) systems and the vibration-damping performance of seismic structures, **a high-damping, high-ductility strain-hardening composite (EA-ECC)** was designed on a micromechanical basis by incorporating a viscoelastic emulsified asphalt (**EA: Emulsified Asphalt**, asphalt/cement ratio $\text{EA/C} = 0\sim 25\%$) into the matrix, and uniaxial direct tension, matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$), dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA), the PSH index ($J_b'/J_{tip}$), and temperature dependence ($-10\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$) were precisely evaluated, achieving **a compressive strength of $32\sim 58\ \text{MPa}$ while extending tensile strain from $3.1\% \rightarrow 5.2\%$ and raising the dynamic loss factor ($\tan\delta$) by more than 3× ($0.025 \rightarrow 0.078$)**: a 2019 landmark original paper in Construction and Building Materials by the joint research team of Prof. Shunzhi Qian (Nanyang Technological University, NTU) and chaired professor Victor C. Li (University of Michigan).

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the mechanism enabling simultaneous high damping and ultra-high ductility through emulsified asphalt incorporation**:
   - The viscoelastic organic asphalt phase dispersed inside the composite lowers the matrix fracture toughness $J_{tip}$ from $0.021 \rightarrow 0.009\ \text{kJ/m}^2$, thereby satisfying the PSH energy performance index ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 3.0$) and extending tensile strain to $5.2\%$.
2. **Demonstration of a 3× improvement in the dynamic loss factor ($\tan\delta$) and of vibration-energy dissipation**:
   - DMA measurements show that EA incorporation sharply increases the loss modulus ($E''$) and the loss factor ($\tan\delta$), with $\tan\delta$ rising $0.025 \rightarrow 0.078$, proving outstanding performance as an impact- and vibration-absorbing cushion material.
3. **Fundamental resolution of the brittle-cracking limitation of high-speed rail track slab (CA mortar)**:
   - The chronic fatigue cracking and fragmentation defects of conventional cement asphalt (CA) mortar are completely overcome by fiber-bridging strain-hardening technology.
4. **Verification of the stability of mechanical behavior under temperature variation ($-10\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - Elucidates the mechanism that prevents low-temperature (sub-freezing) embrittlement caused by asphalt stiffening while preserving ductility at high temperature.

## Methods

- **Mix design and material composition (Sections 2.1–2.2 & Tables 1–2)**:
  - Binder: OPC + fly ash (FA/C = 1.2) + micro silica sand (S/B = 0.36).
  - Emulsified asphalt: cationic asphalt emulsion (60 wt.% solids).
  - Five EA/C replacement levels: `EA-ECC0` (0%), `EA-ECC5` (5%), `EA-ECC10` (10%), `EA-ECC15` (15%), `EA-ECC25` (25%).
  - PVA fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, 1.2% oil coating).
- **Test methods (Section 2.3 & Figs. 1–2)**:
  - Matrix fracture toughness: three-point flexural test on ASTM E399 notched corner specimens $\rightarrow K_m, J_{tip} = K_m^2/E_m$.
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dogbone-shaped specimens (UTM-25, 0.5 mm/min, environmental chamber).
  - Dynamic mechanical analysis: TA DMA Q800 (frequency 1.0 Hz, temperature sweep $-10\sim 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$).

### 2. Interpretation of the viscoelastic damping and micromechanics interaction mechanisms
- **Promotion of saturated PSH strain-hardening via reduced $J_{tip}$**: asphalt particles act as flexible micro-voids at matrix crack fronts, reducing the fracture toughness $J_{tip}$ of the matrix by $57\%$, which delays crack propagation and fully induces the fiber bridging response.
- **Viscoelastic energy dissipation through asphalt molecular entanglement**: under externally applied dynamic vibration loading, relative slippage between asphalt molecular chains that have penetrated among the cement C-S-H gels produces internal viscous friction, rapidly dissipating vibration energy into thermal energy.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original study that combined the viscoelastic characteristics of emulsified asphalt with the ECC micromechanics framework to simultaneously achieve high damping ($\tan\delta = 0.078$) and ultra-high ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 5.2\%$), thereby establishing advanced functional composite-material technology optimized for high-speed rail track and vibration-control infrastructure.
