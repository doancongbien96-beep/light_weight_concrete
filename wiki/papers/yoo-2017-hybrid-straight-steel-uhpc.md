# Flexural Performance and Bridging Curves of Hybrid Straight Steel Fiber UHPC

## Summary

A joint study by the Yoon group at Seoul National University and Banthia at McGill. The study quantitatively evaluated the flexural performance and micromechanical bridging capacity of UHPC while varying the combination ratios of straight steel fibers differing in length and diameter (l_f/d_f: 30/0.3, 19.5/0.2, 13/0.2). Long fiber + intermediate fiber combinations were effective in improving toughness and cracking behavior, whereas long fiber + short fiber combinations were found to degrade performance beyond a fiber content of 1 %.

## Key Contributions

1. **Replacing long fibers with intermediate fibers improves flexural performance; replacing them with short fibers degrades it**: substitution of l_f/d_f 30/0.3 → 19.5/0.2 improved post-cracking strength and deflection capacity, while substitution with 13/0.2 degraded both.
2. **Hardening condition**: deflection-hardening of straight steel fiber UHPC required a fiber reinforcing index RI = v_f×(l_f/d_f) > 0.5.
3. **Discovery of a hybrid synergy effect (negative synergy)**: negative synergy appeared in all specimens; increases in fiber volume fraction did not improve flexural performance proportionally (harmful effects such as fiber agglomeration and impaired dispersion).
4. **Effect on bridging curves**: long + intermediate combinations increased bridging capacity, while long + short combinations decreased it; complementary energy showed the same tendency.

## Methods

- Materials: three types of straight steel fibers (lengths of 30, 19.5, and 13 mm; diameters of 0.3, 0.2, and 0.2 mm), UHPC matrix.
- Variables: single-type vs hybrid (fiber contents of 0.5~2.0 %).
- Evaluation: four-point bending (confirmation of deflection-hardening), derivation of the micromechanical bridging curve σ_B(δ), and calculation of complementary energy J'_b.

## Results

| Item | Long + intermediate | Long + short |
|---|---|---|
| Post-cracking strength | ↑ | ↓ (content >1.0 %) |
| Deflection capacity | ↑ | ↓ |
| Bridging capacity | ↑ | ↓ |
| Complementary energy | smaller decrease | larger decrease |
