# Snubbing and bundling effects and multiple-crack spacing

## Summary

A theoretical paper from Michigan ACE-MRL introducing the snubbing effect (bridging force amplification for inclined fiber pullout) and the bundling effect (reduced effective bond strength of fiber bundles) into the multiple-crack spacing theory of discontinuous randomly fiber-reinforced brittle matrix composites. It shows that the frictionless pulley assumption of Aveston et al. greatly overestimates crack spacing, and achieved predictions matching glass-strand cement composite experimental values by incorporating the snubbing coefficient f and effective bond strength τ_eff.

## Key Contributions

- Formalization of snubbing-coefficient crack-spacing reduction: reduction parameters derived for 1D/2D/3D random distributions. Converges to the existing theory at f=0 (frictionless pulley); crack spacing shrinks as f grows. nylon f=0.994, PP f=0.702.
- Geometric quantification of the bundling effect: τ_eff=τ×[effective area ratio] derived from the exposed area fraction of an elliptical strand cross-section (N filaments, diameter d_f). τ_eff/τ=0.297 applied to Oakley-Proctor glass strands (a=0.325 mm, b=0.055 mm, N=200).
- Trade-off clarified: snubbing favors short crack spacings but is disadvantageous for aligned (1D) orientation — at high f, 2D and 3D can yield smaller crack spacing than 1D.

## Methods

- Theoretical analysis: snubbing exponent (f) and bundle effective bonding (elliptical geometry) integrated into the Aveston-Cooper-Kelly crack spacing model. Verified with Oakley-Proctor's glass strand cement tensile test data (crack spacing versus fiber length), fitted over a τ range of 0.2~1.6 MPa.

## Results

- The frictionless pulley assumption (Eq. 2) overestimates crack spacing; agreement with experiments when assuming f≈0.7.
- τ_eff=0.297τ for a 200-filament strand — bundling cuts bond by about 70 % relative to individual fibers.
