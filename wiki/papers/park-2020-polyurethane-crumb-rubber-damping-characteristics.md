# Structural Damping Ratio Enhancement to $6.25\%$ in Polyurethane Composites with Waste Tire Crumb Rubber and Preplaced Aggregate, Based on Resonant Frequency Analysis

## Summary

To effectively block floor impact noise in apartment buildings and seismic/machinery vibration while promoting large-scale recycling of waste tires, this study manufactured a **high-damping composite material combining waste-tire crumb rubber (CR: mean particle size $308\ \mu\text{m}$) into an elastic polyurethane (PU) binder at weight ratios ($0, 25, 50, 75, 100\ \text{wt.}\%$) with coarse aggregate preplaced for stiffness enhancement**, and precisely evaluated the resonant frequency ($f_0$) and the **evolution of structural damping ratio ($\xi$)** through impact-hammer non-destructive vibration testing and the FFT half-power bandwidth method, achieving **at 100 % rubber content a damping ratio of $6.25\%$ (more than 6 times ordinary cement concrete) while simultaneously securing structural stiffness and a damping ratio of $4.82\%$ through aggregate preplacing**. This is the landmark origin paper of the team of Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (corresponding author), researcher Se-Eon Park, and Prof. Jeong-Il Choi at Chonnam National University, published in the Journal of the Korea Institute of Resources Recycling in 2020.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of ultra-high damping ($\xi = 6.25\%$) through organic hybridization of polyurethane and waste rubber**:
   - Adopting a viscoelastic polyurethane polymer instead of cement secured perfect chemical compatibility with rubber particles, amplifying the damping ratio to $6.25\%$, six times the typical concrete level of $1.0\%\sim 1.5\%$.
2. **Elucidation of linearly proportional damping improvement with crumb rubber content**:
   - As crumb rubber content increased from $0 \rightarrow 100\ \text{wt.}\%$ relative to PU, the damping ratio rose continuously by $62.3\%$ from $3.85\% \rightarrow 6.25\%$.
3. **Establishment of the optimal dynamic stiffness-damping balance via preplaced aggregate**:
   - Filling the mold with coarse aggregate before injecting the PU-rubber matrix reinforced dead-load supporting stiffness while successfully maintaining a high damping ratio of $4.82\%$.
4. **Quantification of dynamic loss mechanisms through the FFT half-power method**:
   - The viscoelastic energy dissipation characteristics inside the composite were precisely analyzed via amplitude decay of accelerometer time histories and half-power bandwidth of frequency response curves.

## Methods

- **Materials and composite mix design (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Binder: two-part liquid polyurethane (resin:hardener = 1:1 by weight, density $1.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$, tensile strength $15.5\ \text{MPa}$, elongation $120\%$, pot life 40 minutes).
  - Crumb rubber powder: waste tire derived ($0.375\sim 2000\ \mu\text{m}$, mean particle size $308\ \mu\text{m}$, density $1.13\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - Aggregate: $13\sim 20\ \text{mm}$ crushed coarse aggregate (density $2.65\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - Mix groups:
    1. `PU-CR0, CR25, CR50, CR75, CR100`: crumb rubber 0~100 wt.% of PU weight.
    2. `P-PU-CR0, P-PU-CR50, P-PU-CR100`: mixes with preplaced coarse aggregate.
- **Dynamic test program (Section 2.2 & Figs. 2~3)**:
  - Specimens: $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ prisms.
  - Acceleration measurement: nylon-tip impact hammer (086C03) → high-sensitivity accelerometer (352C33) → NI USB-5132 DAQ (5 repetitions).
  - Damping ratio: after FFT, $\xi = (f_2 - f_1) / (2f_0)$ (half-power bandwidth method).

### 2. Interpretation of the vibration energy absorption mechanism of the viscoelastic polymer matrix
- **Viscoelastic relaxation of polyurethane molecular chains**: intimate intermolecular bonding between solid rubber and the organic polyurethane generates frictional relaxation of polymer chains under impact waves, rapidly converting vibration wave energy into heat.
- **Harmony of aggregate skeleton and shockwave attenuation**: the preplaced coarse aggregate supports primary wave propagation while the PU-CR matrix in the aggregate gaps absorbs shear deformation energy, realizing a high damping ratio of $4.82\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the landmark origin proving breakthrough applicability as an interlayer noise reduction and seismic damping material, in which the Chonnam National University team led by Prof. Bang Yeon Lee developed an ultra-high damping composite of $6.25\%$ damping ratio by combining polyurethane with waste tire crumb rubber and satisfied structural stiffness and damping simultaneously through the preplaced aggregate method.
