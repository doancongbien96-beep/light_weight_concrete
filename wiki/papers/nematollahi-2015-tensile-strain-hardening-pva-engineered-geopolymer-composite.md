# Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 4.30\%$) Behavior and Micromechanics Bridging Energy ($J_b'/J_{tip}$) Design Mechanism of Eco-Friendly Fly-Ash-Based Engineered Geopolymer Composite (PVA-EGC)

## Summary

To fundamentally block the massive carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) emissions generated in conventional Portland cement (OPC) manufacturing and overcome structural brittleness, the research team of **Chair Professor Jay Sanjayan and Dr. Behzad Nematollahi (corresponding author) at Swinburne University of Technology, Australia** developed an eco-friendly engineered geopolymer composite (**PVA-EGC: 28-day compressive strength $43.0\ \text{MPa}$**) that uses **low-calcium Class F fly ash, a coal-fired power plant by-product, as a $100\%$ cement-free binder and reinforces a geopolymer matrix activated with a liquid sodium silicate-sodium hydroxide combined alkali activator with micro PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, and analyzed uniaxial direct tension tests, single-fiber interfacial bond pull-out tests, matrix fracture toughness ($K_m$) measurement, and the two principal micromechanics PSH criteria to demonstrate **securing a bridging energy margin ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 3.8$ through reduced matrix fracture toughness ($J_{tip} = 14.5\ \text{J/m}^2$)) yielding a uniaxial direct tensile elongation of $4.30\%$, a uniaxial ultimate tensile strength of $4.75\ \text{MPa}$, saturated fine multiple cracking ($w \le 35\ \mu\text{m}$), and a carbon emission cut of $76\%$ versus existing OPC-ECC** — the highest-authority landmark original paper of the Construction and Building Materials 2015.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first establishment of PSH micromechanics design for cement-free fly ash geopolymer-based EGC**:
   - Mathematically proved that the low elastic modulus and fracture toughness characteristics of alkali-activated geopolymer matrices are ideal for satisfying the PSH energy criterion ($J_b'/J_{tip}$).
2. **Demonstration of uniaxial direct tensile elongation $\varepsilon_u = 4.30\%$ and saturated multiple cracking**:
   - Induced optimal interfacial frictional bonding ($\tau_0 = 2.1\ \text{MPa}$) with $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ oil-coated PVA fiber, stably forming more than 40 microcracks.
3. **Control of the geopolymerization reaction through optimization of heat-curing conditions ($60\ ^\circ\text{C}$)**:
   - Combined 24 h heat curing followed by ambient air curing to secure early strength development and long-term curing stability simultaneously.
4. **Establishment of a global standard for cement-zero ($0\%$) carbon-neutral high-ductility materials**:
   - Demonstrated ultimate tensile properties on par with existing commercial OPC-ECC while reducing carbon emissions by $76\%$.

## Methods

- **Materials and geopolymer mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Precursor: Class F fly ash 100 wt.% (Gladstone coal-fired power station).
  - Activator: $D$-grade sodium silicate solution ($\text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 2.0$) + $8\ \text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ solution ($W/B = 0.33$).
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, aggregate/binder ratio $0.30$).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray REC15 PVA micro fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$).
- **Mechanical and micromechanical evaluation program (Sections 2.2~3.4 & Figs. 4~8)**:
  - Uniaxial tension: dogbone specimens (80 mm gauge, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Single fiber pull-out: interfacial shear bond strength ($\tau_0$) and chemical bond ($G_d$) extracted via micro pull-out jig.
  - Matrix fracture toughness: notched beam three-point bend test ($K_m, J_{tip}$).

### 2. Bridging constitution curve and crack integrity interpretation
- **Appropriate chemical bonding ($G_d = 1.8\ \text{J/m}^2$)**: the geopolymer gel does not excessively erode the PVA fiber surface, keeping premature fiber fracture below $8\%$.
- **Self-restraint of crack width**: even at the point of reaching $4.0\%$ tensile strain, individual crack widths remain at or below $35\ \mu\text{m}$, preserving watertightness and durability.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-tier landmark original paper in eco-friendly high-ductility materials engineering, in which the research team of Prof. Jay Sanjayan and Dr. Behzad Nematollahi developed an eco-friendly cement-free PVA-EGC exhibiting a uniaxial tensile elongation of 4.30% through micromechanical bridging design of the fly ash geopolymer matrix and demonstrated a carbon emission reduction of 76%.
