# Crack Characteristics Assessment and Weibull Probabilistic Evolution Modeling of 100 % Seawater Sea-Sand Engineered Cementitious Composites (SS-ECC)

## Summary

To construct sustainable marine infrastructure, offshore islands, and ports amidst regional freshwater and river sand shortages, a global collaborative team led by Professor Jian-Guo Dai (PolyU), Christopher Leung (HKUST), Jing Yu (SYSU), and Victor C. Li (Univ of Michigan) developed a high-strength, ultra-ductile **seawater sea-sand engineered cementitious composite (SS-ECC: 28 d compressive strength > 130 MPa, tensile strength > 8 MPa, tensile strain capacity 5~7 %)** mixing 100 % natural seawater and unwashed sea-sand directly. Evaluating the influence of sea-sand particle sizes ($d_{max} = 1.18\sim 4.75\ \text{mm}$), high-strength PE fiber lengths (6, 12, 18 mm), and volume fractions (1.0, 1.5, 2.0 vol.%) on **probabilistic crack width evolution (Weibull vs Log-normal distributions)** and single-fiber interfacial pullout mechanics, the authors demonstrated that SS-ECC incorporating 18 mm PE fibers at 2.0 vol.% achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **135.2 MPa**, an ultimate tensile strength of **9.2 MPa**, and an extraordinary tensile strain capacity of **7.2 %**. Throughout tensile elongation up to 5.0 %, the average crack width remained **below $60\ \mu\text{m}$**, and a two-parameter Weibull probability model tracked the statistical right-tail evolution of crack widths with high precision ($R^2 > 0.97$).

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of 135 MPa ultra-high-strength and 7 % high-ductility SS-ECC from 100 % seawater and sea-sand**:
   - Mixed raw unwashed sea-sand with seawater directly, achieving 4 times the compressive strength (> 130 MPa) and 700 times the tensile ductility (> 7.0 %) of conventional concrete.
2. **Weibull and log-normal probabilistic crack width evolution models**:
   - Formulated the statistical distribution of crack widths across 40~60 saturated microcracks as a function of Weibull scale ($\lambda$) and shape ($k$) parameters ($R^2 \ge 0.975$).
3. **Micromechanical optimization across PE fiber lengths ($6\sim 18\ \text{mm}$) and sand gradings**:
   - Increasing fiber length from 6 mm to 18 mm enhanced complementary bridging energy ($J_b'$) by 2.8-fold, increasing tensile strain capacity 3-fold from **2.5 % to 7.2 %**.
4. **Design paradigm for corrosion-free marine structures with FRP rebar**:
   - Combined non-corrosive FRP rebars with $60\ \mu\text{m}$ crack-controlled SS-ECC, establishing the foundation for 100-year maintenance-free coastal infrastructure.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–3, mass ratios)**:
  - Binders: OPC 52.5 (1.0), silica fume (0.25), GGBS (0.15), W/B = 0.16.
  - Mixing water: Artificial seawater (ASTM D1141 formula: $\text{NaCl}, \text{MgCl}_2, \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$, etc.).
  - Aggregates: Natural unwashed sea-sand (S1: $d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S2: $d_{50} = 300\ \mu\text{m}$, S4: $d_{max} = 4.75\ \text{mm}$, S/B = 0.36).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fibers ($d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 3000 MPa, modulus 100 GPa):
    - Lengths: 6 mm, 12 mm, 18 mm.
    - Dosages: $V_f = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$.
- **Testing program**:
  - Direct tension: JSCE dogbone specimens ($330\ \text{mm} \times 60\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$, gauge length 80 mm).
  - Optical microscopy: 500× magnification crack width and spacing measurements.
  - Single-fiber pullout: Micro-testing frame ($L_e = 1\sim 3\ \text{mm}$).
  - Microstructure: JEOL-6390 SEM fracture surface analysis.

### 2. Weibull probabilistic crack evolution mechanics
- **Weibull cumulative distribution**:
  $$F(w) = 1 - \exp\left[ -\left(\frac{w}{\lambda}\right)^k \right]$$
  - As tensile strain $\varepsilon$ increased from 0.2 % to 5.6 %, scale parameter $\lambda$ grew moderately ($39.1\ \mu\text{m} \rightarrow 63.7\ \mu\text{m}$), while shape parameter $k$ remained stable ($2.09 \rightarrow 2.49$), mathematically demonstrating that runaway crack opening was strictly prevented.
- **Seawater-accelerated hydration**: $\text{Cl}^-$ and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ ions in seawater accelerated early hydration, increasing 7 d early compressive strength by 15 % compared to freshwater controls.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that 100 % seawater and unwashed sea-sand can produce 135 MPa ultra-high-strength ECC with 7.2 % tensile ductility and established a calibrated Weibull model for microcrack evolution.
