## Summary

For the design of high-performance protective members resisting seismic, vehicular collision and rockfall impact loads, this study experimentally established the number of impacts at initial cracking ($N_1$), the number of impacts at final failure ($N_2$), the dynamic increase factor for tensile strength ($DIF$) and the impact failure energy absorption behavior of a high-ductility composite (PVA-ECC, $f_c = 48 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $3.8 \%$) and ordinary concrete (NC 45 MPa) under ACI 544 drop-weight impact loading (4.5 kg hammer, drop height 457 mm). PVA-ECC was demonstrated to increase the failure impact count by a factor of **45** over plain concrete, absorb an impact energy of **8850 J**, and completely suppress brittle splitting failure.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of the drop-weight impact energy absorption mechanism of PVA-ECC**: the fiber bridging membrane disperses impact stress waves, suppressing penetrating shear fragmentation.
- **Impact ductility index ($I_d = N_2/N_1$) improved 6.8-fold**: after initial fine cracking the specimen supports its shape over an extended period without fragment scattering.
- **Completion of dynamic compressive/tensile DIF models**: establishment of strength and toughness envelopes as dynamic strain rate increases.
- **Proposal of slimmed design standards for road barriers and rockfall protection walls**: a 40 % reduction in cross-section thickness achieved.

## Methods

- Specimens: disc-shaped impact specimens (diameter 152 mm, thickness 64 mm) and $100 \times 100 \times 400 mm$ prismatic beams.
- Materials: PVA-ECC (Kuraray REC15, $V_f = 2.0 \%$) versus ordinary concrete (NC 45 MPa).
- Impact testing: ACI 544.2R standard drop-weight impact machine (drop hammer 4.5 kg, $h = 457 mm$), high-speed accelerometers and DIC crack measurement.
