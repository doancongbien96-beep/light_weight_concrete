# Taguchi ($L_{16}$) Robust Design and ANOVA-Based Optimization of Fly Ash PE-EGC for Low Density ($1650\ \text{kg/m}^3$), Ultra-High Ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 8.20\%$), and $65\%$ Carbon-Emission Reduction

## Summary

To develop a next-generation cement-free composite (Engineered Cement-Free Composites, F-ECFC / PE-EGC) that dramatically reduces carbon emissions from the cement industry while simultaneously satisfying ultra-high ductility and structural lightweighting, this landmark 2023 Journal of Building Engineering paper by the joint research team of **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University (corresponding author) and John E. Bolander Endowed Chair Professor at UC Davis** combined the **Taguchi $L_{16}(4^5)$ orthogonal-array design of experiments, analysis of variance (ANOVA), and multiple regression analysis** to quantify the statistical contributions of curing temperature ($60, 80, 100, 120\ ^\circ\text{C}$), heating time ($12, 24, 36, 48\ \text{h}$), and PE fiber volume fraction ($V_f = 0.5\%, 1.0\%, 1.5\%, 2.0\%$); for the derived optimum mix (`80°C-48H-2%`), property verification and life-cycle carbon footprint (CF) assessment were carried out, demonstrating **a density of $1650\ \text{kg/m}^3$, a 28-day compressive strength of $39.2\ \text{MPa}$, a uniaxial tensile strength of $7.85\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strain capacity of $8.20\%$, and a $65\%$ reduction in $\text{CO}_2$ emissions relative to conventional OPC-ECC**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of a statistical mix-optimization framework via the Taguchi $L_{16}$ orthogonal array and ANOVA**:
   - The cross-effects of numerous experimental variables (temperature, time, fiber fraction) were analyzed with a minimal number of experiments (16 batches), quantitatively isolating the dominant influence factor for each property (density, compressive strength, first-cracking strength, tensile strength, tensile strain capacity).
2. **Achievement of an ultimate tensile strain of $8.20\%$ under the low-density ($1650\ \text{kg/m}^3$) condition**:
   - While reducing the self-weight of structures by $31\%$ compared with normal concrete ($2400\ \text{kg/m}^3$), the composite exhibits a world-leading ductility of $8.20\%$ that overwhelms conventional cement-based ECC ($\varepsilon_u = 3\sim 5\%$).
3. **Elucidation of the hot-curing interface chemistry between PE fibers and alkali-activated fly ash gel**:
   - The $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heat curing for 48 h condition completes the polycondensation reaction of the N-A-S-H gel, optimizing the slip-hardening interfacial friction coefficient ($\tau_0$).
4. **Demonstration of a $65\%$ carbon-footprint reduction through a whole-life environmental impact assessment**:
   - By replacing cement with $100\%$ fly ash, the embodied-carbon emission per unit volume is cut by $65\%$, numerically verifying its eco-friendly sustainability.

## Methods

- **Taguchi $L_{16}$ experimental design and factor levels (Sections 2~3 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Factor A (curing temperature $T$): $60, 80, 100, 120\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (4 levels).
  - Factor B (heating time $CT$): $12, 24, 36, 48\ \text{h}$ (4 levels).
  - Factor C (PE fiber volume fraction $V_f$): $0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ (4 levels).
  - Matrix composition: F-grade fly ash 100% + silica sand (S/FA = 0.36) + D-Grade sodium silicate + 10M $\text{NaOH}$ (W/GP solids ratio = 0.40).
  - Fiber: ultra-high-molecular-weight PE fiber ($d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 3000\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 88\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Testing and statistical analysis procedure (Sections 2.2~3.3 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dog-bone specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, 0.5 mm/min).
  - Analysis of variance (ANOVA): F-test and contribution rate (Contribution %) estimation.
  - Multiple regression modeling: derivation of polynomial regression equations and numerical optimization $\rightarrow$ derivation of the optimum mix and one-to-one verification through physical experiments.

### 2. Interpretation of the Taguchi main effects and the micromechanics strain-hardening mechanism
- **Decomposition of per-factor contributions**: The ANOVA results showed that the PE fiber volume fraction made an overwhelming contribution of $68.5\%$ to the ultimate tensile strain ($\varepsilon_{ts}$), while the curing temperature ($T$) and heating time ($CT$) accounted for a contribution of more than $75\%$ to the compressive strength.
- **Saturated multiple cracking mechanism**: The dense N-A-S-H gel formed under $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ curing establishes a slip-hardening-type frictional bond with the PE fiber surface, uniformly generating more than 40 densely distributed microcracks until the strain reaches $8.20\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original study in the field of next-generation eco-friendly construction materials, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's research team at Chonnam National University and Prof. John Bolander of UC Davis established the optimum processing conditions of a cement-free fly ash PE-EGC through the Taguchi robust design method and perfectly demonstrated the light weight of $1650\ \text{kg/m}^3$, a world-leading ultimate tensile ductility of $8.20\%$, and a carbon-emission reduction of 65%.
