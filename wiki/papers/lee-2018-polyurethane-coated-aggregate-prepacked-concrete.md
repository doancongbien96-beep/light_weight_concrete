# High-Damping Prepacked Concrete (PC-P) Using PU-Coated Coarse Aggregate: Damping Ratio of $6.21\%$ (a 4.3× Improvement) and Hilbert-Transform-Based Modal Damping of $10\%$ in the Floor Impact Noise Band ($< 200\ \text{Hz}$)

## Summary

To control apartment-building floor impact noise (heavy/lightweight impact sound) and structural vibration while overcoming the economic inefficiency of conventional approaches that consume large quantities of expensive polyurethane (PU) resin, **Prof. Bang Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University (corresponding author) and co-authors Kang Seok Lee, Jeong-Il Choi, Se-Eon Park, and Jae-Seung Hwang** developed a **novel high-damping prepacked concrete (PC-P) in which coarse aggregates are pre-coated with only a thin viscoelastic PU film (15 % of the aggregate weight), pre-placed in a mold, and then injected with cement paste**, and through ASTM C215 impact resonance testing together with **Hilbert-transform (Hilbert Transform) free-vibration envelope optimization analysis** on large beams ($100 \times 100 \times 1000\ \text{mm}$) established **the mechanism by which the composite achieves a damping ratio of $6.21\%$ — 4.3 times higher than ordinary prepacked concrete (1.45 %) — while attaining a modal damping ratio of about $10\%$ in the core floor-impact-noise frequency band ($< 200\ \text{Hz}$) and securing a flexural strength of $5.8\ \text{MPa}$**: a landmark 2018 original paper in Cement and Concrete Composites.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first development of high-damping prepacked concrete (PC-P) applying PU-coated coarse aggregate**:
   - By locally coating only the aggregate surfaces with PU, the content of the expensive resin per total volume is minimized while the damping ratio is still **drastically improved by $328\%$ over ordinary concrete ($1.45\% \rightarrow 6.21\%$)**.
2. **Modal damping analysis by Hilbert-transform-based free-vibration envelope optimization**:
   - Separated and quantified the amplitude-dependent damping behavior within the compound signal, mathematically proving that the modal damping ratio in the main floor-impact-noise band ($< 200\ \text{Hz}$) reaches **about $10\%$**.
3. **Elucidation of the energy dissipation mechanism of the viscoelastic coating layer at the aggregate-cement interfacial transition zone (ITZ)**:
   - The PU elastic layer inserted between the stiff aggregate and the cement paste absorbs and dissipates localized shear-deformation energy under vibration, relieving stress concentration.
4. **Retention of structural flexural strength ($5.8\ \text{MPa}$) and secured economy**:
   - Minimizing the strength reduction relative to an all-resin-matrix composite, it retains an excellent flexural capacity of $5.8\ \text{MPa}$ — only a $6\%$ decrease from ordinary concrete ($6.2\ \text{MPa}$).

## Methods

- **Materials and mix proportions (Section 2 & 3.1, Fig. 2)**:
  - Coarse aggregate: crushed stone (maximum size 25 mm, density $2.65\ \text{g/cm}^3$).
  - PU coating: two-component viscoelastic polyurethane resin, uniformly coated on the surface and cured at 15 % of the aggregate weight.
  - Injection paste: ordinary Portland cement + water (W/C = 0.40) + high-range water reducer.
  - Two comparison mixes:
    1. `PC`: ordinary coarse aggregate preplaced + cement paste injection (control prepacked concrete).
    2. `PC-P`: PU-coated coarse aggregate preplaced + cement paste injection (high-damping prepacked concrete).
- **Test program and signal processing (Section 3.2~3.4 & Figs. 3~9)**:
  - Small prisms ($40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$): ASTM C215 impact resonance frequency and half-power bandwidth damping ratio ($\xi$) measurement.
  - Large beams ($100 \times 100 \times 1000\ \text{mm}$): impact hammer excitation $\rightarrow$ accelerometer instrumentation $\rightarrow$ least-squares optimization of the free-vibration damping envelope $A(t) = A_0 e^{-\xi \omega t}$ obtained via the Hilbert transform.
  - Static mechanical tests: compression (50 mm cubes), three-point flexure (span 100 mm).

### 2. Hilbert-transform damping analysis and interfacial dissipation mechanism
- **Damping maximized in the low-frequency floor impact noise band**: In the $50\sim 200\ \text{Hz}$ heavy impact sound band where humans feel discomfort most strongly, the 1st~3rd mode damping ratios of the PC-P beam remain consistently at about $10\%$, rapidly attenuating floor slab vibration.
- **Viscoelastic interfacial shear behavior**: As vibration waves pass through, the PU interfacial layer between the aggregate and the cement matrix undergoes periodic shear deformation that dissipates kinetic energy into heat, realizing selective damping without drastic degradation of compressive/flexural stiffness.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a key landmark original paper in architectural acoustics and vibration control, in which Prof. Bang Yeon Lee's team at Chonnam National University fused polyurethane-coated coarse aggregates into the prepacked concrete method, improving the damping ratio 4.3-fold in an economical and sustainable manner and raising modal damping in the floor impact noise frequency band to 10 %.
