## Summary

An official 2007 Caltrans research report that comprehensively analyzes the supply-demand status of Class F fly ash ($CaO < 10 \%$) for suppressing alkali-silica reaction (ASR) and improving sulfate resistance while reducing $CO_2$ across California Department of Transportation highway, bridge, and pavement infrastructure; trends in power-plant coal-ash discharge; durability-based service-life extension as a function of cement replacement rate (25 ~ 50 %); and future supply-shortage risk. It demonstrated that California infrastructure concrete requires roughly **800,000 tonnes** of Class F fly ash per year and that 25 % cement replacement suppresses ASR expansion by **85 %** and cuts life-cycle cost by **30 %**.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of a fly-ash supply-demand matrix for California infrastructure concrete**: logistics analysis of Class F fly ash from supply sources in California (coal-fired power plants in the four neighboring western states).
- **Definition of the critical replacement rate ($25 \%$) for ASR expansion suppression**: ASTM C1260 and ASTM C1567 test results show that 25 % Class F fly ash replacement achieves a 14-day expansion rate of $0.05 \%$ or less.
- **Quantification of $CO_2$ reductions of $0.85$ t per tonne of cement clinker**: contributes to meeting the state greenhouse-gas reduction target (AB 32).
- **Establishment of an alternative-pozzolan supplementation policy (slag powder, natural pozzolan, metakaolin)**: presents a supply-diversification roadmap responding to long-term coal-fired plant closures.

## Methods

- Survey scope: 12 coal-fired power plants in the western United States; Caltrans annual concrete placement of 3 million m³.
- Test data: ASTM C618 (fly ash specification), ASTM C1260 (ASR mortar-bar test), ASTM C1012 (sulfate resistance).
- Life-cycle evaluation: analysis of 50-year road pavement based on the Caltrans standard LCCA software RealCost.
