# Structural Adhesives for Concrete and Steel-Concrete Composite Joints: Interfacial Shear Bond Mechanics, Surface Preparation, and 12 Governing Factors Review

## Summary

To rehabilitate aging reinforced concrete (RC) and steel-concrete composite structures, execute crack injection repairs, bond external reinforcements (FRP sheets and steel plates), and overcome localized stress concentrations in mechanical shear studs through continuous bonded connections, researchers at MNIT Jaipur (Prof. Pankaj Kumar, Prof. Amar Patnaik, and Prof. Sandeep Chaudhary) compiled a comprehensive 22-page review in the *International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives* (2017). The paper details the **chemical and physical characteristics of structural adhesives (epoxies, polyurethanes, methacrylates, inorganic cementitious geopolymer adhesives, and bio-adhesives), adhesive and cohesive interfacial failure modes, composite shear transfer mechanics, and 12 governing factors influencing connection performance (adhesive layer thickness $t_a$, substrate surface roughness, curing temperature/humidity, moisture exposure, mineral filler additions, fatigue, and creep)**. The review identifies an optimal adhesive layer thickness range of $0.5\sim 1.5\ \text{mm}$, beyond which shear strength drops by up to 30 % due to internal shrinkage stresses and micro-voids, and establishes design standards combining sandblasting surface preparation with nano-filler reinforcement.

## Key Contributions

1. **Classification and interfacial fracture mechanics of structural construction adhesives**:
   - Standardized mechanical properties ($\sigma_t = 30\sim 80\ \text{MPa}, E = 1.5\sim 8.0\ \text{GPa}$) and fracture toughness criteria across epoxy, polyurethane, and acrylic adhesives.
2. **Continuous shear transfer mechanics in steel-concrete composite joints**:
   - Replaced point-contact mechanical stud shear connectors with surface-bonded adhesive layers, eliminating localized concrete crushing and slip.
3. **Formulation of 12 environmental and geometric governing parameters**:
   - Quantified parametric dependencies on adhesive thickness ($t_a$), effective bond length ($L_e$), substrate width-to-thickness ratio ($b/t \ge 60$), relative humidity ($\text{RH} > 80\%$), and temperature ($T > T_g$).
4. **Surface preparation and nano-filler reinforcement guidelines**:
   - Formulated surface preparation criteria (laitance removal, steel profile roughness $R_a = 50\sim 100\ \mu\text{m}$) and nano-silica/carbon nanotube filler additions to optimize bond area and durability.

## Methods

- **Structural adhesive categories and mechanical ranges (Sections 1–2 & Tables 1–3)**:
  - Epoxy resins: Tensile strength 30~90 MPa, modulus 2.0~5.0 GPa, shear strength 20~45 MPa, elongation 1~6 %, $T_g = 50\sim 80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (most widely utilized).
  - Polyurethanes: Tensile strength 10~30 MPa, high elongation ($>50\%$), superior vibration/impact damping.
  - Acrylics/methacrylates: Fast curing (< 1 h), moisture-tolerant and oil-tolerant bonding.
- **Connection test methods and 12 governing parameters (Section 3 & Figs. 1–5)**:
  - Direct shear tests (single/double lap shear): $\tau = P / (b \cdot L_b)$.
  - Pull-off tests (ASTM D4541, 4541): Direct tensile bond measurement.
  - 12 governing variables: (1) bond thickness, (2) surface roughness, (3) curing temp/RH, (4) moisture exposure, (5) strain rate, (6) filler types, (7) freeze-thaw, (8) fatigue cycles, (9) elevated temperature/fire, (10) creep, (11) joint geometry, (12) aging durability.

### 2. Adhesive thickness and interfacial fracture mechanics
- **Optimal adhesive layer thickness ($t_a = 0.5\sim 1.5\ \text{mm}$)**: Overly thin layers ($t_a < 0.2\ \text{mm}$) fail to fill substrate asperities, causing dry contact; overly thick layers ($t_a > 2.0\ \text{mm}$) concentrate internal curing shrinkage stresses and initiate premature shear cracking.
- **Substrate failure assurance**: A properly detailed epoxy joint achieves shear bond strengths ($>25\ \text{MPa}$) far exceeding concrete tensile/shear strengths ($3\sim 5\ \text{MPa}$), ensuring 100 % failure occurs within the concrete substrate.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark review systematized interfacial mechanics, 12 governing factors, and failure criteria for structural adhesives, providing design guidelines for concrete retrofitting and steel-concrete composite joints.
