# Comprehensive review establishing the nanoscale gel-phase structures (C-A-S-H / N-A-S-H), solid-state NMR spectroscopy, and reaction kinetics mechanisms of alkali-activated materials (AAMs)

## Summary

To enable the full replacement of Portland cement, which accounts for roughly $8\%$ of global greenhouse-gas emissions, with sustainable low-carbon alkali-activated binders (AAMs: Alkali-Activated Materials) and geopolymers, this paper comprehensively establishes the theoretical foundation of modern earth-materials and inorganic polymer science: **the nanoscale gel network structures of high-calcium (slag-based C-A-S-H gel), low-calcium (fly ash/metakaolin-based N-A-S-H gel), and hybrid ((C,N)-A-S-H) systems; solid-state $^{29}\text{Si}$ and $^{27}\text{Al}$ MAS-NMR spectroscopy; synchrotron atomic pair distribution function (PDF); small-angle X-ray/neutron scattering (SAXS/SANS); the reaction-kinetic phase transition from the initial aluminum-rich gel (Gel 1) to the silicon-rich gel (Gel 2); and alkali-silica reaction (ASR)/sulfate durability mechanisms**. Authored by **Prof. John L. Provis, chair professor at the University of Sheffield, UK**, it is the highest-authority landmark review paper of Annual Review of Materials Research, 2015.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of nano-molecular structure models for low-calcium N-A-S-H gel vs. high-calcium C-A-S-H gel**:
   - Clear separation and definition of the phase boundary between three-dimensional tetrahedral network geopolymer gels and layered tobermorite-like C-A-S-H gels according to the $\text{Ca/Si}$ and $\text{Al/Si}$ ratios on the $\text{CaO-Al}_2\text{O}_3\text{-SiO}_2$ phase diagram.
2. **Elucidation of the local coordination environments of $\text{Si}$ and $\text{Al}$ via solid-state MAS-NMR spectroscopy**:
   - Quantification of the degree of polymerization and the aluminum cross-linking degree through interpretation of the $Q^4(m\text{Al})$ ($m = 0\sim 4$) resonance peaks of $^{29}\text{Si}$ NMR and the four-coordinated $\text{Al(IV)}$ peak of $^{27}\text{Al}$ NMR.
3. **Establishment of the two-stage reaction kinetics (Gel 1 $\rightarrow$ Gel 2) phase-transition mechanism**:
   - Demonstration that the Al-rich precursor gel (Gel 1), formed rapidly during early dissolution, progressively takes up dissolved $\text{Si}$ and reorganizes into a thermodynamically stable Si-rich three-dimensional framework (Gel 2).
4. **Elucidation of ASR expansion suppression and sulfate-attack resistance mechanisms**:
   - Interpretation showing that AAMs systems lacking free $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ exclude ettringite expansive breakdown, and that C-A-S-H gel with high $\text{Al}$ substitution adsorbs alkaline cations to suppress ASR expansion.

## Methods

- **Scope of reviewed literature and analytical techniques (Sections 2~6 & Figs. 1~5)**:
  - Synthesis of 138 state-of-the-art primary references in condensed-matter physics/materials science.
  - Key analytical techniques: solid-state nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy ($^{29}\text{Si}, ^{27}\text{Al}, ^{23}\text{Na}$ MAS-NMR), synchrotron atomic pair distribution function (PDF), small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS), nanoindentation.
- **Binder classification scheme**:
  1. Low-calcium family: Class F fly ash, metakaolin (N-A-S-H gel dominated).
  2. High-calcium family: ground granulated blast-furnace slag GGBS (C-A-S-H gel dominated).
  3. Hybrid family: slag-fly ash blended systems ((C,N)-A-S-H gel coexistence).

### 2. Interpretation of thermodynamic gel stability and ion immobilization mechanisms
- **Charge-balancing mechanism**: to compensate the negative charge of tetrahedral $\text{AlO}_4^{-}$, $\text{Na}^+$ or $\text{K}^+$ ions bind in hydrated form within the gel nanopores, maintaining charge neutrality.
- **Radionuclide and heavy-metal isolation**: zeolite-like cage structures permanently trap hazardous heavy-metal ions such as $\text{Cs}^+, \text{Sr}^{2+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}$ inside the gel framework, preventing environmental release at its source.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is the historic highest-authority landmark review in which Prof. John L. Provis synthesized the nanoscale gel chemical structures of alkali-activated materials, the interpretation of NMR spectroscopy, and the two-stage reaction kinetics, completing the core theoretical standard of modern AAMs/geopolymer materials-science research.
