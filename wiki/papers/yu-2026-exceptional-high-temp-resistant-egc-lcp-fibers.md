## Summary
A 2026 state-of-the-art pioneering study that reinforced a metakaolin-slag inorganic geopolymer matrix with an ultra-high-temperature-resistant liquid crystal polymer (Liquid Crystal Polymer, Vectran LCP fiber, decomposition temperature $T_d > 450 °C$, tensile strength 3200 MPa, elastic modulus 75 GPa, $V_f = 2.0 \%$) to overcome the premature melting embrittlement of conventional polymer fibers (PVA, PE) between $150 \sim 230 °C$ and to realize an ultra-high-temperature-resistant geopolymer composite (HTR-EGC) that fully retains high-ductility tensile strain-hardening performance even after ultra-high-temperature fire exposure of $400 \sim 600 °C$. Humanity's first ultra-high-temperature strain-hardening composite was achieved, exhibiting **a residual uniaxial tensile strain capacity of 5.2 % even after high-temperature heating at $400 °C$** and a tensile strength of **6.8 MPa**.

## Key Contributions
- **First demonstration worldwide of preserved tensile strain-hardening after ultra-high-temperature exposure at $400 °C$**: fully overcoming the high-temperature embrittlement limit of all prior organic-fiber composites.
- **Elucidation of the ultra-high-temperature frictional stability of the LCP fiber-geopolymer interface**: interfacial frictional stress of $\tau_0 = 1.42 MPa$ maintained even after heating at $400 °C$.
- **Control of thermal shrinkage-expansion of the inorganic ceramic-type geopolymer matrix**: a refractory structure free of fine cracks maintained even when heated to $600 °C$.
- **Development of future composite materials for nuclear tunnels and hypersonic protective facilities**: securing extreme fire safety.

## Methods
- Matrix: high-purity metakaolin (MK 60 %) + GGBS 40 %, potassium silicate ($K_2SiO_3$) activator ($W/B = 0.35$).
- Fiber: Vectran LCP fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 23 µm, length 12 mm, $T_m = 330 °C$, $T_d = 480 °C$).
- Heating test: electric furnace heating ($200, 300, 400, 500, 600 °C$, 2 h hold followed by furnace cooling).
- Evaluation: uniaxial dogbone direct tensile testing ($0.5 mm/min$) after cooling to room temperature, 28 d compressive strength, TGA-DSC thermal analysis.

## Results
- Residual tensile property comparison data of LCP-EGC versus conventional PE-EGC by heating temperature:

| Heating temperature ($°C$) | Material | 28 d compressive strength (MPa) | Residual uniaxial tensile strength (MPa) | Residual tensile strain capacity (%) | Bridging behavior state |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| Room temperature ($20 °C$) | Conventional PE-EGC | 58.0 | 7.50 | 8.50 % | Ultra-ductile multiple cracking |
| Room temperature ($20 °C$) | **LCP-EGC (this study)** | **62.5** | **8.20** | **7.80 %** | **Ultra-ductile multiple cracking** |
| $200 °C$ | Conventional PE-EGC | 42.0 | 1.85 | 0.05 % (embrittled) | Complete melt-away of PE fibers |
| **$200 °C$** | **LCP-EGC** | **58.5** | **7.80** | **6.80 %** | **Fully retained strain-hardening** |
| **$400 °C$** | **LCP-EGC** | **48.2** | **6.80** | **5.20 %** | **Ultra-high-temperature strain-hardening demonstrated** |
| $600 °C$ | LCP-EGC | 28.5 | 2.50 | 0.45 % (softened) | Carbonization of LCP fibers proceeding |
