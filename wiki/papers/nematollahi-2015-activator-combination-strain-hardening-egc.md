# Fresh Flowability $59\%$ Improvement and Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 4.30\%$) Mechanism of Fly-Ash-Based PVA-EGC by Sodium vs. Potassium Alkali Activator

## Summary

To optimize the fresh viscosity and uniaxial tensile strain-hardening performance of eco-friendly low-carbon engineered geopolymer composites (EGC) that exclude Portland cement $100\%$, the research team of **Prof. Behzad Nematollahi and Prof. Jay Sanjayan of Swinburne University of Technology, Australia** applied sodium-based activator ($\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{NaOH}$) and potassium-based activator ($\text{K}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{KOH}$) to a **Class F fly ash matrix incorporating 2 vol.% oil-coated PVA fiber**, and precisely evaluated slump flow, 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct tensile stress-strain curves, and multiple fine-cracking characteristics — a landmark original paper published in the Journal of the Australian Ceramic Society in 2015. The results demonstrate that the **EGC-K mix with the potassium-based activator improved fresh flowability by $59\%$ ($135 \rightarrow 215\ \text{mm}$) owing to the low viscosity of potassium silicate solution and maximized fiber dispersion, achieving excellent pseudo-strain-hardening (PSH) multiple fine-cracking behavior with a 28-day compressive strength of $36.2\ \text{MPa}$, uniaxial tensile strength of $4.45\ \text{MPa}$, and ultimate tensile strain of $4.30\%$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Elucidation of the geopolymer matrix viscosity and fiber dispersion mechanism according to alkali cation ($\text{Na}^+$ vs. $\text{K}^+$)**:
   - The $\text{K}^+$ ion has a smaller hydrated radius and markedly lower solution viscosity than $\text{Na}^+$, improving matrix flowability by $59\%$ and completely blocking the balling (Ball-effect) phenomenon of PVA fiber.
2. **Demonstration of ultra-ductile tensile strain-hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 4.30\%$) in cement-free PVA-EGC**:
   - Under $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heat curing, an appropriate interfacial frictional bond formed between the three-dimensional N-A-S-H network gel and PVA fiber, expressing more than 20 dense multiple cracks.
3. **Quantitative comparison of the mechanical strength balance of sodium vs. potassium activators**:
   - Proved that EGC-K outperforms EGC-Na in both compressive strength ($36.2\ \text{MPa}$ vs. $27.6\ \text{MPa}$, $+31\%$) and tensile strength ($4.45\ \text{MPa}$ vs. $3.41\ \text{MPa}$, $+30\%$).
4. **Establishment of eco-friendly composite technology with $\text{CO}_2$ emission reduction of $70\%$ versus cement-based PVA-ECC**:
   - Secured high ductility and environmental performance simultaneously through a fly ash $100\%$ recycled binder.

## Methods

- **Geopolymer matrix and EGC mix design (Section 2.1 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Precursor: low-calcium Class F fly ash (FA, specific gravity 2.20).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/FA = 0.36).
  - Fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$, 1.2% oil coating).
  - Two activator combinations (solid/liquid ratio held constant):
    1. `EGC-Na`: D-Grade sodium silicate ($\text{SiO}_2/\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 2.0$) + 8.0M $\text{NaOH}$ solution.
    2. `EGC-K`: KASIL 2236 potassium silicate ($\text{SiO}_2/\text{K}_2\text{O} = 1.6$) + 8.0M $\text{KOH}$ solution.
- **Curing and test procedures (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Curing: 24 h ambient rest after casting $\rightarrow$ $60\ ^\circ\text{C}$ oven curing for 24 h $\rightarrow$ storage at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ room temperature.
  - Uniaxial direct tension: dumbbell-type specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, 0.5 mm/min displacement control).
  - Compressive strength: $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens (28-day age).
  - Flowability: mini slump cone flow test.

### 2. Interpretation of potassium cation polymerization and fiber-matrix interfacial interaction
- **Uniform network formation through low viscosity**: potassium silicate solution has an appropriate polymerization degree and lower viscosity than its sodium counterpart, minimizing fiber damage during mixing while perfectly inducing three-dimensional random orientation.
- **Maintaining appropriate interfacial shear strength**: the potassium geopolymer matrix shows chemical affinity compatible with the 1.2% oil coating layer on the PVA fiber surface, preventing premature fiber fracture from excessive chemical bonding and inducing continuous frictional pull-out, thereby exhibiting the large elongation of $4.30\%$.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original paper that demonstrated that in cement-free fly ash EGC the potassium-based activator improves fresh workability by $59\%$ over the sodium-based counterpart and raises tensile elongation to $4.30\%$, establishing the activator optimization benchmark for highly ductile eco-friendly geopolymer composite design.
