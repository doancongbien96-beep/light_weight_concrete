# A Decade of ECC Research Distilled and a Review of Its Real-Structure Applications: Micromechanics-Based Pseudo-Strain-Hardening (PSH) Design Principles, Tensile Ductility ($>3\%$), and the Immortal Textbook of the Bridge Link Slab

## Summary

This is the Journal of Advanced Concrete Technology (JACT) 2003 immortal, supreme landmark classic review by **Prof. Victor C. Li of the University of Michigan, USA**, which comprehensively consolidates **the past decade of materials design theory, physical properties, and worldwide real-structure application achievements of Engineered Cementitious Composites (ECC)** — invented in the early 1990s on the basis of micromechanics theory to overcome the limits of brittle cementitious materials and realize metal-like ductile tensile behavior. It systematically organizes the successful practical deployment of **the theoretical foundations of the two micromechanical PSH steady-state cracking and strength criteria ($J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 1.0, \sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} \ge 1.0$); the mechanism behind uniaxial direct-tension strain capacity of $3\sim 5\%$ and saturated multiple fine cracking with crack widths at or below $0.06\ \text{mm}$ under low fiber volume fractions of 2.0 vol.% or less; self-compacting casting/extrusion/spray processing technologies; bridge jointless link slabs; dam repair; and super-high-rise seismic vibration-control dampers (Damping Elements)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Consolidation of the core philosophy of ECC materials design and the micromechanics framework**:
   - It establishes a systematic design methodology that mechanically interlinks the physical parameters of fiber, matrix, and the fiber-matrix interface ($\tau_0, G_d$), excluding random trial-and-error fiber mixing attempts.
2. **Formulation of the energy and strength design criteria for achieving saturated pseudo-strain-hardening (PSH)**:
   - It formalizes the complementary energy condition ($J_b' \ge J_{tip}$) that guarantees steady-state flat-crack propagation and the stress condition ($\sigma_0 \ge \sigma_{fc}$) that guarantees sequential cracking activation across the cross-section.
3. **Completion of the technology for suppressing fiber rupture via PVA fiber surface oiling coating ($1.2\%$)**:
   - It resolved the early brittleness problem in which fibers ruptured due to strong chemical bonding by inducing interfacial slip, raising the strain capacity from $0.5\% \rightarrow 4.0\%$.
4. **Comprehensive presentation of global infrastructure real-structure landmark projects**:
   - It includes demonstration cases of the jointless link slab of the Mihara Bridge in Japan, the surface repair of the Yubari Dam in Hokkaido, and the vibration-control coupling beams of a Tokyo super-high-rise building.

## Methods

- **Micromechanics design equation system (Sections 2~3 & Figs. 1~6)**:
  - Single-fiber bridging stress-crack opening displacement: $\sigma(\delta) = \frac{4 V_f}{\pi d_f^2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \int_0^{L_f/2} P(\delta, z, \theta) p(\theta) p(z) \, dz \, d\theta$.
  - Complementary energy: $J_b' = \sigma_0 \delta_0 - \int_0^{\delta_0} \sigma(\delta) \, d\delta \ge J_{tip} = K_m^2 / E_m$.
  - Stress margin: $\sigma_0 > \sigma_{fc}$.
- **Evaluation of material properties and processability (Sections 4~6 & Tables 1~3 & Figs. 7~12)**:
  - Standard M45 PVA-ECC mix: cement + fly ash (FA/C = 1.2, W/B = 0.27, 2.0 vol.% Kuraray REC15 PVA fiber).
  - Processing methods: self-compacting casting, shotcrete spray (Spray-ECC), vacuum-assisted extrusion.

### 2. Innovation in Structural Serviceability and Analysis of Smart Self-Healing
- **Root-cause solution to bridge expansion-joint leakage**: applying ECC link slabs to bridge deck connections removes the expansion joints, permanently preventing chloride-induced corrosion of the piers below.
- **Permanently watertight self-healing**: because the crack width is strictly limited to $60\ \mu\text{m}$ or less, contact with moisture achieves $100\%$ crack healing within 14 days through calcium carbonate precipitation.
