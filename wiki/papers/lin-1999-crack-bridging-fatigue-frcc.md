## Summary

A micromechanics theory-and-experiment paper that precisely elucidates the crack-bridging stress decay mechanism of short-fiber-reinforced cementitious composites (FRCC and ECC) under tensile cyclic/fatigue loading. The behavior in which the bridging capability ($\sigma-\delta$ envelope) progressively degrades with the number of repetitions ($N$) — driven by frictional wear at the fiber-matrix interface and fatigue rupture of the fibers themselves during repeated pull-out and sliding — is formalized through a cyclic interfacial shear model. A theoretical model is established that predicts the fatigue limit stress at which the steady-state cracking condition is still maintained under repeated loading.

## Key Contributions

- **Introduction of an interfacial friction coefficient decay function over fatigue cycles**: establishes a wear-degradation law of the form $\tau_0(N) = \tau_0(1) (1 - \alpha \ln N)$ to derive the fatigue bridging curve.
- **Formulation of a fiber shear fatigue rupture criterion under repeated loading**: models the degradation of effective fiber strength caused by repeated bending fatigue of inclined fibers with angle $\theta$.
- **Prediction of the tensile fatigue endurance limit of ECC**: derives the maximum fatigue load ratio ($S_{max} \approx 0.60 \sim 0.70$) that can sustain multiple fine cracking even under $10^6$ fatigue cycles.

## Methods

- Single-fiber bidirectional cyclic pullout test.
- Micromechanics-based fatigue damage integral analysis and direct tensile fatigue testing (stress ratio $R = 0.1$, frequency $2 \sim 5 Hz$).
- Mixes: PVA-ECC and PE-ECC composites.

## Results

- Owing to wear of hydrophilic hydration products, PVA fibers show about a 30 % reduction in interfacial frictional stress after $10^4$ cycles; a fatigue life of $10^6$ cycles is achieved when the fatigue load is 65 % or less of the static tensile strength.
- PE fibers, thanks to their excellent wear resistance, are demonstrated to maintain stable bridging performance even at fatigue load ratio $S = 0.75$.
