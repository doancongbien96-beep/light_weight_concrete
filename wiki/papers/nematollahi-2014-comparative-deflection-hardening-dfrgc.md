# Comparative Deflection-Hardening Behavior of Short Fiber-Reinforced Geopolymer Composites

## Summary

A study (Nematollahi group, Swinburne University, 2014) comparing the matrix fracture properties and flexural deflection-hardening behavior of four ductile fiber-reinforced geopolymer composites (DFRGCs) — two Na-based, one K-based, and one Ca-based solid activator — each reinforced with 2 vol % PVA fiber in a Class F fly-ash-based geopolymer, against a cement-based DFRCC control at an identical water-to-binder ratio. The activator type directly governs matrix and composite properties, with the NaOH 8 M + sodium silicate combination (DFRGC-Na-1) showing the highest strength and deflection capacity. The core finding is a paradoxical result: geopolymers are favored for deflection-hardening precisely because of their lower matrix fracture toughness relative to cement systems. In-body bibliographic note: CBM 70, 54-64, received April 12, 2014.

## Key Contributions

- The activator combination is the property set: comparison of four systems — Na-1 (8 M NaOH + D-grade Na₂SiO₃ solution, mass ratio 2.5, Ms 2.0), Na-2 (8 M NaOH alone), K (8 M KOH + KASIL 2236, Ms 2.23), and Ca (Ca(OH)₂ + GD-grade Na₂SiO₃ powder, mass ratio 7.5) — spanning compressive strength from 63.7 MPa (Na-1) down to 13.6 MPa (Ca), a 4.7× gap. Na-1 is optimal across cost, strength, and flexure alike.
- Geopolymer vs cement flexural showdown: DFRGC-Na-1 shows lower first-crack strength fLOP than the control DFRCC (3.95 vs 5.37 MPa) yet higher modulus of rupture MOR (11.5 vs 9.92 MPa) and 2.6× the deflection capacity dMOR (39.7 vs 15.5 mm) — the lower matrix fracture toughness (Km 0.436 vs 0.590 MPa√m) promoted multiple cracking.
- Demonstration of 'just add water' with a Ca-based solid activator: both the handling of corrosive activator solutions and 60 °C heat curing can be eliminated (ambient curing gave 28-day compressive strength equivalent to heat curing) — the springboard toward the 2017 one-part SHGC development (nematollahi-2017). Caveat: strength of only 13.6 MPa remains the improvement task.
- Quantitative deflection-hardening frame: Kim et al.'s LOP criterion + MOR/fLOP ratio + ductility index (dMOR/dLOP) + ASTM C1018 toughness indices I5·I10·I20 and residual strength factors R5,10·R10,20 applied to geopolymers. Every mix exceeded I20>20 and R>100, confirming deflection hardening beyond elastic-perfectly-plastic behavior.
- Crack control: Na-1 showed a uniform crack distribution densely packed with fine cracks (tight crack-width control); K, Na-2, and Ca showed few widely spaced cracks. The activator governs even the crack pattern.

## Methods

- Mix: fly ash 100 % (Gladstone Class F, SiO₂ 51.11 % + Al₂O₃ 25.56 % + Fe₂O₃ 12.48 %), activator solution/fly ash 0.35, W/GP solids 0.20 fixed, PCE high-range water reducer 1 % (0.01 for the Ca system), PVA fiber (Kuraray RECS15, diameter 40 µm, length 8 mm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, elongation 6 %) at 2 vol %. The control DFRCC used W/C 0.35.
- Curing: DFRGCs were sealed then heat-cured at 60 °C for 24 h and tested at 3 days (strength development after heat curing negligible, so interpreted as OPC 28-day equivalent). DFRCC was cured 24 h at room temperature followed by 28 days in water.
- Tests: mini-slump (relative slump Cp=(d/d₀)²−1), compression per ASTM C109 (50 mm cubes), three-point bending notched beams 60×60×280 mm (effective crack model ECM for Em·Km·Jtip extraction, displacement rate 0.18 mm/min), four-point bend panels 400×75×10 mm (span 300 mm, 0.5 mm/min).

## Results

- Matrix fracture properties: Em 8.5 (Na-1)/5.7 (Na-2)/5.2 (K)/1.8 (Ca)/11.3 GPa (DFRCC); Km 0.436/0.312/0.237/0.086/0.590 MPa√m; Jtip 0.0224/0.0171/0.0108/0.0041/0.0308 kJ/m².
- Flexure (Table 6): fLOP 3.95±0.744 (Na-1)/3.79/3.28/2.31/5.37 MPa (DFRCC); MOR 11.5±1.25/10.3/5.47/4.92/9.92 MPa; dLOP 1.11/0.650/1.22/0.770/0.523 mm; dMOR 39.7±6.70/16.8/23.6/30.1/15.5 mm. The MOR/fLOP ratio was highest for Na-1 (other mixes 6.9~41.4 % lower).
- Toughness indices and residual strength factors: I5·I10·I20 for all mixes exceeded the elastic-perfectly-plastic reference values (5/10/20). R5,10 159.5 (Na-1)/186.2 (Na-2)/133.5/132.8/121.5 (DFRCC); R10,20 174.8/221.8/145.1/141.2/143.6 — every geopolymer mix outperformed the DFRCC in inelastic energy absorption.
- Density: composites 1779.2 (Ca)~1828.9 kg/m³ (K); DFRCC was 11.6 % higher than Na-1. Fiber-induced damage (increased porosity) makes composite density lower than that of the matrix.
- Ca-based activator trade-off: workability +135 % and ductility index +9.2 % (vs Na-1) but compressive strength −78.6 % — improving solid-activator practicality alongside strength is stated as the explicit follow-up task.
