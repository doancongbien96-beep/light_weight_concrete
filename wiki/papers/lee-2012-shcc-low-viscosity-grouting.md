## Summary

This study evaluated the rheological mix design and direct tensile performance of a low-viscosity, highly flowable strain-hardening cementitious composite (low-viscosity SHCC) applicable to crack repair, seismic anchor embedment, and gap grouting of deteriorated structures. Combining a high volume of fly ash (FA/C = 2.5~4.0) with an optimally dosed polycarboxylate superplasticizer lowered plastic viscosity below $0.45 Pa\cdot s$, securing pressureless self-leveling injectability while achieving excellent strain-hardening multiple fine cracking behavior, with a 28 d direct tensile strength of 4.5 MPa and a tensile strain capacity of 3.2 % through PVA fiber incorporation.

## Key Contributions

- **Establishment of a low-viscosity SHCC mix design window for grouting applications**: the ball-bearing effect of spherical fly ash particles secured injectability into micro gaps (< 1.0 mm) without fiber clumping.
- **Demonstrated retention of tensile ductility after grout injection**: a tensile strain capacity of at least 3.0 % was maintained even after injection into narrow gaps.
- **Overcoming the debonding failure limits of conventional brittle epoxy or shrinkage-compensating mortar grouts**: dissipation of interfacial stress concentration through ductile fiber bridging.

## Methods

- **Material proportions**: cement + Class F fly ash (replacement ratios 60 %, 70 %, 80 %), PVA fibers ($V_f = 1.5 \%, 2.0 \%$, REC15), PCE superplasticizer.
- **Rheology and constructability evaluation**: V-funnel flow time, narrow-gap injection test, slump flow.
- **Mechanical tests**: uniaxial dog-bone direct tension test, 28 d compressive strength.

## Results

- Optimal mix (FA 70 %, $V_f = 1.75 \%$): V-funnel flow time 8.2 s, slump flow 650 mm, 28 d compressive strength 38.5 MPa, tensile strength 4.52 MPa, and tensile strain capacity 3.45 %.
- Complete filling of fine cracks was confirmed, restoring structural integrity.
