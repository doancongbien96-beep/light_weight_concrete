## Summary
This study, conducted by the research team of Professor Kequan Yu (K.Q. Yu) at Tongji University, demonstrated the microstructural densification and uniaxial tensile behavior that maintain ultra-high strength-ultra-high ductility while upcycling recycled concrete powder (RCP, $d_{50} = 15 \mu m$) as a cement replacement binder (0 ~ 40 wt %) to substantially reduce the manufacturing unit cost and carbon emissions of ultra-high performance strain-hardening composites (UHP-ECC). At an RCP replacement level of 20 wt %, the composite achieved a 28 d compressive strength of **118.5 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **12.5 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **7.8 %**, while reducing embodied carbon by **35 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of a recycling mechanism for recycled concrete powder (RCP) in ultra-high-strength composites**: demonstration of the secondary pozzolanic reaction of unhydrated cement particles and the filling effect.
- **Optimization of matrix fracture toughness**: RCP addition relaxed $K_m$ to $0.38 MPa\sqrt{m}$, achieving a PSH energy index of $J_b'/J_{tip} = 8.5$.
- **Preservation of a tensile strain capacity of 7.8 %**: induction of stable slip pull-out bridging by PE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$).
- **Realization of high-value resource recovery of construction waste**: laying the groundwork for commercialization of circular-economy UHP-ECC.

## Methods
- Binder: low-heat cement + silica fume (20 %) + RCP (0, 10, 20, 30, 40 wt %), $W/B = 0.18$.
- Fibers: UHMWPE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm).
- Tests: dog-bone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strains), 28 d compressive strength, XRD, and MIP porosity.
