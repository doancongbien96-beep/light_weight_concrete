# Experimental Analysis of the Shear and Flexural Behavior of High-Performance Self-Compacting Concrete (HPSCC, $f_c > 100\ \text{MPa}$) Reinforced Concrete Beams: Steel-Fiber-Bridged Ultimate Shear Capacity of $440\ \text{kN}$ ($45\%$ Amplification), Shear Span-to-Depth Ratio ($a/d$), and Ductile Failure Mechanisms Without Shear Reinforcement

## Summary

To address the vulnerable risk of brittle diagonal shear failure (Brittle Diagonal Shear Failure) faced by ultra-high-strength concrete members ($f_c > 100\ \text{MPa}$) and the degraded constructability caused by congested reinforcement detailing, **high-performance self-compacting concrete was developed (HPSCC: 28-day compressive strength $105.0\ \text{MPa}$, slump flow $720\ \text{mm}$), in which a highly flowable high-strength matrix excluding coarse aggregates is reinforced with hooked-end micro steel fibers ($V_f = 1.0, 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 35\ \text{mm}, \sigma_f = 1100\ \text{MPa}$)**, and four-point flexure-shear loading tests were performed according to the shear span-to-depth ratio ($a/d = 1.5, 2.0$) and the longitudinal reinforcement ratio ($\rho = 1.13\%, 2.01\%$) of reinforced concrete beams, demonstrating **that with $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ steel fiber addition an ultimate shear load of $440\ \text{kN}$ is achieved even without shear reinforcement ($45\%$ amplification vs. the plain mix), the brittle shear failure mode is converted into a complete flexurally ductile hinge failure, and diagonal shear crack widths are strictly restrained below $0.15\ \text{mm}$** — the highest-authority 2019 landmark original paper in Elsevier Structures by the research team of **Prof. Ehsan Nikbakht and Dr. Amin Al-Fakih of Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS (UTP), Malaysia**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstration of complete overcoming of the shear brittleness of HPSCC beams**:
   - The abrupt brittle shear fracture arising in the $105\ \text{MPa}$ ultra-high-strength matrix is prevented by the three-dimensional diagonal tensile bridging force of steel fibers, inducing a gradual ductile flexural failure.
2. **Securing an ultimate shear capacity ($440\ \text{kN}$) capable of completely replacing stirrups (shear reinforcement)**:
   - At a shear span-to-depth ratio of $a/d = 1.5$, the member reinforced with $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ steel fibers amplifies its shear capacity by a dramatic $45\%$ compared with the plain control ($305\ \text{kN}$).
3. **Derivation of the combined mechanical correlation of the shear span-to-depth ratio ($a/d$) and the longitudinal reinforcement ratio ($\rho$)**:
   - Even in the combined flexure-shear region of $a/d = 2.0$, the deflection ductility index ($\mu = \Delta_u/\Delta_y$) is improved from $1.8 \rightarrow 4.2$, a 2.3-fold enhancement.
4. **Proposal of a shear-reinforcement-free slender design standard for global high-performance building and bridge members**:
   - Completes an engineering solution that maximizes constructability in congested reinforcement zones (beam-column joints, shear walls).

## Methods

- **HPSCC mix and material properties (Section 2 & Tables 1~4 & Figs. 1~3)**:
  - Binder: ordinary Portland cement + high-purity silica fume (15 wt.%, $W/B = 0.22$).
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand ($d \le 2.0\ \text{mm}$, coarse aggregate excluded).
  - Reinforcing fibers: hooked-end steel fibers ($V_f = 0, 1.0, 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 35\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.55\ \text{mm}$).
  - Flowability: slump flow $720 \pm 20\ \text{mm}$, $T_{500} = 3.5\ \text{s}$.
- **Structural test program (Sections 2.2~3.4 & Figs. 4~8)**:
  - Specimen dimensions: cross-section $150 \times 200\ \text{mm}$, total length $1500\ \text{mm}$, span $1200\ \text{mm}$.
  - Variables: fiber volume fraction (0%, 1%, 2%), $a/d$ ratio (1.5, 2.0), longitudinal reinforcement ratio ($\rho = 1.13\%, 2.01\%$, no shear stirrups).

### 2. Interpretation of diagonal crack bridging and arch action
- **Multiple fine dispersion of diagonal tension cracks**: $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$ steel fibers densely bridge the shear crack planes, blocking the opening of a single shear crack and dispersing the shear into multiple fine diagonal cracks.
- **Preservation of the concrete compression zone**: The lateral confinement of the steel fibers prevents premature crushing of the upper compression-flange concrete, allowing the section to mobilize $100\%$ of its plastic flexural moment.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a top-level landmark original paper in the fields of high-performance structural concrete and shear mechanics engineering, in which Prof. Ehsan Nikbakht's research team amplified the shear capacity to 440 kN, a 45% increase, even under no-shear-reinforcement conditions through 2.0 vol.% steel fiber reinforcement of HPSCC beams, and converted brittle shear failure into complete ductile flexural failure.
