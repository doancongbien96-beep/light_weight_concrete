# Sectional repair of severely corroded RC structures with a 150 MPa-class high-strength SHCC

## Summary

To replace the massive demolition and rebar lap-splicing that repairing flexurally corroded RC
members (25–30 % rebar area loss from chloride attack and carbonation) otherwise demands,
Prof. Christopher Leung's team (HKUST) developed a high-strength SHCC — M5 mix, W/B 0.16, PE
fiber 2.0 vol.% — with **150 MPa** 14-day compressive strength, **10.8 MPa** uniaxial direct
tensile strength, and **2.4 %** tensile strain capacity. Direct rebar pullout tests and
full-scale four-point bending of RC beams validated the efficiency of capacity and yield-load
recovery against minimum bond length ($4.0D\sim 5.5D$). The beam with 25 % bar loss (REF-R)
fell to just 72 % of the sound beam's (REF-F) yield load, yet the B55 beam — SHCC patch cast
with only $5.5D$ (110 mm) bond length — recovered yield load to **96 %** and ultimate flexural
load to **102 % (98.5 kN)**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of an ultra-high-tensile-strength (10.8 MPa) strain-hardening SHCC mix (M5)**:
   W/B 0.16 ultra-low ratio with silica fume 20 % substitution; optimally dispersed
   high-strength PE short fibers (diameter 20 µm, length 12 mm, strength 3000 MPa,
   2.0 vol.%) deliver 10.8 MPa tension, 2.4 % elongation, and 150 MPa compression in 14 days.
2. **Proven ultra-short bond transfer length between corroded rebar and SHCC ($4.0D\sim
   5.5D$)**: the demolition depth of $30D\sim 40D$ (600–800 mm) required by conventional lap
   splicing is cut to $4.0D$ (80 mm) – $5.5D$ (110 mm) while still transferring full rebar
   yield stress into the SHCC patch without bond failure.
3. **Complete restoration of flexural capacity and ductility demonstrated**: on Y20 bars with
   25 % nominal section loss (30 % yield-load loss), the B55 patch raised yield load from
   66.3 kN to **88.5 kN (96 % recovery)** and ultimate load from 85.5 kN to **98.5 kN (102 %
   — beyond-full recovery)**.
4. **Durability shielding through interfacial fine multiple-crack dispersion**: harmful cracks
   of the substrate disperse into numerous fine cracks within the SHCC patch — a dual
   durability shield blocking future moisture and chloride re-ingress at its source.

## Methods

- **High-strength SHCC mix design (mass ratios, Table 1)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement 0.80, silica fume 0.20, W/B 0.16 (M5 mix).
  - Silica sand ($d < 1.4\ \text{mm}$) 0.50, polycarboxylate SP 0.024.
  - Fibers: high-strength PE (diameter 20 µm, length 12 mm, tensile strength 3000 MPa,
    E 100 GPa) at **2.0 vol.%**.
- **Corroded-rebar uniaxial pullout specimens**:
  - Y20 bars (nominal 20 mm, $A_s = 314\ \text{mm}^2$) machined over a central 50 mm to
    17.3 mm diameter ($A_s = 235\ \text{mm}^2$; 25 % section loss).
  - Bond lengths $4.0D = 80\ \text{mm}$, $5.5D = 110\ \text{mm}$, $7.0D = 140\ \text{mm}$;
    SHCC patches cast, then direct-tension pullout.
- **Full-scale four-point bending (6 beams, Table 5)**:
  - Beam: overall 2300 mm, clear span 2000 mm, section $150\ \text{mm} \times 250\ \text{mm}$,
    pure-moment zone 500 mm.
  - Specimens:
    - **REF-F**: undamaged sound rebar control.
    - **REF-R**: 25 %-loss rebar, unrepaired.
    - **B40 (2)**: 25 % loss + $4.0D$ SHCC patch.
    - **B55 (2)**: 25 % loss + $5.5D$ SHCC patch.
  - Loading: 500 kN machine, displacement control (1.2 mm/min → 3.0 mm/min after yield);
    LVDTs for midspan deflection and curvature in the pure-moment zone.

## Results

### 1. High-strength SHCC mechanical performance (14-day, Table 3)

| Mix | W/B | Sand ratio (S/B) | 14 d compression (MPa) | 14 d max tension (MPa) | Strain capacity (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | 0.18 | 0.50 | 148 | 8.4 | 4.6 % |
| **M2** | 0.18 | 0.80 | 154 | 9.0 | 1.8 % |
| **M3** | 0.18 | 0.50 | 147 | 9.1 | 1.8 % |
| **M4** | 0.17 | 0.50 | 138 | 9.5 | 1.2 % |
| **M5 (adopted)** | **0.16** | **0.50** | **150** | **10.8** | **2.4 %** |
| **M5 (field-cast repair)** | 0.16 | 0.50 | 132 | 9.8 | 1.6 % |

### 2. Four-point bending results and recovery rates (Table 7)

| Beam | Rebar condition and repair | Yield load (kN) | Yield moment ($\text{kN}\cdot\text{m}$) | Yield recovery | Ultimate load (kN) | Ultimate recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **REF-F** | sound undamaged | 92.6 | 34.7 | 100 % (baseline) | 96.8 | 100 % (baseline) |
| **REF-R** | **25 % loss, unrepaired** | **66.3** | **24.9** | **72 % (collapsed)** | **85.5** | **88 %** |
| **B40-1** | 25 % loss + $4.0D$ patch | 84.0 | 31.5 | 91 % | 91.7 | 95 % |
| **B40-2** | 25 % loss + $4.0D$ patch | 85.8 | 32.2 | 93 % | 97.1 | 100 % |
| **B55-1** | **25 % loss + $5.5D$ patch** | **87.0** | **32.6** | **94 %** | **98.0** | **101 % (fully recovered)** |
| **B55-2** | **25 % loss + $5.5D$ patch** | **88.5** | **33.2** | **96 %** | **98.5** | **102 % (fully recovered)** |

### 3. Repair mechanism and failure behavior
- **B55**: no interface splitting cracks over the $5.5D$ (110 mm) bond zone; uniform fine
  multiple cracking formed across the whole patch, maintaining perfect composite action until
  top-concrete crushing.
- **B40**: even $4.0D$ (80 mm) recovered 95–100 %, but eccentric tensile stress produced fine
  splitting cracks at the top of the patch; with safety margin considered, ≥ $5.5D$ is proposed
  as optimal bond length.

- **Overall conclusion**: partial sectional replacement using a ~10 MPa-tensile SHCC is the
  optimal repair technique for rapidly and economically restoring the flexural capacity of
  aging infrastructure with severe rebar loss to above its original design level.
