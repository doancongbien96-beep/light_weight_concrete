## Summary
This study evaluated the mechanical properties and workability of high-strength concrete in which an abundant, economical natural pozzolan (volcanic ash) and a highly reactive silica fume were jointly incorporated as a ternary binder system. The delayed early-age strength development encountered when natural pozzolan is used alone was overcome through micro-filling and pozzolanic-reaction synergy with ultra-fine silica fume (15 wt %), economically producing high-strength composites at $W/B = 0.28 \sim 0.35$ with 28 d compressive strength of $65 \sim 85 MPa$, flexural strength of $9.5 \sim 12.0 MPa$, and elastic modulus of $40 GPa$ or more.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the natural pozzolan–silica fume ternary binder synergy**: combining 15 wt % silica fume with a 15 ~ 30 wt % natural pozzolan replacement increased 28 d strength by more than 20 % relative to plain OPC.
- **Interpretation of early- and long-term strength development mechanisms**: demonstrated the mutual complementarity between the early-age nucleation/reactivity of silica fume and the delayed, long-term pozzolanic reaction of natural pozzolan.
- **Derivation of an economical high-strength matrix mix proportion**: established an optimal mix design capable of substituting for the costly use of silica fume alone at high dosage.

## Methods
- **Materials**: OPC, Jordanian volcanic natural pozzolan (fineness $380 m2/kg$), silica fume ($20000 m2/kg$).
- **Mix proportions**: $W/B = 0.35, 0.31, 0.28$, natural pozzolan replacement ratio (0 ~ 30 %), silica fume replacement ratio (0 ~ 15 %).
- **Test items**: compressive strength (3 d, 7 d, 28 d, 90 d), flexural modulus of rupture (MOR), splitting tensile strength, elastic modulus.

## Results
- Optimal mix (OPC 70 % + natural pozzolan 15 % + silica fume 15 %, $W/B = 0.28$): 28 d compressive strength $86.5 MPa$, 90 d compressive strength $94.2 MPa$, flexural strength $11.8 MPa$.
- Micro-pore filling by silica fume markedly improved the denseness of the aggregate–paste interfacial transition zone (ITZ).
