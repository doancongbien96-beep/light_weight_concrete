## Summary
This recent study incorporated colloidal nano-silica (Nano-Silica, $SiO_2$ particle diameter $15 \sim 30 nm$) at 0.5 ~ 3.0 wt % into fly ash–blast furnace slag-based geopolymer composites, dramatically improving early-age polymerization reaction promotion under ambient curing conditions, micropore densification, mechanical performance, and durability. Through XRD, FTIR, high-resolution SEM-EDS, and NMR analyses, it was revealed that nano-silica provides nucleation sites for forming high-density $Si-O-Si$ oligomers in the initial stage of aluminosilicate precursor dissolution, increasing 3 d compressive strength by more than 45 % and markedly lowering porosity.

## Key Contributions
- **Elucidation of the mechanism by which nano-silica accelerates geopolymer polymerization**: its high specific surface area ($> 200 m2/g$) and reactivity optimize the initial Si/Al reactivity, shortening the gelation time.
- **Drastic reduction of fine porosity and effective pore size**: MIP analysis demonstrated a 65 % reduction in the volume of harmful coarse capillary pores larger than $50 nm$.
- **Simultaneous improvement of compressive and flexural-tensile strength**: at the optimal 1.5 wt % nano-silica dosage, a 28 d compressive strength of $78.5 MPa$ and a flexural strength of $10.2 MPa$ were achieved.

## Methods
- **Mix proportioning**: Class F fly ash 50 % + GGBS 50 %, alkaline activator ($Na_2SiO_3 / NaOH = 2.0$), water-dispersed colloidal nano-silica (0.5 wt %, 1.0 wt %, 1.5 wt %, 2.0 wt %, 3.0 wt %).
- **Curing**: ambient air curing ($23 °C, 60 \% RH$).
- **Evaluation**: isothermal calorimetry heat-of-reaction measurement, compressive and flexural strength, rapid chloride migration coefficient (RCM), and carbon emission estimation.

## Results
- 1.5 wt % nano-silica mix: 3 d compressive strength of $48.2 MPa$ (46 % increase over the control), 28 d compressive strength of $78.5 MPa$ (28 % increase), and a sharply reduced chloride diffusion coefficient of $1.8 \times 10^{-12} m2/s$.
- Excessive dosage (3.0 wt %): local defect formation caused by nanoparticle agglomeration and reduced flowability were confirmed.
