# Heavy-Weight Floor Impact Sound in Reinforced Concrete Structures

## Summary

This study evaluates the low-frequency ($63 \sim 125\ \text{Hz}$) vibration and sound pressure transmission mechanisms of heavyweight floor impact sound (automobile tire drop impact from a bang machine) in multi-family reinforced concrete (RC) buildings through field measurements and laboratory scale models. The authors investigated the effects of slab thickness (150 ~ 210 mm), dynamic stiffness of resilient isolators, and wall-slab joint boundary conditions on acoustic isolation, proposing methods to suppress low-frequency room mode resonance.

## Key Contributions

- **Quantification of low-frequency ($63\ \text{Hz}$) sound pressure transmission**: Formulated the coupled acoustic resonance between the fundamental structural vibration mode of the slab and low-frequency room acoustic modes excited by the bang machine.
- **Impact sound reduction with increasing slab thickness**: Demonstrated that increasing slab thickness from 150 mm to 210 mm reduces $63\ \text{Hz}$ band heavyweight impact noise levels by $4 \sim 6\ \text{dB}$.
- **Dynamic property criteria for resilient floor interlayers**: Mapped impact isolation performance as a function of the dynamic stiffness ($\text{MN/m}^3$) of interlayer materials.

## Methods

- Full-scale residential field acoustic measurements using standardized bang machine excitation (KS F 2810-2).
- Measured $1/3$ octave-band sound pressure levels using frequency analyzers, accelerometers, and precision sound level meters (B&K).

## Results

- Combining a 210 mm slab with low-dynamic-stiffness resilient isolators achieved a heavyweight impact level of $L_{i,Fmax,AW} = 48\ \text{dB}$ (satisfying Grade 1 acoustic criteria).
- Confirmed that combining increased structural mass with viscoelastic damping interlayers is key to controlling low-frequency impact noise.
