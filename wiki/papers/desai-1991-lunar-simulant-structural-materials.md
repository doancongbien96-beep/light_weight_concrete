# Development and Mechanical Properties of Waterless Space Structural Materials via $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ Thermal Liquefaction of Lunar Simulant and Metal Fiber Reinforcement

## Summary

To drastically reduce earth-to-space transportation costs for lunar base and space habitat construction and achieve 100 % in-situ resource utilization (ISRU), Professor Chandra S. Desai and co-workers at the NASA Space Engineering Research Center (SERC), University of Arizona, manufactured waterless structural ceramic composites by thermal liquefaction and sintering of lunar regolith simulant (MLS-1) at $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ under water-free conditions, reinforced with steel fibers (15~30 wt.%, volume fraction 4.65~10.95 vol.%), stainless steel fibers (7.5~15 wt.%), and aluminum fibers (7.5~15 wt.%) to prevent brittle failure. The team systematically evaluated prismatic beam flexural behavior ($10\ \text{mm} \times 25\ \text{mm} \times 250\ \text{mm}$), composite density ($2.17\sim 2.61\ \text{g/cm}^3$), and vacuum triaxial compression behavior under simulated lunar space environments ($10^{-4}\ \text{Torr}$). The investigation established that the severe brittleness of pure fused lunar simulant was successfully mitigated through ductile metal fiber bridging and the development of residual compressive stresses (thermal prestressing) caused by thermal expansion mismatch during cooling, securing sufficient structural stability for space roadways, radiation shielding walls, foundations, and habitat modules.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of waterless $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ thermal liquefaction ceramic matrix processing**:
   - Synthesized a dense silicate ceramic matrix with a density of $2.44\ \text{g/cm}^3$ through a thermal cycle simulating solar concentrators and microwave heating (holding at $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 1 h followed by slow cooling over 8 h).
2. **Mitigation of brittleness and creation of tough ceramic composites via metal fiber reinforcement**:
   - Incorporated metal fibers reducible from lunar regolith (iron, titanium, aluminum) up to 11 % volume fraction, dramatically enhancing flexural tensile fracture toughness.
3. **Identification of self-prestressing mechanism via thermal expansion mismatch**:
   - Induced permanent residual compressive stresses in the ceramic matrix upon cooling due to the difference in coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) between metal fibers and matrix, increasing first-cracking strength.
4. **Development of a lunar high-vacuum triaxial compression testing device**:
   - Established a space geomechanics and structural testing apparatus capable of measuring friction angle, cohesion, and stress-strain responses of simulant and solidified composites under high vacuum ($10^{-4}\ \text{Torr}$) and confining stress states.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and simulant properties (Figure 1 & Table 2)**:
  - Lunar regolith simulant: MLS-1 (Minnesota basalt-based simulant, particle size $0.001\sim 1.0\ \text{mm}$, specific gravity 3.10).
  - Reinforcing fibers/particles (Table 2):
    - Carbon steel fibers: 15.0 wt.% ($V_f = 4.65\ \%$) and 30.0 wt.% ($V_f = 10.95\ \%$).
    - Stainless steel fibers: 7.5 wt.% ($V_f = 2.32\ \%$) and 15.0 wt.% ($V_f = 4.54\ \%$).
    - Aluminum fibers/shavings: 7.5 wt.%, 10.0 wt.%, and 15.0 wt.% ($V_f = 7.27\ \%$).
- **Specimen fabrication and thermal processing**:
  - Prismatic beams ($1.0\ \text{cm} \times 2.5\ \text{cm} \times 25\ \text{cm}$) and cylindrical specimens ($\phi 5.5\ \text{cm} \times 15\ \text{cm}$) cast in graphite and titanium molds.
  - Electric furnace heating: isothermal hold at $1100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 1 h, followed by controlled slow cooling to room temperature over 8 h.
- **Mechanical testing program**:
  - Prismatic beam three-point/four-point bending tests (MTS testing machine, flexural strength, and load-deflection curves).
  - Space vacuum triaxial testing (Vacuum Triaxial Rig, $10^{-4}\ \text{Torr}$ high-vacuum chamber, confining pressures $0\sim 500\ \text{kPa}$).

### 2. Structural mechanics implications for lunar base construction
- **Waterless solidification viability**: On the lunar surface where water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) required for cement hydration is extremely scarce, thermal liquefaction ceramics proved to be an effective route for manufacturing high-strength structures directly from local regolith without consuming water.
- **Multiaxial stress reliability**: Vacuum triaxial testing demonstrated that fiber-bridged composites maintain shear strength and suppress volumetric dilation under complex three-dimensional stress states caused by micrometeorite impacts and internal pressurization (1 atm).

- **Overall conclusion**: This work laid the foundational cornerstone for space In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) construction engineering by manufacturing waterless ceramic composite structural materials from lunar regolith and metal fibers via thermal liquefaction.
