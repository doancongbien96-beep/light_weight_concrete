# Single-Fiber Pullout Interface Mechanics of PVA Fibers ($G_d, \tau_0, \beta$) and Suppression of Premature Fiber Rupture via Oiling Coating ($1.2\%$): the Mechanism of Achieving ECC Ultra-High Ductility ($\varepsilon_u \ge 4.0\%$)

## Summary

To complete a practical strain-hardening cementitious composite (PVA-ECC) by replacing expensive PE fibers with economical polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) fibers, the research team **applied the single-fiber pullout test and micromechanics-based fracture criteria to precisely measure the chemical debonding energy ($G_d$), frictional shear stress ($\tau_0$), and slip-hardening coefficient ($\beta$) of the hydrophilic PVA fiber**, and — to solve the premature fiber rupture (Fiber Rupture) caused by the excessively hydrophilic hydrogen bonding of the PVA fiber surface — **developed and applied, for the first time, a silicone-based Oiling Agent surface-coating technology over $0.0\sim 1.2\ \text{wt.}\%$, optimizing $G_d$ from $4.5 \rightarrow 1.8\ \text{J/m}^2$ to suppress fiber rupture completely and realize stable pseudo-strain-hardening (PSH) at a uniaxial tensile strain of $\varepsilon_u \ge 4.0\%$**. This is an immortal landmark classic paper of 2001 in the ASCE Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering by **the endowed-chair research team of Prof. Victor C. Li of the University of Michigan and the research laboratory of Kuraray Co., Ltd., Japan**.

## Key Contributions

1. **World-first quantification of the micromechanical interface parameters ($G_d, \tau_0, \beta$) of the hydrophilic PVA fiber-cement matrix interface**:
   - Elucidated the chemical-bond fracture behavior governed by the difference in surface microtexture between the wet-gel-spun fiber (Fiber A) and the dry-spun fiber (Fiber B).
2. **Established the mechanism for preventing premature fiber rupture through Oiling Agent Coating surface modification**:
   - As the oiling content increases $0.0 \rightarrow 1.2\ \text{wt.}\%$, $G_d$ decreases by $60\%$, switching to the optimal bridging mode in which fibers pull out rather than break.
3. **Formulated the fracture-based debonding criterion for single-fiber pullout**:
   - Derived the theoretical expression $G_d = \frac{2(P_a - P_b)^2}{\pi^2 E_f d_f^3}$ from the pullout load drop ($P_a \rightarrow P_b$) to precisely evaluate the interfacial chemical energy.
4. **Built the key foundation for developing the world-standard PVA-ECC mix (M45)**:
   - Realized multiple fine cracking behavior with a uniaxial ultimate tensile strength of $4.5\ \text{MPa}$ and an ultimate tensile strain capacity of $4.5\%$ at a fiber volume fraction of $V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$.

## Methods

- **Materials and single-fiber specimen fabrication (Sections 2~3 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Fiber types: Kuraray PVA fibers (diameter $d_f = 14, 27, 45, 100\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$, elastic modulus $E_f = 40\ \text{GPa}$).
  - Surface treatment: organic silicone oiling agent applied (0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.2 wt.% of fiber).
  - Matrix: OPC + silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/C = 0.8, W/C = 0.45, HRWRA).
  - Embedment length: $L_e = 0.5\sim 3.0\ \text{mm}$.
- **Single-fiber pullout and composite uniaxial tension tests (Sections 3~4 & Figs. 2~5)**:
  - High-precision micro-pullout testing machine (displacement-controlled at $0.005\ \text{mm/s}$).
  - Uniaxial tension: dogbone specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$).

### 2. Hydrogen-Bond Blocking and PSH Strain-Hardening Micromechanics Interpretation
- **Suppression of excess interfacial chemical bonding**: forming an ultra-thin hydrophobic oiling film on the hydrophilic PVA fiber surface lowers the density of hydrogen bonds with cement hydrates (C-S-H) to an appropriate level, inducing interfacial debonding (Debonding) before the fiber tensile strength $\sigma_f$ is reached.
- **PSH energy criterion satisfied**: the reduction of $G_d$ maximizes the fiber-bridging complementary energy $J_b'$, securing a sufficient energy margin of $J_b'/J_{tip} \ge 3.0$ and thereby forming saturated multiple fine cracking.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is an immortal, highest-authority landmark origin paper in the history of construction composite materials, in which Prof. Victor C. Li and the Japanese Kuraray research team established the PVA fiber-cement matrix bond parameters and blocked premature fiber rupture at its source through a 1.2% oiling coating, completing a PVA-ECC with a 4.5% tensile strain capacity.
