# Dynamic Drop-Weight Impact Resistance and Fracture Behavior of Concrete Slabs Reinforced with Bidirectional Recycled Waste PET Bottle Grids

## Summary

To recycle post-consumer plastic beverage bottles (waste PET) as sustainable structural reinforcement and eliminate brittle shattering in concrete elements, Professors Dora Foti and Francesco Paparella at the Polytechnic of Bari developed bidirectional woven grids (PET Grids: tensile strength 85 MPa, elongation at break 50 %, elastic modulus 3.1 GPa) mechanically cut from collected PET bottles. Embedding the grids into the mid-plane of $500\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ concrete slabs supported at four corners, the authors conducted dynamic drop-weight impact tests by releasing a 290 N (29.6 kg) cylindrical steel impactor from 1.0 m (single impact energy 290 J) while capturing 4-channel dynamic strain histories at 2400 Hz. The unreinforced control slab shattered catastrophically into 4 separated pieces under a single 290 J impact via cross-shaped brittle splitting, whereas the **waste PET grid-reinforced slab utilized large tensile plastic elongation (50 %) to distribute impact energy, preventing through-punching and fragmentation (100 % containment)** and maintaining complete post-impact structural integrity.

## Key Contributions

1. **Development of recycled bidirectional woven grids from post-consumer PET bottles**:
   - Fabricated ductile 2D PET grids via mechanical slitting and weaving without chemical reprocessing, deploying them as structural tensile reinforcement in concrete.
2. **Elimination of brittle shattering under dynamic drop-weight impact (290 J)**:
   - While plain slabs disintegrated into four separate fragments upon a single blow, PET grid-reinforced slabs held the fractured concrete matrix intact, transitioning failure to ductile non-shattering energy absorption.
3. **Elucidation of stress wave attenuation via 2400 Hz high-speed dynamic strain analysis**:
   - Dynamic strain gages positioned symmetrically on top and bottom slab surfaces demonstrated that the PET grid attenuated peak dynamic tensile stress by over 45 % and broadened load pulse duration.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and concrete mixture proportions (Tables 1–2)**:
  - Binders: CEM II/A-LL 42.5 R cement $300\ \text{kg/m}^3$ (W/C = 0.50).
  - Aggregates: Sand 78 % ($0\sim 4\ \text{mm}$), gravel 22 % ($7\sim 15\ \text{mm}$), 28 d compressive strength 32.5 MPa.
  - PET grid reinforcement properties (Table 1):
    - Slit PET bottle strips (width $5.0\ \text{mm}$, thickness $0.35\ \text{mm}$).
    - Tensile strength 85 MPa, ultimate elongation 50 %, elastic modulus 3100 MPa.
    - Bidirectional orthogonal grid mesh ($25\ \text{mm} \times 25\ \text{mm}$ grid spacing).
- **Specimens and impact apparatus (Figures 1–2)**:
  - Slab dimensions: $500\ \text{mm} \times 500\ \text{mm} \times 50\ \text{mm}$ (square plates).
  - Boundary conditions: 4-corner point supports (span $400\ \text{mm} \times 400\ \text{mm}$).
  - Impact equipment:
    - Impactor: cylindrical steel anvil (diameter 140 mm, height 240 mm) + 290 N (29.6 kg) steel drop weight.
    - Drop height: $H = 1.0\ \text{m}$ ($E_{imp} = mgh = 290\ \text{J}$, impact velocity $v = 4.43\ \text{m/s}$).
  - Data acquisition: HBM MGCplus system (2400 Hz sampling), 4-channel 50 mm dynamic strain gages.

## Results

### 1. Dynamic impact response and fracture modes of plain vs PET grid-reinforced slabs

| Specimen ID | Reinforcement configuration | Impact energy (J) | Failure mode after 1 drop | Crack pattern & fragment scattering | Peak dynamic tensile strain ($\mu\varepsilon$) | Post-impact integrity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Slab-Control-1** | Plain unreinforced | 290 J | **Catastrophic shattering** | Cross-shaped 4-part split, fragments scattered | $> 3500\ \mu\varepsilon$ (Gage failed) | **Total collapse (0 %)** |
| **Slab-Control-2** | Plain unreinforced | 290 J | **Catastrophic shattering** | Full-depth cross splitting | $> 3500\ \mu\varepsilon$ (Gage failed) | **Total collapse (0 %)** |
| **Slab-PET-1** | Recycled PET grid | 290 J | **Ductile flexural cracking** | Fine cross microcracking, **0 % fragment flight** | **1850 ± 120 µε (−47 %)** | **Fully retained (100 %)** |
| **Slab-PET-2** | Recycled PET grid | 290 J | **Ductile flexural cracking** | Yield hinge formation, **0 % fragment flight** | **1920 ± 130 µε (−45 %)** | **Fully retained (100 %)** |

### 2. Energy dissipation and environmental benefits
- **Influence of 50 % ultimate elongation in PET**: Unlike steel rebar or carbon fibers which are susceptible to brittle fracture or bond debonding under high strain rates, PET polymers absorb kinetic energy into plastic strain and thermal dissipation.
- **Waste valorization**: Transforming landfill-destined bottles into structural reinforcement lowers construction embodied carbon and promotes circular economy.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work demonstrated that bidirectional recycled PET grids eliminate brittle shattering in concrete slabs under dynamic impact, maintaining full post-impact integrity for eco-friendly impact mitigation structures.
