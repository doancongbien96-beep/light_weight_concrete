# Mechanism of $32.5\%$ Splitting Tensile Strength Enhancement and Dynamic Vibration Damping Characteristics of Concrete According to PVA Fiber Volume Fraction ($0.125\sim 0.50\ \text{vol.}\%$) and Length ($6\ \text{mm}, 12\ \text{mm}$)

## Summary

To suppress the vulnerable tensile cracking and brittle fracture of structural concrete and to clarify its dynamic vibration characteristics, this landmark 2013 Construction and Building Materials paper by the research team of **Bijan Samali Endowed Chair Professor and Dr. Amin Noushini at the University of Technology Sydney (UTS), Australia** systematically designed **PVA short-fiber-reinforced concrete (PVA-FRC) with two fiber lengths ($6\ \text{mm}, 12\ \text{mm}$) and four volume fraction levels ($V_f = 0.125\%, 0.25\%, 0.375\%, 0.50\ \text{vol.}\%$)**, and precisely evaluated the 28-day compressive strength, cylinder splitting tensile strength, four-point flexural modulus of rupture (MOR), and static elastic modulus (MOE), together with the **natural frequencies from impact-hammer modal testing, dynamic elastic modulus ($E_d$), and structural damping ratios ($\xi$) per longitudinal and transverse vibration mode**, demonstrating that **the $6\ \text{mm}$ PVA fiber mix at 0.25 vol.% increases the splitting tensile strength by $32.5\%$ and the flexural strength by $21.5\%$ while clarifying the dynamic behavior in the small-amplitude elastic region**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Optimization of the static mechanical performance of concrete through low-volume-fraction ($\le 0.50\ \text{vol.}\%$) PVA fibers**:
   - Incorporation of $6\ \text{mm}$ micro PVA fibers at 0.25 vol.% dramatically improved the splitting tensile strength by $32.5\%$ and the flexural modulus of rupture (MOR) by $21.5\%$ through strong hydrogen-bond adhesion with cement hydration products.
2. **Quantification of the dynamic elastic modulus and damping ratio per vibration mode via impact-hammer modal analysis**:
   - Non-destructive impact vibration tests measured the transverse and longitudinal natural-frequency responses, precisely extracting a dynamic elastic modulus of ($E_d \approx 38\sim 42\ \text{GPa}$) and structural damping ratios of ($\xi = 0.55\sim 0.72\%$).
3. **Elucidation of the mechanical trade-off according to fiber length ($6\ \text{mm}$ vs. $12\ \text{mm}$) and dispersibility**:
   - It was demonstrated that short fibers ($6\ \text{mm}$), with their large number of fibers per unit volume, excel at constraining early microcracks, whereas long fibers ($12\ \text{mm}$) can induce micro voids at high volume fractions due to increased mixing viscosity.
4. **Clarification of the fiber damping limit in the small-amplitude elastic region and the post-fracture energy absorption mechanism**:
   - Analysis revealed that while the influence of low-volume-fraction PVA fibers on the damping ratio is limited in the uncracked elastic state, they provide substantial dissipated energy during frictional pull-out after crack initiation.

## Methods

- **Materials and concrete mixtures (Sections 2~3 & Tables 1~4)**:
  - Binder: Australian standard General Purpose (GP) cement (OPC).
  - Aggregates: $10\ \text{mm}$ and $20\ \text{mm}$ crushed coarse aggregate + natural sand (W/C = 0.50).
  - PVA fibers: two types of Kuraray K-II fiber:
    1. `6PVA`: $L_f = 6\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 14\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 41\ \text{GPa}$.
    2. `12PVA`: $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$, $E_f = 41\ \text{GPa}$.
  - Mix groups: reference plain concrete (`NC`), four $6\ \text{mm}$ fiber mixes (`6PVA-0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.50%`), four $12\ \text{mm}$ fiber mixes (`12PVA-0.125, 0.25, 0.375, 0.50%`).
- **Static and dynamic testing procedure (Sections 3.2~3.3 & Figs. 2~6)**:
  - Static mechanical tests: $\phi 100 \times 200\ \text{mm}$ compression and splitting tension, $100 \times 100 \times 400\ \text{mm}$ four-point bending tests (AS 1012 standard).
  - Dynamic modal test: PCB accelerometers attached to a $100 \times 100 \times 500\ \text{mm}$ prism $\rightarrow$ striking with a PCB impact hammer $\rightarrow$ acquisition of time-acceleration histories $\rightarrow$ derivation of the damping ratio ($\xi$) based on the Logarithmic Decrement and FFT natural frequencies.

### 2. Interpretation of the microfiber bridging and viscoelastic vibration dissipation mechanisms
- **Maximization of tensile resistance through hydrophilic hydrogen bonding**: The $-\text{OH}$ functional groups on the PVA fiber surface form strong chemical bonds with cement C-S-H hydration products, and the $6\ \text{mm}$ short fibers suppress microcrack opening at an early stage, amplifying the splitting tensile strength by $32.5\%$.
- **Separation of uncracked elastic behavior and post-fracture energy absorption**: In the small-amplitude vibration of the intact state, the elastic stiffness of the cement concrete matrix dominates so that the change in damping ratio remains gradual, but it was confirmed that upon crack occurrence the fiber frictional pull-out and viscoelastic characteristics are activated, greatly enhancing fracture toughness.

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper is a landmark original study that completely elucidated the static mechanical strength improvement and the dynamic vibration modal characteristics of PVA short-fiber-reinforced concrete according to fiber length and volume fraction, establishing quantitative criteria essential for tensile crack control and impact-resistant design of civil structures.
