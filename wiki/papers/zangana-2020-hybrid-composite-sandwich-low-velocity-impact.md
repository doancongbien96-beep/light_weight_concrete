## Summary
To maximize drop impact resistance and fracture energy absorption capacity for railway sleepers, marine structures, and protective sandwich panels, a research team led by Dr. W. Ferdous at the University of Southern Queensland (USQ) precisely characterized the low-velocity drop-weight impact behavior (impact energy 20 ~ 80 J), load-displacement histories, contact duration, and localized crushing damage mechanisms of a novel sandwich composite core ($100 \times 100 \times 40 mm$) fabricated by laminating basalt fiber mesh and glass fiber reinforced epoxy ribs (GFRP Ribs) in a three-dimensional hybrid configuration onto a high-density rigid polyurethane foam (Rigid PU Foam) core. Through the multilayer membrane tensile truss action of the hybrid core, it was demonstrated that the impact energy absorption rate was increased by **240 %** and the residual penetration depth reduced by **65 %** compared with a plain foam core.

## Key Contributions
- **Structural design of a 3D basalt fiber-rigid foam hybrid sandwich core**: combining the compressive buckling energy absorption of the foam with the in-plane tension distribution of the basalt fiber mesh.
- **Establishment of drop impact load-time history and contact time models**: peak impact load lowered by 45 %, substantially improving the impact cushioning index.
- **Demonstration of failure mode transition**: shift from shear core fragmentation and face sheet delamination to a multistage plastic energy dissipation mode.
- **Presentation of a design standard for railway and bridge cushioning panels**: establishment of a durable protective structural system against repeated impacts.

## Methods
- Sandwich panel: top and bottom GFRP woven face sheets (thickness 3 mm) + hybrid core (PU foam density $120 kg/m^3$, three embedded layers of basalt mesh, thickness 40 mm).
- Impact testing: Instron Dynatup 9250HV instrumented drop-weight impact tester (hemispherical steel tip diameter 16 mm, mass 5.5 kg, impact energies of 20, 40, 60, 80 J).
- Damage analysis: precise measurement of internal delamination area through ultrasonic C-scan and 3D X-ray CT tomography.
