# Comprehensive review of lunar economic resources and in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) technologies

## Summary

A review article written by Prof. Ian A. Crawford (Birkbeck, University of London) that comprehensively examines the economic value, extraction processes, paths to conversion into local construction materials (ISRU), and strategies for building a space-industrial ecosystem for the mineral, volatile, and energy resources hosted on the Moon — lunar regolith, polar water ice, oxygen, hydrogen, ilmenite, helium-3, and KREEP-hosted rare earth elements — in support of the terrestrial economy and space exploration.

## Key Contributions

1. **Classification and economic assessment of lunar resources**:
   - **Oxygen and water (polar ice)**: the highest-priority core resources for life support and local supply of hydrogen–oxygen rocket propellant.
   - **Construction-grade regolith**: abundant in silicon, iron, aluminum, calcium, and titanium, allowing direct use as locally sintered bodies, geopolymer concrete, and radiation-shielding structures.
   - **Mineral and metal resources**: an organized account of the endowment potential of high-titanium ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$), non-ferrous metals, rare earth elements (REE), and platinum-group elements.
2. **Hierarchy of applications**:
   - Stage 1: local material supply for the survival of the lunar surface exploration crews themselves and for base construction (bootstrapping).
   - Stage 2: production of propellants and structural materials for the Earth–Moon orbital economy (cislunar economy).
   - Stage 3: evolution into support bases for Mars and deep-space exploration.
3. **Resource extraction and environmental impact analysis**: presents the balance between preserving the scientific value of the lunar surface and commercial mining, together with the governance challenges of international space treaties.

## Methods

- Integrated literature analysis combining Apollo/Luna sample analysis data with remote-sensing survey data from lunar orbiters including Clementine, Lunar Prospector, LRO, and Chandrayaan.

## Results

- Establishes that lunar resources derive their immediate, large-scale economic viability chiefly from reducing space-infrastructure costs through local construction material and propellant production rather than from extraction of a single high-value mineral (e.g., $^3\text{He}$).
