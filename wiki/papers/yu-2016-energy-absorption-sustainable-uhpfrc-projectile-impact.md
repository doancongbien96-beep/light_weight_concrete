## Summary
A representative study of Eindhoven University of Technology clarifying through experiments and fracture mechanics models the quasi-static tensile/flexural energy absorption capacity and high-velocity projectile impact ($v = 600 \sim 850 m/s$) resistance of an eco-friendly sustainable ultra-high performance fiber reinforced concrete (Sustainable UHPFRC, 28 d $f_c = 155 MPa$) that cuts cement clinker consumption by more than 50 % based on the modified Andreasen and Andersen continuous particle packing model. Dense bridging by micro straight steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$) dissipates the projectile shock wave energy across the whole matrix; it was demonstrated that penetration depth fell by 35 % and rear-face spalling was fully suppressed while embodied carbon was cut by 45 % relative to a same-strength commercial UHPC.

## Key Contributions
- **Eco-friendly UHPFRC mix design based on optimum particle packing completed**: composite substitution of slag and nano silica substantially reduced binder embodied energy.
- **Linear correlation between quasi-static fracture energy and high-velocity impact energy absorption established**: impact resistance maximized when quasi-static $G_F > 25000 N/m$ is secured.
- **Ultra-high-speed bullet ($800 m/s$) penetration resistance demonstrated**: full perforation protection succeeded in 50 mm thick target panels.

## Methods
- Modified particle packing model (EMMA software), slag (40 %), silica fume (15 %), micro straight steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm).
- 7.62 mm military rifle projectile impact test ($v = 820 m/s$), three-point flexural fracture energy test, 28 d compressive strength.

## Results
- 28 d compressive strength $156.8 MPa$, modulus of rupture $28.5 MPa$, impact penetration depth $18 mm$, zero rear-face fragment spalling.
