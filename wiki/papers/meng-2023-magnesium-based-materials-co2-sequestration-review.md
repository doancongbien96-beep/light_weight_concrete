# Comprehensive review of permanent $\text{CO}_2$ capture and mineralization ($110\ \text{wt.}\%$) in reactive magnesia (MgO) cement and the development mechanisms of high-ductility low-carbon composites (MgO-ECC)

## Summary

To overcome the massive greenhouse-gas emissions of conventional Portland cement manufacture and turn the construction industry from a carbon source into a carbon sink, this paper consolidates **the $\text{CO}_2$ mineralization carbonation-curing mechanisms, hydration kinetics, mechanical performance ($f_c = 40\sim 80\ \text{MPa}$), and high-ductility MgO-ECC composite development technology of low-temperature-calcined reactive magnesium oxide cement derived from seawater and reject brine (Reactive MgO Cement: RMC), magnesium phosphate cement (MPC), and magnesium oxysulfate cement (MOS)**, and presents **the attainment of RMC's theoretical $\text{CO}_2$ uptake potential of $110\ \text{wt.}\%$, nanostructural densification through precipitation of hydrated magnesium carbonate (HMC: Nesquehonite, Dypingite, Hydromagnesite) crystals, and a roadmap for whole-process carbon-negative construction materials** — the highest-authority landmark review of 2023 in Cement and Concrete Composites by the research team of **Prof. En-Hua Yang and Prof. Shunzhi Qian of Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore, and Prof. Cise Unluer of the University of Glasgow, UK**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishing the principle of direct $\text{CO}_2$ capture and permanent mineralization ($110\ \text{wt.}\%$) by reactive MgO binders**:
   - Identifies the carbon-negative mechanism whereby more than the binder's own mass of $\text{CO}_2$ is captured as permanent solid mineral through the reaction $\text{MgO} + \text{CO}_2 + n\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{MgCO}_3\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
2. **Presenting an environmentally friendly supply chain based on low-temperature calcination ($500\sim 700\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and extraction from seawater reject brine**:
   - Lowers the calcination temperature by $50\%$ compared with cement clinker burning ($1450\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and precipitates and extracts magnesium hydroxide from desalination reject brine, drastically reducing production carbon emissions.
3. **Compressive strength development through carbonation curing ($40\sim 80\ \text{MPa}$)**:
   - Forced carbonation curing forms a dense network skeleton of hydrated magnesium carbonate (HMC) within 24 h, completing high-strength precast blocks.
4. **Completing the design basis of next-generation high-ductility MgO-ECC composite materials**:
   - Systematizes carbon-capture high-ductility composite technology that combines PVA/PE fibers with an MgO-based matrix to absorb $\text{CO}_2$ while exhibiting tensile strain capacity above $3\%$.

## Methods

- **Scope of analyzed literature and technical domains (Sections 1~7 & Tables 1~5)**:
  - Consolidates some 220 state-of-the-art primary studies worldwide on magnesium binders and $\text{CO}_2$ mineralization.
  - Magnesium binder families covered:
    1. Reactive magnesium oxide cement (RMC: $\text{MgO} + \text{CO}_2$).
    2. Magnesium phosphate cement (MPC: $\text{MgO} + \text{KH}_2\text{PO}_4$, ultra-rapid-strength repair material).
    3. Magnesium oxychloride/oxysulfate (MOC/MOS).
    4. Carbon-negative high-ductility composites (Carbon-Negative MgO-ECC).
- **Evaluation metrics**:
  - $\text{CO}_2$ uptake ratio ($\text{wt.}\%$), compressive strength ($f_c$), heat of hydration, carbonation reaction rate, water resistance, and durability.

### 2. Interpretation of the HMC nanoscale microstructure densification mechanism

- **Pore blocking by acicular nesquehonite crystals**: during $\text{CO}_2$-injection carbonation, the acicular nesquehonite ($\text{MgCO}_3\cdot 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$) crystals formed cross-fill micropores in three dimensions, cutting capillary porosity by more than $80\%$.
- **Thermodynamic stability of stored carbon**: carbonated magnesium crystal phases keep $\text{CO}_2$ safely locked in permanent solid form below $400\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is the highest-authority landmark review in which Prof. En-Hua Yang and Prof. Shunzhi Qian consolidated the principle of permanent $\text{CO}_2$ capture and mineralization of reactive magnesia materials together with high-ductility MgO-ECC composite technology, establishing the future vision and academic standard for 21st-century carbon-negative construction materials.
