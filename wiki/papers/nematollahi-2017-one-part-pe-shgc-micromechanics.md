# High-Ductility Behavior of PE Fiber-Reinforced One-Part Geopolymer Composites: A Micromechanics Interpretation

## Summary

A micromechanics-based study applying UHMWPE fibers to a one-part (dry-mix) strain-hardening geopolymer composite (SHGC) that eliminates corrosive alkali solutions and heat curing. It quantifies the effects of curing condition (heat vs ambient) and fiber type (PVA vs PE) on matrix fracture properties, interface parameters, and composite tensile performance, and explains the behavior by computing the bridging law and PSH indices with the Yang model.

## Key Contributions

1. Elucidation of the curing-condition paradox: ambient curing raises compressive and tensile strength but reduces ductility. Mechanism: the ambient-cured matrix has higher toughness (K_m 0.316 vs heat-cured 0.287 MPa·m1/2), raising σ_fc by 33 %, while the accompanying increase in interface roughness raises τ_0 and β → σ_0 rises. However, the PSH indices (strength 1.50 vs 1.57, energy 2.52 vs 3.04) are higher for heat curing, so the strain capacity follows heat-cured 5.5 % > ambient 4.9 %.
2. Fiber-type effect: hydrophilic PVA shows overall superior interface parameters (G_d 1.03 J/m2, τ_0 2.14 MPa, β 0.041) over PE (chemically bond-free, τ_0 1.36, β 0.019) → first-crack and ultimate strengths each 46 % and 10 % higher. However, PVA's lower tensile strength (1600 vs 3500 MPa) lowers J′_b, leaving its strain capacity at 4.2 % < PE's 4.9 % — stiff fibers (PE) favor ductility while strongly bonded fibers (PVA) favor strength.
3. Matrix toughness–interface roughness chain: K_m↑ → fracture-surface roughness↑ → contact area, abrasion, and jamming during fiber slip↑ → τ_0 and β↑. Empirical demonstration of the pathway in which the matrix microstructure mediates the interface parameters.
4. One-part mix practicality: fly ash:slag 1:1 + anhydrous sodium silicate powder 0.08, W 0.35, with no heat curing needed. Density 1837~1849 kg/m3 (satisfying lightweight-concrete criteria).

## Methods

Three-point bending single-notched beams (ECM method, Karihaloo-Nallathambi) to derive E_m and K_m, with J_tip = K_m²/E_m. Single-fiber pullout (Redon et al. method) for G_d, τ_0, and β. σ_B(δ) computed with the Yang et al. micromechanics model, from which PSH indices were derived. Direct-tension coupons of 400×75×10 mm.

## Results

- Strain hardening and saturated multiple cracking confirmed in all mixes. All PSH indices exceed 1.
- Compressive strength 33.9~48.7 MPa. PE composites run 9~24 % lower than their matrices due to fiber-induced entrained voids.
