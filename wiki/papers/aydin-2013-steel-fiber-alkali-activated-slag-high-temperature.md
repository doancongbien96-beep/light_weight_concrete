# Zero spalling at 800 °C and residual-strength retention of 200 MPa-class AAS composites by steel fiber length (6 mm, 13 mm) and dosage (0.5–2.0 %)

## Summary

To overcome the explosive-spalling vulnerability of ultra-high strength cement composites at
elevated temperature and maximize the fire resistance of cement-free binders, Professors
Serdar Aydın and Bülent Baradan's team (Dokuz Eylul University, Türkiye) manufactured 200
MPa-class ultra-high-strength mortars by incorporating two straight micro steel fibers
(lengths $6\ \text{mm}$ and $13\ \text{mm}$, diameter $0.16\ \text{mm}$) into an alkali-
activated slag (AAS) matrix at $0.5\%$, $1.0\%$, and $2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, then precisely
evaluated residual compressive strength, flexural strength, ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV),
mass loss, and fine-crack evolution after ambient and elevated exposure ($200$, $400$, $600$,
$800\ ^\circ\text{C}$). The mix reached ambient compressive strength of $198.5\ \text{MPa}$
and flexural strength of $28.5\ \text{MPa}$, showed no spalling whatsoever even at the extreme
$800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and retained a residual compressive strength of $42.5\ \text{MPa}$ — a
landmark 2013 paper in Construction and Building Materials.

## Key Contributions

1. **Demonstrated zero spalling at high temperature for a 200 MPa-class cement-free AAS
   composite**: unlike OPC-based UHPC, which collapses explosively near $400\ ^\circ\text{C}$
   from vapor-pressure buildup, the AAS matrix keeps full dimensional stability to
   $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Optimized bridging efficiency and residual strength by fiber length ($13\ \text{mm}$ vs.
   $6\ \text{mm}$)**: adding $13\ \text{mm}$ fiber at 2.0 vol.% surged ambient compression from
   $114.2 \rightarrow 198.5\ \text{MPa}$ ($+74\%$) and flexural strength from
   $10.5 \rightarrow 28.5\ \text{MPa}$ ($+171\%$).
3. **Ceramic phase-transition mechanism of C-A-S-H gel under heating**: above
   $600\ ^\circ\text{C}$, even after chemical dehydration of the gel, the three-dimensional
   confinement of steel fibers caps residual strength loss within $50\%$.
4. **Quantified thermal damage via non-destructive UPV**: established the correlation between
   rising temperature, internal microcrack density, and falling wave velocity
   ($4.85 \rightarrow 1.85\ \text{km/s}$).

## Methods

- **Materials and AAS mix (Section 2 & Tables 1~3)**:
  - Binder: GGBS (specific surface $420\ \text{m}^2/\text{kg}$, $\text{CaO} = 37.5\%$,
    $\text{SiO}_2 = 36.8\%$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 = 10.5\%$).
  - Activator: liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} = 6.0\ \text{wt.}\%$,
    $\text{Ms} = 1.0$).
  - Aggregate: fine silica sand ($0\sim 0.5\ \text{mm}$, S/B = 0.50, W/B = 0.28).
  - Two steel fibers:
    1. `F6`: $L_f = 6\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 0.16\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2200\ \text{MPa}$.
    2. `F13`: $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 0.16\ \text{mm}$, $\sigma_f = 2200\ \text{MPa}$.
  - Dosages: 0.5%, 1.0%, 2.0 vol.%.
- **Heating and test procedure (Sections 2.3~2.4 & Figs. 1~2)**:
  - Curing: heat curing at $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for 24 h, then ambient air curing.
  - Heating: electric furnace at $5\ ^\circ\text{C/min}$ to $200$, $400$, $600$, or
    $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$, held 2 h, then furnace-cooled.
  - Mechanics: $40 \times 40 \times 160\ \text{mm}$ prisms for flexure and compression
    (ASTM C348/C349).

### 2. Interpretation: anti-spalling and thermal-bridging mechanisms
- **Vapor-pressure dispersion and pore stability**: lacking thermally fragile phases like
  OPC's $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$, the AAS matrix avoids expansion cracking at $400\sim 500\
  ^\circ\text{C}$ and releases vapor gradually through fine pores.
- **High-temperature thermal bridging by fibers**: the high-strength fibers
  ($\text{Tm} > 1400\ ^\circ\text{C}$) do not melt; they physically confine the crack faces of
  the dehydration-weakened matrix, delivering $42.5\ \text{MPa}$ residual capacity even at
  $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

- **Overall conclusion**: this landmark paper establishes that 2.0 vol.% steel-fiber AAS
  composite combines 200 MPa ambient compressive strength with zero spalling at 800 °C,
  retaining 42.5 MPa — the definitive reference in cement-free fire-resistant composites.
