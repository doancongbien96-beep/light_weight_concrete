## Summary
A recent study that evaluated the thermal fatigue resistance, microcracking behavior, residual compressive strength, and direct tensile performance of strain-hardening geopolymer composites (EGC) exposed to the extreme diurnal thermal cycling ($-100 \sim +100 °C$) of harsh space environments such as the Moon and Mars, or to extreme ground climates. High-strength PE fibers were incorporated into a metakaolin/slag-based geopolymer matrix, which was then subjected to up to 200 severe heating-cooling thermal cycles. The study analyzed the micro-interfacial damage mechanism arising from the mismatch in coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) between matrix and fiber, and demonstrated that EGC possesses outstanding thermal fatigue durability, maintaining a tensile strain capacity above 5.0 % even after 200 thermal cycles.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of EGC mechanical performance data under space-simulated extreme thermal cycling**: derivation of tensile stress-strain curves after 200 rapid-heating/quenching cycles of $-100 °C \leftrightarrow +100 °C$.
- **Elucidation of the interfacial thermal fatigue debonding mechanism**: discovered the paradoxical effect whereby a gentle decrease in interfacial frictional stress ($\tau_0$) caused by the transverse shrinkage/expansion of polymeric fibers ($1.8 \rightarrow 1.2 MPa$) instead contributes to enhanced ductility.
- **Quantification of microporosity increase and dynamic modulus decay**: ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) measurements confirmed a residual dynamic modulus of elasticity of 88 % after 200 cycles.

## Methods
- **Mix proportions**: metakaolin 70 % + GGBS 30 %, potassium silicate activator, UHMWPE fibers ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, tensile strength 3000 MPa).
- **Thermal cycle testing**: in a special environmental chamber, one cycle consisted of $-100 °C$ (held for 1 h) $\rightarrow +100 °C$ (held for 1 h), repeated for 50, 100, and 200 cycles.
- **Test items**: uniaxial dog-bone direct tension test, compressive strength, UPV, and SEM fracture surface analysis.

## Results
- After exposure to 200 cycles: compressive strength $52.4 MPa$ ($85 \%$ residual), ultimate tensile strength $7.85 MPa$ ($88 \%$ residual), with a tensile strain capacity of $5.20 \%$ retained ($90 \%$ preservation relative to the initial $5.80 \%$).
- Demonstrated that no brittle failure occurs under thermal fatigue and that the multiple fine-crack bridging capability is fully maintained.
