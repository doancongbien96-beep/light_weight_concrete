# Self-healing of Portland and slag cement binder systems incorporating CFBC bottom ash

## Summary

A user-group paper (corresponding author Hyeong-Ki Kim, Chosun University; co-authored by
Bang Yeon Lee, Chonnam National University) evaluating crack self-healing when CFBC
(Circulating Fluidized Bed Combustion) bottom ash — rich in sulfate, free lime, and
portlandite — replaces 10 and 20 % of the binder in Portland cement (PC) and slag cement
(PCS) systems. Classifying specimens by an 'ideal crack width' back-calculated from flow
through Darcy's law and Poiseuille flow, instead of measured surface widths, improved
correlation with healing performance; and in the slag system, portlandite from CFBC ash
activated the slag to improve healing by +20 percentage points only when atmospheric
carbonation was blocked (sealed immersion).

## Key Contributions

1. Introduction of ideal crack width: combining Poiseuille flow
   (Q = Lb^3/(12μ)·dP/dx) with Darcy's law (k=b²/12) defines an equivalent crack opening
   back-calculated from flow rate — absorbing the tortuosity and roughness of the crack path,
   which surface width cannot capture, into a single parameter. Its correlation with flow
   after 28 days beats mean surface crack width (R² comparison).
2. Attenuation coefficient ξ ≈ 0.49: the offset between theoretical k on ideal-width basis
   and measured k — far above Picandet et al.'s 0.1–0.001 range, interpreted from the
   difference in crack creation method (tensile splitting with a jig).
3. Environmental dependence of healing mechanisms: under water immersion allowing
   atmospheric carbonation, PC_C20 healed ~85 % (+15 %p over PC, large scatter); under sealed
   immersion blocking carbonation, PCS_C20 ~75 % versus PCS ~55 %, +20 %p — the only mix to
   overcome the sealed>water condition. The logic: portlandite from CFBC ash hydrates needs
   protection from carbonation consumption to activate the slag (consistent with Huang et
   al.'s saturated Ca(OH)2 activation results).
4. Healing product characterization via SEM/EDS/XRD: in slag+CFBC systems, extra sulfate
   supply forms long, interlocking ettringite networks densely. Ettringite density however
   does not correlate with permeability reduction (both best- and worst-healing mixes show
   dense networks). Some calcite appears even in sealed curing — sealing cannot fully block
   carbonation.
5. Reconfirmation of the healable-width limit: specimens at 0.3 mm mean crack width showed
   no healing in any mix or curing condition; healing manifests only below ~0.2 mm.

## Methods

- Six mixes (PC/PC_C10/PC_C20/PCS/PCS_C10/PCS_C20), s/b=2, w/b=0.4, SP 1 %. CFBC bottom ash
  collected from Gunsan combined-cycle power plant, passing a 75 μm sieve. QXRD (Rietveld):
  anhydrite 29.1 %, portlandite 30.4 %, lime 4.8 %, quartz 7.3 %, amorphous 22.5 wt%.
- Cylindrical mortars (r60×40 mm), water-cured 28 days, cracked by tensile splitting with
  clamp-controlled width (target 0.1±0.05 mm; the 0.3 mm group used 0.1 mm plastic strip
  spacers). After side silicone sealing, constant-head permeability testing at 0.60 m —
  five flow measurements at weekly intervals; healing ratio = (Q0−Qi)/Q0.
- For healing-product analysis, prisms (15×15×80 mm) received two crack types: three-point
  flexural cracks (rough faces, for SEM) and diamond-saw cut cracks (flat faces, for XRD);
  vacuum drying at 5.9×10^-5 atm, 50 °C, 48 h.

## Results

- Best water-immersion result was plain PCS (k=5.5×10^-11 m2, ~89 % healed); adding 20 %
  CFBC lowered it slightly to 82 %.
- Under sealed immersion healing ratios were generally 0.6–0.9 times those of water
  immersion — most self-healing stems from residual-binder hydration and matrix swelling
  rather than carbonation.
- XRD: water immersion consumed ettringite and portlandite while producing calcite
  (carbonation proceeding); sealed PC_C20 showed the strongest portlandite peak (the CFBC
  supply remaining unreacted with slag).
