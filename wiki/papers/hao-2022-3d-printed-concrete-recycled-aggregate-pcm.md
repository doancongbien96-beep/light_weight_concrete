# Anisotropic Thermal Conductivity and Mechanical Behavior of 3D-Printed Concrete Incorporating Recycled Fine Aggregate (RFA)-Based Paraffin Composite PCM

## Summary

To achieve construction waste recycling and zero-energy building envelopes, Professor Jianzhuang Xiao and co-workers at Tongji University synthesized a shape-stabilized phase change material (Paraffin/RFA PCM, 12.8 wt.% loading, latent heat $15.4\ \text{J/g}$) by vacuum adsorbing paraffin wax (melting point $35.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $120.5\ \text{J/g}$) into porous recycled fine aggregate (RFA: porosity 28.15 %, water absorption 13.7 %). Replacing natural quartz sand by 0 %, 12.5 %, 25 %, and 50 wt.% with Paraffin/RFA in 3D-printed thermal regulation concrete (3DPTR), the authors evaluated layer buildup, toolpath orientation, printing speed, 28 d compressive strength (30.1~45.0 MPa), and **3D anisotropic thermal conductivity ($k$) across printing interfaces** using guarded hot plate (GHP) and transient hot wire (THW) methods. Paraffin impregnated in RFA pores showed 0.0 % leakage across 100 thermal cycles. The 50 wt.% mix (3DPTR50) achieved a compressive strength of **30.1 MPa**, lowered thermal conductivity by **34.8 % ($1.35 \rightarrow 0.88\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$)**, and demonstrated thermal transfer anisotropy where **vertical interlayer conductivity ($k_z$) was 8 ~ 14 % lower than longitudinal extrusion conductivity ($k_x$)** due to boundary micro-voids.

## Key Contributions

1. **Synthesis of leakage-free paraffin SSPCM using porous recycled fine aggregates (RFA)**:
   - Utilized the high porosity (28.15 %) of crushed waste concrete fine aggregate as a carrier to encapsulate 12.8 wt.% paraffin, sealing off pores to prevent secondary water absorption.
2. **Quantification of print-interface-induced anisotropic thermal conductivity ($k_z < k_y < k_x$)**:
   - Proved that interlayer boundaries and micro-voids act as thermal resistance barriers, making thermal conductivity in the vertical stacking direction ($k_z = 0.88\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) 13 % lower than along the print extrusion direction ($k_x = 1.01\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$).
3. **Optimal balance of structural capacity (30.1 MPa) and thermal insulation ($0.88\ \text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$, $7.7\ \text{J/g}$)**:
   - Maintained printable buildability while substantially enhancing passive cooling and thermal lag in building walls and roofs.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and Paraffin/RFA synthesis (Tables 1–2 & Fig. 1)**:
  - Binder: OPC 42.5, W/B = 0.35.
  - Porous carrier: Recycled fine aggregate (density $2.35\ \text{g/cm}^3$, water absorption 13.7 %, porosity 28.15 %, $d_{50} = 450\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Phase change material: Paraffin wax (melting point $35.0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, latent heat $120.5\ \text{J/g}$).
  - Vacuum impregnation: Liquid paraffin absorbed into RFA at 60 °C $\rightarrow$ Paraffin/RFA composite (latent heat $15.4\ \text{J/g}$).
  - 3D printing admixtures: HPMC $1.28\ \text{kg/m}^3$, nanoclay $5.0\ \text{kg/m}^3$, sodium gluconate retarder $0.7\ \text{kg/m}^3$, polycarboxylate SP.
- **3D printing parameters and specimen fabrication**:
  - Gantry 3D concrete printer (nozzle $30\ \text{mm} \times 15\ \text{mm}$, extrusion speed $100\sim 300\ \text{mm/s}$, layer thickness 15 mm).
  - Printing toolpaths: Parallel unidirectional and cross-hatch configurations.
- **Testing program**:
  - Thermal conductivity: Guarded Hot Plate (GHP-300) and transient hot wire (TC3000E) measuring 3-axis ($X, Y, Z$) directional conductivity.
  - Compressive strength: GB/T 17671 (40 mm × 40 mm × 160 mm prisms at 7 and 28 d).
  - Microstructure: Mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) and X-ray micro-CT.

### 2. Interlayer thermal barrier mechanics and environmental benefits
- **Z-axis thermal resistance**: Interlayer interfacial air voids act as continuous insulation barriers perpendicular to stacking, reducing $k_z$ by 13 % relative to $k_x$.
- **100 % circular upcycling**: Decreased natural sand extraction by 50 % while converting demolished concrete waste into functional thermal energy storage structures.

- **Overall conclusion**: This work established the science of integrating recycled fine aggregate-PCM composites into 3D-printed concrete, balancing 30 MPa structural capacity with low thermal conductivity and anisotropic thermal insulation.
