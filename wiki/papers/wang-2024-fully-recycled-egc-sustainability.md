## Summary
A 2024 landmark study by Professor Jian-Guo Dai's research team at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University clarifying the micromechanics-based mix design, single PE fiber interface characteristics, direct tensile strain hardening, and full life-cycle carbon assessment (LCA) of a Fully Recycled EGC that excludes natural silica sand and cement 100 % entirely and applies recycled concrete powder (RCP), recycled glass sand (RGS), and slag/fly ash as 100 % recycled aggregate/binder. Even under the 100 % recycled-feedstock condition, a 28 d compressive strength of **48.5 MPa**, a direct tensile strength of **6.2 MPa**, and a tensile strain capacity of **7.2 %** were achieved while embodied carbon was cut by **82 %**.

## Key Contributions
- **Creation of a fully circular high-ductility composite with 100 % upcycling of construction waste**: zero use of natural sand and cement realized.
- **Mechanism by which ground waste glass and recycled concrete powder relax matrix fracture toughness clarified**: $K_m = 0.28 MPa\sqrt{m}$ secured, achieving a PSH energy index $J_b'/J_{tip} = 9.8$.
- **Tensile strain capacity of 7.2 % and formation of a saturated multiple fine-crack network**: mean crack width $22 \mu m$ with 85 cracks distributed.
- **LCA carbon footprint cut by 82 % and natural aggregate conservation**: groundwork laid for commercialization of a fully circular-economy composite material.

## Methods
- Binder: recycled concrete powder (RCP 40 %) + GGBS 40 % + fly ash 20 %; aggregate: 100 % waste glass sand ($d_{50} = 150 \mu m$).
- Fiber: UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, length 12 mm).
- Tests: dogbone direct tensile test ($0.5 mm/min$, DIC strain), 28 d compressive strength, SEM-EDS interface analysis, SimaPro 9.0 LCA analysis.
