## Summary
A study that established the retention ratios and degradation mechanisms of the direct tensile and direct shear mechanical properties of high-ductility cement composites (ECC) under high-temperature fire exposure environments ($100 \sim 600 °C$). Uniaxial direct tension tests and double-shear tests (push-off shear test) were performed to quantify the fiber melting ($230 °C$ for PVA, $150 °C$ for PE), the dehydration of the matrix C-S-H, and the loss of crack bridging capacity as temperature rises. Through hybrid polymer-steel fiber or heat-resistant matrix design, it presented a fire-resistant design criterion that maintains more than 60 % of shear strength without brittle explosive spalling even after $400 °C$ exposure.

## Key Contributions
- **Constructed ECC tensile-shear strength retention curves by temperature range**:
  - $T \le 200 °C$: tensile strength retained at $85 \%$ through drying shrinkage of the fiber interfacial zone.
  - $T = 300 \sim 400 °C$: organic fibers melt and disappear, losing tensile strain-hardening capacity and shifting to quasi-brittle softening.
  - Shear strength remains at $50 \sim 65 \%$ after the loss of macro fiber bridging, carried by aggregate interlocking and matrix shear friction.
- **Interpreted microstructural porosity changes after high-temperature exposure**: mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) and TGA thermogravimetric analysis confirmed a threefold expansion of the capillary pore volume of $0.1 \sim 1.0 \mu m$ above $400 °C$.

## Methods
- **Materials**: PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0 \%$) and PVA-steel fiber hybrid ECC ($V_{PVA} = 1.5 \%, V_{steel} = 0.5 \%$).
- **Heating test**: in an electric furnace, target temperatures of $100 °C, 200 °C, 300 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C$ were reached, held for 2 h, and furnace-cooled.
- **Mechanical tests**: after cooling to room temperature, direct tension test and JSCE-standard direct shear test.

## Results
- $200 °C$ exposure: tensile strength $4.2 MPa$ ($88 \%$ of the room-temperature value), tensile strain capacity $3.1 \%$, shear strength $6.8 MPa$ ($92 \%$ of the room-temperature value).
- $400 °C$ exposure: tensile strength $1.8 MPa$ (softening failure), shear strength $4.5 MPa$ ($60 \%$ retention), zero explosive spalling.
