# Contour Crafting 3D Printing for Autonomous Robotic In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) Construction on the Moon and Mars: Landing Pads, Blast Walls, Sulfur Concrete, and Molten Regolith Architecture

## Summary

To drastically reduce the exorbitant transport cost ($> \$100,000/\text{kg}$) of terrestrial construction materials and eliminate astronaut hazard in extreme vacuum, radiation, and thermal environments, Professor Behrokh Khoshnevis (inventor of Contour Crafting) and colleagues at the University of Southern California integrated **Contour Crafting (CC)** with In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). In this comprehensive 136-page NASA NIAC Phase II final report, the authors develop robotic systems to autonomously construct four critical lunar and Martian infrastructure classes: **landing pads and aprons against engine plume blast, roads for rover transport, blast walls and shade walls against hypervelocity ejecta, and radiation/meteorite-shielded hangars and habitats**. The study establishes material formulations for **waterless sulfur concrete, high-temperature molten regolith extrusion ($1100\sim 1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$), high-strength sintered regolith interlocking ceramic tiles (compressive strength $>100\ \text{MPa}$), alongside deployable JPL ATHLETE hexapod rovers and cable-suspended CC robotic manipulators**. Experiments using NASA JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant validated 3D printing toolhead precision and 100 MPa compressive strengths, combined with extensive thermal and radiation shielding simulations in collaboration with NASA KSC and MSFC.

## Key Contributions

1. **Establishment of ISRU 3D Contour Crafting construction architecture**:
   - Formulated standardized automated additive manufacturing processes using indigenous regolith, reducing dependency on Earth-imported materials by over 95 %.
2. **Robotic construction protocols for landing pad plume mitigation and blast walls**:
   - Engineered interlocking tile pads and parabolic blast walls to arrest $1500\ ^\circ\text{C}$ exhaust gases and $1000\ \text{m/s}$ high-velocity regolith spray during 45~50-ton lander (Altair-class) touchdowns.
3. **Validation of waterless sulfur concrete and molten regolith 3D printing**:
   - Developed sulfur concrete extrusion systems melting sulfur ($\text{S}$) at $130\sim 150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for rapid hardening without water, alongside $1200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ molten regolith extrusion nozzles.
4. **Integration of JPL ATHLETE and cable-suspended robotic platforms**:
   - Implemented coordinated motion control combining legged mobile robotics with cable suspension systems to print large-span structures without heavy gantry cranes.

## Methods

- **ISRU material systems and properties (Chapters 3–5)**:
  - Regolith simulant: NASA JSC-1A (lunar mare basaltic simulant, $d_{50} = 70\ \mu\text{m}$).
  - Sulfur concrete: Purified sulfur (20~30 wt.%) + regolith aggregate (70~80 wt.%), melt-extruded at $135\sim 145\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
  - Sintering and molten extrusion: 2.45 GHz microwave and focused resistance-heated extrusion nozzles ($1100\sim 1300\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
  - Hardening rate: Sulfur concrete reaches 100 % design strength within 1 hour upon cooling.
- **Contour Crafting robotic hardware (Chapter 2 & Figs. 1–15)**:
  - 3D printing toolhead: Dual-trowel surface smoothing system and extrusion orifice nozzle.
  - Mobility platforms: JPL Tri-ATHLETE hexapod-mounted end-effectors and ultra-lightweight cable-suspended robots.
  - Four target infrastructure typologies:
    1. Landing Pad (50 m radius oval) and apron.
    2. Blast Wall (height 5 m, $60^\circ$ parabolic slope).
    3. Road networks (4 m wide sintered dense pavement).
    4. Habitats / Hangars (15 m diameter compound curvature domes).

## Results

### 1. Processing parameters, mechanical properties, and infrastructure suitability across ISRU materials (Chapters 4–6 & Tables 1–8)

| ISRU Material System | Primary Binding Mechanism | Processing Temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$) | Curing & Hardening Time | Compressive Strength ($f_c$, MPa) | Primary Infrastructure Application |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sulfur Concrete** | Thermoplastic sulfur solidification | $135\sim 145\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (Low energy) | **< 1 hour (Upon cooling)** | **45.0 ~ 65.0 MPa** | **Roads, aprons, blast barriers** |
| **Molten Regolith Extrusion** | Basalt vitrification and melting | $1200\sim 1350\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (High energy) | 2 ~ 4 hours (Controlled cooling) | **120.0 ~ 150.0 MPa (Ultra-high)** | **Landing pad core, thermal blast walls** |
| **Sintered Interlocking Tiles** | Solid-state thermal particle diffusion | $1050\sim 1150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (Medium energy) | 30 min sintering | **90.0 ~ 130.0 MPa** | **Modular landing pad wearing surface** |
| **In-Situ Solar Sintering** | Localized partial surface melting | $1000\sim 1200\ ^\circ\text{C}$ | Immediately after heating | 25.0 ~ 45.0 MPa | Unpaved road dust stabilization |

### 2. Thermo-mechanical durability and radiation shielding
- **Cryogenic thermal cycling resistance**: Hybrid reinforcement using 1.0 vol.% alumina/carbon microfibers prevented microcracking in sulfur concrete across lunar thermal cycles ($-180\ ^\circ\text{C} \sim +120\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Plume deflection dynamics of blast walls**: The $60^\circ$ inclined blast wall deflected 92 % of rocket exhaust upward, eliminating kinetic dust impingement on rear habitat modules.

- **Overall conclusion**: This NASA NIAC report established the master blueprint for autonomous robotic construction of 4 critical space infrastructure classes on the Moon and Mars using waterless sulfur concrete and molten regolith Contour Crafting.
