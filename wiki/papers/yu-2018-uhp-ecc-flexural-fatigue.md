# Flexural fatigue characteristics of UHP-ECC

## Summary

A paper evaluating fatigue life and deformation behavior by repetitively applying sinusoidal loads (8 Hz) at stress levels of 0.5·0.65·0.8·0.9 to UHP-ECC beams (four-point bending, 300 mm span) (MDPI Polymers). It clarified that even in a high-strength matrix, fatigue life is governed by the accumulated damage of fiber-matrix bond, and that as the stress level rises, the proportion of pullout failure on the fracture surface increases.

## Key Contributions

1. **Fatigue failure mechanism**: final fatigue fracture is attributed to cumulative damage in the bond performance between fibers and cement matrix — the fracture surface shows mixed pullout failure plus fiber rupture. As fatigue stress grows, the ratio of rupture area to pullout area increases markedly.
2. **Three-stage deformation development model**: midspan deflection progresses through Stage I (rapid growth) → Stage II (stabilization, crack propagation plus new crack formation) → Stage III (unstable failure), and fatigue life is governed by the length of Stage II.
3. **PVA versus PE fiber fatigue difference**: PVA-ECC, with its higher bond strength, secures longer fatigue lives than PE-ECC at high stress levels, whereas PE-ECC performs better at low stresses — fiber selection criteria per stress level presented.

## Methods

- Mix (UHP-ECC): OPC 52.5R + GGBS + limestone powder (LP) + silica fume binder system. LP acts both as filler and as an accelerator of C₃S hydration, reacting with C₃A to form calcium carboaluminate (C₃A·CaCO₃·11H₂O).
- Specimens: beams under four-point bending (span 300 mm, load points 100 mm apart).
- Static tests: displacement control 1 mm/min on a 250 kN MTS.
- Fatigue tests: load control, sine wave 8 Hz, stress levels 0.5~0.9.
- Instrumentation: LVDT ×3 (supports and midspan deflection), an LVDT for COD at the bottom face of the pure bending zone, crack width microscope, VIC-3D digital image correlation (DIC).

## Results

- UHP-ECC maintained ductile failure even after fatigue loading — the high-strength matrix had no adverse effect on fatigue life.
- Stress level ↑ → midspan deflection ↑ + shortened Stage II → reduced fatigue life.
- Fracture surface observation: higher stress raised the fiber pullout fraction; pulled-out fibers showed surface scratching and round-to-flat deformation, while ruptured fibers accompanied wire-drawing phenomena.
