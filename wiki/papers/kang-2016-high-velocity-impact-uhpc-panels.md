# High-Velocity Projectile Impact Resistance of Steel Fiber-Reinforced UHPC Panels

## Summary

To develop ultra-lightweight, high-performance architectural cladding for irregular and high-rise structures, this experimental study evaluates the high-velocity projectile impact resistance, penetration depth, and back-face scabbing resistance of thin panels (thickness 20 ~ 30 mm) fabricated with 180 MPa-grade ultra-high-performance concrete (UHPC) compared against natural granite panels (Mungyeong and Goheung granite). Under high-velocity steel projectile impact ($v = 150 \sim 350\ \text{m/s}$, $150 \sim 350 m/s$), the dense 3D fiber bridging network of straight steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0\%$) effectively dispersed stress shockwaves and arrested crack propagation. While identical-thickness granite panels shattered completely into fragments, the UHPC panels absorbed the kinetic impact energy solely through localized microcracking without back-face scabbing.

## Key Contributions

- **Ballistic-grade impact resistance of ultra-thin (20 mm) UHPC panels**: Demonstrated over 10-fold higher impact resistance than natural granite under high-velocity projectile collision.
- **Suppression of back-face spalling via steel fiber bridging**: 3D straight steel fiber bridging prevented the ejection of shear punch-out cones.
- **Cladding design optimization**: Reduced cladding self-weight by over 40 % while maintaining structural safety against windborne debris and high-velocity impacts.

## Methods

- **Mixture design**: $W/B = 0.20$, silica fume 25 %, straight steel fibers ($V_f = 2.0\%$, diameter 0.2 mm, length 13 mm, tensile strength 2500 MPa), 28 d compressive strength 182 MPa ($182 MPa$).
- **Impact testing**: Pneumatic gas gun launcher with 50 g steel projectiles ($v = 150 \sim 350\ \text{m/s}$, $150 \sim 350 m/s$).
- **Test targets**: $300 \times 300\ \text{mm}$ ($300 \times 300 mm$) panels with thicknesses of 20 mm and 30 mm (UHPC and granite).

## Results

- Granite panels (30 mm thickness): Experienced complete shattering and perforation at an impact velocity of 180 m/s ($180 m/s$).
- UHPC panels (20 mm thickness): Prevented perforation even at 320 m/s ($320 m/s$), maintaining overall integrity with minor front cratering (depth 6 mm) and tightly controlled rear microcracks.
