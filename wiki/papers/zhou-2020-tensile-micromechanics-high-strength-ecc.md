## Summary
A representative study that completed the micromechanical mix design theory of High-Strength ECC, a high-strength, ultra-high-ductility composite that simultaneously exhibits a compressive strength above 100 MPa and a direct tensile strain capacity above 6.0 %, and validated it experimentally. To overcome the brittle fracture limit caused by the high fracture toughness ($K_m$) of the high-strength matrix, matrix flaws were refined with a combined slag and silica fume binder, and the interfacial frictional stress ($\tau_0$) of the high-strength PE fiber was optimally controlled at $2.2 MPa$. Through this, an energy performance index $J_b'/J_{tip} = 6.5$ was secured, achieving a 28 d compressive strength of 108 MPa, a tensile strength of 12.5 MPa, and a tensile strain capacity of 6.8 %.

## Key Contributions
- **Establishment of the PSH design baseline in the high-strength regime**: mapping of the critical fiber-interface properties required when the matrix compressive strength exceeds 100 MPa.
- **Demonstration of the mechanism for attaining ultra-high strength and ultra-high ductility simultaneously**: overcoming the trade-off between strength and ductility and establishing high-strength seismic and blast-resistant materials.
- **Multiple fine crack control**: formation of a saturated cracking network with a mean crack width of $25 \mu m$ and more than 80 cracks.

## Methods
- **Mix proportion**: cement + GGBS (35 %) + silica fume (15 %), $W/B = 0.18$, UHMWPE fiber ($V_f = 2.0 \%$, diameter 12 µm, strength 3000 MPa).
- **Test items**: uniaxial dogbone direct tensile test, single-fiber pull-out test, 28 d compressive strength, notched beam matrix fracture toughness test.

## Results
- 28 d properties: compressive strength $108.5 MPa$, ultimate tensile strength $12.5 MPa$, tensile strain capacity $6.80 \%$, first-cracking strength $6.2 MPa$.
- A high tensile energy dissipation density ($720 kJ/m3$) was secured.
