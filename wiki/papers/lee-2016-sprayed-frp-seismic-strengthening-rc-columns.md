# A Seismic Strengthening Technique for Reinforced Concrete Columns Using Sprayed FRP

## Summary

An experimental study that established the spray FRP method for seismic strengthening, in which chopped glass and carbon fibers are mixed with epoxy/vinyl-ester resin in ambient air and sprayed directly onto the irregular surface of concrete columns (Department of Architecture, Chonnam National University: first author Kang Seok Taek + corresponding author Prof. Bang Yeon Lee + Soo Yeon Seo of Korea National University of Transportation; funded by a Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport research grant). Material tests on 160 specimens determined the optimum mix (fiber length 38 mm, fiber:resin = 1:2 weight ratio), from which an equivalent design thickness matching the tensile strength of one ply of conventional FRP sheet was derived; reversed cyclic loading tests on five shear-failure-dominated columns confirmed mean improvements of 1.31 times in maximum shear strength and 1.43 times in displacement at maximum load, and strength reduction factors for spray FRP to be inserted into the JBDPA/AIJ shear strength equations were proposed.

## Key Contributions

- Spray-FRP material optimization: 120 specimens across 24 glass-fiber variables (lengths 14/28/38/56 mm x resin ratios 1:4~1:2) plus 40 specimens across 8 carbon-fiber variables (lengths 28/38 mm), tensile-tested per JIS K 7054. Fiber length 38 mm and weight ratio 1:2 were the condition giving the highest strength with the least clumping. The equivalent design thickness was derived from the sheet strength relation (sheet strength x sheet thickness = spray material strength x design thickness).
- Practical advantages over sheets summarized: no prior surface treatment required, no splice problems, low cost — the method logic that spraying resolves the three major weaknesses of conventional FRP sheet strengthening. Note, however, that blending epoxy lowered the viscosity relative to neat premix resin alone, improving workability.
- Reversed cyclic loading tests proved that strength and displacement are raised simultaneously: against unretrofitted SC-N (325 kN), SC-S-GE (glass+epoxy) reached 461 kN (SR 1.42), SC-S-CV (carbon+vinyl ester) 423 kN, SC-S-GV (glass+vinyl ester) 422 kN (SR 1.30), and SC-S-CE (carbon+epoxy) 397 kN (SR 1.22). Carbon fiber gives the higher absolute strength, but the glass-fiber combination is more balanced in terms of cost.
- Proposal of spray-FRP-specific strength reduction factors for existing FRP sheet shear-strengthening design equations (JBDPA equation, AIJ modified arch-truss equation): alpha_1 (JBDPA basis) mean 1.51 with range 0.93~2.25, alpha_2 (AIJ basis) mean 0.41 with range 0.35~0.48. Since AIJ's alpha_2=0.67 overestimates the test values, it was concluded that the JBDPA equation applying alpha_1=0.93 is the most practical for safe-side design.

## Methods

- Materials: roving-type glass fiber (Central Glass ERS 2310-233/C), roving-type carbon fiber (Mitsubishi Rayon TR330-50K), epoxy (flexural strength 45 MPa)/vinyl ester (30 MPa) resins. Specimens were cured for 7 days in air at 25 degrees C and tension-tested per JIS K 7054 speed A (1 ± 0.5 mm/min) with a mini 5 t UTM.
- Equivalent design thicknesses (Table 3): glass+VE 4.4 mm, glass+epoxy 4.2 mm, carbon+VE 3.3 mm, carbon+epoxy 3.0 mm (reference sheets: glass 500 MPa x 1 mm, carbon 3550 MPa x 0.167 mm).
- Structural test: five shear-failure-dominated columns with a 400 x 400 mm section, clear height 1400 mm (ho/D = 3.5), main bars D22 SD40, hoops D10@250 (rho_t 0.97 %, rho_s 0.14 %). Design standard strength 30 MPa (measured 33.0 ± 1.2 MPa), axial force 480 kN (10 % of design compressive strength). Building Research Institute-type L-frame loading: reversed cyclic loading at story drift angle R = 0.08 %~5 %, three cycles each, using a 2000 kN actuator.
- After spraying, surfaces were finished with a roller brush to secure adhesion. Failure of the retrofit layer proceeded with the concrete and the retrofit material behaving monolithically until simultaneous cracking and debonding near maximum load.

## Results

- Displacement at maximum load (vs SC-N 14.38 mm): SC-S-GV 19.22 mm (DR 1.34), SC-S-GE 21.28 mm (1.48), SC-S-CV 20.72 mm (1.44), SC-S-CE 21.08 mm (1.47).
- Failure progression: initial cracking began as flexural cracks around R = 0.15 %, diagonal shear cracks followed at R = 0.31~0.49 % (including widths exceeding 2 mm), audible rupture of fibers in the retrofit material occurred around R = 1 %, and the test ended in widespread rupture and debonding at R = 1.54~3 %. The unretrofitted specimen collapsed in shear failure at R = 5 % (displacement 70 mm).
- Design-equation verification: with alpha_1 = 1.51 (mean), the calculated/experimental ratios converged to the 0.91~1.06 level across specimens; with alpha_1 = 0.93 (minimum), predictions fell below the experimental values for all specimens (safe side). With alpha_2 = 0.41 (AIJ mean), calculated values still exceeded experimental values by 15~67 %, confirming the overestimation tendency.
