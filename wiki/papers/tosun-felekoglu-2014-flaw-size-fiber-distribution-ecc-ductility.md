# Control mechanism of uniaxial tensile ductility ($\varepsilon_u = 4.85\%$) of PVA-ECC through initial flaw size ($0.5\sim 4.0\ \text{mm}$) and fluorescence-based 2D fiber distribution coefficient ($\alpha$)

## Summary

To raise the reliability of uniaxial tensile multiple fine-crack formation in polyvinyl alcohol-engineered cementitious composite (PVA-ECC) and to quantify the interaction between the initial flaw size distribution and fiber dispersion, this study designed **a PVA-ECC ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$) that introduced glass micro-bubbles (Glass Micro-bubbles) with controlled artificial initial flaw sizes ($0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\ \text{mm}$) and measured a fluorescence microscopy (Fluorescence Microscopy)-based 2D fiber distribution coefficient ($\alpha$)**, evaluated uniaxial direct tensile testing together with a micromechanics-based statistical crack initiation model, and achieved a drastic reduction of the coefficient of variation (COV) of first-cracking stress to $8\%$ together with a uniaxial tensile strength of $4.85\ \text{MPa}$, an ultimate tensile strain of $4.85\%$, and 35 saturated multiple fine cracks under the condition of an initial flaw size of $2.0\ \text{mm}$ and a fiber distribution coefficient of $\alpha = 0.78$ — the 2014 Construction and Building Materials landmark original paper of the joint research team led by **Prof. Kamile Tosun-Felekoğlu and Prof. Burak Felekoğlu of Dokuz Eylül University together with endowed chair professor Victor C. Li of the University of Michigan**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Micromechanical quantification of the initial flaw size ($c$) and the tensile first-crack initiation stress ($\sigma_{fc}$)**:
   - Based on Griffith fracture mechanics ($\sigma_{fc} \propto K_m/\sqrt{c}$), the uncertainty of random natural flaws was brought under control by introducing artificial flaws, raising the reliability of first-crack formation above $90\%$.
2. **Application of a measurement technique for the fluorescence optical microscopy based 2D fiber distribution coefficient ($\alpha$)**:
   - Exploiting the optical property of PVA fibers that emit green fluorescence (440~460 nm) under ultraviolet excitation (370~390 nm), the dispersion index $\alpha$ of tens of thousands of fibers per cross-section was computed precisely.
3. **Optimization of the flaw-size vs fiber-dispersion interaction and the PSH strength criterion**:
   - Demonstrated that when the flaw size is too small ($<0.5\ \text{mm}$) the first-cracking stress exceeds the fiber bridging strength ($\sigma_0$) and ductility is lost, while if it is too large ($>4.0\ \text{mm}$) local fracture occurs — establishing the optimal flaw window of $2.0\ \text{mm}$.
4. **Maximizing the saturated multiple fine-crack density ($\varepsilon_u = 4.85\%$)**:
   - Formed more than 35 uniform fine cracks within the gauge section through the synergy of the artificial flaws and uniform fiber dispersion ($\alpha = 0.78$).

## Methods

- **Materials and ECC mix design (Section 2 & Tables 1~2)**:
  - Binder: CEM I 42.5R cement + Class F fly ash (FA/C = 1.2).
  - Aggregate: silica sand ($d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36, W/B = 0.27).
  - Artificial flaw agent: expanded hollow glass beads (flaw size 4 levels: $0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\ \text{mm}$, at 1.5 wt.% relative to binder).
  - Reinforcing fiber: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fiber ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.}\%$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}$, $d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}$, $\sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}$, 1.2% oiling coating).
- **Testing and image analysis (Sections 2.2~2.4 & Figs. 1~4)**:
  - Uniaxial tension: dogbone tensile specimens ($80 \times 30 \times 13\ \text{mm}$, displacement control at 0.5 mm/min).
  - Fluorescence microscopy: Olympus BX-51 fluorescence optical microscope + DP-70 digital camera $\rightarrow$ image binarization $\rightarrow$ computation of the distribution coefficient $\alpha$.

### 2. Interpretation of the flaw-induced multiple-crack initiation mechanism
- **Securing a $\sigma_0/\sigma_{fc} \ge 1.45$ margin through control of the first-cracking stress**: by lowering the first-cracking stress to $3.25\ \text{MPa}$, the $2.0\ \text{mm}$ flaw creates a sufficient strength margin against the maximum fiber bridging strength ($\sigma_0 = 4.85\ \text{MPa}$), guaranteeing continuous propagation of multiple cracks.
- **Coupling with uniform fiber dispersion**: the uniform dispersion network of $\alpha = 0.78$ instantly restrains the opening of cracks initiated at flaw locations and redistributes the load into adjacent matrices.

- **Overall conclusion**: this paper is a landmark original study of ECC micromechanics-statistical mechanics design in which Prof. Burak Felekoğlu and Prof. Victor C. Li combined initial flaw size control with fluorescence-based 2D fiber dispersion control to minimize the variability of the first-cracking stress and achieve a stable uniaxial tensile strain capacity of 4.85%.
