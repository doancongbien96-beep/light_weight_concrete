# Development of ECC with limestone powder and blast furnace slag

## Summary

Paper in which Delft Microlab (TU Delft) and Michigan ACE-MRL (with V. C. Li involved) developed a low-cement ECC from locally available Dutch materials. Mixes were designed experimentally within a ternary system of Portland cement (CEM I 42.5 N), limestone powder (LP) and blast furnace slag (BFS), securing the M6 mixture whose cement content is only 15 % of the powder mass (28-day strain capacity 3.3 %, crack width under load 57 μm, compressive strength 38 MPa). The paper further showed that an ECC simplified to a two-component system of BFS cement (CEM III/B 42.5 N) plus limestone powder performed equivalently (strain capacity 3.1 %, compressive strength 40 MPa, crack width 76 μm).

## Key Contributions

1. Direct experimental demonstration of the strain capacity-strength margin correlation: the tensile strain capacities of M1-M6 correlated strongly and linearly with the margin (ultimate tensile strength minus first-cracking strength), R^2 = 0.9802. Representative experimental data behind the PSH design logic of lowering matrix tensile strength while enlarging the bridging strength reserve.
2. Inverse-U optimum of limestone powder content: with increasing LP content, flexural deflection capacity and strain capacity first increased and then decreased. The optimum lay at binder:LP = 1.1 (M3, strain capacity 3.1 %). The mechanism combines (a) the positive effect that LP particles crossing the crack plane are crushed, lowering matrix tensile strength and toughness, against (b) the negative effect that, in excess, the interfaces become overly weak (Li's warning).
3. Effect of the BFS replacement ratio: lowering the PC/BFS ratio from 1.0 (M5) to 0.43 (M6) raised strain capacity from 3.1 % to 3.3 %. ESEM-BSE observation confirmed that fine BFS particles pack the fiber-matrix interface and that dense C-S-H forms instead of Ca(OH)2, improving the frictional bond; slag operates along two axes, reducing matrix toughness while improving the interface.
4. Two-component simplification: taking note that the PC/BFS ratio of M6, 0.43, matches CEM III/B (clinker 29 %, BFS 71 %), ECC was produced using only interground BFS cement plus LP. Performance was equivalent, although crack width increased somewhat from 57 to 76 μm because of differences in particle size distribution.
5. Reliable short direct-tension setup: clamping plates combined with adhesively bonded aluminum tabs, together with eccentricity monitoring by LVDTs mounted on both sides (difference between measurements < 10 %), provided a procedure that quantitatively controls eccentricity error in ultra-ductile testing. Flexural deflection capacity also correlated linearly with strain capacity (R^2 = 0.958).

## Methods

PVA fibers were used at 2 vol % (length 8 mm, diameter 40 μm, tensile strength 1600 MPa, oil coating of 1.2 %); w/p was 0.26-0.27 and high-range superplasticizer dosage was 0.018-0.025. The M45 standard mixture served as the reference mixture. Four-point bending tests (120 x 30 x 10 mm, span 110 mm, displacement rate 0.01 mm/s) and direct tension tests (gauge length 70 mm, 0.005 mm/s) were performed. Crack width under load was computed as peak displacement divided by the average number of cracks (about twice the residual crack width, after Yang et al.). ESEM-BSE imaging followed freeze-drying, epoxy impregnation and 0.25 μm diamond polishing, at 20 kV.

## Results

- Summary of Table 6 (flexural deflection capacity in mm / strain capacity in %): M1 2.0/1.7, M2 3.2/2.4, M3 3.8/3.1, M4 3.3/2.6, M5 3.8/3.1, M6 3.9/3.3.
- The average crack width under load remained below 100 μm in every mixture. Higher LP and BFS contents improved interfacial packing through the filling action of 1-10 μm fine particles, reducing crack width.
- Compressive strength: M3, M5 and M6 all exceeded 38 MPa, satisfying the engineering requirement. Despite a cement content at the level of ordinary concrete (15 %), M6 retained equivalent compressive strength.
- The within-mix standard deviation of strain capacity was below 0.6 % (less than 20 % of the strain capacity), confirming setup reproducibility.
