# Effects of Silica Sand Content (S/B Ratio) on Tensile Strain-Hardening, Concrete Substrate Tensile Bond, and Rebar Pull-Out Behavior of Hybrid Fiber-Reinforced EGC

## Summary

To replace conventional cement-based ECC, cut carbon emissions by over 70 %, and develop permanent repair and retrofitting materials for deteriorated concrete infrastructure, Prof. Jian-Guo Dai's group at Hong Kong PolyU investigated strain-hardening Engineered Geopolymer Composites (EGC). The system utilized GGBS (50 wt.%) and low-calcium fly ash (Class-F FA 50 wt.%) activated with alkali silicates, reinforced with a hybrid fiber system of brass-coated steel fibers ($0.5\ \text{vol.\%}$) and high-modulus PE fibers ($1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$). The authors systematically analyzed the effects of sand-to-binder weight ratio ($\text{S/B} = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0$) on fresh workability, 7 d and 28 d compressive strengths ($55\sim 72\ \text{MPa}$), uniaxial tensile response (DIC microcrack mapping), autogenous shrinkage, **direct pull-off bond strength to concrete substrates, and rebar pull-out bond performance**. Increasing the S/B ratio from 0.3 to 1.0 reduced drying/autogenous shrinkage by 45 %, eliminating restrained tensile microcracks along the repair interface and elevating direct pull-off bond strength from **$1.85\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 3.12\ \text{MPa}$ (a 68.6 % increase)**, shifting the failure mode from interfacial debonding to complete concrete substrate failure. At S/B = 0.5~0.7, rebar pull-out bond strength reached **up to 21.5 MPa** while stably maintaining **3.2 ~ 4.5 % tensile strain capacity**.

## Key Contributions

1. **Quantification of 68.6 % bond strength gain across EGC-concrete repair interfaces with increasing S/B**:
   - Reduced paste volume suppressed shrinkage stresses and increased matrix elastic modulus, improving interfacial shear strain compatibility and increasing pull-off bond strength from **$1.85\ \text{MPa} \rightarrow 3.12\ \text{MPa}$** to induce substrate failure.
2. **Preservation of tensile strain capacity (> 3.5 %) at high sand contents ($\text{S/B} \ge 0.5$) via hybrid fiber reinforcement**:
   - Short steel fibers ($L_f = 13\ \text{mm}$) arrested microcrack initiation while longer PE fibers ($L_f = 18\ \text{mm}$) provided large-deformation crack bridging, maintaining strain-hardening in high-fracture-toughness matrices.
3. **Maximization of EGC-rebar pull-out bond strength (21.5 MPa)**:
   - Synergized high bearing matrix stiffness with fiber tensile confinement to achieve 2.5-fold higher rebar bond strength than standard concrete, enabling 50 % reductions in development length.

## Methods

- **Raw materials and mixture proportions (Tables 1–4, fixed W/B = 0.35)**:
  - Precursors: GGBS 50 wt.% + Class-F fly ash 50 wt.% (Blaine fineness $4200, 3600\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$).
  - Activator: $10\ \text{M}\ \text{NaOH}$ aqueous solution + liquid sodium silicate ($\text{Na}_2\text{O}\cdot 2\text{SiO}_2$, activator-to-binder ratio 0.40).
  - Aggregate: Fine silica sand ($d_{max} = 300\ \mu\text{m}, d_{50} = 150\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0).
  - Hybrid fibers:
    1. Brass-coated straight steel fibers (ST, $L_f = 13\ \text{mm}, d_f = 0.2\ \text{mm}$, tensile strength 2850 MPa, $V_f = 0.5\ \text{vol.\%}$).
    2. High-modulus PE fibers ($L_f = 18\ \text{mm}, d_f = 24\ \mu\text{m}$, tensile strength 3000 MPa, modulus 100 GPa, $V_f = 1.5\ \text{vol.\%}$).
- **Testing procedures**:
  - Uniaxial tension: JSCE dumbbell specimens (100 mm gauge length, $0.5\ \text{mm/min}$) + 2D DIC strain mapping.
  - Pull-off bond testing: BS EN 1542 standard ($\phi 50\ \text{mm}$ cored dollies pulled in tension, substrate concrete $f_c = 42\ \text{MPa}$).
  - Rebar pull-out: RILEM RC6 standard ($\phi 12\ \text{mm}$ HRB400 rebar in cubes, embedment length $5d_b = 60\ \text{mm}$).

### 2. Interface mechanics and structural repair design
- **Shrinkage reduction and bond optimization**: At S/B = 1.0, autogenous shrinkage decreased from $1200\ \mu\varepsilon$ to $650\ \mu\varepsilon$ (a 45 % reduction), eliminating interfacial shear debonding stresses.
- **Optimal repair formulation (S/B = 0.5~0.7)**: Established an engineering design window simultaneously satisfying high tensile ductility ($\varepsilon_{tu} > 3.5\%$) and high substrate bond ($f_{bond} > 2.5\ \text{MPa}$ with substrate failure).

- **Overall conclusion**: This paper demonstrated that adjusting the sand-to-binder ratio in alkali-activated EGC mitigates shrinkage, enhances concrete substrate bond by 68 %, and maintains 4 % tensile strain-hardening for structural repairs.
