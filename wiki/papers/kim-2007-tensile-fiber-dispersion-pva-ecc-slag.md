# Quantitative Evaluation of Fiber Dispersion ($\alpha_{disp}$) and Uniaxial Tensile Strain-Hardening ($\varepsilon_u = 4.20\%$) in PVA-ECC with Ground Granulated Blast-Furnace Slag (GGBS) Using Fluorescence Image Analysis

## Summary

To alleviate fiber clumping caused by the hydrophilicity and fine inter-fiber attraction of PVA microfibers and maximize the reliability of strain-hardening tensile ductility, Prof. Yun Yong Kim (Chungnam National University), Prof. Jin-Keun Kim (KAIST), and Prof. Bang Yeon Lee (Chonnam National University) investigated **PVA-ECC (2.0 vol.% Kuraray REC15) replacing cement with ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS: substitution levels $0\%, 20\%, 40\%, 60\%$, Blaine $4200\ \text{cm}^2/\text{g}$)**. The authors developed a **2D fluorescence image analysis technique using UV dye impregnation to measure grid-based fiber dispersion coefficients ($\alpha_{disp}$)**, correlating these metrics with uniaxial direct tension responses, mini-slump flow, and multiple microcrack densities. At $40\sim 60\%$ GGBS replacement, electrical double-layer adsorption of superplasticizer optimized slurry viscosity, **substantially elevating the fiber dispersion coefficient from $\alpha = 0.52 \rightarrow 0.85$, achieving a uniaxial ultimate tensile strain capacity of $4.20\%$, an average crack spacing of $1.5\ \text{mm}$, and a compressive strength of $48.5\ \text{MPa}$**.

## Key Contributions

1. **First quantitative formulation of fiber dispersion coefficient ($\alpha_{disp}$) via fluorescence image analysis**:
   - Impregnated fluorescent epoxy into polished cross-sections under UV illumination, automatically identifying tens of thousands of PVA fibers and standardizing spatial dispersion statistics.
2. **Clarification of electrochemical fiber dispersion mechanism via GGBS particles**:
   - Glassy slag particles efficiently adsorbed superplasticizer to form electrical double layers, enhancing matrix fluidity and eliminating fiber agglomeration.
3. **Verification of positive linear correlation between dispersion index ($\alpha_{disp}$) and tensile ductility ($\varepsilon_u$)**:
   - Proved that increasing the fiber dispersion coefficient from 0.52 to 0.85 drives a 2.3-fold increase in tensile strain capacity from $1.8\%$ to $4.2\%$.
4. **Development of low-carbon, high-ductility ECC mixture standards**:
   - Replaced up to $60\%$ of cement with GGBS to reduce carbon emissions and raw material costs while maximizing tensile strain-hardening.

## Methods

- **Materials and mixture proportions (Section 2 & Tables 1–3 & Figs. 1–3)**:
  - Binders: Type I Portland cement + GGBS (0%, 20%, 40%, 60% replacement, W/B = 0.45).
  - Fine aggregates: Micro-silica sand ($d_{50} = 110\ \mu\text{m}$, S/B = 0.36, polycarboxylate SP).
  - Reinforcing fibers: Kuraray K-II REC15 PVA fibers ($V_f = 2.0\ \text{vol.\%}$, $L_f = 12\ \text{mm}, d_f = 39\ \mu\text{m}, \sigma_f = 1600\ \text{MPa}, E_f = 42.8\ \text{GPa}$).
- **Fluorescence imaging and direct tension testing (Sections 2.2–2.4 & Figs. 4–6)**:
  - Fluorescence method: Vacuum resin impregnation of polished sections $\rightarrow$ UV excitation and CCD imaging $\rightarrow$ $10 \times 10$ grid division for fiber counting.
  - Dispersion coefficient formula:
    $$\alpha_{disp} = \exp\left[ -\sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \mu)^2}{N}} \Big/ \mu \right]$$
  - Direct tension: Dogbone specimens ($80\ \text{mm}$ gauge length, 0.2 mm/min displacement control).

### 2. Fiber dispersion uniformity and multiple microcracking mechanics
- **Suppression of localized flaw clustering**: In the Slag60 mix, uniform spatial fiber distribution eliminated fiber-deficient zones, generating over 60 saturated multiple microcracks across the gauge length and delivering $4.20\%$ strain capacity.
- **Pozzolanic contribution of slag**: Latent hydraulic reactivity preserved 28 d compressive strength at $46.0\ \text{MPa}$ while maintaining robust interfacial fiber-matrix friction.

- **Overall conclusion**: This landmark paper demonstrated that substituting up to 60 % GGBS in PVA-ECC substantially improves fiber dispersion ($\alpha = 0.85$) and achieves a high tensile strain capacity of 4.20 %.
