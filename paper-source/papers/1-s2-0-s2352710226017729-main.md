# Synergistic enhancement of strength and thermal insulation in 3D printed lightweight concrete using cold-bonded core-shell cenospheres

## Abstract from PDF

Lightweight concrete offers advantages in reducing structural weight and improving building energy efficiency. However, its application in 3D printing imposes greater demands on rheo - logical behavior and mechanical strength to address the interlayer weakness inherent to layered deposition. This study proposes a material design strategy based on cold-bonded core-shell fly ash cenospheres (CSFACs) to balance the competing requirements of printability, density, strength, and thermal insulation. Rheological results revealed that replacing fly ash cenospheres (FACs) with CSFACs reduced both static and dynamic yield stresses, thereby improving extrusion behavior. The mixture containing 30% CSFAC replacement achieved a thermal conductivity of 0.622 W/m ⋅ K, close to that of F100 at 0.598 W/m ⋅ K, while increasing the compressive strength from 13.4 MPa to 22.2 MPa. As a result, its specific strength to thermal conductivity ratio was the highest within the tested mixtures, demonstrating improved structural efficiency while main - taining thermal insulation. X-ray computed tomography analysis further revealed that partial CSFAC replacement promoted a more homogeneous pore distribution and increased the pro - portion of spherical pores within the printed filament. The proposed core-shell aggregate design shows potential for balancing density, strength, and thermal insulation in 3D printed lightweight concrete under the investigated conditions.
