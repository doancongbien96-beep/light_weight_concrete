# Lightweight concrete production with glass foam for complete aggregate replacement

## Abstract from PDF

This work proposes the use of waste glass to produce foamed glass materials, employing an industrial-grade MnO2 foaming agent, as a lightweight substitute for the complete replacement of natural coarse aggregates in lightweight concrete. A 23 full factorial design evaluated the effects of foaming agent content (4.0–7.0 wt. %), heating rate (10–15 ◦C/min), and burning temperature (820 or 860 ◦C). Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) showed that interactions between heating rate and burning temperature, as well as between foaming agent content and burning temperature, significantly affected density reduction. The chemical composition was determined using X-ray fluorescence (XRF) and Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS), with LIBS revealing the diffusion of manganese ions into the silica matrix and a heterogeneous distribution of manganese depending on the optical appearance of the foamed regions. Particle size distribution was measured using static light scattering (SLS), and powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) confirmed the presence of a glass-ceramic material with crystalline phases resulting from the decomposition of a manganese compound. Increasing heat exposure time reduced apparent density. Concrete incorporating foamed glass, produced under optimal conditions, achieved a compressive strength of 29.15 MPa after 28 days, offering a sustainable and innovative alternative for construction with the replacement of the natural coarse aggregate and using a foaming agent without CO2 release.
