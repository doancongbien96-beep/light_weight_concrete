# Assessing the mechanical, durability, and leaching performance of lightweight concrete made of tailings-based lightweight aggregates

## Abstract from PDF

In recent years, numerous studies have explored the reuse of mine tailings (MTs) in producing sustainable construction materials, including ceramics, supplementary cementitious materials, clinkers, and geopolymers. A more recent innovation in this field is the development of geo - polymer lightweight aggregates (LWAs) using MTs. While a few studies have investigated the use of tailings-based geopolymer LWAs in lightweight concrete (LWC), they have primarily focused on mechanical properties; critical aspects such as durability and the leaching potential of heavy metals have received limited attention. To further address this research gap, the present study evaluated the bulk density, compressive strength, freeze-thaw durability, and leaching behavior of LWC incorporating tailings-based geopolymer LWAs. Four concrete mixtures were designed using various combinations of natural aggregates, artificial commercial LWAs, and tailings-based geopolymer LWAs. The results showed that LWC containing tailings-based geopolymer LWAs achieved compressive strengths exceeding 50 MPa and exhibited outstanding freeze-thaw resis - tance, maintaining a relative dynamic modulus of elasticity (RDME) above 94% and mass retention above 99% after 300 cycles. Furthermore, the leachability of heavy metals remained very low over 63 days, with barium showing the highest concentration but with cumulative levels below 0.475 mg/L. These findings demonstrate the potential of tailings-based geopolymer LWAs for use in concrete applications.
