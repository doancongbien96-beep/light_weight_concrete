# Novel lightweight concrete with structural and energy storage integration based on low-grade diatomite phase change aggregates

## Abstract from PDF

Herein, a novel structural – energy storage integrated lightweight concrete (ESLACM) based on low-grade diat - omite phase change aggregates was designed. The stability of ESLACM was evaluated through mechanical and thermal performance tests as well as long-term high – low-temperature cycling. In addition, its practical perfor - mance was assessed through a three-month thermal response test under natural environmental conditions. Notably, the phase change aggregates exhibited a phase change temperature of 21.80 ◦ C, phase change enthalpy of 39.11 J/g, thermal conductivity of 0.82 W/m ⋅ K, and cylinder compressive strength of 5.84 MPa. The syner - gistic incorporation of silicon carbide – coated phase change aggregates and 0.2 % carbon fibers reduced microcracks in the interfacial transition zone and pores in the cement matrix of the ESLACM microstructure, resulting in a compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, and thermal conductivity of 37.04 MPa, 4.83 MPa, and 1.69 W/m ⋅ K, respectively. After 450 high – low-temperature cycles, the properties of ESLACM remained stable, meeting the requirements for structural concrete used in buildings. Furthermore, compared with shale ceramsite concrete panels, ESLACM panels applied in a house model located in a low-latitude plateau monsoon climate reduced indoor temperature by 3.83 ◦ C (on average) during hot periods and increased it by 1.67 ◦ C (on average) during cold periods while maintaining long-term peak-shaving and valley-filling performance. How - ever, this thermal regulation function was weakened under conditions of large diurnal temperature differences or during the rainy season. Overall, this research provides a new material for the low-carbon operation of building structures in low-latitude plateau monsoon regions.
