# Engineering properties of sustainable high strength lightweight concrete with recycled fibers

## Abstract from PDF

This study developed a lightweight concrete (LWC) reinforced with recycled tire steel fibers (RTSF), aiming to explore its potential as a material for prefabricated components or steel-concrete composite structures, as well as a repair or replacement material for existing buildings. Twenty-seven groups of mixed proportions were designed, and a comparative analysis was conducted on the material properties of those made with ISF and RTSF. The workability, dry density, compressive strength, elastic modulus, splitting tensile strength, flexural toughness, compressive toughness, and water absorption of LWC were tested. The results show that the LWC mixed with RTSF exhibits good workability; when the RTSF content exceeds 0.5 %, the compressive strength and crack resistance of the concrete are significantly enhanced; when the RTSF content reaches 0.75 %, the LWC has good flexural toughness. With the increase of RTSF content and the strength grade of LWC, the compressive toughness of LWC has been significantly improved. Economic analysis and environmental footprint analysis indicate that the use of RTSF to produce LWC has good cost-effectiveness and environmental sustainability. If the production process standards of RTSF can be optimized and standardized, it is believed that its application range will be further expanded.
