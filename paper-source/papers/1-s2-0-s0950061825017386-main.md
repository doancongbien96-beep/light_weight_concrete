# Innovative energy-efficient lightweight concrete with improved thermal and mechanical properties using PCM-impregnated LECA aggregates

## Abstract from PDF

The rising demand for sustainable construction materials has highlighted phase-change materials (PCMs) as an effective solution for enhancing building thermal performance. However, research on using PCMs in lightweight concrete (LWC) remains limited, leaving much of its potential unexplored, particularly in challenging environ - mental conditions. For the first time, this study aims to develop an innovative LWC with superior thermal and mechanical properties by incorporating polyethylene glycol (PEG) 600 and PEG 1000 as PCMs into the light - weight expanded clay aggregate (LECA). A series of novel tests was designed to evaluate the performance of this concrete under simulated sunlight and humidity conditions representative of warm, humid climates. Crucial parameters were also analyzed, including thermal performance, conductivity, water absorption, and mechanical properties. Then, the concrete mix designs were optimized by varying the LECA content and the properties of PCM. The results revealed that the water absorption increased substantially, rising by 119 % at a 75 % LECA replacement level. However, this was reduced to 58 % with the addition of the PCM. Conversely, thermal conductivity dropped sharply by 63 %. Adding PCMs further enhanced this reduction, achieving a total improvement of up to 70 % in insulation properties. This study also addresses the lack of predictive models for the thermal conductivity of LWC with PCMs. A new model, based on the content of lightweight aggregates, is proposed and validated against experimental results and current codes, offering a reliable approach for opti - mizing thermal performance in sustainable construction.
