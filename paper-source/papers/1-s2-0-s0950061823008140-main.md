# Utilization of pumice as aggregate in the concrete: A state of art

## Abstract from PDF

Pumice that formed during the solidification of molten lava under rapid cooling is a natural sponge-like light - weight aggregate. In this study, it was aimed to enlighten the readers about the developments in light of available studies focused on the use of pumice as aggregate (PA) in concrete for sustainable construction. Use of PA in concrete production has recently attracted attention due to its superior properties such as low unit weight that allows the construction of light buildings with reduction the load applied to the load-bearing structural elements. Initially, the current study is established based on introducing the chemical, physical and microstructural properties of PA. Thereafter, performance evaluation focused on mechanical properties including compressive strength (CS), flexural strength (FS), splitting tensile strength (STS)) and durability in case of water absorption (WA), permeability, sorptivity, sulfate resistance (SR), freeze – thaw resistance (FTR), abrasion resistance (AR), high-temperature resistance (HTR), drying-shrinkage (DS) and alkali-silica reaction (ASR) are presented. The findings showed that using of PA into concrete has certain benefits including significant increase in workability, HTR, SR and decrease in density of concrete and ASR expansion. However, using PA has certain negative effects on concrete properties such as a decrease in FTR, AR, CS, FS and STS an increase in WA and sorptivity and DS. It is clear from the current review that there is a necessity for more investigation on usability of PA in concrete production. Especially, there is a significant gap on investigation based on mechanical, durability and micro - structural analyzes whether the using of PA is feasible in concrete. Considering the basis of the study subject, review strategies and initiatives can be used to provide insight into future experiments and areas that will be highly beneficial for research into using of PA in concrete by research centers and construction industries.
